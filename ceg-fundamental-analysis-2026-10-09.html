Investment thesis

Scarce clean baseload, sold forward to the buyers who need it most.

The AI-power thesis is no longer a forecast. Constellation has converted it into signed contracts with investment-grade customers — while keeping the most valuable part of the fleet, nuclear baseload, at the center of the economics.

The Google package signed October 6 covers roughly 3.59 GW: 890 MW of new nuclear output under a 20-year PPA and about 2.7 GW of existing PJM capacity under a 15-year supply agreement. Amazon’s September 30 Calvert Cliffs contract adds 690 MW over 20 years, including roughly 190 MW of uprated capacity.

That combination matters. Long-duration contracts reduce merchant exposure, customer quality improves confidence in collections, and reactor uprates monetize irreplaceable sites at a fraction of new-build timelines. Calpine, closed January 7, broadens the platform to roughly 55 GW with dispatchable gas, geothermal, storage and a large retail book.

The restraint is valuation and cash conversion. At roughly 24× forward earnings, CEG commands a sharp premium to Vistra and Talen. Levered free cash flow is negative, post-Calpine leverage is about 3.0× EBITDA, and the 890 MW Google uprate does not begin producing power until 2028.

Desk verdict

Buy, conviction 6/10, $345 target. The strategic asset is exceptional, but the stock’s 12.2% Google-day move pulled forward part of the contract value.

Recommended gate: add at or below $260 toward roughly 1% of portfolio. The gate is not installed. Buy the fade toward the pre-Google price, not the pop.

What has to be true

Calpine must convert scale into cash, contracted nuclear growth must arrive close to schedule and budget, and PJM rulemaking must preserve attractive economics without creating a political backlash. The thesis is not “power demand rises”; it is “CEG captures that demand after financing the buildout.”

Business & moat

A merchant generator with an infrastructure monopoly hiding in plain sight.

Constellation is not a rate-regulated utility. Its earnings depend on power and capacity prices, hedge outcomes, state credits and contracts — but the nuclear fleet gives it scarce, licensed, grid-connected baseload that cannot be replicated on a normal corporate planning horizon.

Nuclear scarcity

Twenty-one reactors, high capacity factors and long remaining license lives form the core moat. The fleet produced 88.8 TWh in the first half of 2026 at a 92.7% capacity factor.

Calpine breadth

The $21.8B company-stated transaction added roughly 23 GW of gas, geothermal, solar, battery and retail assets, lifting combined capacity to about 55 GW.

Customer reach

The enlarged retail platform serves roughly 2.5M customer accounts and approximately 80% of the Fortune 100, creating more ways to pair generation with load.

The moat is physical, contractual and regulatory

A reactor uprate extends output from an existing site with interconnection, operating staff and licensing in place. That is economically and temporally different from a greenfield reactor. Google’s 890 MW program spans 11 reactors and requires more than $4.3B of investment, but first power is expected from 2028 rather than a decade-plus new-build cycle.

The fleet is also becoming less purely merchant. Microsoft, Meta, Amazon, Google and Walmart now anchor a portfolio of long-term arrangements. About 920 MW of new long-term nuclear PPAs were signed in Q2 2026 alone, with an average duration of roughly 18.5 years and full ramp by 2032.

Deal-value discipline. The report uses Constellation’s $21.8B figure for Calpine. Press totals range from $16.4B equity value to about $26.6B enterprise value including assumed debt; those figures are not interchangeable.
Financial pulse

Adjusted earnings are rising; reported earnings are noisy.

The Q2 headline was deliberately uncomfortable: revenue rose 23%, yet GAAP net income fell 39%. Adjusted operating EPS increased 34%. Investors have to separate operating progress from mark-to-market, merger and outage effects without dismissing the cash-flow warning.

Why the GAAP bridge matters

Q2’s 6.8% GAAP net margin was roughly half the year-earlier level. Non-cash fair-value adjustments and acquisition costs explain part of the gap, while 86 planned refueling outage days versus 41 and lower quarterly ZEC recognition added operating noise.

Why cash is the proof point

Levered free cash flow was approximately negative $6.6B on the cited trailing basis. With a heavy capex plan and Calpine debt on the balance sheet, the quality of adjusted EPS will be judged by conversion over 2026–27.

Guidance has moved the right way

Management raised FY2026 adjusted operating EPS guidance to $11.50–$12.50 from $11.00–$12.00. The midpoint was above the FactSet consensus at the time. October analyst compilations place FY2026 EPS near $12.06–$12.38 and revenue in a wide $33.6B–$36.4B range; these are analyst estimates, not company guidance.

Valuation

The premium is deserved. It is not free.

At $298.07, CEG trades near 24× forward earnings — well above Vistra and Talen. The $345 target values FY2027 consensus EPS of $13.31 at roughly 25.9×, a premium that requires the nuclear-contract story to remain intact.

Target construction

$13.31 FY2027 consensus EPS × 25.9× = approximately $345. The multiple sits above peers to recognize the 21-reactor moat and signed hyperscaler demand, but below any valuation that assumes all long-dated contract value converts without delay or cost pressure.

Entry discipline

The target supports the Buy rating; the current setup does not support chasing. A $260 gate would place entry near 19.5× FY2027 consensus EPS and near the pre-Google-deal level.

Risks & disconfirmers

The binding risks are political, financial and temporal.

High · regulatory

PJM’s rules are not settled

FERC suspended the proposed reliability backstop procurement for five months on September 29, to February 28, 2027, over cost allocation. A separate colocation framework remains unresolved; the reported October 12 BYONC vote outcome was not available as of this report.

High · political

Power affordability can reset the economics

Utility-bill increases above 20% in some areas have intensified pressure from governors and regulators. FERC’s position that data centers should bear their infrastructure costs is politically logical, but it also defines the dispute delaying PJM’s backstop.

High · cash flow

Calpine has to prove itself in cash

Total debt is about $24.7B, net debt is approximately 3.0× EBITDA and levered FCF was negative on the cited trailing basis. The integration case needs the promised annual cash contribution to materialize while growth spending stays high.

Medium · execution

The contract is signed; the power comes later

Google’s 890 MW of new nuclear output begins in 2028. Delays, cost inflation, outage overruns or weaker-than-expected uprate performance would reduce the value of contracts whose pricing remains undisclosed.

Medium · valuation

A premium multiple leaves less room for surprise

The stock’s October 6 jump took CEG from $267.62 to $300.40 in one session. At roughly 24× forward earnings, disappointing contract economics or a regulatory setback can compress the multiple before earnings estimates catch up.

Thesis-break checklist

Reassess if Calpine fails to improve free-cash-flow conversion through 2027; if PJM rules materially impair data-center contracting or capacity revenue; if Google/Amazon projects slip beyond stated windows; or if net leverage remains near 3× despite rising adjusted earnings.

Capital allocation

Growth first, balance sheet second — with buybacks still in the mix.

Management’s framework funds high-return uprates, storage and restart work, repurchases shares, grows the dividend and deleverages. The low payout ratio makes the dividend secure on earnings, but it is not the reason to own the stock. The debate is whether repurchases should remain aggressive while levered FCF is negative and growth capex is peaking.

Credit remains investment grade: S&P BBB+ and Moody’s Baa1, both with stable outlooks in the cited materials. That is a competitive advantage in capital-intensive contracting, not a license to ignore the post-Calpine debt load.

Capital-allocation tension. Management’s 20%+ base EPS growth frame through 2029 excludes buyback accretion. That creates upside if cash conversion arrives — and risk if repurchases compete with deleveraging before it does.
