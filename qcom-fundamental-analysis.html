1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Qualcomm is a maturing handset-and-licensing cash engine being repriced as a diversification story. The legacy earnings driver is in a cyclical trough: Q3 FY2026 handset revenue fell 20% y/y on industry-wide memory supply constraints and pricing, and management expects FY2026 Android handset revenue down ~20% vs FY2025 — a greater-than-$1.50 annual EPS headwind R. Offsetting this, automotive grew 61% y/y in Q3 FY2026 — its 23rd consecutive quarter of double-digit growth R — and a newly credible data-center roadmap (FY2029 revenue target of $15B+, a Meta multi-generation CPU agreement, custom-silicon ramp with hyperscalers) underpins the June 24, 2026 Investor Day targets: $40B of non-handset revenue by FY2029 (~2× the prior $22B target), ~40% FY25–29 CAGR, non-handsets ≈ two-thirds of QCT revenue, and non-GAAP EPS above $18 R.

At $198.27 the stock trades at ~17.5× TTM non-GAAP EPS ($11.36) C and ~19× TTM free cash flow (5.2% FCF yield) C — undemanding versus AI-infrastructure peers, but the multiple fairly embeds two live headwinds: (1) the Apple modem step-down is now visibly accelerating — Qualcomm said on July 29, 2026 that modem share for the fall-2026 iPhone launch would be "materially lower" than its prior 20% planning assumption, with the step-down in Apple product revenues accelerating from Q4 FY2026 R; and (2) QCT EBT margins are compressing on mix and input-cost inflation (31% in Q1 FY2026 → 26% in Q3, guided 23–25% in Q4) R. The stock has run +54% over six months into this report E; much of the Investor Day re-rating is already priced.

Thesis O: the fundamental debate is whether FY2027 non-handset growth (+60%+ guided R) can arrive fast enough to offset Apple attrition and a still-soft handset cycle. Consensus FY2027 revenue of ~$44.4–44.9B E implies only ~3.5% growth — notably more conservative than management's framework — so estimate-revision breadth is the swing factor. At ~18× our FY2027E non-GAAP EPS estimate of ~$11.40, the shares are roughly fairly valued: the diversification ramp is real but back-half-loaded, and near-term margin and Apple headwinds are real too. Collect the 1.86% dividend, let the $20B buyback authorization work against the share count, and reassess on the Q4 FY2026 print. Hold with a constructive bias; add on expectation-driven weakness rather than chasing the run.

Bottom line: HOLD with a $205 12-month target (~3% upside from $198.27) = 18× our FY2027E non-GAAP EPS estimate of ~$11.40 O/C — a market multiple on normalized earnings, reflecting that the diversification re-rating is largely in the price while the FY2027–29 ramp must still be proven. Total expected return including the dividend: ~5%.

2. Business Overview

  • Qualcomm Incorporated (NASDAQ: QCOM), San Diego. Two reportable segments plus QSI (strategic investments, excluded from non-GAAP) R.
  • QCT (Qualcomm CDMA Technologies) — semiconductors: Snapdragon application processors/modems (handsets), automotive (Snapdragon Digital Chassis: cockpit, connectivity, ADAS), IoT (consumer, industrial, networking, XR/PC/wearables), and now data center (Dragonfly portfolio: CPUs, AI accelerators, custom silicon, connectivity) R.
  • QTL (Qualcomm Technology Licensing) — patent licensing; structurally ~70% EBT margins R; scales with 4G/5G device units R.
  • Licensing remains litigious by nature: FY2025 non-GAAP revenue excludes $143M of licensing revenue from a dispute settlement, not allocated to segment results R.

4. Segment Deep Dive: QCT & QTL

QCT — the diversification engine R

  • Handsets: FY2025's $27,793M (+12%) record was followed by FY2026 payback. Q3 FY2026 handsets of $5,086M (−20%) reflected memory-driven OEM inventory cuts. Management expects FY2026 Android handset revenue down ~20% vs FY2025 — a >$1.50 annual EPS headwind. Partial offset: premium-tier demand is intact (Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy; record Q1 handset revenue of ~$7.8B on flagship launches).
  • Automotive: the standout compounder — Q3 FY2026 $1,588M (+61%), the 23rd consecutive quarter of double-digit growth. Annualized revenue run-rate exceeded $5B in Q2 FY2026, expected >$6B by end of FY2026. Design-win momentum: expanded multiyear Stellantis award (Snapdragon Digital Chassis, Ride Pilot ADAS stack for L2+ across the global portfolio); Volkswagen Group letter of intent signed in Q1 FY2026. No current aggregate design-win pipeline dollar figure was disclosed — do not use stale pipeline numbers O.
  • IoT: Q3 FY2026 $1,830M (+9%), driven by consumer and networking; includes XR/PC/wearables (Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 powers Samsung/Google intelligent eyewear; Snapdragon START turnkey wearable toolkit).
  • QCT EBT margins: 30% (FY2025) → 31% (Q1 FY2026) → 27% (Q2) → 26% (Q3) — compressing on mix (handset decline) and input-cost inflation; Q4 FY2026 guide 23–25%.

QTL — the high-margin annuity R

  • Revenue: FY2025 $5,582M (flat); Q3 FY2026 $1,278M (−3%). EBT margins: 72% (FY2025), 77% (Q1 FY2026), 72% (Q2), 69% (Q3); Q4 FY2026 guide 68–72%.
  • Scales with 4G/5G device units; Apple's patent license was extended through March 2027 (disclosed Feb 2024).

7. Strategic Initiatives: Data Center & Apple

Data center — the largest growth opportunity R

  • Dragonfly portfolio (unveiled with Q3 FY2026): Dragonfly C1000 CPU (production 2H 2028), Dragonfly AI300 inference accelerator, AI250, High Bandwidth Compute (HBC) memory architecture, connectivity, plus custom silicon — four product lines ramping in stages toward the $15B+ FY2029 data-center revenue target.
  • Roadmap: connectivity ramp began FY2026 ($65B 2029 TAM) → custom silicon Q1 FY2027 ($115B TAM) → AI accelerator 2H FY2027 ($680B TAM) → CPU 2H FY2028 ($200B TAM); company frames a $1T+ total 2029 TAM.
  • Customer traction: Meta multi-generation CPU agreement (Dragonfly C1000 for Meta's compute footprint); Hugging Face expanded relationship (16M developers onboarding onto Dragonfly); "leading hyperscaler" custom-silicon engagement on track for initial shipments later in calendar 2026 (per Q2 FY2026 commentary); two hyperscaler customers each >$1B revenue targeted in custom silicon.
  • M&A: Alphawave (high-speed connectivity IP) and Ventana (RISC-V) completed in Q1 FY2026; Modular Inc. completed in Q3 FY2026 — an AI-native software platform (Mojo, MAX, Modular Cloud continue as open-ecosystem brands) intended as the software foundation for generative/agentic AI from edge to cloud.
  • FY2026E data-center revenue is only $0.3B — the ramp is almost entirely ahead of investors; FY2027 opportunity >$1B.

Apple relationship R

  • Modem supply agreement (Sept 2023) covers smartphone launches in 2024, 2025, and 2026; Qualcomm had planned on ~20% of 2026 iPhone modem supply, with Apple transitioning to in-house modems over time.
  • 29 Jul 2026 update: "we now expect an acceleration in the step down of Apple product revenues, starting in the fourth fiscal quarter, as our modem share for the upcoming iPhone launch is expected to be materially lower than our prior estimate of 20%" — attributed in part to Qualcomm's own supply constraints (memory).
  • Patent license: Apple exercised its option extending the global patent license through March 2027 (disclosed Feb 2024). The licensing annuity is more durable than the modem revenue.
  • No dollar figure for Apple product revenue, and no modem-supply terms beyond the 2026 launches, were disclosed in reviewed materials — do not speculate O. Qualcomm frames FY2027 non-handset growth as replacing total FY2026 Apple product revenues.

Snapdragon / on-device AI R

  • Premium handset positioning intact (Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy; record Q1 FY2026 handset revenue on flagship launches); Samsung collaboration expanded across phones, watches, and eyewear; PC (Oryon-based) and XR momentum cited at Investor Day. Near-term handset economics are dominated by the memory cycle, not share.

8. Risks

  • Apple concentration & modem attrition R/O: the in-house-modem transition is visibly accelerating (fall-2026 iPhone share "materially below" 20%). The open question is the pace of the step-down versus the FY2027 non-handset ramp — management asserts the latter replaces the former, but the dollar figures on both sides are undisclosed.
  • Memory cycle / input costs R: industry-wide memory supply constraints and pricing, plus broad input-cost inflation (wafer fab, assembly, test, advanced packaging), are compressing QCT margins (26% in Q3, guided 23–25% in Q4). Pricing actions are expected to help "gradually."
  • China exposure R: Qualcomm's own forward-looking-statement risk factors cite "a significant portion of our business being concentrated in China, which is exacerbated by U.S./China trade and national security tensions." No quantified China revenue split or Huawei/export-control impact figure was verified — do not cite one.
  • QTL licensing model R: structurally high-margin but perennially contested (FY2025 included a $143M dispute-settlement licensing item; a legacy 2019 EC fine surfaced in Q3 FY2025 supplemental items). Key license renewals and OEM royalty pressure are evergreen risks.
  • Execution on data center O: the $15B FY2029 target and >$18 non-GAAP EPS target embed a steep, back-half-loaded ramp from a $0.3B FY2026 base against entrenched incumbents. Custom-silicon concentration (two hyperscalers) is both validation and customer-concentration risk.
  • Tax/GAAP noise R: OBBB-related valuation-allowance items swung GAAP EPS by ±$5.7B across FY2025–FY2026; screen GAAP multiples with care.

10. Catalysts & What to Watch

  • Q4 FY2026 results — expected early November 2026 E (estimated; FY2025 results were reported 5 Nov 2025 — not confirmed by Qualcomm IR): the print that tests the Apple step-down, the China-OEM handset rebound ("double-digit sequential growth" guided), and the QCT margin trough (23–25% guide).
  • Memory normalization: any easing in DRAM supply/pricing is the single biggest near-term EPS lever (>$1.50 annual EPS impact quantified for FY2026) R.
  • Data-center proof points: hyperscaler custom-silicon shipments (flagged for late CY2026), the FY2027 >$1B revenue opportunity, and any incremental customer disclosures.
  • Capital allocation: pace of the $20B buyback authorization against the 1,057M share count; dividend trajectory.
  • Licensing: QTL renewals and any new disputes; QTL margin trajectory (68–72% Q4 guide).
