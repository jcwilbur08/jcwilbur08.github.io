01 · Desk call

The target survives. The multiple does not deserve an upgrade.

Maintain Hold, 5/10 and the $360 twelve-month target. The raised net-interest-income outlook and raised expense outlook roughly net out; the Q3 miss does not break the thesis, but neither does it justify paying more than the current premium valuation.

JPM remains the strongest large-bank franchise in the portfolio: scale advantages, broad revenue diversity, benign credit and a 20% reported return on tangible common equity. The print reinforced the durability of the core engine even as headline EPS and revenue missed.

The target remains anchored near 2.9× estimated September 2027 tangible book value per share of roughly $125. At the October 13 close, the implied 8.6% price upside is insufficient for a Buy under the standing conviction-led framework.

Portfolio verdict

Hold the 357.851-share position. No add gate and no dashboard gate change. Trim discipline remains unchanged: consider trimming only above roughly $365 or if the portfolio weight rises above 8%.

The quarterly dividend remains $1.65 per share; capital support now leans more heavily on retained earnings than repurchases.

02 · Quarter scorecard

The miss was narrow, visible and mostly above the credit line.

GAAP EPS missed by $0.36. Even after excluding the discretionary $658 million pre-tax securities loss, EPS missed by about $0.18. The shortfall came through fee revenue and operating leverage—not a deterioration in borrower health.

Quality of the miss

The $658 million pre-tax securities loss reduced EPS by about $0.18, but excluding it does not turn the quarter into a beat. The clean reading is not “one-time item only”; it is a modest core miss cushioned by better NII and credit.

03 · Franchise read-through

Equities surged; fixed income broke the streak.

The diversified model worked, but the mix mattered. Record third-quarter equities performance could not fully offset a fixed-income miss and softer underwriting.

Consumer & Community

Revenue reached $21.2 billion, up 9%, and net income rose 13% to $5.7 billion. Card spend increased 10%; 650,000 net new checking accounts and 4% deposit growth point to continued franchise momentum.

Commercial & Investment Bank

Revenue rose 14% to $22.7 billion. Equities jumped 49% to $4.95 billion, but FICC fell 4% to $5.37 billion versus $6.0 billion expected—the first decline in more than two years.

Asset & Wealth

Revenue rose 14% to $6.9 billion and net income increased 11% to $1.8 billion. Assets under management reached $5.21 trillion, up 13%, modestly below the $5.27 trillion consensus.

Credit did not crack

Provision was lower than expected, net charge-offs fell 15% year over year, and nonperforming assets of $10.0 billion were better than the $11.29 billion estimate. Management said the $539 million reserve build reflected new wholesale lending activity, not downgrades or a weaker economic view.

04 · Guidance & reaction

The guidance rescued the open. It could not rescue the close.

JPM raised full-year NII to roughly $106.2 billion and introduced a 2027 NII ex-Markets central case of roughly $107 billion—both about $1 billion above prevailing estimates. That is genuine earnings support. The market still faded the relief rally.

What improved

FY26 total NII guidance rose to about $106.2 billion from $105.5 billion. FY26 NII ex-Markets rose to about $97.2 billion, and the preliminary 2027 NII ex-Markets central case of roughly $107 billion sits above the roughly $104.5 billion Street view.

What offset it

FY26 adjusted expense guidance rose to roughly $108.6 billion from $107.5 billion. For 2027, management said consensus near $114 billion was not far from its preliminary view, while the budget remained open and AI investment was still a moving part.

Q4 guide: NII ex-Markets of roughly $25.5 billion, total NII of roughly $28 billion and expenses of roughly $27.5 billion.

05 · Capital allocation

Price discipline—or a missing support.

Repurchases collapsed from $6.7 billion in Q2 to roughly $1 billion in Q3. Jamie Dimon framed the move as rational capital hierarchy; the more defensive read is that lower buybacks and a 30-basis-point CET1 decline remove two supports at once.

Management’s hierarchy

Dividend first. Organic growth second. Buybacks last, especially when the share price is high. With roughly $30 billion of estimated excess capital, management argues that reinvesting in the franchise can earn more than the roughly 6% earnings yield available from repurchasing stock.

The new $50 billion authorization remains in place, so the issue is cadence, not capacity. But an authorization is not a commitment.

Desk interpretation

The slower cadence is both valuation discipline and a real change in shareholder-return support. It reinforces Hold: management is itself reluctant to retire shares aggressively around the current valuation.

The $1.65 quarterly dividend remains the steadier leg of capital return.

Data caveat: public coverage reported Q3 repurchases from $664 million net to $1.6 billion, with $1.06 billion cited as gross from the financial supplement. All sources agree on the direction: repurchases fell roughly 80–90% from Q2. This report uses “roughly $1 billion” and does not imply false precision.
06 · Risks & watchpoints

The next debate is operating leverage, not loan losses.

Primary risk

Expense growth outruns revenue

Expenses rose 12% against 8% revenue growth. If the 2027 cost base lands near $114 billion without enough NII and fee growth, the current premium multiple becomes harder to defend.

Capital risk

Repurchases remain depressed

Another quarter near the Q3 buyback cadence would lower the per-share growth and capital-return support embedded in the prior thesis.

Revenue risk

FICC weakness broadens

Q3 weakness spanned commodities, rates, credit and public finance. A single soft quarter is manageable; a multi-quarter trend would challenge the assumption that equities strength and advisory recovery can carry fees.

Rate risk

Deposit costs absorb the NII upside

Deposit costs increased seven basis points quarter over quarter versus roughly one basis point expected in Goldman’s sales-and-trading desk read. The higher NII guide is bullish, but deposit repricing remains the key offset.
