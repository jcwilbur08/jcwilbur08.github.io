1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Disney's fiscal third quarter was the clearest evidence yet that the turnaround is working on both engines at once: revenue of $25.25B (+7% y/y), total segment operating income of $5.6B (+21%), and adjusted EPS of $2.06 (+28%, ahead of the $1.88 consensus). The streaming business — a $1.5B annualized loss machine as recently as fiscal Q4 2022 — posted $712M of quarterly operating income, more than double the prior year, at a ~13% margin. The Experiences segment posted record fiscal-Q3 revenue of nearly $10B (+10%) with operating income up 20%. Management raised the buyback to at least $9B and expects double-digit EPS growth in fiscal 2027.

The stock, at $106.15, has gone nowhere for a decade — which is precisely the opportunity. It trades at ~15× forward earnings against a street consensus target of ~$127–128 (~20% upside) and Morningstar's $125 fair value (4 stars). New CEO Josh D'Amaro, in the seat since March 2026, is executing visibly: an ESPN spin-off quashed in favor of fixing it, a YouTube veteran installed to run direct-to-consumer, a first-ever CTO hired from the AI world, and 1,000 marketing jobs cut. We initiate at BUY with a $125 12-month target — ~18% price upside plus a ~1.4% dividend yield.

The "One Disney" strategy under new leadership coordinates franchises, data, and technology across physical and digital touchpoints.

4. Segment Analysis

Entertainment — the turnaround is real

Entertainment operating income of $1.68B (+64%) is the segment's best print of the turnaround era. Two drivers: streaming profitability (below) and a studio rebound — Toy Story 5 crossed $1B at the global box office (reported) and arrives on Disney+ by year-end 2026, feeding the content flywheel. The drags are familiar: linear ad revenue fell 1% to $1.63B on lower rates, and content sales fell 6% to $1.60B. The segment's mix is shifting decisively from linear to streaming — pain in the transition, profit on the other side.

Experiences — compounding at 30% margins

The crown jewel delivered: record fiscal-Q3 revenue of $9.97B (+10%), operating income of $3.02B (+20%), global guest counts +4%, and domestic per-capita spending +4%, with new Disney Cruise Line ships adding capacity. Operating margins near 30% through the first nine months of the fiscal year (reported) make this the highest-quality earnings stream in media. The multi-year, multi-billion-dollar parks investment program announced in 2023 is the forward growth lever — and the oft-cited statistic that for every park guest there are ten-plus people with Disney affinity who never visit frames the addressable expansion.

Sports — the soft spot, being fixed rather than spun

The Sports segment was the quarter's laggard: revenue edged ~2% higher while operating income slipped ~5% on higher programming costs — ESPN's linear decline remains the structural headwind. The strategic decision matters more than the quarter: D'Amaro quashed a proposed ESPN spin-off and instead committed to fixing profitability in-house, expecting mid-single-digit sports operating-income growth in 2026. ESPN's direct-to-consumer future (including the ESPN tile on Disney+ and the 53-country European/APAC expansion) is earlier in the monetization cycle than Disney+/Hulu — this is a 2027–2028 story, and the market is right to demand proof.

5. Key Business Drivers

Four drivers carry the thesis; each is underappreciated at the current price.

DTC path to sustained profitability

Streaming has gone from a $1.5B annualized loss as recently as fiscal Q4 2022 to $712M of quarterly operating income at a ~13% margin in FQ3 2026 — more than double the prior year and well ahead of management's 10% full-year target. The drivers are durable: subscription revenue +15%, pricing power exercised across Disney+ and Hulu, and a falling content-cost ratio as the library amortizes over a larger subscriber base. The management changes reinforce it — a YouTube veteran now runs direct-to-consumer, bringing exactly the ad-tech and engagement expertise the business lacked. The next legs are the ESPN tile on Disney+ (deepening the bundle to reduce churn) and ESPN's 53-country European/APAC expansion. This is a 2027–2028 margin story, and the market still values it as a breakeven experiment.

The parks investment cycle

The multi-year, multi-billion-dollar parks investment program announced in 2023 is entering its compounding phase: new Disney Cruise Line ships are already adding capacity, domestic per-capita spending rose 4% and global guest counts 4% in FQ3 2026, and Experiences margins sit near 30%. The oft-cited ten-to-one ratio — for every park guest, ten-plus people with Disney affinity never visit — frames the addressable expansion; converting even a fraction of that affinity into visits or cruises is high-margin growth. Parks capex is the highest-return use of capital in the company, and we estimate the current cycle should support mid-teens Experiences operating-income growth for several years, albeit with the usual cyclical caveats.

The ESPN fix

D'Amaro's first big strategic call — quashing the ESPN spin-off — was the right one. A spin would have crystallized a low multiple on the asset while leaving Disney without its best sports lever; fixing it in-house keeps the ESPN-to-Disney+ bundle intact and avoids a messy separation. The cost is near-term: a −5% OI quarter and a higher programming-cost bar. Management expects mid-single-digit sports operating-income growth in 2026 — that is management guidance, not a reported result — and the 2027–2028 ESPN DTC expansion is the optionality the market gives zero value today.

Leadership execution under Josh D'Amaro

D'Amaro, CEO since March 2026, has moved faster in six months than most CEOs manage in two years: the spin-off kill, a YouTube veteran installed over DTC, the first-ever CTO hired from the AI world, and 1,000 marketing jobs cut — cost discipline and technology ambition in one package. The buyback raise to at least $9B and the new ~$4.9B FQ4 segment-OI disclosure signal a management team that wants to be measured on numbers, not narratives. The honest counterweight: six months is not a track record, and the Iger-era CEO churn means execution must be re-proven every quarter.

6. Competitive Position

Disney is the only major media company that competes — and wins — on three axes at once: premium streaming, live sports, and physical experiences. No competitor spans all three.

CompetitorWhere they competeDisney's edge
NetflixPremium streamingFranchise IP moat (Marvel, Pixar, Star Wars) drives lower churn and pricing power; the parks/cruise flywheel monetizes IP beyond the screen. Netflix wins on scale and margins today; Disney's streaming margin trajectory (~13% and rising) narrows the gap.
YouTubeWatch time, ad dollarsDifferent model — user-generated breadth vs Disney's exclusive premium IP. The DTC chief's YouTube pedigree is aimed precisely at closing the engagement-tech gap.
Universal / Comcast parksExperiencesEpic Universe raised the bar, yet Disney posted +4% guest counts and +4% per-capita in FQ3 — share is holding. Moat: deeper IP bench, cruise fleet, and the ten-to-one affinity base no rival can replicate.
Legacy media (WBD, Paramount)Studios, linearDisney's studios rebounded (+64% Entertainment OI; Toy Story 5 above $1B) while peers still restructure; linear exposure is the shared drag, but ESPN remains the strongest sports asset in the bundle.

The structural point: competitors monetize one or two of Disney's three engines. The "One Disney" strategy — franchises, data, and technology coordinated across physical and digital — is a synergy story rivals cannot copy, because they do not own the underlying IP at comparable depth.

7. Risks

Headline risk: the parks business is discretionary and highly operationally levered — a consumer downturn would compress those ~30% margins fast. The stock's decade of flat performance is the market's standing verdict; breaking it requires execution to keep beating guidance, quarter after quarter.
  • Consumer cyclicality. Experiences is ~54% of segment operating income (reported, fiscal 2026 to date); a recession hits attendance, per-capita spending, and cruise pricing first. High fixed costs mean margin compression, not just revenue softness.
  • Linear decline accelerating. ESPN OI fell 5% on higher programming costs; if cord-cutting and rights-cost inflation outpace the DTC offset, sports becomes a multi-year drag rather than a fix.
  • Content execution is lumpy. Toy Story 5's above-$1B run powered the +64% Entertainment OI print (reported); franchise fatigue (Marvel, Star Wars) or a dry slate reverses it. Studios are a hit business.
  • Streaming competition and price elasticity. Further price hikes risk churn; Netflix and YouTube set the engagement bar.
  • New leadership, short track record. D'Amaro has six months as CEO — cost cuts and hires are promising, but a full-cycle test, including a downturn, lies ahead.
  • Capital allocation tension. The ≥$9B buyback, the multi-billion parks capex cycle, and sports rights all compete for cash; the A+E stake-sale proceeds that aided this year's buyback were one-time, and the amount was not disclosed.
  • Multiple compression. In a rising-rate environment, an ~18× forward multiple has room to contract even if earnings hit — the price target assumes valuation holds.

The ~$7.00 FY2027 adjusted-EPS estimate is anchored to management's own guides: ~12% FY2026 adjusted-EPS growth (~16% including the 53rd week), compounded at the double-digit FY2027 pace management expects, with streaming margin expansion and the buyback's share-count reduction doing the work. The ~18× multiple is justified three ways: it is a discount to Netflix's higher forward multiple (Disney's streaming margins are still climbing toward Netflix's level), a premium to declining linear-media peers (Disney's earnings are now majority growth-engine), and it is exactly the multiple implied by Morningstar's own $125 fair value on the same ~$7.00 EPS base.
