2. Market backdrop: what changed in October

Rates and inflation. The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% on Oct 28 by a 10–2 vote; both dissenters (Governor Cook and Cleveland Fed's Hammack) wanted a 25bp hike. September CPI was 3.7% y/y (core 2.6%, energy +20.4%); futures implied ~85% odds of a hike on Dec 9. On Oct 28 the 10-year was ~5.04% and the 30-year ~5.37% intraday (Admiral Markets).

Oil. Disruption in the Strait of Hormuz has kept Brent above $100 (≈$117 on Oct 23, then -6% on Oct 26) and WTI above $100 in the week of Oct 19–23. Energy is +52% YTD.

Equities. The S&P 500 hit a record on Oct 6 and was ~1% below it on Oct 28. Under the surface: Technology -7.7% (week of Oct 12–16) as the 'most crowded winner' unwound, with staples, energy, health care and materials leading; Communication Services -4.4% (Oct 19–23). On Oct 29 Microsoft beat (Azure ~+48%) while Alphabet fell ~12% on reported revenue/profit misses and negative free cash flow; Google and Microsoft reportedly did not raise capex guidance. Amazon reported after the Oct 29 close (shares -1.4% on Oct 30). On Oct 30 our quotes show NVDA -4.1%, AVGO -5.5%, AMAT -7.5%, ANET -8.3%, AMD -10.8%.

Read-across to the book. Two scenarios matter. (1) Oil stays high and inflation sticks: the Fed hikes, long-duration assets (tech, REITs, utilities) compress, and your energy and financials positions help. (2) Hormuz normalizes: oil and yields retreat, defensives and energy lose leadership, and the tech de-rating becomes a buying opportunity. The recommended stance (hold energy and financials, build tech, industrials and health care gradually) is deliberately balanced across both.

4. Methodology and data caveats
