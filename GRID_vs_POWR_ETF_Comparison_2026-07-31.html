GRID vs. POWR

Electric-grid equipment or the full U.S. power stack?

EXECUTIVE VERDICT: GRID IS THE BETTER PRIMARY SATELLITE

For a growth-oriented investor with a 10-year horizon, GRID offers the stronger combination of proven execution, liquidity,

global equipment exposure, and direct leverage to transmission and grid-modernization spending. POWR is cheaper and

broader across U.S. generation, utilities, fuel supply, and grid equipment, but its current strategy began only on 29 October

2025. The fund's older record belongs to a materially different global-energy mandate and should not be used to validate

today's portfolio.

Why GRID fits your portfolio better

You already own 377 shares of Southern Company. POWR currently assigns about 4.1% to SO and carries a much larger

U.S.-utility exposure, so it adds more duplication. GRID emphasizes global electrical equipment leaders and contractors - Eaton,

Schneider Electric, Johnson Controls, ABB and Quanta - which are closer to the grid-capex bottleneck and more complementary to

your direct utility holding. Its higher valuation means position sizing still matters.

Suggested implementation: use GRID as a 1.5%-3.0% thematic satellite rather than a core holding. If you want both funds, a 75%

GRID / 25% POWR split keeps the long-record fund dominant while adding U.S. generation and utility exposure. Reassess POWR

after it has at least three full years under the new index.

Key decision hinge

Choose GRID if you believe the scarce assets are grid hardware, automation and engineering capacity. Choose POWR if you want

a cheaper, U.S.-only bet spanning fuel supply through regulated utilities and generation - and accept that the strategy is still

unseasoned.

READ-THROUGH

GRID has won the recent cycle because equipment makers and contractors captured the scarcity premium from accelerating

load growth and grid investment. That is also its main risk: the portfolio is expensive and concentrated in several large winners.

POWR's lower valuation offers some cushion, but its exposure to utilities and energy suppliers introduces rate, regulatory,

commodity and capital-intensity risks.

Sizing and pairing

A thematic ETF should remain subordinate to your broad-market core. For your relatively high risk tolerance and 10-year horizon,

1.5%-3.0% in GRID is a reasonable initial satellite range. If using both, keep total exposure in that range and consider 75% GRID /

25% POWR. That pairing broadens the value chain but does not eliminate thematic risk; the shared economic driver is rising

electricity demand and grid capital expenditure.

Avoid adding POWR merely to diversify ticker count. Its utility and generator sleeve may already be represented through Southern

Company and other portfolio holdings, while its overlapping electrical-equipment positions duplicate roughly one-fifth of GRID's

top-ten weight.

Review triggers

Review quarterly after index rebalances and company earnings. Revisit the allocation if: (1) GRID's valuation exceeds earnings

growth for several quarters; (2) utility capex plans flatten; (3) POWR accumulates a clean three-year record with competitive

tracking; (4) either fund's top-five weight rises materially; or (5) the combined portfolio's direct and indirect utility exposure becomes

excessive.

BOTTOM LINE

GRID is the stronger choice today for a 10-year, growth-oriented satellite allocation. POWR is an interesting watch-list fund and

a possible small complement, but its current mandate needs time to establish a genuine record. The 0.16% fee advantage is

not enough to offset the strategy-history problem and greater overlap with your existing Southern Company position.
