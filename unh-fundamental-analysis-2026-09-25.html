01 | Executive Summary

The recovery is real; the price doesn't believe it yet

UnitedHealth's 2025 collapse — a first earnings miss in roughly twenty years, a suspended outlook, a CEO change, and a formal DOJ inquiry — cut the stock 63% peak to trough. Since then the operating turnaround has taken hold: the medical care ratio fell to 86.7% from 89.4% a year ago, UnitedHealthcare's earnings nearly doubled year over year, and management has raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance twice, to $19.50–$20.00. At $373.44 the stock trades at 18.9x forward adjusted earnings — the low end of its historical 18–22x band — on still-depressed earnings. That is a trough multiple on trough earnings, and it leaves roughly 27% upside to the $475 target.

02 | Business Overview

Four segments, one integrated machine

UnitedHealth pairs the largest U.S. health insurer (UnitedHealthcare) with Optum, a health-services platform spanning care delivery, pharmacy benefits, and data analytics. In Q2 2026 Optum contributed just over half of consolidated earnings from operations — the diversification that is supposed to make UNH more than a managed-care spread business.

Membership: shrinking on purpose

Total UnitedHealthcare medical membership is 48.5M, down 3.2% year over year — a deliberate shrink-for-margin trade. Medicare Advantage stands at 7.565M members (down 9.4% year over year, −880K since year-end 2025); Medicaid at 6.78M (down 9.5% year over year, including −380K in Q2 on the Louisiana exit). Within UnitedHealthcare, Medicare & Retirement is $42.4B of segment revenue (49.3%), Community & State $23.6B (27.5%), and Employer & Individual $20.0B (23.2%). Optum Rx filled 387M adjusted scripts in Q2, down 6.5% on the membership declines, with earnings growth carried by specialty generics.

Medical care ratio trend: Q3 2025 89.9% → full-year 2025 89.1% (88.9% adjusted) → Q1 2026 83.9% → Q2 2026 86.7%, versus 89.4% a year earlier. The 2026 MCR outlook is now 88.1% ± 25 bps, improved from 88.8% ± 50 bps. UnitedHealth does not disclose segment-level medical loss ratios, so the mix of the improvement across books cannot be verified from filings.

Three policy fronts

Medicare Advantage funding is tightening. The CY2026 final rate was +5.06% (~$25B); the CY2027 final rate is only +2.48% (~$13B), after a +0.09% proposal — and CMS excluded unlinked chart-review diagnoses from risk scores (~$7B in CMS savings), a direct shot at coding-driven margin. UnitedHealth itself says 2027 medical trend will run "meaningfully above" the 2.48% funding. Pricing discipline into 2027 bids is the single most important operating variable after the MCR.

PBM reform is now law. The Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2026, signed February 3, 2026, mandates Part D compensation delinking effective January 1, 2028, plus 100% commercial rebate pass-through, a spread-pricing ban, and Part D pharmacy nondiscrimination. Optum Rx is about 34% of UnitedHealth's 2026 outlook revenue (~$150.5B of ~$439B) — direct exposure to the 2028 reshaping of rebate and spread profit pools. (The "Big Beautiful Bill" Medicaid spread-pricing ban was dropped in the Senate.)

Medicaid is shrinking and getting cut. Enrollment is 73.5M (May 2026, KFF), down ~20M from the peak; states project flat FY2026 enrollment but +7.9% spending. Roughly $911B in federal Medicaid cuts are enacted, with work requirements by end of 2026 and 6-month redeterminations from January 2027. AM Best does not expect managed-Medicaid profitability to return until late 2026 or 2027 — and press reports put UnitedHealth's Community & State margin at −1% to −1.7% for 2026 (not company-disclosed).

08 | Risks

What can break the trade

  1. DOJ overhang. Formal criminal and civil requests are confirmed in the July 24, 2025 10-Q; the company is complying. No charges have been filed and no DOJ press release has surfaced as of September 25, 2026. Everything about scope, timeline, and outcome beyond the filing language is press-reported and unconfirmed — which cuts both ways: resolution could come as a manageable settlement or as a prolonged overhang. This is the single largest component of the ~27% uncertainty haircut.
  2. Medical trend re-acceleration. The Q2 MCR improvement is partly timing ($860M of favorable prior-period development). Management itself frames 2026 as "pushing down an already high number." If underlying trend re-accelerates, the 88.1% full-year MCR outlook is at risk.
  3. Medicare Advantage funding squeeze. The CY2027 final rate of +2.48% sits well below the medical trend UnitedHealth expects ("meaningfully above" 2.48%), and the exclusion of unlinked chart-review diagnoses from risk scores removes ~$7B of industry coding margin. 2027 bids must be priced with unusual discipline.
  4. PBM delinking (January 1, 2028). Part D compensation delinking reshapes Optum Rx's rebate and spread profit pools — and Optum Rx is ~34% of revenue. The company has two years to reposition; the earnings mix impact is not yet quantified.
  5. Medicaid policy. ~$911B in federal cuts, work requirements by end of 2026, and 6-month redeterminations from January 2027 land on a book whose managed-Medicaid margins are already negative and may not recover until late 2026 or 2027.
  6. Deliberate membership shrinkage. Margin over membership is the right trade, but −1.2M members since year-end 2025 (including −880K in MA) shrinks the premium base that future margin must be earned on. Shrink too far and operating leverage works in reverse.
09 | Catalysts & Next Steps

October 13 is the proving ground

Q3 2026 earnings are confirmed for Tuesday, October 13, 2026, before market open (per the company's September 15 IR notice). Some aggregators still show October 27 — those are stale estimates; go with the company's date.

What to watch on the print: (1) the MCR ex-prior-period development — is the underlying trend still bending down? (2) any move in the 88.1% full-year MCR outlook; (3) commentary on 2027 MA bids against the +2.48% final rate; (4) Optum Rx positioning ahead of 2028 delinking; (5) buyback execution against the ≥$5B guide. Beyond earnings, the next fundamental markers are 2027 MA open enrollment, any DOJ development, and the CY2028 rate proposal cycle in early 2027.

Analyst's watchlist into year-end: hold the BUY through Q3 unless the MCR deteriorates ex-reserves or 2027 bid commentary signals margin surrender. Add on weakness per the §7 staging — the risk/reward is best when the tape, not the thesis, is the problem.
