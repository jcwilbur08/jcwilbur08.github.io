What Has Changed Since the Previous Underwriting?

The prior internal house framing was simple: PEP was an income / defensive anchor whose key proof points were volume trends and margin recovery. That framing remains directionally right, but the evidence has become more nuanced and the security has become cheaper.

Improved

  • Valuation compressed to ~14.9x forward earnings and 19.1x FCF, below recent PEP history and well below KO/PG.
  • International businesses remain the growth engine, with Q2 organic growth of 6%-9% across major international divisions.
  • TTM FCF improved to about $9.3B and 9.6% of revenue.
  • Dividend rose 4% to $5.92 annualized, marking 54 consecutive annual increases.

Still Unproven

  • PFNA Q2 organic revenue fell 2%; volume was flat despite lower prices.
  • PBNA beverage volume fell 4% in Q2 even though reported revenue benefited from acquisitions.
  • Core operating margin contracted 40 bps in Q2 despite productivity efforts.
  • Management says North America improvement will be gradual.

New / Higher Risk

  • GLP-1 adoption and health/value behavior increasingly challenge habitual snacking demand.
  • Higher oil and freight/input costs raise the margin hurdle in H2.
  • A 5%+ Treasury environment reduces the premium multiple a slow-growing staples equity deserves.
  • Higher leverage than ideal limits the flexibility of capital returns.

Company Quality assessment: High-quality franchise, currently executing below its own historical standard. The advantage is breadth: PepsiCo can route innovation and marketing through one of the world’s largest direct-store-delivery and retail networks, monetize both food and beverage occasions, and offset U.S. weakness through international scale. The weakness is that North America convenient foods - historically one of the company’s best businesses - now faces both cyclical affordability pressure and a plausible structural change in eating behavior.

Strategic adaptation is rational, but not yet proven

PepsiCo cut suggested retail prices by up to nearly 15% on major U.S. snack brands in February 2026, while also increasing emphasis on portion control, functional benefits, protein/fiber, zero-sugar beverages, cleaner labels and acquired growth platforms such as Siete and poppi. These moves address the right consumer problems. The investment question is whether they restore unit velocity fast enough to protect brand economics and operating leverage.

Valuation - The Security Has Become More Interesting

PEP’s valuation now compensates investors for a meaningful portion of the operating uncertainty. The current forward P/E of about 14.9x is below year-end 2025 (16.9x), 2024 (18.1x), 2023 (21.2x) and far below the 2021-22 premium years. The PEG ratio of 2.94x is not objectively cheap versus growth, but it is much less demanding than the stock’s former premium valuation.

Why not use a 20x+ multiple?

PEP historically earned premium multiples because investors could underwrite dependable pricing, volume resilience and dividend growth. Today, the 5%+ long-rate environment raises the opportunity cost of owning slow growth; North America volume elasticity is weaker; and GLP-1/health/value shifts create a wider range of demand outcomes. A 16.5x-17.0x base multiple is therefore intentionally below prior peak norms and below the cleanest consumer-staples comparables.

Variant Perception and What the Market Is Pricing In

What the market may be underpricing

  • International organic growth can keep total-company growth in the 2%-4% range even if North America remains sluggish.
  • A 4.56% dividend yield plus 5.24% FCF yield creates a more credible floor than when PEP traded at 20x+ earnings.
  • Price reductions, innovation and acquired growth platforms can rebuild unit velocity over multiple quarters rather than immediately.
  • A modest rerating to only 16.5x-17.0x FY27 EPS creates a satisfactory return without assuming full historical premium restoration.

What the market may be correctly discounting

  • GLP-1 and health-conscious behavior may permanently lower frequency in salty snacks.
  • North America may need prolonged reinvestment in affordability and brand support, limiting margin recovery.
  • International growth is less valuable if FX, commodity or geopolitical costs absorb it.
  • The dividend is durable, but the payout ratio and leverage reduce optionality if earnings disappoint.

Thesis-Break Conditions

  • North America structural deterioration: PFNA organic revenue remains negative and volume turns materially negative through multiple quarters despite affordability and innovation actions.
  • Margin break: core operating margin falls below roughly 16% on a sustained basis without a credible recovery path, indicating pricing reinvestment and input costs are overwhelming productivity.
  • Estimate erosion: FY27 EPS falls below roughly $8.50 while the business still requires elevated brand investment, reducing base-case intrinsic value into the low-$130s even at a mid-teens multiple.
  • Balance-sheet / capital-allocation deterioration: leverage rises materially above current levels because of M&A or weak cash conversion while dividend coverage tightens.
  • International offset fails: international organic growth falls toward low-single digits at the same time North America remains weak, removing the key diversification pillar.

Final Investment Thesis

PepsiCo is a high-quality franchise experiencing a genuine but potentially manageable transition in its most important North American categories. International growth, brand scale, cash flow, distribution and the dividend remain strong. The prior premium multiple no longer exists, which is favorable for new capital. At roughly 14.9x forward earnings and a 4.56% yield, investors are now paid to wait for proof - but the correct posture is staged accumulation, not a blind defensive allocation.

The base case assumes PepsiCo can sustain 2%-4% organic growth, hold mid-single-digit EPS growth, stabilize core margin and gradually restore North America unit velocity. Under those conditions, a 16.5x-17.0x FY27 multiple supports approximately $148-$153 per share, with the dividend lifting prospective total return. The strongest contrary case is that GLP-1 adoption, health preferences and affordability permanently reduce snack frequency while cost inflation forces prolonged price investment; if that evidence strengthens, the stock deserves a structurally lower multiple.
