Executive investment view

Research conclusion: Qualcomm remains investable, but at its current valuation and portfolio role it no longer deserves a full 1.10% allocation purely on marginal-capital efficiency. The Amazon collaboration materially improves the data-center pathway, automotive is executing strongly, licensing remains a high-margin cash engine, and the balance sheet/capital-return capacity is sound. Against that, handset revenue is contracting, Apple modem insourcing is structural, QCT margins are under pressure, near-term consensus earnings are not growing, and the stock now trades at a premium to its own recent forward-multiple history.

All-or-none question: the underwrite does not support liquidating the entire position at roughly $178. The better research posture is retain / no-add, with a partial trim submitted for Portfolio CIO review if the goal is to fund higher-utility portfolio exposures. A trim toward roughly 0.6%-0.7% of NAV would preserve the new data-center/automotive optionality while freeing approximately $6,800-$8,500 for diversification or higher-conviction dislocations.
Why not a full exit now? Qualcomm is only days away from Snapdragon Summit (September 22-24), the September 8 Amazon agreement is a real strategic de-risking event rather than a marketing headline, and Friday's 5.8% selloff occurred without a newly identified company filing that breaks the thesis. Selling 100% would exchange a now-improved optionality set for certain opportunity-cost benefits, but at a price that already retraced part of the recent rally.

What has changed since the prior portfolio view?

Positive changes

  • Amazon became a real anchor customer. A September 3 warrant filing ties Amazon vesting to commercial arrangements, binding purchase orders and actual purchases, with up to $60B of payments over the warrant term.
  • Data center is now a measurable strategic leg. Qualcomm targets more than $15B of data-center revenue by FY2029 and more than $40B of total non-handset revenue.
  • Automotive accelerated. Q3 automotive revenue grew 61% YoY to $1.588B; the design-win pipeline is now targeted at $65B.
  • Share count continues to shrink. Shares outstanding are down about 3.7% YoY; Qualcomm still had $20.6B of repurchase authorization at June quarter-end.

Negative / unresolved changes

  • Handsets remain the earnings drag. Q3 handset revenue fell 20% YoY to $5.086B.
  • Apple risk is no longer theoretical. Qualcomm explicitly expects Apple to increasingly use its own modems, with a significant negative effect on QCT revenue and cash flow.
  • QCT margins compressed. QCT EBT margin fell to 26% from 30%, driven by higher product cost and lower revenue.
  • Competition is intensifying. MediaTek's 2nm Dimensity 9600 Pro targets the premium handset tier while MediaTek is also moving into AI accelerators.

Qualcomm's moat is unusual because it combines two economic models. QCT monetizes silicon/platform execution; QTL monetizes foundational wireless IP through licensing. The licensing franchise dampens cyclicality and funds R&D, dividends and buybacks. The strategic question is whether non-handset QCT businesses can scale quickly enough to replace declining Apple/handset contribution without structurally lowering margins.

The best evidence today is automotive: 23 consecutive quarters of double-digit YoY growth and Q3 automotive revenue of $1.588B. The less-proven leg is data center. Amazon materially improves credibility, but warrant economics and the staged nature of purchase milestones mean investors should not treat the headline $60B maximum as guaranteed revenue.

Management and capital allocation

Management has chosen to spend through the diversification transition rather than maximize short-term margins. That includes the Alphawave transaction, the Modular acquisition, higher capex, expanded data-center R&D and continued aggressive buybacks. This is strategically coherent because Qualcomm's core handset cash engine is mature and Apple's modem insourcing creates a known runoff that must be replaced.

What management is doing well

  • Setting explicit 2029 non-handset and EPS targets.
  • Building a diversified compute stack spanning edge, automotive and cloud.
  • Using balance-sheet capacity and buybacks to offset dilution and shrink shares.
  • Securing hyperscaler validation before the data-center business is large.

What must be proven

  • Data-center revenue can scale without permanently diluting QCT margins.
  • Alphawave / optical integration earns attractive returns.
  • Buybacks create value rather than merely mask slower organic EPS growth.
  • R&D leverage arrives as the new businesses scale.

Liquidate, trim, or hold? Capital-allocation alternatives

Option 1 — Hold the full 1.10% position
Best if the priority is preserving maximum upside to the new Amazon/data-center pathway and you are comfortable with the 4.90% direct semiconductor sleeve. The main drawback is that approximately $18.6K remains tied to a security with only mid-single/high-single-digit probability-weighted price upside in the base underwriting.
Option 2 — Partial trim to roughly 0.6%-0.7% of NAV Preferred research path
At current prices, this would mean retaining roughly 57-66 shares and selling approximately 38-48 shares, freeing roughly $6.8K-$8.5K. This preserves meaningful QCOM optionality while improving capital efficiency. It is the cleanest compromise between company-level thesis improvement and portfolio-level opportunity cost.
Option 3 — Full liquidation
Frees roughly $18.6K and reduces the direct semiconductor sleeve from about 4.90% to 3.80%. This is defensible only if your objective is a deliberate de-risking of semiconductors or if you want to accelerate diversification. The fundamental evidence does not currently require a zero weight.

Thesis-break conditions

  • Data-center credibility breaks: Amazon purchase commitments fail to translate into meaningful orders/shipments or Qualcomm materially reduces the >$15B FY2029 data-center target.
  • Margins structurally reset lower: QCT EBT margin remains below roughly 24%-25% for multiple quarters without a clear ramp/recovery path.
  • Apple runoff is not replaced: handset/Apple losses drive FY27/FY28 EPS expectations materially below roughly $9 while non-handset growth disappoints.
  • Automotive/IoT diversification stalls: combined non-handset growth decelerates into low-single digits before data center becomes material.
  • Licensing economics weaken: material adverse legal/regulatory outcomes reduce QTL royalty economics or renewal terms.
  • Capital allocation deteriorates: acquisitions and data-center investment materially increase leverage while organic cash returns fail to improve.

Strongest contrary case

The strongest case against trimming QCOM is that the market is still valuing the company primarily as a handset semiconductor just as its revenue mix is about to change. If Amazon validates multi-generation data-center silicon at scale, automotive reaches $10B, IoT exceeds $14B, and management delivers >$18 EPS by FY2029, today's roughly 19x forward multiple could prove inexpensive and a trim would sacrifice a meaningful rerating opportunity. This is why the underwrite favors retaining a core position rather than liquidating it.
The strongest case for a larger sale is portfolio-relative, not company-relative. Even if QCOM reaches the base case, its expected return may be similar to the market while VXUS/AVUV improve diversification and UBER/GEHC offer greater idiosyncratic upside from very small starting weights. The hurdle for a 1.10% QCOM allocation is therefore higher than simply proving that Qualcomm is a good company.

Research conclusion and proposed CIO review item

Equity Research Conclusion

Retain / no-add at current price. Do not support a full liquidation based on present evidence. Propose a Portfolio CIO review of trimming QCOM from 1.105% to roughly 0.6%-0.7% if capital is immediately redeployed to higher-utility diversification or low-overlap opportunities.

  • Company Quality: High, with improving diversification but still material handset dependence.
  • Valuation Attractiveness: Fair. Probability-weighted value around $192 versus $177.72; the expected-return spread is positive but not exceptional.
  • Portfolio Fit: Moderate. QCOM is not individually oversized, but semiconductor exposure is already 4.90% before look-through and the portfolio has clearer diversification deficits.
  • Suggested review action: partial trim rather than exit; direct proceeds first toward portfolio construction (VXUS / AVUV / AVDV) and/or very small high-conviction sleeves (UBER / GEHC), using staged entries.
  • What would change the conclusion: strong Snapdragon Summit/product evidence, Amazon order visibility and QCT margin recovery could justify the full position; weak FY27 guidance, sub-25% QCT margins and failed data-center conversion could justify a full exit.
