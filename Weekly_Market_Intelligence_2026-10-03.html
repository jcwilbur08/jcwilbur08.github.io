The one-paragraph version. Bonds and oil, not earnings, are running this tape. The 10-year yield touched its highest level in more than two decades on Thursday, Brent sits near $100 with the Iran war now in its eighth month, and the dollar is at an 18-month high. Friday’s soft payrolls (+29K vs. ~84K expected, unemployment 4.2%) cut October hike odds from roughly 70% to the mid-teens and sparked a relief rally, but it fixed nothing structural: inflation is 3.4% and September CPI (Oct 14) is forecast near 3.7%. The index sits near records on narrow, AI-led leadership: only about one in five S&P members is above its 50-day average. For our book the practical read is this: direct AI-infrastructure exposure is smaller than the headlines imply (7.5% of NAV), but we carry 16.5% in hyperscalers whose capex is increasingly debt-funded, 22% in financials heading into Tuesday’s bank prints, and roughly 20% in rate-sensitive assets (TLXIX, utilities/REIT, small-cap). This week’s calendar is light; the real tests (CPI, JPM/GS, ASML, TSMC) land the week after. Posture: no forced action. Use the quiet week to pre-commit decision rules and keep the standing VXUS/AVUV redeployment moving.

2. Five drivers that move our book this week

Driver 1 — Rates and the Fed: the dominant variable

The Fed restarted tightening in September and still projects one more hike this year. Williams and Vice Chair Jefferson then leaned against another move in October, Goldman pushed its next-hike call to December, and Friday’s jobs report finished the repricing. The risk is not the Fed itself; it is the term premium. Long yields are rising on their own (a five-week global bond rout), Treasury auction demand has been soft, and the $39B 10-year sale on Tuesday and the Wednesday FOMC minutes are the in-week tests. Book exposure: TLXIX (9.7%, bond sleeve inside a target-date fund), SO/AMT/SCHD (5.3%), small-cap funds AVUV/FSSNX/VTWO (4.5%), and long-duration growth multiples such as GEV (~80× clean 2026E at the July review). Financials (22.3%) are mixed: higher yields help net interest income, but September saw financials and discretionary lag as the bond market tightened conditions.

Driver 2 — Oil, Iran and the Houthi front

Weekend headlines raise gap risk for Monday. A tanker was struck near Hormuz early Saturday (the fifth this week), the Houthis claim a missile and drone strike on an Aramco facility near Riyadh, and about 9,000 more U.S. personnel and a third carrier group are heading to the region by month-end. Against that, the G7 release and Gulf export recovery are real supply offsets, and the administration is signalling the war ends after the November midterms, with renewed strikes possible beforehand. Book exposure: XOM (3.7%) is the only direct beneficiary. Oil above ~$100 feeds the inflation and yield channel that hurts most of the rest of the book, so for us oil is net negative above this level. Consumer-facing names (AMZN, DIS, NFLX, UBER, 7.76% + 3.5%) carry fuel-cost and spending-mix risk.

Driver 3 — AI capex: funding and return-on-investment scrutiny

Goldman estimates 2026 hyperscaler capex near $750–800B against roughly $778B of operating cash flow, with investment-grade issuance of ~$250B this year and $400B+ projected for 2027. AI-linked issuers now pay ~115 bp over Treasuries versus ~78 bp for the broader investment-grade index, and a Goldman note on Sept 25 argued the five largest spenders need ~$300B of annual AI revenue to break even. That note and Meta’s enterprise-platform launch triggered a 4.8% drop in META on Monday after a ~36% September run. The supply chain benefits from visible orders first and is hit first if 2027 capex guides are trimmed. Book exposure: hyperscalers 16.5%, core AI-infrastructure cluster 7.5%. NVDA is near its 52-week high after Morgan Stanley reinstated it as a top semiconductor pick.

Driver 4 — The dollar and Q3 earnings translation

A dollar near 102 is a headwind for multinational reported results. That matters most for AAPL, MSFT, QCOM, KO, PEP and PM; the first read is PepsiCo on Thursday. It also colors VXUS (2.7% of NAV): foreign holdings translate lower when the dollar rises, which is a timing consideration for the standing redeployment, not a reason to change the target.

Driver 5 — Breadth, seasonality and politics

Midterm-year Octobers have averaged roughly +3% for the S&P since 1950, and FactSet projects Q3 S&P earnings growth near 29%, a third straight quarter above 25%. Offsetting that: breadth is poor, the FOMC (Oct 27–28) lands less than a week before the Nov 3 midterms, and consensus is leaning on AI leaders to carry the index.

7. Counter-arguments: where this view could be wrong

The case that we are too relaxedThe case that we are too worried
  • Weak labor, sticky 3.4–3.7% inflation and a hiking Fed is a stagflation-lite mix; a 5%+ 10-year competes directly with equities.
  • VIX near 16 prices little of the oil, rates and election overlap.
  • AI funding indigestion (spreads at 115 bp, $400B+ issuance next year) would show up first in 2027 capex guides, hitting the equipment layer and hyperscaler multiples together.
  • ~45% of NAV is in financials, hyperscalers and AAPL: richly valued or tied to the same financing story.
  • Earnings are doing the work: Q3 S&P growth near 29% expected; consumer spending remains resilient.
  • The Fed is leaning patient and a hike is no longer priced for October.
  • Oil supply is recovering (Gulf exports near pre-war) and the G7 release adds 100M barrels.
  • Horizon is ten years with an $80K guaranteed pension floor: macro noise is not a reason to sell compounders.

What would change the view: a 10-year close above 5.35%, Brent above $110 sustained, CPI above 3.8% on Oct 14, widening AI-issuer spreads, or any hyperscaler trimming its 2027 capex plan.
