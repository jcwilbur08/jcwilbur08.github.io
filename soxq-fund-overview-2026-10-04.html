1. Executive Summary & Rating

SOXQ is still the cheapest broad semiconductor benchmark vehicle available: a 0.19% expense ratio (reported) tracking the industry-standard PHLX Semiconductor Sector Index — the 30 largest US-listed semiconductor companies — against 0.35% for both rivals SOXX and SMH (reported). It carries a 5-star Morningstar fund rating and a Zacks ETF Rank of 1 (Strong Buy) (both reported). The 1-year total return is ~98% (reported), and the fund has roughly tripled since inception in June 2021.

The rating is on the position, not the vehicle. At 28 shares, ~$2,895, 0.17% of the portfolio (calculated), the fund is decorative — it cannot move the book — and it sits inside a portfolio that already owns five direct chip names (NVDA, AVGO, TSM, QCOM, ASML), two of them rated Strong Buy at 8/10 conviction. The vehicle is fine; the position earns its exit.

Bottom line — REDUCE, conviction 3/10. Liquidate SOXQ and redeploy the ~$2,895 into Cam's standing NVDA and TSM programs. A 0.17% placeholder duplicates his highest-conviction direct names, carries ~zero expected excess return over its own benchmark by construction, and costs a trivial ~$249 pre-tax gain to exit. The marginal dollar earns more inside the staged NVDA add band ($215–235) or the TSM gate (≤$460) — ~13 NVDA shares at ~$225 or ~6 TSM shares at ~$473 — than it ever can as a decorative satellite. Advisory only; Cam decides.

3. Objective & Index Tracked

The fund's objective is to track the investment results of the PHLX Semiconductor Sector Index (SOX) before fees and expenses (reported, per Invesco). The index measures the performance of the 30 largest US-listed securities of companies engaged in the semiconductor business — design, distribution, manufacture, and sale of semiconductors, including memory, microprocessors, integrated circuits, and related equipment (reported).

Key index mechanics (reported): the fund normally invests at least 90% of assets in index constituents; the index is reconstituted annually in September (most recently September 18, 2026) and rebalanced quarterly (March, June, September, December). The weighting is tiered/modified market-cap — the largest names are capped, which is why NVDA's ~11–14% weight here is well below its ~19% weight in the market-cap-heavier SMH. The fund is classified non-diversified (reported).

The case for liquidation

  • Conviction hierarchy. NVDA and TSM are both Strong Buy, 8/10 conviction, with $328 and $545 targets implying ~40% and ~15% upside. SOXQ's honest forward excess return over its own benchmark is ~zero by construction. The marginal dollar has a clear best use.
  • Size. At 0.17%, SOXQ cannot move the book. Cam will not build it to a 1% satellite — his chip exposure is already ~5%+ through five direct names — so the only alternatives to liquidation are a placeholder or a bigger duplicate of what he owns.
  • Strength to trim into. The fund is +2.4% Friday to $103.41 on the weak-payrolls rotation, and the sector just posted one of its best years on record. This is the reduce-side of the cycle, not the add-side.
  • Trivial friction. The +9.4% gain costs roughly $40–50 to realize — less than the annual cost drag of a single day of volatility at 1.86 beta. No tax reason to stay.
  • Program fit. Cam already runs a staged NVDA add program ($215–235 band, toward ~1% weight) and a TSM gate (≤$460). Routing the proceeds there accelerates his own plan rather than inventing a new one.

The honest counterpoint — what he gives up

  • The memory/analog/equipment tail. Micron, Intel, Marvell, AMD, TI, ADI, NXP, Applied Materials, Lam, KLA — ~two-thirds of the ETF. That tail drove 2026's return, which is precisely why it is now the most stretched part of the stack.
  • Equipment exposure. ASML is his only direct equipment name; SOXQ's AMAT/Lam/KLA layer leaves with the fund. Partial, not total, loss of the layer.
  • Behavioral discipline. A sector fund rebalances away from winners mechanically; direct names require Cam to trim. At these weights, this is theoretical, not practical.
Analyst recommendation: Liquidate SOXQ on strength and route the ~$2,895 to NVDA first (the higher-conviction, higher-upside name: $328 target, ~40% upside, staged adds already running inside his $215–235 band), with TSM as the alternative or a split if he prefers balanced construction. If the NVDA band is unavailable at execution, the TSM ≤$460 gate is the fallback. Neither requires new machinery — just his existing program. Advisory only: Cam executes.

10. Risks / What Could Change the View

  • AI-capex melt-up continues. If memory pricing stays tight and hyperscaler capex keeps accelerating, SOXQ's Micron/Intel/AMD tail keeps outperforming Cam's direct names — the liquidation forgoes that convex upside. (Estimated probability: meaningful near-term, fading over 12 months.)
  • NVDA/TSM drawdown. Redeploying $2,895 into names that then fall 20% would have been "better" in the fund — but both are Strong Buys with staged entries precisely for that, and the sum is too small to matter.
  • SOXX's performance edge. If the ICE-index rival keeps outperforming by double digits, even a rebuild case would favor SOXX over SOXQ — the "cheapest fee" argument is not the whole story.
  • Cyclicality. Beta 1.86, non-diversified, 100% Technology: a semiconductor downturn hits the fund hardest, and it would hit the redeployed NVDA/TSM dollars too. The consolidation concentrates, not diversifies.
  • Behavioral. Exiting the only diversified chip vehicle leaves the book with zero pure equipment exposure outside ASML. Watch that layer, not the fund.

A BUY/rebuild trigger would be: Cam deciding he wants a genuine ~1% semiconductor satellite with equipment-and-memory breadth he will not buy directly — in which case SOXQ remains the right vehicle at 0.19%. He has not shown that appetite; the direct sleeve is his chosen instrument.

Appendix B — Glossary

PHLX Semiconductor Sector Index (SOX) — the industry-standard benchmark of the 30 largest US-listed semiconductor companies. Reconstitution — the annual (September) rebuild of the index's constituent list; rebalancing — the quarterly reset of constituent weights. Tiered / modified market-cap weighting — largest constituents are capped so no single name dominates. Non-diversified — the fund may concentrate more than 25% of assets in a small number of issuers. Beta — sensitivity to market moves; 1.86 implies ~86% more volatile than the market. Expense ratio — annual operating costs as a % of assets. TTM — trailing twelve months.
