Executive Summary

GE HealthCare (GEHC), the January 2023 spin-off from General Electric, is the global leader in large medical imaging equipment — a roughly $21 billion-revenue medtech compounder trading at a depressed multiple after a ~21% year-to-date decline. The shares closed at $66.27 on September 22, 2026, valuing the company at $29.9B E.

The second quarter (reported July 29, 2026) showed the business inflecting: revenue of $5.295B grew 3.5% organically, adjusted EPS of $1.13 beat consensus by ~9%, and — most importantly — organic orders surged 11.1% with a 1.15× book-to-bill, lifting backlog to $23.9B R. Pharmaceutical Diagnostics is growing double-digits at ~30% margins; the newly formed Advanced Imaging Solutions segment is solid. The blemish is Patient Care Solutions: revenue down 13.5% organically with negative EBIT, now under strategic review R.

We initiate with a BUY rating and a $84 twelve-month target (~27% upside), based on ~15.5× 2027E adjusted EPS of $5.36 E. The stock trades at just ~13.6× 2026E and ~12.4× 2027E adjusted EPS C — a multiple that prices in the margin pressure from ~$250M of input-cost inflation and the PCS drag, but not the order-book inflection, the PDx growth engine, or a clean PCS resolution. Needham's initiation at Buy/$93 on this very day (Sept 22) makes a similar re-rating case E.

Portfolio note: Cam holds 50 shares (~$3,314, 0.19% of the $1.70M portfolio). Morningstar rates GEHC 4 stars with an $88 fair value vs. the $66.27 price — directionally consistent with our Buy, though our $84 target is more conservative.

1. Company Overview

GE HealthCare is a global medical technology, pharmaceutical diagnostics, and digital solutions company headquartered in Chicago, Illinois, operating as an independent company since its January 2023 spin-off from General Electric R. It develops, manufactures, and services imaging equipment, contrast media and radiopharmaceuticals, patient monitoring systems, and enterprise imaging / AI software.

Segment structure — important reporting change

In Q2 2026, GEHC combined its legacy Imaging and Advanced Visualization Solutions (AVS) businesses into a single reportable segment, Advanced Imaging Solutions (AIS) — described as a roughly $14.6B combined business R. The company now reports three segments:

  • Advanced Imaging Solutions (AIS): MR, CT, molecular imaging, X-ray, ultrasound, women's health, image-guided and enterprise imaging solutions. Q2 revenue $3.771B (+5.0% organic), EBIT margin 13.9% R.
  • Pharmaceutical Diagnostics (PDx): contrast media and radiopharmaceuticals, including theranostics. Q2 revenue $843M (+14.6% organic), EBIT margin 29.6% R.
  • Patient Care Solutions (PCS): patient monitoring, anesthesia, maternal/infant care. Q2 revenue $675M (−13.5% organic), EBIT −$26M — strategic options under review R.
Analyst note: Because of the resegmentation, no company-disclosed Imaging-vs.-Ultrasound split exists for any period after Q2 2026. Legacy four-segment figures are used only for FY2025 and earlier, clearly labeled as such.

Competitive position

GEHC is widely regarded as the global leader in large medical imaging equipment (CT, MRI, X-ray), with Siemens Healthineers and Philips as the principal global peers O. UBS has flagged growing pressure from Chinese domestic rivals and a new U.S. competitor challenging its Omnipaque contrast franchise E. Modality-level market-share figures could not be verified from primary sources and are excluded from this report.

2. Financial Performance

Q2 2026 — quarter ended June 30, 2026 R

Reported July 29, 2026. Revenue of $5.295B rose 5.7% as reported and 3.5% organically. Adjusted EPS of $1.13 beat the ~$1.04 consensus by roughly 9%. Adjusted EBIT was $750M at a 14.2% margin (down 40 bps y/y). GAAP net income was $561M ($1.24 diluted EPS), up from $486M a year earlier. Operating cash flow was $168M and free cash flow $68M on $100M of capex.

The demand picture was the standout: organic orders grew 11.1%, book-to-bill was 1.15×, and backlog reached $23.9B. Tariff-related IEEPA refunds contributed a $129M net-income benefit and $107M of operating-cash-flow benefit in the quarter. The company paid a $0.035/share quarterly dividend and repurchased 3.3M shares for $200M, with $500M remaining under authorization. Cash stood at $2.1B against $10.1B of total debt outstanding.

5. Investment Thesis — Why BUY

  • Order-book inflection. Organic orders +11.1% with a 1.15× book-to-bill and a $23.9B backlog is the strongest demand signal since the spin-off — it de-risks 2027 revenue and supports the return to mid-single-digit growth Needham underwrote R.
  • PDx is a high-quality growth engine. $843M of quarterly revenue growing 14.6% organically at a 29.6% EBIT margin, levered to radiopharmaceuticals and theranostics — a structurally attractive, less cyclical profit pool R.
  • Depressed multiple on transient issues. ~13.6× 2026E EPS prices in the $250M input-cost headwind and the PCS drag. Memory-chip and freight inflation is cyclical; pricing pass-through lags but is underway O.
  • PCS optionality. A strategic review of the loss-making PCS unit (−$26M EBIT in Q2) creates a free call option: a sale or fix removes the conglomerate discount and could fund buybacks or deleveraging O.
  • Product cycle ahead. Photonova Spectra photon-counting CT is expected to contribute meaningfully from H1 2027, and the Intelerad acquisition ($2.3B, closed H1 2026) deepens the enterprise-imaging/AI stack R.
  • Fresh sell-side sponsorship. Needham's Sept 22 initiation at Buy/$93 and BTIG's reiterated Buy/$79 signal the narrative turn is beginning E.

6. Key Risks

  • Input-cost inflation persistence. The ~$250M gross headwind (memory chips, oil/freight) is the central re-rating debate — if pricing cannot catch up, the 15.4–15.7% margin guide is at risk R.
  • PCS deterioration. Revenue down 13.5% organically with negative margins and acknowledged fulfillment issues; a poor strategic-review outcome could crystallize value destruction R.
  • China exposure. China was $2.251B (~11%) of FY2025 revenue and declined 4.6%; procurement remains fragile after the anti-corruption campaign, and an unverified press report of a China-unit stake sale adds headline risk R.
  • Tariff/trade re-escalation and competitive pressure from Siemens Healthineers, Philips, and Chinese domestic rivals, including on the Omnipaque contrast franchise O.
  • Capital allocation: the $2.3B Intelerad deal and 1.6× net-debt/EBITDA leverage leave limited room for error on integration R.

7. China & Tariffs

China contributed $2.251B (~10.9%) of FY2025 revenue, down 4.6% y/y, after the multi-year anti-corruption campaign froze hospital procurement (China sales fell ~15% in 2024) R. Equipment-renewal stimulus has been delayed into 2025–26; the pace of 2026 recovery needs primary confirmation and should not be overstated. Separately, an unverified September 2026 press report (nextmsc.com) claims GEHC is exploring a stake sale in its China unit — flagged as reported news, not company-confirmed E.

On tariffs: the 2025 gross impact was ~$250M; 2026 is expected below that. Q2 brought $129M of IEEPA tariff refunds to net income ($107M cash). Guidance excludes refunds. Keep tariff effects analytically separate from the ~$250M of non-tariff input inflation R.
