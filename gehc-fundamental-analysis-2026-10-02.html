Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

The business beat, the guidance held, and the stock fell 13% anyway. GE HealthCare's second quarter delivered a genuine beat — revenue and adjusted EPS ahead of consensus, record organic orders up 11.1%, a 1.15x book-to-bill, and a record $23.9B backlog — and full-year guidance was reaffirmed in full. The September selloff was driven by a conference reiteration of known headwinds (tariffs, supply chain, soft China), a persistent litigation overhang, and an unresolved strategic review of the weak Patient Care Solutions segment. None of it was new information. At $64.29, the stock trades at roughly 13 times 2026 consensus earnings with a ~5.3% free-cash-flow yield — a clear discount to medtech peers at 16–25x — while the imaging franchise posts its strongest demand signals since the spin-off.

Research posture: ADD | $92 base value maintained | Add gate: $62

The $92 base value is unchanged — ~18.8x the $4.90 midpoint of reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance, in line with medtech peers once the PCS/China discount fades. At $64.29 that implies ~43% upside. 01 | News Flow

What changed since August 13

Seven weeks, one earnings beat, one dividend hike, one new bull, and a CFO transition. The fundamentals improved; the price did not.

Jul 23 — CFO transition announced

Jay Saccaro announced he is stepping down as CFO; George Newcomb (Controller and Chief Accounting Officer) serves as interim CFO and presented alongside CEO Arduini at the September Wells Fargo conference. A permanent appointment is pending — interim-CFO periods always carry a guidance-credibility discount.

Jul 29 — Q2 beat, guidance reaffirmed

Revenue $5.295B (+5.7%; +3.5% organic) and adjusted EPS $1.13 beat consensus; record organic orders (+11.1%) and record $23.9B backlog. Full-year 2026 guidance reaffirmed in full: 3–4% organic revenue growth, $4.80–$5.00 adjusted EPS, 15.4–15.7% EBIT margin, ~$1.6B free cash flow.

Sep 8–17 — Product pipeline advances

StarGuide GX 4D SPECT/CT filed for FDA 510(k) clearance (CE-marked in Europe); the Vivid Explorer/Advanced/Focus cardiovascular ultrasound portfolio plus EchoPAC v211 software earned CE marking with FDA clearance pending; CareIntellect for Operations — an AI hospital-operations SaaS app — launched on AWS. Steady, incremental franchise-building.

Sep 9 — Wells Fargo conference

Management reiterated known tariff, supply-chain, and soft-China headwinds. The stock fell ~3% that day and slid for five straight sessions to $64.24 — the September low. Nothing new was disclosed; the market repriced known risks lower anyway.

Sep 17 — BTIG reiterates; litigation continues

BTIG reiterated Buy and raised its target $75 → $79. The same week, the Glancy Prongay securities-fraud investigation (relating to the April Q1 disclosure) continued — no lawsuits filed, but the overhang persists.

Sep 22 — Dividend raised; Needham initiates

The board raised the quarterly dividend 14% to $0.04/share ($0.16 annualized), payable November 13 to holders of record October 23 — the third consecutive annual increase. Needham initiated coverage at Buy with a $93 target, arguing the stock prices in a bear case the fundamentals don't support.

Sep 23 – Oct 1 — AI and partnerships

Expanded generative-AI collaboration with Mass General Brigham for radiation therapy; expanded the DeepHealth mammography AI collaboration internationally; named a Chief AI Officer. Unconfirmed reports of ~$1B deal talks for Sofie Biosciences (radiopharma) — worth watching, not underwriting.

Net assessment. The news flow skews positive — beat, reaffirmed guide, dividend hike, new Buy initiation — yet the stock sits at its September low. That divergence is the opportunity, provided the PCS review and Q3 print don't introduce genuinely new negatives.

PCS: the swing factor

Patient Care Solutions is the entire bear case in one segment: revenue down 13.3% and a $26M EBIT loss on operational fulfillment issues and critical component shortages. The mitigating signs are real but early — strong first-half orders, a good start to July, supply and manufacturing fixes in place, and expected sequential improvement in the second half. The strategic review (retain, sell, or other) is the swing factor: a clean disposal could unlock value; a dilutive outcome or a decision to retain a still-bleeding business would extend the overhang.

China: assumed down for 2026

Management continues to assume China sales decline for the full year — a 2026 nationwide anti-corruption and procurement-oversight campaign, volume-based procurement pricing pressure, local competition, and tight hospital budgets. China is roughly 11–13% of revenue. There is no stimulus-driven recovery visible yet; any stabilization commentary on the Q3 call would be a positive surprise, not the base case.

Why the discount exists. The market is pricing three things: a PCS segment that may need surgery, a China business in managed decline, and an interim CFO. The first two are operational and fixable; the third resolves with a permanent appointment. None of them impairs the imaging franchise generating record orders.
