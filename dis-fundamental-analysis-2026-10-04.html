Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Disney's fiscal third quarter was the clearest evidence yet that the turnaround is working on both engines at once — revenue of $25.25B (+7%), total segment operating income of $5.6B (+21%), and adjusted EPS of $2.06 (+28%, ahead of the $1.88 consensus). The streaming business posted $712M of quarterly operating income at a ~13% margin, more than double the prior year. Experiences delivered record fiscal-Q3 revenue of nearly $10B (+10%) with operating income up 20%. Management raised the buyback to at least $9B and expects double-digit EPS growth in fiscal 2027.

Research posture: Buy | Conviction 7/10 | 12-month target $125

At the $102.19 reference price, Disney trades at roughly 13.7x consensus fiscal-2027 adjusted EPS of ~$7.47, with a street consensus target near $128 (~25% upside) and Morningstar's $125 fair value (4 stars) matching our target exactly. The $125 target implies ~22% price upside plus a ~1.5% dividend yield — about 24% total return. Under the new conviction-anchored rating scale effective October 3, conviction 7/10 with 22% upside maps to Buy (6–7 conviction and at least 10% upside); it is not a Strong Buy because the new CEO has six months in the seat, the linear decline is still running, and the parks engine is cyclically exposed. The September 27 initiation's legacy BUY translates cleanly — no fundamental input changed, only the ruler.

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

Two profitable growth engines, finally firing together

Streaming has gone from a $1.5B annualized loss as recently as fiscal Q4 2022 to $712M of quarterly operating income at a ~13% margin — more than double the prior year and ahead of management's 10% full-year target. Experiences compounds at ~30% operating margins with record revenue. For the first time in the turnaround, both engines are profitable and growing simultaneously.

2

The restructuring is the right medicine, applied quickly

The October 1 television restructuring — consolidating ABC Entertainment, 20th Television, Hulu Originals and Freeform around streaming customers — is exactly the reorganization the linear-to-streaming transition demands. Combined with the September 30 HR/tech cuts, the April marketing reduction and the quashed ESPN spin-off, the pattern is clear: the new CEO is shrinking the cost base of the declining business to fund the growing one.

3

Capital return is becoming a real pillar

The fiscal 2026 buyback target was raised to at least $9B, and the company generated $3.07B of free cash flow in FQ3 alone (+63% year over year). With trailing revenue set to top $100B for the first time, the cash machine is funding both the parks capex cycle and accelerating shareholder returns.

4

The price still discounts a decade of disappointment

At $102.19 the stock is down 10% year to date, 13% below its 52-week high, and roughly flat over five years on a total-return basis. It trades at ~13.7x consensus fiscal-2027 EPS against a street consensus target near $128. The market is pricing the turnaround as fragile; the operating results say it is durable.

5

The overhang is real, which is why this is a Buy and not a Strong Buy

A decade of flat returns is the market's standing verdict and breaking it requires execution to keep beating guidance. The new CEO has six months in the seat, the linear decline is still running (sports operating income fell 17% in FQ3), and the parks engine — 54% of segment operating income — is cyclically exposed. Conviction is 7/10, not 8.

Variant perception. The market sees the October layoffs as confirmation that the business is shrinking. The differentiated view is that Disney is shrinking the right parts — legacy linear cost structures — while the growing parts (streaming margins, parks, cruises) compound. Cost discipline that funds the growth engines is a reason to own the stock, not a reason to fear it.
02 | Newsflow

What changed since September 27

Television restructuring reported (October 1)

The Wall Street Journal reported that Disney plans a restructuring of its television business that could result in hundreds of layoffs and the consolidation of divisions. The plan is being led by Disney Entertainment Television Chairman Debra O'Connell and may not be finalized before year-end. It aims to organize the business around streaming customers instead of brands built decades ago for linear television, and is expected to affect executives running ABC Entertainment, 20th Television, Hulu Originals and Freeform. Disney did not immediately respond to requests for comment. The stock fell about 3% on the news to $101.33, then recovered to $102.19 on October 2.

Third 2026 workforce reduction (September 30)

Disney cut roughly 300 employees, mainly in human resources and technology — its third reported round of 2026 after about 1,000 positions in April and a July round. Under the prior CEO, the company eliminated roughly 8,000 jobs between 2023 and 2025 with about $7.5B of reported savings. A voluntary early-retirement program is also in place. The repetition of cuts is worth noting honestly: it signals ongoing cost pressure, even as it improves the forward margin trajectory.

Streaming price increases taking effect

Disney+ price increases are now flowing through, which should lift average revenue per subscriber — the key variable being whether retention holds. Management's bet is that the bundle (Disney+, Hulu, ESPN tile) has enough pricing power to absorb increases without churn spikes.

TikTok partnership

Disney announced a partnership with TikTok allowing creators to incorporate characters and scenes from Disney movies and television shows into short-form videos, with a curated selection also featured on Disney+. The strategic read: meeting younger audiences where they consume video, and potentially funneling engagement back to the streaming platform.

Analyst actions

Goldman Sachs reiterated its Buy rating but trimmed its price target to $140 from $144 on slightly lower EBITDA expectations (September 28), while forecasting FQ4 adjusted EPS of $1.73 against a Visible Alpha consensus of $1.66. Bank of America reiterated its Buy rating and $125 target (September 29), with fiscal-2027 revenue and EPS estimates near $105B and $7.52. Bernstein reiterated Buy at $129 earlier in the month. The street consensus remains a Moderate Buy with an average target near $127.61 across 21 analysts.

Price action

The stock closed September 25 at $106.15, drifted to $104.90 by September 30, fell 3% on the restructuring news to $101.33 on October 1, and closed October 2 at $102.19 (+0.85%). It remains 12.7% below the $117.09 52-week high and above the $92.19 March low.

Streaming: from loss machine to margin compounder

Disney+/Hulu revenue of $5.53B (+11%) with subscription revenue up 15% and $712M of operating income at ~13% margin — more than double the prior-year $329M and ahead of the 10% full-year target. The October restructuring is aimed squarely at this segment's cost base: consolidating the television units that feed streaming content should reduce duplicative overhead. The next legs are the ESPN tile on Disney+ deepening the bundle and the 53-country European/APAC ESPN expansion.

Experiences: the compounder, with one watch item

Record fiscal-Q3 revenue, +4% guest counts, +4% domestic per-capita spending, new cruise ships adding capacity, and Consumer Products +7% on Toy Story 5 and Mandalorian merchandise. Management has acknowledged softer conditions in Shanghai and Hong Kong — the one geographic watch item into the holiday quarter. A ~$100M tariff refund flattered the quarter by roughly four points of the 20% operating-income growth; the underlying trend remains double-digit.

Sports: the fix is the story

Revenue +4% but operating income −17% on programming costs is the quarter's blemish. The strategic setup for fiscal 2027 is more constructive: the NFL Network acquisition and the Super Bowl are multi-year tailwinds, and the ESPN direct-to-consumer expansion is the optionality the market values at zero today. Management guides mid-single-digit sports operating-income growth in 2026 — that is guidance, not a reported result, and the Q4 print will test it.

06 | Risks

Risks

  • Consumer cyclicality. Experiences is ~54% of segment operating income; a recession hits attendance, per-capita spending and cruise pricing first, and high fixed costs mean margin compression, not just revenue softness.
  • Linear decline outrunning the offset. Sports operating income fell 17% in FQ3; if cord-cutting and rights-cost inflation outpace streaming gains, the restructuring cuts prove insufficient and sports becomes a multi-year drag.
  • Content execution is lumpy. Toy Story 5's $1B+ run powered the quarter, but management has already flagged Moana's underperformance weighing on Q4 — franchise volatility cuts both ways.
  • Streaming price elasticity. Disney+ price hikes are taking effect now; if churn spikes, the ARPU gain reverses and the margin story stalls.
  • Restructuring execution risk. Consolidating four television units under one chairman while cutting hundreds of jobs risks creative-talent loss and organizational disruption — the savings are not yet quantified.
  • New leadership, short track record. Six months as CEO is promising but not proven through a downturn; the Iger-era CEO churn means execution must be re-proven quarterly.
  • Multiple compression. With the 10-year near multi-decade highs, even a ~14× forward multiple has room to contract if earnings merely meet rather than beat.
