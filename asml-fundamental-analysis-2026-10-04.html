01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

The only EUV supplier on earth — and the moat is widening

ASML is the sole commercial supplier of extreme ultraviolet lithography, with roughly 90–94% of the total lithography market. Three decades and tens of billions in cumulative R&D, Zeiss optics integration and system complexity put any EUV competitor 5–10 years away at minimum. High-NA EUV — at roughly twice the ASP of low-NA — is now processing wafers in high-volume manufacturing at Intel, with TSMC and Samsung engaged and Samsung targeting next-generation DRAM mass production by 2028.

2

Multi-year demand visibility is unusually concrete

2027 low-NA EUV capacity is nearly fully booked, and management is adding 30% capacity into 2027 across EUV and DUV immersion, with a further 30% under study for 2028. TSMC is in 2nm volume production with 2026 capex of $52–56B, memory lithography sales are guided up 75% in FY2026 on the HBM/DRAM cycle, and subsidy-backed greenfield fabs across the US, EU, Japan and Korea underwrite a decade of equipment demand. This is a capex supercycle with purchase orders, not just forecasts.

3

The service annuity dampens cyclicality and funds the multiple

Installed Base Management — services, upgrades and software — contributes roughly 25–30% of revenue at superior margins. Q2's $3.2B in installed-base sales came in about $350M above expectations on software-led upgrades: the high-margin annuity doing exactly what it is supposed to do. As the installed base grows with each system shipment, this recurring stream compounds and deserves a premium multiple.

4

Margin expansion is in progress, not just promised

Gross margin has moved from 51.3% in FY2024 to 52.7% in FY2025 to 54.0% in Q2 2026, with Q3 guided at 55–57% and the 2030 framework targeting 56–60%. EUV mix (57% of Q2 system sales), High-NA ASP uplift and the software-rich service mix are structural, not cyclical, drivers. The margin story is being delivered quarter by quarter.

5

Valuation leaves room at the lower end of Buy — barely

At 35x FY2027E consensus EPS of $58.49, the $2,050 target implies ~10% upside: enough to clear the new Buy bar (conviction 6–7 with ≥10% upside) but far from Strong Buy territory. The target sits between the $1,970 street consensus and bullish outliers at $2,400–2,500. This is a fair-value-plus-growth call, not a re-rating call — the re-rating already happened.

Variant perception. The market is focused on the AI capex supercycle and has priced ASML as its purest equipment beneficiary. The differentiated view is narrower: the business is genuinely exceptional and worth owning, but at 58x trailing earnings with a record quarter guided, the next 10% of upside must be earned through flawless execution — it is no longer a valuation gift. Patience on entry is the entire edge from here.
02 | Business & Industry

Business quality and competitive position

ASML designs and manufactures photolithography systems — the machines that print transistor patterns onto silicon wafers. Every advanced chip on earth passes through an ASML tool. The portfolio spans four platforms, each effectively a monopoly or near-monopoly:

PlatformUse caseIndicative ASPMarket share
EUV low-NA (NXE:3600D/3800E)7nm and below — leading-edge logic & DRAM$180–200M100%
EUV High-NA (EXE:5000/5200)2nm and below — single-exposure patterning$350–380M+100%
DUV immersion (NXT series)Mid-range & mature nodes, China capacity$60–80M~90%
DUV dry (KrF/ArF/i-line)Legacy nodes, broad industrial base$30–50M~85%

In Q2 2026, EUV was 57% of system sales and ArFi immersion 29%, with logic and memory roughly evenly split — a healthy balance. Shipments skewed to South Korea (43%) and Taiwan (30%), reflecting where leading-edge capacity is being built; China was 14%, down from 27% a year earlier.

China's domestic response is the long-term watch item. A September report found domestically built immersion DUV systems have entered production in China, and Beijing is reportedly pursuing a parallel EUV route based on alternative light sources rather than replicating ASML's platform. These efforts are far behind ASML's technology today, but they represent the only conceivable long-term threat to the monopoly — measured in decades, not quarters.

Competitive landscape

Nikon has no EUV answer and recently posted a record ~¥86B loss; Canon's nanoimprint remains far behind on overlay and throughput. China's SMEE serves mature nodes, with state-backed plans for ~20 DUV tools in 2027 versus ASML's ~130 immersion shipments in 2026. There is no investable-horizon competitor in advanced lithography.

04 | Management & Capital Allocation

Disciplined operators spending into a supercycle

CEO Christophe Fouquet has run ASML since 2024, continuing the company's long-standing playbook: concentrate R&D on the next node transition, co-develop with the three leading-edge customers, expand capacity ahead of demand, and return excess cash. The September start of construction on the new Eindhoven campus — a multi-phase site planned for ~350,000 square meters and up to 20,000 workplaces with an advanced flow factory for TWINSCAN production — is the physical expression of that confidence.

Positive

Capacity commitment

+30% low-NA EUV and +30% DUV immersion capacity into 2027, with a further +30% under study for 2028, backed by customer commitments. Management is spending ahead of a visible order book, not speculatively.

Positive

Capital returns

$14B buyback through 2028 plus a 17%-raised dividend, funded from a net-cash balance sheet. Repurchases continued into late September even near all-time highs — a signal of internal confidence in value.

Watch

Mistral AI stake

The $1.5B Series C lead investment is strategically coherent (AI inside lithography and operations) but non-core; monitor that it stays a capability investment rather than the start of venture-style capital allocation.

Watch

Bookings opacity

Quarterly net-bookings disclosure was discontinued from Q1 2026; management now characterizes intake only qualitatively ("extremely strong" for H1). Less visibility — but also less quarterly volatility — into the order book.

Explicit thesis-break conditions

  1. Control break: a full DUVi export ban or MATCH Act enactment removes ~14–15% of revenue — the growth algorithm breaks and the target falls to the bear case.
  2. Demand break: 2027 customer capex guides are cut and capacity additions go underutilized; EUV layer-count growth stalls.
  3. Execution break: Q3 or Q4 misses the guided record trajectory at a 58x multiple, or gross margin fails to progress toward 56%+.
  4. Technology break: High-NA adoption stalls beyond Intel while a credible alternative patterning route emerges — neither is visible today.
  5. Multiple break: forward P/E re-expands past ~40x on FY2027E without a fundamental change — the discipline case for adding evaporates.
