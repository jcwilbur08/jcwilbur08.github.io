01 | Fund & Index

Objective and index tracked

GRID seeks investment results that correspond generally, before fees and expenses, to the price and yield performance of the Nasdaq Clean Edge Smart Grid Infrastructure Index (a total-return index; also published under the name NASDAQ OMX Clean Edge Smart Grid Infrastructure Index). The index captures companies involved in smart-grid infrastructure: transmission and distribution equipment, grid software and automation, energy storage, and the electrical components that move and manage power. It is a global, passive strategy — no currency hedging — issued by First Trust and launched November 16, 2009.

Clean Edge’s methodology selects and weights companies by smart-grid business exposure rather than broad market capitalization. That is why the fund’s roster looks nothing like a sector index: large industrial conglomerates (Eaton, Schneider, ABB) sit beside transmission builders (Quanta), European utilities (National Grid, E.ON), and electrical-component specialists (nVent, Hubbell). The methodology is the reason the fund delivers genuine thematic purity rather than closet industrials beta — and it is also why concentration is a feature, not a bug.

What changed since September 27 (verified)

September 30, 2026 (Wednesday)

Executive Order 14420 — the president declared a national emergency over foreign-produced bulk-power equipment (substation transformers, grid-connected inverters, battery energy-storage systems, high-voltage circuit breakers, industrial control systems, and associated software). The order is aimed at steering U.S. utilities and infrastructure operators toward domestic or secure suppliers as the Department of Energy writes implementation rules. For GRID’s portfolio this is a demand-shoring tailwind (Eaton, Quanta, Hubbell are the obvious beneficiaries), with the watch item that the European core (ABB, Schneider, Prysmian) will want clean implementation detail. Rule-making, not headlines, decides the size of the prize.

The demand backdrop behind the theme

1

AI data-center load is the forcing function

Hyperscaler AI buildout has made power availability a hard industry ceiling — Dell exited its quarter with a record $95B AI backlog and named power availability as the constraint. Grid investment is running near $550B a year and climbing, and more than 2,500 GW of generation and large-load projects sit in global connection queues while transformer lead times stretch past three years.

2

The order books are the thesis

The fund’s top five — Eaton (~8.6%), Quanta (~8.6%), Johnson Controls (~8.3%), ABB (~7.9%), Schneider (~7.8%) — sit at ~41% of net assets, directly in the order flow from utilities expanding transmission. The valuation question resolves on order intake: at ~27x earnings with visible multi-year backlogs the multiple is defensible; with decelerating orders it is not.

3

Policy is now a tailwind, not a variable

Executive Order 14420 adds a demand-shoring argument to the existing capex cycle: utilities favored toward secure suppliers is incremental demand for the fund’s U.S. names. Watch the DoE rule-making for the actual teeth.

07 | Decision

The decision

Research posture: BUY | Conviction 6/10 | Forward outlook ~12–15%

Thesis: Intact and policy-reinforced — EO 14420 adds a demand-shoring tailwind to the AI data-center power cycle.
Vehicle: Best-in-lane — ~$12B AUM, 0.56% ER (below thematic average), tight tracking, 5-star Morningstar rating.
Conviction 6, not higher: 1.26 beta, top-ten ~58%, ~27–30x basket multiple, AI-capex-cycle dependence, European FX drag.
Action: None at $182.91 — the installed ≤$169 gate toward ~1% stands. Add only on weakness (~40 more shares / ~$6,800 at the gate).

Risks and what could change the verdict

  • AI-capex stall. The single thesis-breaking risk. If hyperscaler capex pauses, the order books at Eaton, Schneider, and ABB roll over — and a ~30x basket has a long way to fall.
  • Concentration drawdown. Five names at ~41% with a 1.26 beta: in a market selloff GRID falls meaningfully more than the broad market. The $144.62 52-week low shows what that looks like.
  • Rate and capex-cycle sensitivity. Grid buildout is capital-intensive; the 10-year near 5.3% raises utility financing costs and can defer projects.
  • European exposure. A heavily Europe-weighted core brings euro/dollar currency drag and exposure to softer European industrial demand. EO 14420 implementation could also create friction for foreign suppliers.
  • Multiple compression. A ~27–30x trailing basket P/E prices in growth; the fund can fall on valuation alone even if earnings hold.
What would change the verdict: a sustained break in the grid-capex cycle (hyperscaler order cancellations, a multi-quarter collapse in electrical-equipment backlogs) would flip this to Hold with a lower outlook or Reduce; a position drifting above ~1.25% on strength would trigger a trim back to theme size; an expense-ratio increase or index-methodology overhaul would force a re-underwrite. None of these conditions currently hold.
