1. Executive Summary

View: Uber has moved decisively from a scale-at-all-costs marketplace into a cash-generative global platform. Q2 2026 Gross Bookings grew 22% in constant currency, trips increased 18%, Adjusted EBITDA rose 33%, and non-GAAP operating income rose 40%. Trailing 12-month free cash flow exceeded $10 billion for the first time. [1] The stock, however, trades at only about 15.9x trailing free cash flow and about 18.0x 2027 consensus adjusted EPS, leaving a valuation that does not require heroic assumptions if bookings remain in the high teens and incremental margins continue to rise. [8] [9]

Senior analyst conclusion
At $78.82, the risk/reward is favorable enough for an ADD / OUTPERFORM stance with a $105 base-case target. The strongest part of the thesis is not ride-hailing alone; it is the combination of global demand aggregation, cross-product engagement, advertising, membership, and increasingly powerful free cash flow. The reason this is not a maximum-conviction rating is that Uber is simultaneously committing substantial capital to autonomy and pursuing a large, debt-funded acquisition of Delivery Hero. Those moves increase strategic optionality but reduce the purity of the historical asset-light thesis.

What is working

  • Gross Bookings have sustained 20%+ constant-currency growth for multiple quarters.
  • Earnings growth is materially faster than bookings growth, showing operating leverage.
  • Mobility and Delivery are both expanding margins while maintaining strong growth.
  • Uber One, ads, grocery/retail, U4B and sparse-market penetration provide incremental growth without requiring a new core marketplace.
  • Buybacks are more than offsetting dilution, with shares outstanding down about 2.3% year over year. [9]

What needs to be proven

  • Autonomy must strengthen, not disintermediate, Uber's demand and dispatch layer.
  • More than $10B of planned AV-related capital commitments must earn attractive returns. [3]
  • Delivery Hero must close, integrate and deliver EPS accretion without impairing balance-sheet flexibility.
  • Delivery profitability must keep expanding even as global competition intensifies.
  • Free cash flow quality must remain strong after accounting for stock compensation and M&A-related capital deployment.

2. Investment Thesis and Variant Perception

01
Scale is becoming a monetization flywheel, not merely a network-effect story.

At 208 million monthly active platform consumers and 3.9 billion quarterly trips, Uber can spread fixed technology, support and marketplace costs over a massive transaction base. Q2 non-GAAP operating income grew 40%, substantially faster than 22% constant-currency bookings growth. [1]

02
Cross-platform density creates structural economics that single-product competitors cannot easily replicate.

Uber can move a consumer among rides, restaurant delivery, grocery, retail, business travel and future autonomous trips within one identity and payments layer. Management says cross-platform users generate roughly 3x the Gross Bookings and profits of single-product users. [5]

03
Delivery is no longer a cash-burning adjunct.

Delivery Gross Bookings grew 25% in constant currency in Q2 while segment operating income margin reached 3.8% of Gross Bookings. Grocery and Retail reached a $15B annualized bookings run-rate and ads exceeded a $2.5B annualized revenue run-rate. [3]

04
Autonomy is an option on a larger commercialization layer, but capital intensity is now a real valuation constraint.

Uber's multi-partner approach improves the probability that its app remains a key demand aggregator. Yet management expects more than $10B of AV-related capital commitments over coming years. The thesis works best if Uber supplies demand, dispatch, fleet services and financing infrastructure without becoming the economic owner of the autonomous driving stack. [3]

Variant perception
The market debate is often framed too simplistically as "AV kills Uber" versus "Uber wins AV." The more relevant economic question is who owns the customer relationship, utilization and commercialization layer when multiple autonomous fleets coexist. My base case is that a multi-provider AV market raises the value of Uber's demand aggregation, but the market is right to discount the stock for the possibility that Uber must commit materially more capital than it historically needed to generate that value.
What appears priced in
At roughly 18x 2027 adjusted EPS and about 16x trailing FCF, the market appears to price in meaningful deceleration and/or a persistent autonomy/M&A risk discount. That is reasonable, but it looks too severe if bookings remain high-teens or better and earnings growth stays above 20%. [8] [9]

3. Business Model, Competitive Position and Moat

Uber is best understood as a global marketplace and operating system for local movement. It connects demand with mobility drivers, couriers, merchants and freight capacity, then monetizes the network through service fees, membership, advertising and adjacent products. The competitive advantage is not just app recognition; it is the combination of liquidity, local supply operations, dispatch, pricing, payments, identity, insurance relationships, regulatory experience and cross-product demand.

Demand aggregation

Large consumer scale lowers customer-acquisition friction and supports new-product launches. More use cases improve retention and frequency.

Supply density

More drivers and couriers improve wait times and utilization, which reinforces consumer reliability and provider earnings.

Cross-platform economics

Mobility can feed Delivery and vice versa, while Uber One and advertising monetize engagement across both sides.

Moat assessment: Strong, but changing
Uber's moat has strengthened as the network has become profitable, because competitors now need to match not only growth but also consumer scale, provider liquidity and cross-product economics. The emerging AV market could either deepen that moat by making Uber the neutral aggregator of multiple fleets or weaken it if the largest AV suppliers become vertically integrated consumer platforms.

Earnings quality

GAAP net income is a poor stand-alone valuation anchor because equity-investment revaluations are volatile. Q2 GAAP net income of $2.4B included a $1.6B pre-tax gain from equity investments. [1] I therefore put greater weight on non-GAAP operating income, Adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow.

Stock compensation

SBC was $556M in Q2 and $1.029B for the first half of 2026. At June 30, unvested awards carried $4.9B of unrecognized compensation cost. [2] This remains material, but the economic dilution is being more than offset by repurchases: Uber retired 46.6M shares for $3.5B in H1. [2]

Mobility: core cash engine

Mobility combines strong growth with the highest incremental profitability. The U.S. reaccelerated as Uber reinvested insurance savings, expanded premium and affordable products, and pushed into sparser markets. Management said U4B Gross Bookings grew more than 40%, while suburban/sparse markets grew about 1.5x faster than dense markets and at higher margins. [3]

Delivery: second profit pool

Delivery is increasingly important because its bookings growth is faster than Mobility while margins continue to expand. Grocery and Retail is now a $15B annualized bookings business growing around 40%, and Uber One members account for more than 70% of Delivery Gross Bookings. [3]

Freight remains the weak link
Freight grew bookings quickly in Q2 but still produced a segment operating loss. It does not impair the consolidated thesis at current scale, but it has yet to prove that it can earn an attractive return on management attention and capital.

7. Autonomous Vehicles: The Central Strategic Debate

Autonomy is both Uber's largest long-duration opportunity and the most credible threat to the existing mobility economics. The question is not whether robotaxis will grow; it is whether Uber remains the neutral consumer and utilization layer when they do.

Bull case: Uber as commercialization layer

  • Multiple AV developers create a need for demand aggregation across brands and geographies.
  • Uber's marketplace can improve vehicle utilization, a critical determinant of robotaxi economics.
  • Uber can provide dispatch, fleet operations, insurance, charging, financing, regulatory relationships and customer support.
  • AVs were live on Uber in 7 cities at Q2 and management targeted as many as 15 by year-end 2026. [3]
  • Partners had committed approximately 120,000 vehicles to Uber's network over coming years. [3]

Bear case: asset-light model erodes

  • The largest AV platforms may prioritize first-party apps and reserve the best economics for themselves.
  • Uber has already moved from pure marketplace capex to equity investments, infrastructure and vehicle offtake commitments.
  • Management expects more than $10B of AV-related capital commitments over coming years. [3]
  • Pricing could become more competitive if robotaxi supply grows faster than demand.
  • The long-run take rate on autonomous trips is not yet disclosed or proven at scale.
Current scale gives Uber time
Management indicated on the Q2 call that AV rides remain below 0.5% of Uber's overall trip volume, while the company runs roughly 300 million trips per week. [11] That does not eliminate disruption risk, but it makes near-term earnings impairment from autonomy unlikely. The bigger near-term issue is capital allocation: Uber is funding the transition before the economics are fully visible.

If Uber's AV strategy evolves from commercialization infrastructure into persistent ownership of hardware-heavy fleet assets or large recurring vehicle losses, the appropriate valuation multiple should fall even if bookings growth remains strong.

8. Delivery Hero Acquisition: Strategic Logic vs. Balance-Sheet Complexity

Uber's proposed acquisition of Delivery Hero is the largest strategic change in the current underwrite. Uber is offering EUR 41.50 per share, implying $14.8B of equity value for 100% of Delivery Hero, or $13.7B adjusted for Uber's prior stake purchases. The assets Uber expects to acquire span 50 markets with $42B of 2025 Gross Bookings; the combined company would operate in 99 markets with pro forma 2025 Gross Bookings of $236B. [5]

Why it can create value

  • Markets with both Mobility and Delivery nearly double from 34 to 58. [5]
  • Cross-platform engagement should lower acquisition costs and increase frequency.
  • Uber One, advertising, AI and merchant tools can be deployed across a much larger installed base.
  • Management expects immediate non-GAAP EPS accretion at close and high-single-digit percentage accretion by year three. [5]

Why it raises risk

  • Large cross-border regulatory process; closing is expected in H2 2027.
  • Integration spans multiple local brands, technology stacks and regulatory regimes.
  • Uber is financing the offer with cash plus new debt and has a roughly EUR 14B bridge facility. [5]
  • Gross leverage is targeted below 2x, but this is a meaningful change from an increasingly cash-rich standalone trajectory. [5]
Underwriting treatment
I do not give full valuation credit for management's year-three accretion target today. The base target assumes the core Uber business can compound earnings strongly on its own and treats Delivery Hero as incremental upside only after closing and integration evidence improves. This is deliberately more conservative than capitalizing all stated synergies immediately.

Explicit thesis-break conditions
  1. Growth break: two consecutive quarters of constant-currency Gross Bookings growth below roughly 15% without a clear macro shock.
  2. Operating leverage break: non-GAAP operating income growth persistently falls below Gross Bookings growth while incentive intensity rises.
  3. Cash break: trailing free cash flow falls below roughly $9B and does not recover, excluding discrete acquisition cash costs.
  4. AV economics break: AV trip volume scales materially while Uber's monetization deteriorates, or major AV partners bypass Uber at scale and erode marketplace liquidity.
  5. Capital structure break: Delivery Hero / AV spending pushes leverage materially above management's stated sub-2x gross leverage framework without a clear deleveraging path.
  6. Dilution break: net share count stops declining despite a sustained $10B+ annual FCF run-rate.

What would make me more constructive
Evidence that AV deployments scale across multiple providers while Uber retains attractive economics, plus a Delivery Hero closing path that preserves sub-2x gross leverage and ongoing share retirement, would justify raising both conviction and the valuation multiple.
What would make me less constructive
The most important early warning would be a simultaneous rise in capital intensity and deceleration in core marketplace growth. That combination would undermine the reason Uber deserves a platform multiple rather than a transportation / fleet multiple.

Final Investment View

Uber's investment case has matured. The question is no longer whether the company can become profitable; it has demonstrated substantial operating leverage and more than $10B of trailing free cash flow. The next phase is about the durability and quality of that cash flow as Uber expands internationally, monetizes local commerce, integrates Delivery Hero and positions itself for autonomy.

At $78.82, the valuation offers enough margin of safety for an ADD / OUTPERFORM rating and a $105 base-case target. The core platform can support that value without requiring a heroic autonomous-vehicle outcome. Autonomy is upside if Uber becomes the dominant commercialization layer, but it is also the most important source of downside if capital requirements rise or direct AV distribution weakens Uber's marketplace role.
