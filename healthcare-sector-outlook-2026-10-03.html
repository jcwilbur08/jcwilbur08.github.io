Investment conclusion

Healthcare is the cheapest large sector in the market relative to its own history — and the fundamentals are inflecting, not deteriorating. The sector trades at ~18–19x forward earnings versus a ~20.5x ten-year average and just 0.8x the S&P 500, near the historic relative low (it was 1.4x in the late 1990s). Yet S&P 500 healthcare companies are expected to deliver double-digit earnings growth from Q4 2026 through 2027 with margin expansion — a combination the market has not priced. The 2026 story is stock-specific, not beta: oral GLP-1s (Lilly's Foundayo, the fastest NME approval since 2002), a surprisingly generous 2027 Medicare Advantage rate (+4.98% effective), $93.8 billion of H1 biotech M&A, and a violent medtech derating (IHI −18% YTD) that has created the most interesting contrarian setup in the sector. We are selectively OVERWEIGHT healthcare: quality large pharma (LLY, JNJ, ABBV), large biotech (AMGN, VRTX, GILD), derated medtech (BSX, ISRG, ABT), and managed care on margin recovery (ELV; held name UNH). For the portfolio — currently just ~2.5% in healthcare across BMY, UNH, and GEHC — the cleanest path to weight is a core XLV/VHT position with tactical IHI on medtech weakness.

1. Sector scoreboard: trailing the market at a historic discount

XLV +7.4% YTD vs S&P 500 ~+12% · forward P/E ~18–19x

Healthcare has been a 2026 comeback story that still trails the broad market: XLV is up about 7.4% year to date on price (~9–10% total return) against ~12–13% for the S&P 500 — a 4–5 point gap that opened after the sector peaked on August 19 and pulled back ~6%. The valuation, though, is the argument: ~18–19x forward earnings is a modest discount to the ~20.5x ten-year average and just 0.8x the S&P 500's multiple — near the historic relative low, down from 1.4x in the late 1990s. The innovation premium has fully eroded at the index level even as innovation itself (oral GLP-1s, surgical robotics, radiopharma) accelerates. That dislocation — cheap multiple, double-digit forward earnings growth expected from Q4 2026 through 2027 — is the core of the overweight case.

2. Five themes driving 2026

GLP-1 orals · MA rates · M&A · IRA · medtech volumes

The oral GLP-1 wave. The FDA approved Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron) on April 1, 2026 — the first small-molecule oral GLP-1 for chronic weight management and the fastest new-molecular-entity approval since 2002 — following Novo's oral semaglutide in December 2025. At $149/month self-pay, orals collapse the access barrier; the oral GLP-1 market is estimated at $137 billion by 2035. Lilly's Q1 revenue rose 56% and full-year guidance was raised to $82–85 billion.

Medicare Advantage: relief, then reality. CMS finalized the 2027 MA rate at +2.48% base (~4.98% effective, over $13 billion) on April 6 — a sharp reversal from the +0.09% proposed — sparking a relief rally in UNH, HUM, and CVS. But CMS kept the 5.9% coding adjustment, is expanding RADV audits to all ~550 contracts a year, and commercial medical-cost trend still runs above 11%. Margin recovery is a 2028 story, not a 2026 one.

Biotech M&A at full boil. Large-cap biopharma announced $93.8 billion in H1 2026 deals, Lilly alone closing eleven ($25 billion). The majors — AbbVie/Apogee $10.9B, GSK/Nuvalent $10.6B, Merck KGaA/Bio-Techne $11.3B, Vertex/Crinetics ~$10B, Boston Scientific/Penumbra $14.5B — show scarcity value for de-risked growth, with takeout premiums routinely above 40%.

IRA negotiation now bites. Round-1 maximum fair prices took effect January 1, 2026; round-2 (15 drugs including the semaglutides at $274/month, down 71–80%) goes live January 1, 2027; round-3 — the first to include Part B drugs — was announced in January 2026 for 2028. The exposure is now quantifiable rather than feared, which is why large pharma has started to work.

Medtech: solid volumes, tariff drag. Procedure demand is healthy — orthopedics migrating to ASCs, surgical robotics growing ~15% annually — but tariffs (GEHC: $265M headwind in 2025), a soft China, and a pending Section 232 device probe overhang margins. The group is the sector's laggard and its best contrarian setup.

4. Held names check: BMY, UNH, GEHC

Three positions, three different cycles · combined ~2.5% of NAV

Bristol-Myers Squibb (BMY, $61.15) — HOLD maintained, $68 target. Q2 was a genuine beat: revenue $12.97B (+6%, $1.2B ahead of consensus), non-GAAP EPS $2.04 (+40%), and FY2026 guidance raised to $49–50B revenue / $6.75–$7.00 EPS. The Growth Portfolio (+15%, ~60% of revenue) is doing its job as Eliquis eventually fades. The $0.63 quarterly dividend (~4.0% yield, 17 straight raises) is intact and well covered. At ~9x forward earnings with a $67 average target, BMY remains deep value with pipeline optionality (milvexian and Cobenfy readouts pushed to early 2027 — watch, don't underwrite). Our September 27 HOLD/$68 stands.

UnitedHealth (UNH, $371.90) — constructive; accumulate gate stands. Q2 was the quarter the turnaround became visible: adjusted EPS $6.38 (+56%, crushing the $4.91 consensus), medical care ratio down to 86.7% from 89.4%, and FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised to $19.50–$20.00, with buybacks doubled to at least $5B. The 2027 MA rate (+4.98% effective) eased the policy scare. The overhangs are real but known: unresolved DOJ criminal/civil MA-billing probes, September OIG audits alleging $24–47M in overpayments (disputed), and commercial cost trend above 11% with full margin recovery guided beyond 2027. Consensus sits at Strong Buy with targets ~$466–$482 (~25–30% upside). Our dashboard gate — accumulate toward 2% weight — remains the right posture: add the turnaround, don't chase it into October 13 earnings.

GE HealthCare (GEHC, $64.29) — ADD maintained, $92 target. Nothing has changed since our October 2 report: record orders (+11.1% organic), record $23.9B backlog, 1.15 book-to-bill, guidance reaffirmed — against a stock down 20% YTD on PCS weakness (under strategic review), China softness, and tariff noise. At ~13.5x forward versus the sector's ~18.8x, the discount prices the haircut; the $62 add gate stays live.

7. Portfolio fit: from 2.5% toward a full weight

~$41.6K of $1.69M · benchmark weight ~11–13%

Healthcare is the portfolio's most underweight sector: BMY ($19.6K), UNH ($18.7K), and GEHC ($3.3K) sum to roughly $41.6K — about 2.5% of NAV against an S&P 500 benchmark weight of ~11–13%. For a retirement portfolio, that is a structural gap, not a tactical call: healthcare's earnings are among the least cyclical in the market, and the sector's current 0.8x relative multiple is the entry point.

Recommended path: (1) Establish a core sector position via XLV or VHT (0.08–0.09% expense) toward 5–8% of NAV — instant diversification across pharma, biotech, and medtech at the historic relative discount. (2) Add IHI tactically on medtech weakness for the contrarian sleeve (BSX/ISRG/ABT exposure in one ticket). (3) Keep building the single names on discipline: the GEHC ≤$62 add gate stays live, and the UNH accumulate-toward-2% gate remains the managed-care expression. BMY needs no action at HOLD/$68. No ETF or equity from this report is added to the dashboard gates — gates remain CIO-approved only.

8. Risks and the Q4 catalyst calendar

Policy · medical costs · a dense Q4

Risks: (1) IRA round-2 prices go live January 1, 2027 (semaglutides at $274/month, down 71–80%) — formulary and Part D positioning reprices through Q4. (2) MA scrutiny ratchets: RADV audits expanding to all ~550 contracts a year, October Star Ratings, and UNH's unresolved DOJ probes. (3) Section 232 device tariffs could extend duties to medtech.
