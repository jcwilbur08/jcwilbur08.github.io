03 / Business drivers and competitive position

Mobility: profitability strong, supply weaker

Bookings $29.512B, +17% CC. Segment operating income $2.362B, +27%, roughly 8.0% of bookings. Fuel costs and driver constraints affected certain international markets; management reported initial October improvement. The crucial issue is whether incentives restore supply without eroding contribution margins. [S1] [S2] [S3]

Delivery: broadened commerce engine

Bookings $28.539B, +23% CC. Segment operating income $1.057B, +30%. Grocery/retail was discussed at approximately $15B annual run-rate, growing ~40%, while membership reached ~55M Uber One members. Better selection, frequency and promotion funding create multiple monetization paths. [S1] [S2]

Freight and business mix. Freight bookings grew 35% to $1.768B but the segment remained operating-loss-making (~$33M). Reported Mobility revenue declined 2% despite bookings growth, largely reflecting changes in transaction/accounting presentation rather than equivalent demand deterioration; evaluate bookings, segment margins and non-GAAP operating income together. [S1] [S3]

Competitive context: Scale and hybrid fleet density underpin Uber's marketplace moat, but AV fleet owners can distribute through multiple apps or build direct channels. DoorDash/Instacart consolidation, if completed, could increase Delivery competition despite Uber's greater cross-platform reach. Statements about future outcomes are scenarios, not established facts. [S2] [S11]

Reverse DCF

At $71.92, with a $10.0B normalized FCF starting point, 11.0% cost of equity and 3.5% terminal growth, the market price implies roughly 5.0% annual FCF growth over five years. Low implied growth is favorable only if the cash flow is truly distributable after strategic needs.

DCF formula: Equity value = Σ [FCF₀·(1+g)^t/(1+r)^t] for t=1…5 + [FCF₅·(1+g_terminal)/(r−g_terminal)]/(1+r)^5; per-share = total equity value / 2.04B. Terminal-growth and cost-of-equity assumptions are analyst inputs, not reported values. Sensitivity is material: at fixed base FCF and 12% five-year growth, moving cost of equity from 11.0% to 12.0% reduces value substantially. [S1] [S7]

Valuation against alternatives

DoorDash (DASH) has a more delivery-weighted model, while Lyft (LYFT) has a narrower mobility footprint, making headline peer EV/revenue or P/E comps unreliable without consistent accounting, segment and EBITDA adjustments. This report does not invent fresh comparable-company multiples; the most useful corroboration is the change in Uber's own EV/EBITDA, FCF yield and normalized earnings after Q3.

11 / Checkpoints, risks and thesis-break triggers

Next concrete checkpoints

  1. Q4 2026 (next print; date unconfirmed): Gross Bookings $63.25–65.25B and EPS $0.95–0.99; Mobility growth recovery and incentives.
  2. Year-end 2026: approx. 700 fully driverless vehicles and evidence of paid rides, utilization and partner economics.
  3. 2027: 2,000+ driverless vehicles, contracted fleet-finance structures, no material adverse shift to balance-sheet ownership.
  4. Next two quarters: Uber One retention, grocery/retail organic growth, incremental Delivery margins, net share-count trend.
  5. Acquisition gates: Delivery Hero/ezCater closing, financing details, cash returns and goodwill/asset intensity.

Strongest contrary case

  • Mobility demand is healthy but driver availability becomes structurally expensive.
  • Delivery's topline acceleration has poor incremental returns when incentives and courier costs normalize.
  • Regulatory, labor, insurance and geopolitical shocks reduce platform take rates.
  • AV owners bypass or multihome away from Uber; Uber must finance more assets to preserve distribution.
  • Cash-generative legacy model overstates freely distributable equity FCF after fleet, acquisition and stock-based-compensation costs.
Proposed thesis-break conditions for CIO review: (1) bookings growth under 15% for two quarters without a transitory explanation; (2) recurring normalized equity FCF below ~$8B; (3) sustained Mobility margin compression tied to higher driver-supply costs; or (4) Uber materially absorbs AV fleet ownership/guarantees without evidence of attractive after-capital returns. These are research risk gates, not an authoritative status change.

Research conclusion: Remain constructive. Q3 reinforced Uber's platform, cash generation and operating leverage. Maintain Staged Add / 8 out of 10 research conviction around $65–72 with more compelling opportunity below $65. Do not equate a positive earnings reaction with solved AV economics; preserving capital discipline is essential to the long-term thesis.

Open questions: Exact organic delivery vs acquired contribution; timing and economics of Delivery Hero and other acquisitions; whether AV funding is true non-recourse third-party debt or includes contingent Uber support; detailed net-share repurchase effects; fresh and reconciled 2027 Street EPS/FCF estimates; exact v67 holdings-weight calculation after checksum verification.
