Structural engines

Power & Energy: Q2 2026 segment sales rose 17% to $8.24B and segment profit rose 30% to $2.03B; Power Generation application sales increased 29% to $3.10B. Caterpillar reported large-engine and turbine-related growth primarily in data-center applications. Importantly, the entire $72.1B order backlog should not be called AI demand: it covers all segments. [S1, S2]

Construction & mining: Construction Industries Q2 segment sales rose 35% to $8.35B, while Resource Industries grew 20% to $4.65B. Positive volume and price were broad-based, though North American housing, public-project execution, commodities and high borrowing costs remain cyclical swing factors. [S1, S2]

Competitive position: Cat's equipment durability, dealers, parts availability, operator familiarity and financing ecosystem raise switching costs. Global aftermarket and services improve the quality of profit, yet are not enough to make CAT a non-cyclical compounder.

Management, capital allocation & balance sheet

CEO Joe Creed's business is using elevated cash generation to fund capacity and shareholder returns. In Q2 the company generated $4.4B of enterprise operating cash flow and used $1.5B for repurchases and $0.7B for dividends. Enterprise cash ended the quarter near $6.7B. Management forecasts around $3.5B of 2026 capital expenditure, indicating capacity requirements alongside earnings strength. Cat Financial debt should not be conflated automatically with parent manufacturing leverage; financing-book credit performance warrants separate monitoring. [S1, S2]

Reverse DCF: At $714.20, the model needs roughly 10.6% annual FCFE growth for the first five years beyond the $12.5B run-rate, followed by 6% for five years and 3% terminal growth, at a 9% cost of equity. That is not an obviously undemanding market hurdle for a cyclical industrial. A 9.5% discount rate would further reduce model value. Valuation reconciliation: 24× published FY2027 adjusted EPS of $32.59 yields ~$782, rounded to our $780 scenario base. The equity DCF gives ~$696. A rough equal-weight triangulation yields ~$738. Therefore, the stock is closer to fair value at ~$714 than a Street price target of ~$927 alone might imply. Terminal assumptions, working capital and Cat Financial's financing profile are significant sources of uncertainty.

08 / Catalysts, risks and thesis-break tests

October 29 management call: five questions

  1. Does backlog increase sequentially, especially Power & Energy, and how much is deliverable in 12 months?
  2. Are data-center engine/turbine orders converting to recognized sales without adverse cancellation trends?
  3. Excluding the $392M Q2 tariff-recovery benefit, how durable is the adjusted operating margin?
  4. Does Construction Industries' North America growth represent end-customer retail demand or dealer stocking?
  5. Are FY2027 EPS, cash conversion and manufacturing capacity assumptions moving up, flat or down?

Principal contrary evidence

A data-center power bottleneck can shift sales beyond forecast windows; infrastructure buyers could defer projects in response to rates or AI spending reassessment. Heavy capital spending may consume free cash flow even as EBITDA grows. Tariffs and price-cost lag can erode margins; mining and construction can slow simultaneously. A 20×+ forward multiple is not a recession-proof floor. [S2, S4, S7]

Explicit thesis-break / risk triggers

  • Two sequential quarters of materially weaker Power & Energy net orders/backlog conversion not explained by shipment timing.
  • FY2027 adjusted EPS consensus drops more than ~15% from $32.59 (below ~$27.70) without offsetting valuation compression.
  • Normalized consolidated operating margin remains below ~17% without a credible return path.
  • Persistent negative industrial free-cash-flow surprises versus earnings or sharp deterioration in Cat Financial credit performance.
  • Manufacturing expansion creates cancellations, margin disappointment or structurally unproductive capital spending.
