01 · Investment thesis

A quality compounder, but not a blank-check entry.

Siemens has earned a higher valuation through better mix, stronger margins and unusually deep order visibility. The Buy rests on a wide-moat industrial franchise, a record backlog, improving automation demand and electrification-led growth. Conviction stops at 6/10 because a 2-star valuation, a post-beat selloff and muted FY2027 expectations leave little room for merely good execution.

Visibility

Backlog reached a record €132 billion in Q3, while book-to-bill climbed to 1.34.

Mix upgrade

Smart Infrastructure and industrial software are increasing the share of higher-growth, higher-margin revenue.

Self-help

Portfolio exits, the planned Healthineers deconsolidation and a fresh €6 billion buyback sharpen the equity story.

Portfolio verdict

Initiate Buy, 6/10, with a $166 target. The operating case is strong enough to own, but the current price is not a compelling place to force a full position before November 12.

Recommended gate: add at or below $135 toward roughly 1% of portfolio. This is a recommendation only; the gate has not been installed.

ADR ratio requires compliance confirmation

The working conversion is 2 SIEGY ADRs = 1 Siemens ordinary share. That ratio is strongly supported by price and share-count arithmetic, but no primary Siemens or depositary-bank document was located to confirm it. All ADR-equivalent valuation math in this report should be read with that caveat.

Q3 quality

The market’s message

The ordinary shares fell roughly 4–5% despite the strong Q3 print. The business exceeded expectations; the stock showed that expectations were already demanding.

04 · Demand & outlook

Electrification leads while automation heals.

Smart Infrastructure is the near-term earnings engine. Digital Industries supplies the upside option if automation demand normalizes without giving back software momentum.

Smart Infrastructure

Q3 orders rose 42% and revenue 13%. Full-year revenue growth guidance moved to 10–11%, with margin guided to 18.5–19.5%.

Digital Industries

Q3 revenue grew 10% to €4.93 billion. Orders grew 9% to €4.85 billion but missed consensus, keeping the recovery thesis honest.

Mobility

A €52 billion FY2025 backlog supports visibility, while U.S. tariffs and project execution constrain margin expansion.

Macro offsets remain material

Siemens has framed foreign exchange as an approximately 4% revenue drag and about a 50-basis-point industrial-margin headwind for FY2026. The EU–U.S. tariff regime could add up to €500 million of burden, with Mobility already feeling pressure.

05 · Portfolio reshaping

More software, less conglomerate drag.

Management is recycling capital from listed stakes and mature assets into industrial software while preparing the largest remaining structural simplification: Healthineers deconsolidation.

  • 1
    Altair · $10.6 billionClosed in March 2025. Adds simulation and AI capabilities, with more than $500 million of expected annual revenue synergies midterm and more than $150 million of annual EBITDA cost synergies by year two.
  • 2
    Dotmatics · $5.1 billionAnnounced in April 2025 for life-sciences R&D software. The price exceeds 16× estimated 2025 sales, making integration and cross-selling the burden of proof.
  • 3
    Healthineers · planned 30% spin-offSiemens plans to distribute 30% of its 67% stake, retain about 37%, and seek a shareholder vote at the February 2027 AGM. Tax and regulatory confirmation remain open.
  • 4
    Siemens Energy · below 5%The voting stake was cut to 4.98% in September 2026, versus 35.1% retained at the 2020 spin-off.

Strategic upside

Deconsolidation can make Siemens easier to value and concentrate capital on automation, software, electrification and mobility.

Execution burden

Healthineers may need to refinance up to €13.9 billion of Siemens-backed loans, while the richly priced Dotmatics deal must convert strategic logic into measurable returns.

Dividend per share has risen from €4.00 in FY2021 to €5.35 in FY2025, a 34% increase. Yield compressed because the share price rose faster than the payout.

Balance-sheet capacity

Moody’s kept the Aa3 rating and stable outlook after the Dotmatics announcement. That resilience matters because portfolio simplification and software M&A are happening simultaneously.

08 · Risk ledger

The risk is not demand alone. It is the expectation attached to it.

High · valuation

Excellent execution is increasingly the baseline.

At roughly 24× FY2026 earnings and above Morningstar fair value, Siemens can beat operationally and still fall, as Q3 demonstrated.

Medium-high · macro

Automation recovery remains cyclical.

German industrial investment is muted, China remains uneven, and Q3 Digital Industries orders missed consensus even as revenue improved.

Medium-high · policy

Tariffs and currency can dilute operating momentum.

The potential tariff burden reaches €500 million, while foreign exchange is expected to weigh on both revenue and margins.

Medium · portfolio

Healthineers separation is complex.

Tax, regulatory and refinancing work must be completed without disrupting dividends, credit quality or operating focus.

Medium · integration

Software acquisitions carry a high valuation bar.

Altair and Dotmatics deepen the stack, but the strategic premium must translate into cross-selling, retention and measurable margin lift.
