05 / Four debates likely to move the stock

1. Advertising: AI already earns a return—but how durable is it?

In Q2, Meta advertising revenue reached $59.36B, up roughly 27% year-over-year. Ad impressions rose 14% and average price per ad 12%, suggesting both engagement and advertiser outcomes improved. Watch Q3 impressions, price, ad load, cross-border demand, Advantage+ adoption, Reels monetization and Q4 holiday-ad guidance. The bullish test is continuing ad-productivity gains despite slower macro spending.

2. Muse: product excitement versus paying economics

Muse's consumer launch has improved perceived AI optionality, but downloads/leaderboard positions are not retention, net revenue or economic profit. Demand explicit 30/90-day retention, daily or weekly actives, conversion to paid tiers, enterprise API revenue, inference cost per user, model efficiency and partner permissions. Reports of enterprise security restrictions are a material bearish counterweight.

3. Compute demand: infrastructure spend and depreciation

Management's $130–145B FY26 capital-expenditure outlook dwarfs prior periods. A higher 2027 plan is not inherently negative if the ad/AI cash-return bridge is credible; without return metrics, extra spend pressures the stock's free-cash-flow multiple. Scrutinize depreciation lives, financing leases, power availability, third-party compute, stock compensation and flexibility to slow spend.

4. Quality of earnings and cash flow

Q2 revenue grew 28%, but operating income declined 8%, with a reported 31% margin and only $0.784B of free cash flow. One-off litigation and severance matter, yet the central problem is how much of the cash squeeze is transitory versus a recurring reinvestment cost. Normalize EPS for tax/accounting effects; do not extrapolate distorted Q3 2025 GAAP EPS.

Reverse DCF: what today's $694.25 embeds

Holding base assumptions at 9.5% discount, 3% terminal growth, 2.55B diluted shares, $10B net obligations, a $65B 2027 starting cash-flow assumption and post-year-five growth equal to half early growth, the stock approximately requires 16% FCF growth in years 2–5 and ~8% in years 6–10. If 2027 normalized FCF falls to $50B, that hurdle rises substantially. The embedded assumption is demanding given the ongoing capital cycle and is the clearest reason to wait for guidance before adding.

Valuation conclusion: Earnings-multiple targets imply more upside than cash-flow underwriting. The disagreement is not noise: it is the central investment debate about reinvestment duration and returns. Our post-print add gate requires free-cash-flow credibility to narrow that gap.
