01 · Investment thesis

The market sees terminal decline. We see damaged—but durable—economics.

The long case is mean reversion on a wide-moat franchise, not a return to hypergrowth. At 11.6× forward earnings with a roughly 4.6–5.0% yield, Novo needs only to stabilize—not win—to justify a higher multiple.

Sentiment is washed out for good reasons. Eli Lilly has taken meaningful obesity share, the REDEFINE 4 result weakened confidence in CagriSema, and the US is forcing a volume-over-price transition. Semaglutide still generated roughly 74% of 2025 sales, making the patent cliff and pricing reset enterprise-wide issues rather than product-level footnotes.

But deterioration is no longer accelerating. Novo raised its adjusted 2026 sales and operating-profit outlook twice—from a decline of 5–13% at constant exchange rates to a decline of 0–6%. Oral Wegovy had more than five million cumulative prescriptions within 30 weeks, international growth remains healthier than the US, and the balance sheet can fund manufacturing, R&D, dividends, buybacks, and bolt-on deals.

Desk verdict

Buy · conviction 6/10 · $46 target. The valuation creates room for operational repair, but not enough evidence exists for a Strong Buy. Morningstar's DKK 275 fair value and Morgan Stanley's DKK 250 Underweight call bracket the debate.

The upside is capped by Lilly's lead, US pricing, and a 2031–32 US/EU semaglutide cliff. The next rerating must be earned.

02 · Business & moat

A century-old diabetes franchise built around one extraordinary molecule.

Novo sells into a structurally expanding cardiometabolic market through a trusted global brand, scaled manufacturing, specialist relationships, and hard-won regulatory know-how. That is the moat. Semaglutide concentration is the price of it.

Scale

About 68,800 employees across 80 countries; products marketed in roughly 170. US Operations represented about 56% of 2025 sales.

Control

The Novo Nordisk Foundation, through Novo Holdings, owns 28.1% of capital and 77.3% of votes. Hostile control is effectively off the table.

Concentration

Ozempic, Wegovy, and Rybelsus produced about DKK 228.3B—roughly 74% of 2025 sales.

04 · Pipeline & competition

The pipeline can stabilize the narrative. Lilly still sets the pace.

Novo has credible next-generation assets, but it no longer controls the efficacy narrative. The CagriSema label, high-dose follow-up, and zenagamtide progression must now carry more weight.

CagriSema: mixed, not failed

REDEFINE 4 missed non-inferiority versus tirzepatide: 23.0% weight loss against 25.5% on the efficacy estimand. Later September studies were constructive—21.0% versus 2.0% placebo in REDEFINE 9, and 12.4% versus 9.1% against tirzepatide 5 mg in REIMAGINE 5.

The caveat matters: tirzepatide can be titrated to 15 mg, so the 5 mg comparison applies to a limited population. Goldman Sachs cut its CagriSema peak-sales estimate from $11.8B to $5B.

Lilly's structural lead

Zepbound has taken meaningful share. Oral orforglipron ended Novo's sole-oral window in April 2026, and retatrutide delivered roughly 28% weight loss at its highest Phase 3 dose. Lilly expects to file retatrutide in Q1 2027, potentially putting it on the market before Novo's earlier-stage zenagamtide.

05 · Pricing & patents

Volume can grow while the economics compress.

Novo is shifting from scarcity pricing toward mass-market access. That can expand the treated population, but it lowers the revenue earned per patient in the company's largest market.

US repricing

Wegovy moved from a $1,350 list price to $350 per month on TrumpRx. Medicare and Medicaid access was offered at $245 per month with a $50 beneficiary copay from April 2026.

Compounding

Novo estimates more than one million Americans use compounded versions. The FDA proposed excluding semaglutide, tirzepatide, and liraglutide from the 503B bulk list; 503A litigation continues.

Patent clock

China's semaglutide compound patent expired in March 2026. US and European expiries arrive in 2031–32; Wegovy pill formulation patents extend into the late 2030s.

Why the upside stays capped

The $46 target assumes the franchise can preserve enough unit growth and innovation value to offset lower realized prices. It does not assume a return to the 2023–24 valuation regime.

07 · Capital allocation

The balance sheet can fund the reset without starving shareholders.

Novo's priorities are clear: invest internally, pay an attractive dividend, pursue business development, then use flexible buybacks. Elevated capex is the strategic choice, not a sign of distress.

Cash return

The 2025 dividend was DKK 11.70 per share, the 30th consecutive annual increase and about 51% of diluted EPS. The 2026 buyback authorization is up to DKK 15B; DKK 10.25B had been executed by October 2 at an average DKK 279.95.

Total 2026 shareholder distributions were expected to exceed DKK 60B, while management retained flexibility to reduce buybacks for significant business development.

Reinvestment

PP&E capex reached DKK 60.1B in 2025, up from DKK 47.2B, as Novo expanded API and fill-finish capacity and integrated three former Catalent plants acquired for $11.7B.

Free cash flow recovered to DKK 28.3B in 2025. The 2026 guide is DKK 36–46B.

08 · Risk register

What can invalidate the call.

High · Franchise

Semaglutide concentration

Roughly 74% of 2025 sales came from one molecule. A competitive, safety, payer, or patent shock would hit multiple brands and indications at once.

High · Competition

Lilly extends the efficacy and convenience lead

Zepbound share gains, oral orforglipron, and retatrutide can force both lower prices and more R&D spending before Novo's next-generation assets mature.

High · Pricing

US access becomes a margin trap

The US produced about 56% of 2025 sales. MFN pricing and public-program access may expand volume without preserving historical economics.

High · Patent

The 2031–32 cliff arrives before pipeline replacement

US and European semaglutide protection expires before zenagamtide's long-range opportunity is fully proven; Paragraph IV challengers are already active.

Medium · Execution

Cost saves mask weak demand

Q2's profit beat came mainly from lower selling and R&D expense while adjusted gross margin compressed. A durable recovery needs sales quality, not austerity.

Medium · Governance

Permanent controlling shareholder

The Foundation's 77.3% voting power prevents a takeover and supports long-horizon investment, but minority holders cannot force strategic change.

What would support the $46 target

Stable or improving US volume, evidence that oral Wegovy can sustain demand beyond channel fill, a clean CagriSema approval, and gross-margin stabilization without another spending-led beat.

Portfolio action

Do not chase. Recommend an ADD gate at or below $34 toward roughly 1% of portfolio. The gate is not installed.

Reassess if the Q3 guide weakens, the CagriSema label disappoints, or the pipeline cannot close the gap to Lilly.
