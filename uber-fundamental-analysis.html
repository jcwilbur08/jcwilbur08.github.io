1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Uber is the global leader in rideshare and a top-two player in food delivery, operating a three-sided platform (Mobility, Delivery, Freight) with 208M monthly active platform consumers and 3.9B trips per quarter R. The business has crossed an inflection: TTM free cash flow exceeded $10B for the first time in Q2 2026 R, adjusted EBITDA margins are expanding (4.9% of gross bookings, +40 bps y/y R), and the company is deploying capital across three growth vectors — the transformative Delivery Hero takeover, an aggressive autonomous-vehicle rollout, and a $2.5B-run-rate advertising business growing ~50% R.

The market has not kept up. The stock sits ~30% below its 52-week high and is down 28.5% over the past year R-sec — de-rated on AV-disruption fears even as fundamentals accelerated. Q2 2026 (Aug 5) delivered: gross bookings $58.0B (+24%) R, adjusted EBITDA $2.82B (+33%) R, and non-GAAP EPS $0.81 (+35%) R.

Thesis O: this is a classic expectations reset. At $70.84 the stock trades at ~21.7× FY2026E non-GAAP EPS with a PEG near 0.9, a 7.0% FCF yield, and $16B of buyback authorization remaining — while the two largest forward drivers (Delivery Hero accretion from H2 2027 and AV commercialization, where Uber is winning share in live markets) are priced as risks rather than options. The February 2026 DOL proposal to repeal the 2024 contractor rule removes a major regulatory overhang. Key risks: Delivery Hero execution and leverage, AV economics at scale, and insurance-reserve inflation.

Bottom line: BUY with a $105 12-month target (~48% upside), anchored on ~26× FY2027E consensus EPS of $4.04 C/E — in line with the $103–105 sell-side consensus cluster. The 0.08% position is a stub; the risk/reward favors building it on the current de-rating rather than after the Delivery Hero close de-risks the story.
Portfolio note: Cam holds 20 shares (~$1,417, 0.08% of the $1.70M portfolio). Morningstar rates UBER 3 stars (Sept 2026 export) — our BUY is one notch more constructive, driven by the Delivery Hero and AV optionality the market is discounting.

2. Business Overview

  • Model: three-sided platform across Mobility (rideshare; ~50% of gross bookings), Delivery (Uber Eats, grocery & retail; ~47% of GB), and Freight (3PL brokerage; ~3% of GB) R.
  • Scale: 208M MAPCs (+16% y/y), 3.87B trips/quarter (+18%), 10.2M drivers & couriers earning >$25B in Q2 2026; 79B+ cumulative trips since 2010 R. Trips-per-MAPC grew 2% — growth is user-led, not price-led R/O.
  • Uber One: membership at an all-time high; members account for >70% of Delivery gross bookings (up ~20 pp in two years) R — the loyalty engine behind frequency and retention.
  • Uber Advertising: exceeded a $2.5B annualized revenue run rate in Q2 2026, growing ~50% y/y R — high-margin and still early.
  • Uber for Business: GB +40%+ y/y; premium trips generate ~2× GB and ~4× contribution profit vs UberX R.
  • Grocery & Retail: $15B annualized GB, +~40% y/y for multiple quarters R.
  • Autonomous vehicles (company-stated, Q2 2026 remarks) R: AVs live on Uber in 7 cities, on track for up to 15 by year-end 2026; ~120,000 vehicles committed by partners over coming years; >$10B of capital to be committed (equity, infrastructure, vehicle offtake) with third-party co-financing; Europe deployments with Autobrains and WeRide; advanced partnerships with Nuro, Wayve, Zoox; Wayve received London private-hire licences with launch "over the coming weeks." In mature AV markets (LA, SF, Phoenix), Uber's category share is higher than a year ago.

6. The Delivery Hero Takeover

The defining catalyst — and the deal the market is treating as a risk rather than an opportunity O. Announced July 16, 2026 (primary: Uber businesswire release):

  • Terms: €41.50/share cash; $14.8B equity value ($13.7B net of prior stake) R.
  • Scale: 99 combined markets; $236B pro-forma 2025 gross bookings — roughly doubling Uber's delivery footprint R.
  • Economics: accretive to Non-GAAP EPS on close; high-single-digit accretion by year three R. SSW Partners to buy 14 overlapping markets for ~$1.6B (regulatory remedy). Prosus irrevocably tendering (~17%) → Uber economic interest ~53%.
  • Financing: debt-funded via a €14.2B bridge facility; gross leverage to stay <2×, investment-grade maintained R.
  • Timeline: BaFin approved the offer document Aug 27; acceptance window Aug 27 – Nov 5, 2026; Delivery Hero boards unanimously recommended acceptance Sept 2; close targeted H2 2027 R. FTC review filing submitted (adds timing uncertainty).
What to underwrite: integration execution across 99 markets, multi-jurisdiction approvals, and acceptance through Nov 5, 2026. The strategic logic is clean — Delivery Hero fills Uber's delivery gaps in Europe, MENA and LatAm at a price that is accretive on day one — but until the close, the bridge debt and regulatory path cap multiple expansion O.

7. Competitive Position

  • Rideshare: Lyft is the principal US competitor; BofA estimates Uber holds ~76% of US mobility share, easing to ~73% by 2028 on Waymo/Tesla/Zoox pressure E-sec. Uber's counter: its category share is higher than a year ago in mature AV markets (LA, SF, Phoenix) R.
  • Delivery: DoorDash is the key US rival; the Delivery Hero deal transforms Uber's international delivery position (99 combined markets) R/O.
  • AV as offense, not just defense: rather than being disintermediated, Uber is positioning as the commercialization layer — 7 cities live, up to 15 by year-end, ~120,000 committed vehicles, partnerships with Wayve, Nuro, Zoox, WeRide and Autobrains R.
  • Moat sources O: (1) network liquidity — 208M MAPCs and 10.2M earners make the marketplace self-reinforcing; (2) Uber One lock-in (>70% of Delivery GB); (3) data scale for routing, pricing and AV deployment; (4) the advertising flywheel ($2.5B run rate at ~50% growth).

8. Risks

  1. Delivery Hero execution O: $14.8B equity value, debt-funded via a €14.2B bridge; leverage to stay <2× and investment-grade maintained R — but integration, multi-jurisdiction approvals, acceptance (through Nov 5, 2026), and FTC review timing are all live risks until the H2 2027 close.
  2. AV disruption of the driver model: the long-term structural risk; Uber's hedge is becoming the AV commercialization layer. Near-term evidence is favorable (share gains in AV markets), but unit economics at scale are unproven O/R.
  3. Driver labor classification R-sec: February 2026 — the DOL proposed repealing the 2024 independent-contractor rule, reviving the more gig-friendly standard (60-day comment period) — a confirmed tailwind. State-level litigation and legislative fights (e.g., California) continue; adverse reclassification would raise costs materially (industry estimates up to ~30–40%).
  4. Competition: Lyft (US rideshare), DoorDash (delivery); BofA sees US mobility share 76% → 73% by 2028 on Waymo/Tesla/Zoox pressure E-sec. Brazil two-wheel competition already dented trips growth R.
  5. Insurance/reserve inflation: reserves still building ($3.76B short-term, $9.53B long-term); CA reform relief only from 2027 R.
  6. Macro sensitivity: Delivery is discretionary; a downturn would hit order frequency O.
  7. Freight cyclicality: segment still loss-making (−$24M); exposed to the freight cycle R.
  8. GAAP earnings noise: equity-investment revaluations swing quarterly GAAP results by ±$1.5B+; screen on non-GAAP and cash metrics R/O.

10. Catalysts & What to Watch

  1. Delivery Hero acceptance window (through Nov 5, 2026) — tender progress and any FTC review developments; the single most important de-risking event.
  2. Q3 2026 earnings (~November 3, 2026) R-sec — guide was GB $58.25–60.25B, non-GAAP EPS $0.84–0.88, adj. EBITDA $2.86–2.96B R.
  3. AV rollout: 15-city target by year-end; Wayve London launch ("coming weeks"); ~120,000 committed vehicles; AV Labs data-collection scale-up.
  4. Advertising: $2.5B run rate at ~50% growth — watch for the $3B milestone and margin disclosure.
  5. Buybacks: return to a "normalized level" after the Q2 pause (~$16B remaining authorization).
  6. Regulatory: DOL contractor-rule repeal comment period outcome; CA insurance-reform savings phasing from 2027.
  7. Brazil: two-wheel supply recovery — the one operating soft spot in Q2.
