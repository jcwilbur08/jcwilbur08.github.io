01 · Executive summary & thesis

The print confirmed the operating thesis. The selloff was about funding, marks, and an unquantified 2027.

Alphabet’s third quarter was two stories at once. Operations accelerated: total revenue grew 25% — a thirteenth straight quarter of double-digit growth — operating income rose 30%, and Cloud more than doubled while expanding its margin to 36.2%. The earnings line went the other way because other income swung by $21.0 billion (calculated) on a mark-to-market loss, and the cash line went negative because Alphabet spent $51.4 billion building the capacity behind the Cloud backlog. After-hours trading, in thin snapshots through the evening, priced the second story first.

Against the single consensus provider used for every bar in this report, LSEG, the scoreboard is split: revenue and GAAP EPS missed; Cloud beat. Revenue of $127.577 billion (reported) came in $0.64 billion below the LSEG $128.22 billion consensus. GAAP diluted EPS of $2.12 (reported) missed the LSEG $3.02 consensus by $0.90 — and even $2.67 excluding the equity-securities loss (calculated) would have missed. Cloud revenue of $30.709 billion (reported) beat the LSEG $29.57 billion consensus by $1.14 billion, growing 103%. The number of estimates behind the LSEG figures was not available in any source consulted; that gap is stated plainly rather than filled.

Demand is not the constraint

Cloud backlog reached $561 billion (reported on the call), up from about $514 billion at the end of Q2. Management’s constraint is supply: demand for AI compute is outpacing the capacity coming online.

Search held, monetization lagged

Search grew 12% with query growth at its highest in five years. Revenue growth decelerated from 17% in Q2, and management attributed the step-down mainly to currency, then to advertiser and mix effects, and to lower monetization in AI Mode.

The bill arrived in cash

Capex of $51.355 billion and a second straight negative free-cash-flow quarter, funded in part by $12.4 billion of share issuance and $28.7 billion of notes, with zero buybacks. That is the discipline question for 2027.

The desk’s view, in one sentence: this was a Cloud-demand print wearing a cash-burn tape reaction — the backlog, the margin expansion, and Search resilience argue the franchise is strengthening, while the negative free cash flow, the paused buyback, and an unquantified 2027 capex path argue against paying up for that strength before the Q4 call quantifies it.

Desk verdict — maintain Buy, 6/10

Maintain Buy, conviction 6/10, 12-month target $430. The print confirmed the operating thesis, so the rating and target stand. Conviction stays at 6, not higher, for three stated reasons: free cash flow was negative for a second straight quarter; the quarter had zero buybacks alongside $12.4 billion of ATM share issuance; and 2027 capex is unquantified beyond management’s words that it will “increase significantly.”

No gate is installed and no new gate is recommended in this report. Revisit after the Q4 call, when 2027 capex is quantified. The position on record is 145 Class C shares at about 2.7% weight; this report prices GOOGL Class A only and does not use the single-source Class C close.

Attribution — flagged plainly: single transcript source, not in the release

The earnings release itself does not name any holding. The attribution came only on the call, where CFO Anat Ashkenazi said other income and expense was −$8.2 billion “due primarily to unrealized loss from our investment in SpaceX from a decline in its stock price in the third quarter.” That wording rests on one transcription of the call. No size for the SpaceX component was disclosed, no other holding was named, and no SpaceX valuation was disclosed on the call. No analyst question on the call followed up on the loss.

Do not state the SpaceX attribution as release-confirmed, and do not put a number on it. This report carries it exactly as flagged here: a CFO call remark, via a single transcript source.

Context: the first half ran the other way

The release cash-flow statement shows a year-to-date 2026 “loss (gain) on debt and equity securities, net” of −$126,755 million — that is, a gain of that size — reflecting very large first-half gains. Secondary coverage puts the first-half split at +$36.9 billion in Q1 and +$99.0 billion in Q2, sourced to the earlier 10-Qs. That split is secondary-source only and was not re-verified against the 10-Qs in this research; the year-to-date gain figure itself is from the release. The honest through-line is volatility, not direction: the same equity-securities line that flattered the first half took $0.55 off Q3 EPS. Anyone modelling Alphabet earnings on a single quarter’s other-income line will be wrong in both directions.

05 · Capex, cash flow & capital return

$51.4 billion in one quarter — and the guide did not move.

Capex more than doubled year over year, operating cash flow fell despite 30% operating-income growth, and free cash flow was negative for a second consecutive quarter. Management left the full-year guide unchanged, which arithmetically requires a Q4 spend larger than Q3’s. The funding mix changed at the same time: no buybacks, heavy issuance.

Free cash flow turned in Q2 — and stayed negative

$ billions · reported, non-GAAP definition

Free cash flow here is Alphabet’s non-GAAP definition: operating cash flow less capital expenditures. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow remained positive at $19,747 million (reported) — the quarterly turn, not the trailing figure, is the new information.

Why operating cash flow fell while operating income rose 30%

The CFO gave two reasons on the call, and both matter for how the 2027 story is read. First, a TPU-system inventory build: inventory rose to $13,308 million at September 30, from $2,439 million at December 31, 2025 (reported), and the CFO linked the build directly to TPU systems. TPU inventory is only procured after contracts are signed, per management — so the build is contracted demand pulled forward into working capital, not speculation, but it still consumes cash before revenue arrives. Second, a $5.2 billion cash payment for a 2018 European Commission fine, paid after the conclusion of the appeal (reported on the call). That is a one-off; the TPU inventory cycle is not.

Capex mix in the quarter was about 65% servers and networking (assets with roughly six-year lives) and 35% data centres (seven to forty years), per the CFO. The mix matters because it sets the depreciation tail: management warned on the call that depreciation and data-centre operating costs, including energy, “will continue to weigh on the P&L.”

The guide — resolved, and what it implies

Full-year 2026 capex guidance of $195–205 billion was left unchanged on the Q3 call — the CFO’s words were that the range “remain[s] unchanged.” The path to that range was: $175–185 billion in February 2026 (Q4 2025 call), $180–190 billion in April (Q1 call, +$5 billion at the midpoint), then $195–205 billion in July (Q2 call, +$15 billion at the midpoint) on faster capacity delivery. The $175–185 billion figure in some pre-print coverage was therefore the February guide, stale by two raises.

The arithmetic consequence is stated as arithmetic only: with $131,953 million spent in January–September (calculated from the release figures as cited in the research), landing inside $195–205 billion requires $63.0–73.0 billion of Q4 capex — materially above Q3’s $51.4 billion. Management did not guide Q4 capex. For 2027, management gave no number and no range: capex is expected to “increase significantly in 2027,” with further details on the Q4 call — the same language used at Q2. Figures above $300 billion for 2027 in coverage, including a FactSet analyst projection above $310 billion, are analyst estimates, not Alphabet guidance, and this report does not attribute them to the company.

Desk read — the funding mix is the conviction cap

Opinion: Alphabet did not run out of money — cash and securities more than doubled since year-end, to $265.1 billion, and trailing free cash flow is still positive. What changed is the direction of the equity count: a company that repurchased $39.8 billion of stock in the first nine months of 2025 repurchased none in the same period of 2026 and instead issued $42.9 billion of shares. That is a rational way to fund a $561 billion backlog at speed, and it is also dilution while the buyback is paused. Until the Q4 call quantifies 2027 capex and management says when buybacks resume, the desk will not raise conviction above 6/10 on operating strength alone.

06 · Cloud, backlog & TPUs

A $561 billion backlog, and a second growth engine being built in public.

Cloud revenue more than doubled, Cloud operating income more than tripled, and the backlog grew again. The new information on the call was not the growth rate — it was the shape of what comes next: TPU system sales to outside customers, scaling in Q4 and ramping “meaningfully” in 2027, sold as full systems into customers’ own data centres as well as Google Cloud.

Backlog (reported on the call)

$561 billion, up from about $514 billion at the end of Q2. The majority relates to typical GCP contracts. Just over 55% is expected to be recognised as revenue over the next 24 months. TPU system sales first appear in backlog when agreements are signed; inventory is then built ahead of revenue — the sequence behind the inventory build described in the capex section.

Capacity — the binding constraint

Demand for AI compute is substantially outpacing supply, in management’s framing on the call: “huge demand” and work “to bring more capacity online.” Third-party “bridging capacity” came online only at the very end of Q3, so it had limited Q3 margin impact; usage ramps in Q4, with near-term Cloud margin headwinds expected. Prioritisation runs through a long-range planning and return-on-invested-capital framework, per management.

TPU external sales: customers, partners, and what was not disclosed

Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian described TPU systems as a full system — hardware, compute, storage, networking and software — not just a chip. Nearly 100 customers use TPUs today, and there are more than 100 significant TPU customers starting in Google Cloud. Named on the call: Anthropic (directly and via Broadcom), Apple (described as a public reference customer, scaling pre-training on the latest TPUs), new frontier-model firms Mirendil and Physical Intelligence, capital-markets firms Citadel Securities and Jump Trading, Caltech, and public-sector customers Cassava and Cirrascale. New partnerships to deliver TPU systems were announced with Crux AI and Oracle, “with more in the works.” TorchTPU, built with Meta, lets PyTorch workloads port to TPUs. The current generation: TPU 8t for training, up to 2.7 times better training performance, and 8i, up to 80% better inference price-performance, versus the prior generation (both per Kurian).

Cloud revenue growth excluding TPU system sales was described as “incredibly strong” and “very strong” — no ex-TPU growth rate was disclosed, so this report does not estimate one. GCP accounted for “the vast majority” of Q3 Cloud revenue (Kurian). The CFO described TPU sales as a “meaningful contributor” and said to expect them “to continue to scale in Q4, followed by a meaningful revenue ramp in 2027.” Management declined to break out TPU or product margins. On a question citing a $35 billion-per-gigawatt figure from Barclays, the CFO gave no revenue-recognition percentage. Kurian’s economic argument for selling systems into third-party and customer data centres is that it broadens the addressable market and “offsets the need… to pre-build facilities.”

Flag — future purchase commitments are transcript-only

On the call, the CFO cited $924 billion in pre-commencement leases and $152 billion of other obligations at quarter-end, described as maximum contractual obligations with variable pricing and volume flexibility that could lower actual spend. These figures are transcript-only and unverified against the Q3 10-Q, which was not filed at research time. They are carried here with that flag, not as release-confirmed balance-sheet figures, and they should not be quoted without it.

Outside the call: Oracle and Crux

On October 27, 2026, at Oracle AI World, Oracle said OCI would offer Google TPU 8t/8i starting in calendar 2027 on a phased multi-year ramp, as the first hyperscale cloud outside Google to offer TPUs. No revenue, pricing, or regions were disclosed in the coverage consulted. Kurian confirmed the Oracle partnership on the Q3 call. Separately, secondary trade-press summaries describe Crux AI, a Google–Blackstone joint venture, as a TPU-based neocloud with a reported $5 billion Blackstone equity commitment, targeting 500 MW online in 2027 and scaling toward 2 GW. Flag the Crux financing and capacity figures as a single-source chain via trade-press summaries, not primary-verified announcements. Earlier 2026 TPU demand signals in secondary summaries — Anthropic up to 1 million TPUs with Google and Broadcom, a reported Meta multi-billion-dollar TPU rental deal, and Apple TPU use — are likewise reported, not primary-verified here.

Desk read, opinion: the TPU story is the most consequential new disclosure in the quarter after the backlog itself. If external TPU sales ramp as described in 2027, Alphabet gains a second, capital-light-ish growth engine alongside GCP consumption — but with undisclosed margins, an inventory cycle attached, and 2027 capex unquantified, the desk treats it as an option on 2027 execution, not as 2026 earnings quality.

07 · Search & AI Mode

Queries are growing faster than monetization. Management says that is a sequence, not a break.

Search & other revenue grew 12% to $63.35 billion, down from 17% in Q2. Query growth was the highest in five years, including commercial queries, according to the release and CEO Sundar Pichai. The deceleration, in management’s own ranking, was driven first by foreign exchange, then by advertiser and vertical dynamics, and then by lower monetization as AI Mode query mix grew. The question for the next two quarters is whether monetization catches the query mix — management’s answer is that it is already starting to.

Why Search decelerated — management’s drivers, in order

  • 1
    Foreign exchange — the single largest driver.

    Per Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler and the CFO on the call. The CFO added that Q4 faces a slightly larger FX headwind, primarily in Search and YouTube ads.

  • 2
    Vertical dynamics.

    APAC retail advertisers were hit by the EU elimination of the de minimis exemption; media and entertainment spend normalised after a FIFA World Cup-heavy Q2.

  • 3
    Lower monetization in AI Mode as its query mix grew.

    AI Mode queries are longer and more exploratory. Monetization per query is lower today; management’s monetization work is described below.

  • 4
    Tough comparisons into Q4.

    The CFO said Q4 faces continued lapping of the Search acceleration that began in Q3 2025 — “about 3 points of growth versus Q3… a couple of points” in Q4. Expect the reported Search growth rate to carry that headwind even if underlying demand holds.

AI Mode and AI Overviews: usage, then monetization

Pichai and Schindler described building “one seamless experience across AI Overviews, AI Mode, and traditional Search,” with the priority stated as the best Search user experience first, followed by monetization — the mobile-shift analogy management used. Usage markers stated on the call: AI Mode queries average three times longer than traditional queries; one in five AI Mode searches globally use voice, images, or documents; and there are more than 50 billion visual searches per month.

On monetization, Schindler’s key Q&A point was that the majority of AI Mode queries are incremental, creating more monetization opportunities rather than cannibalising existing ones, and that user research shows comfort with ads in the experience. Click-through rates are already similar between sponsored and organic product list items within AI Mode, per Schindler. Management said it is “now scaling high-quality ads within AI Overviews and AI Mode to more users and queries… taking our monetization footprint global for the first time,” including expansion of ads in AI Mode into international markets alongside multi-turn ads in AI Mode in the United States.

Agentic commerce

The Universal Commerce Protocol enables direct checkout for “hundreds of thousands of brands,” with Chewy, Nike, and Tapestry named; the Agent Payments Protocol and Universal Cart also launched. Management views agentic commerce as additive and expansionary for monetization, with advertising models designed into agentic flows, and is sequencing enterprise Gemini agents first (security and governance bar) before consumer rollout to Gemini app users later this year. No revenue figure for agentic commerce was disclosed.

Gemini — wording flag

Pichai and the release describe the Gemini app at 1 billion users, called “the fastest-growing product in Google’s history.” The Google Blog infographic labels the same figure “1B+ monthly active users.” The release and call wording quoted in the research is “one billion users” without the monthly-active qualifier in the quoted remarks. This report carries the figure as “1 billion users (infographic: monthly active users)” and flags the wording difference rather than resolving it. Supporting reported metrics: first-party model APIs running at about 32 billion tokens per minute, +40% quarter over quarter and more than four times year over year; Gemini 4 Argon launched about two weeks before the call, with Carbon announced on October 28; and the Genie 4 world model announced October 27.

08 · YouTube & Waymo

YouTube’s living room keeps compounding. Waymo’s call disclosure was deliberately thin.

YouTube

YouTube advertising revenue was $11,207 million, up 9% (reported). Brand advertising drove the largest contribution, with strength in the living room, per the CFO. Schindler said the living room is now more than one-third of total YouTube ads revenue, and Pichai said YouTube is the number-one streaming platform in the United States by living-room watch time. More than 20 million videos are uploaded per day. Demand Gen adoption by smaller businesses was cited, with Wayfair at 2.3 times return on ad spend versus traditional video reach (per Schindler); Creator Partnerships now cover more than 3 million creators, and more than 1.3 million channels are in YouTube Shopping, with Amazon joining YouTube Shopping so US creators can tag Amazon products.

09 · Antitrust status

Two cases, two remedy tracks — and no structural break-up ordered in either.

Neither remedies decision forced the divestiture the government sought. The overhang is now behavioural obligations, compliance machinery, and appeals — a slow tax on operations and on optionality, rather than the binary break-up risk that framed the cases a year ago. The desk does not treat antitrust as the reason to own or avoid Alphabet at 6/10 conviction; it treats it as a standing constraint on how Search distribution and ad-tech integration can evolve.

Search case — U.S. v. Google (D.D.C., Judge Mehta)

Liability in August 2024: illegal monopoly in general search and search text advertising. Remedies ruling, September 2, 2025: no forced divestiture of Chrome or Android; no ban on default-placement payments to partners such as Apple — but exclusive distribution deals are prohibited. Google must share specified search data (index and user-interaction data, not proprietary ads data) with “qualified competitors” at marginal cost, and offer search-results and text-ad syndication. Google has appealed liability and remedies to the D.C. Circuit; implementation is continuing.

Ad-tech case — E.D. Va. (Judge Brinkema)

Liability on April 17, 2025: monopoly in the open-web publisher ad server and ad exchange, with unlawful tying of DFP and AdX. Remedies decision, September 2, 2026: declined to order divestiture of AdX, which the DOJ had sought. Instead, behavioural remedies for six years with a compliance monitor and technical committee: integrate DFP/AdX with Prebid, publisher data access and export, separate DFP/AdX contracts, Google Ads bidding via AdX/Prebid (no direct DFP bidding), and a ban on first-look/last-look and unified pricing rules favouring Google tools.

Where the ad-tech case stands now

On October 2, 2026, the DOJ and Google filed competing proposed final judgments of about 41 pages each. Disputes remain on timing (the DOJ seeks Prebid integration in six months; Google seeks twelve), global scope (Google seeks up to 24 months phased outside the US), monitor powers, and data-access triggers. Judge Brinkema had not yet signed a binding order at last check in this research; a 60-day effectiveness clock runs from entry of an order, and Google is expected to appeal. Flag: one secondary summary’s line that “the remedies trial concluded May 2025” appears to conflate the search and ad-tech timelines — the September 2025 remedies trial was in the search case and the ad-tech remedies decision was September 2026. Verify trial dates against the docket before citing them.

No new antitrust development was disclosed on the Q3 earnings call itself. The status above is context from the dockets and legal coverage, as at research time.

11 · Risks

What can break the $430 case, ranked by proximity.

High · Near-term

Capex and free cash flow stay negative longer than the backlog justifies

Two straight negative quarters, a $63–73 billion implied Q4 (calculated), and 2027 “significantly” higher with no number. If the Q4 call quantifies 2027 above the FactSet analyst projection of more than $310 billion — itself an estimate, not guidance — or if TPU inventory keeps building faster than TPU revenue, the funding argument overwhelms the demand argument. Watch: Q4 capex, Q4 free cash flow, and any buyback resumption language.

High · Near-term

Search monetization does not catch query growth

AI Mode queries are mostly incremental and click-through rates on sponsored product lists already match organic ones — but monetization per query is lower today, FX is a slightly larger Q4 headwind, and Q4 laps the acceleration that began in Q3 2025. If Search growth decelerates again in Q4 without a monetization offset, the “sequence, not a break” thesis fails its first test. Watch: Q4 Search growth, AI Mode ad scaling disclosures, and the FX line.

Medium

Cloud margin compresses as bridging capacity ramps

Cloud margin expanded to 36.2% this quarter, but third-party capacity ramping in Q4 is an explicit near-term headwind, and depreciation plus energy costs “will continue to weigh on the P&L.” TPU margins are undisclosed. A Cloud revenue beat with a margin miss next quarter would change the quality of the growth. Watch: Q4 Cloud operating margin and any TPU margin disclosure.

Medium

Equity-securities volatility keeps moving GAAP EPS

The line added very large gains in the first half and took $0.55 off Q3 EPS. With $90.4 billion of marketable equity securities on the balance sheet, GAAP EPS will keep swinging on marks that have nothing to do with Search or Cloud operations. The risk is analytical, not operational: consensus comparability breaks, and tape reactions over-weight a non-operating line — as this quarter’s after-hours move arguably did. Watch: other-income disclosure each quarter, and whether any holding is named in the 10-Q.

Medium

Capital return stays paused while issuance continues

Zero buybacks against $42.9 billion of ATM issuance year-to-date and a $27.6 billion ATM balance remaining. If issuance continues into 2027 without buyback resumption, per-share compounding slows even if the business compounds. Watch: quarterly repurchase and ATM lines in the cash-flow statement.

Medium

AI-agent competition in Search

Cited by Citizens JMP and KeyBanc in their pre-print target cuts (opinion, pre-print): agents that answer rather than link could erode the query funnel that Search monetizes. Management’s counter is that AI Mode queries are incremental and agentic flows will carry advertising models. Neither claim is yet proven in revenue. Watch: query-growth and AI Mode disclosures, and any agent-related Search share data.

Lower · Structural

Antitrust remedies constrain distribution and ad-tech integration

No break-up was ordered, but six years of behavioural remedies in ad tech, data-sharing obligations in search, and a bar on exclusive distribution deals limit how Alphabet can defend both funnels. The October 2 competing judgments leave timing and scope unresolved, and appeals are expected. Watch: the signed ad-tech final judgment, the 60-day effectiveness clock, and D.C. Circuit appeal progress.

Lower · Structural

TPU execution and concentration

External TPU sales depend on a small number of named frontier and capital-markets customers, on delivery through partners (Oracle, Crux AI), and on a supply chain that is already the binding constraint. A slipped ramp, a lost anchor customer, or a margin disclosure that disappoints would dent the second-engine thesis before it earns anything. Watch: 2027 TPU revenue commentary on the Q4 call and any new customer announcements.
