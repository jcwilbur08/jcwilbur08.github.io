1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

TSMC is the world's dominant pure-play semiconductor foundry — a 72.5% global foundry revenue share in Q2 2026 (TrendForce) R-sec — manufacturing leading-edge logic and advanced packaging for the AI/HPC and smartphone supply chains. It is the toll road on AI compute: 77% of wafer revenue now comes from 7nm-and-below nodes, high-performance computing is two-thirds of sales, and 2nm entered commercial production in Q2 2026 with a steep ramp expected in H2.

The Q2 2026 print (July 16) was a beat across the board: revenue US$40.20B (+36.0% y/y in NT$) R, gross margin 67.7%, operating margin 60.3% R — records or near-records — and ADR EPS of US$4.31, +77.4% y/y R. Management raised the full-year outlook twice in effect: USD revenue growth to "slightly more than 40%" (from "more than 30%") and capex to US$60–64B (from US$52–56B) R.

Thesis O: The foundry moat is widening, not narrowing — Samsung's share has roughly halved since early 2023 while TSMC's hit a record. 2nm pricing power, a net-cash balance sheet funding the capex cycle internally, and ~28% forward EPS growth make the 27× FY2026E multiple defensible; PEG sits near 1.0. The stock is priced for execution, not for perfection — but the two are close. Key risks: Taiwan geographic concentration, the US tariff/export-control overhang, and margin dilution from the $265B overseas build (company-acknowledged).

Bottom line: Initiate/maintain BUY with a $530 12-month ADR target (~19% upside), anchored on ~25× FY2027E consensus EPS of $21.20 E. The position is a 0.58% toehold in a core-quality compounder; the risk/reward favors adding on any AI-capex wobble rather than chasing strength.
Portfolio note: Cam holds 22 shares (~$9,835, 0.58% of the $1.70M portfolio). Morningstar rates TSM 4 stars with a $534 fair value (Sept 2026 export) — our $530 target is in line with that assessment.

2. Business Overview & Revenue Mix

Founded in 1987, TSMC pioneered the pure-play foundry model: it manufactures chips designed by others and competes with no customer's products — the "trust model" underpinning its ecosystem lock-in R. In 2025 it deployed 305 distinct process technologies and manufactured 12,682 products for 534 customers R (Q2 2026 earnings-release profile).

  • Q3 2026 R: revenue US$44.6–45.8B; gross margin 65–67%; operating margin 56–58%; based on NT$32.0/US$. Management cites "continued strong demand for our leading-edge process technologies, including the steep ramp-up of our 2-nanometer technology."
  • FY2026 revenue: "slightly more than 40%" growth in USD terms (raised from "more than 30%") R → implies ≥US$171.4B C (vs US$122.42B in 2025).
  • FY2026 capex: raised to US$60–64B (from US$52–56B) R; allocation ~70–80% advanced processes, ~10% specialty, 10–20% advanced packaging/testing/mask. H1 2026 capex was US$26.80B R → H2 run-rate roughly US$33–37B C.
  • Monthly read-through: August 2026 revenue NT$514.86B, +53.3% y/y and +10.1% m/m; January–August NT$3,386.87B, +39.3% y/y R-sec — tracking ahead of the pace needed for the full-year guide O.
Consensus tension O: Zacks consensus FY2026 revenue is US$166.12B (+35.7%) E — below the ~US$171.4B implied by the company's "slightly more than 40%" guide. Either consensus has not fully caught up, or the guide embeds a very strong Q4 (H1 US$76.1B + Q3 guide midpoint US$45.2B = US$121.3B through Q3 → ~US$50B needed in Q4 C). Watch estimate revisions into the print.

6. Technology & Manufacturing Footprint

  • 2nm (N2): first revenue in Q2 2026 — 3% of wafer revenue R. Management: N2 family "to become another large and long-lasting process node," with N2P and A16 enhancements to follow; "steep ramp-up" expected in H2 2026 R. A13 technology debuted at the April 2026 North America Technology Symposium R.
  • 3nm: executing a global N3 capacity expansion — three new N3 fabs planned across Taiwan, Arizona and Japan — plus conversion of N5 tools to N3 in Taiwan R-sec.
  • Arizona: Fab 1 in production (4nm); Fab 2 construction completed early 2026, equipment installation targeted Q3 2026, volume production H2 2027 (3nm); Fab 3 construction advanced; Fab 4 site work started; first on-site advanced-packaging line being readied R-sec. Total US investment commitment now ~US$265B after the additional US$100B announced alongside Q2 results (July 2026) R-sec; CHIPS Act support widely reported as US$6.6B grants + up to US$5B loans R-sec. CFO (July 2026): "very happy with the progress in Arizona" R-sec.
  • Japan (JASM Kumamoto): Fab 1 in volume production since end-2024 R-sec; Fab 2 upgraded to 3nm (~US$17B reported, 2028 target); a July 28, 2026 earthquake caused a precautionary construction pause — resumed after inspections, with calibration-related schedule uncertainty R-sec.
  • Germany (ESMC Dresden): 28/22nm planar + 16/12nm FinFET; 40,000 300mm wafers/month; production targeted late 2027, full capacity ~2029; TSMC 70% ownership R-sec.
  • Advanced packaging (CoWoS): no numerical capacity figures were verifiable in primary materials reviewed — none cited here O. The 10–20% of 2026 capex allocated to advanced packaging/testing/mask confirms its strategic weight R.

7. Competitive Position

Foundry market share (TrendForce, Q2 2026, released Sep 9, 2026) R-sec: TSMC 72.5% (record, up from 72.3% in Q1) · Samsung 5.9% (US$3.26B) · SMIC 5.4% (US$3.01B) · UMC 3.9% (US$2.18B) · GlobalFoundries 3.2% (US$1.79B). Top-10 combined revenue US$53.49B, +11.5% q/q (record). Samsung's share has roughly halved since Q1 2023 (12.4% → 5.9%); SMIC is closing on second place.

Moat sources O: (1) process-technology lead — the only foundry shipping 2nm at scale; (2) scale/cost — 4.3M+ wafers/quarter amortize R&D (NT$73.1B in Q2 alone R) and the capex base; (3) ecosystem lock-in — 534 customers, 12,682 products, 305 process technologies R; (4) the trust model — a pure-play foundry with no competing chip products. At ~72.5% share, TSMC's quarterly foundry revenue (US$40.2B) exceeds Samsung Foundry's by more than 12× — the market is effectively a monopoly at the leading edge.

8. Risks

  1. Taiwan geographic concentration O: the leading-edge base remains in Taiwan; cross-strait escalation is the canonical tail risk. Mitigants in progress (Arizona/Japan/Germany) currently dilute margins (company-stated R) and will take years to reach Taiwan cost structure.
  2. US trade policy — Section 232 R-sec: a narrowly scoped 25% tariff on specified advanced computing chips took effect January 15, 2026, with carve-outs for US data centers, startups and consumer electronics; a broader "Phase 2" (Sept–Oct 2026) is discussed by trade publications but remains speculative. A US–Taiwan reciprocal tariff framework reportedly caps most Taiwanese goods at 15% (single-source). Do not model a settled broad-based tariff — scope and timing are unresolved O.
  3. Export controls: US restrictions on advanced AI chips to China persist; direct China revenue was 6% in Q2 2026 R (down from 9% in Q2 2025 R) — manageable directly, but second-order customer-demand effects (e.g., NVIDIA/AMD China exposure) matter O.
  4. Customer concentration O: Apple and NVIDIA are widely believed to be the two largest customers, but TSMC does not disclose splits — size single-customer risk qualitatively, not with invented percentages.
  5. Capex intensity & FCF: US$60–64B capex in 2026 vs ~US$36B TTM FCF — the dividend is safe (net cash; 27% payout on 2025 earnings C), but FCF conversion stays depressed until the build moderates O.
  6. FX: NT$ appreciation compresses reported margins (cited as a drag in 2025) R; Q3 2026 guide assumes NT$32.0/US$ R.
  7. Cycle risk: HPC is 66% of revenue R — AI-capex digestion or a smartphone downturn would hit utilization; inventory days are already rising on the N2 ramp O.
  8. Execution: 2nm yield/ramp, Arizona Fab 2 timeline, and Kumamoto earthquake recovery are the near-term operational watch items O.
