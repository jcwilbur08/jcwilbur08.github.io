1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

This note updates the 22 September 2026 initiation, which set a BUY with a $105 12-month target (~48% upside from $70.84). Four days later the thesis is intact and two material developments have arrived: Delivery Hero's boards formally recommended Uber's $14.8B takeover bid, and CEO Dara Khosrowshahi bought ~$10M of stock in the open market. The share price has drifted to $69.62 — essentially flat to the initiation reference — while the fundamental picture has, if anything, firmed.

Uber remains the dominant global mobility-and-delivery platform: $58.0B of Q2 gross bookings (+24% y/y), a fourth consecutive quarter above 20% growth; 3.9B trips (+18%) across 208M monthly active consumers (+16%); and trailing-twelve-month free cash flow above $10B for the first time. The business is compounding on both the top line and the margin line — adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 4.9% of bookings — while the stock trades ~31% below its 52-week high on robotaxi anxiety that has yet to show up in any operating metric.

Bottom line: Maintain BUY with a $105 12-month target — ~51% upside from $69.62, anchored on ~24× 2027E consensus non-GAAP EPS of ~$4.38, a PEG below 1.0 on ~30% expected EPS growth. The target is unchanged because the framework is unchanged; what changed is the entry — the stock is cheaper than at initiation with a stronger near-term catalyst path (Q3 print in early November, Delivery Hero acceptance deadline November 5).
Portfolio note: Cam holds 70 shares (~$4,873; 0.29% of the ~$1.70M portfolio) at an average cost of $69.60 — essentially breakeven. A 0.29% weight is a starter, not a position: it cannot move the portfolio. Recommend a staged add of ~50 shares (~$3,480) toward a ~0.5% weight, funded from cash, with a second tranche reserved for post-Q3-print confirmation or sub-$65 weakness. See Section 11.

4. Delivery Hero: Deal Anatomy & Strategic Logic

On July 16, 2026 Uber agreed to acquire Germany's Delivery Hero at €41.50 per share — $14.8B equity value — to create the largest food-delivery group outside China. On September 2 both Delivery Hero boards formally recommended shareholders accept the offer. This is the most consequential strategic move since the Postmates acquisition, and it materially changes the delivery segment's terminal scale.

Deal termDetail
Offer price€41.50/share — ~34% premium to 3-month VWAP, ~40% above the pre-rumor "undisturbed" price
Equity value$14.8B (Uber already the largest shareholder at ~25%)
Acceptance threshold50% plus one share; Prosus (17% holder) irrevocably committed to tender
Acceptance periodRuns to November 5, 2026
Expected closeH2 2027, after a "long slow march" of regulatory review
Antitrust remedyDelivery Hero to sell 14 overlapping markets to SSW Partners for ~€1.4B (~$1.6B)
Combined scale99 markets; $236B pro-forma 2025 GMV — nearly doubles markets with both mobility and delivery
AccretionCompany expects non-GAAP EPS accretion from closing, reaching high-single-digit % by year three

Strategic logic. Delivery is already Uber's fastest-growing major segment (+26% bookings). The combination gives Uber Eats genuine global scale against DoorDash (fresh off its $3.87B Deliveroo purchase) and Prosus-owned Just Eat — and, critically, expands Uber into markets where it currently has no meaningful delivery presence. The industrial logic is consolidation: Grab bought Foodpanda Taiwan ($600M), Uber bought Getir ($335M) — the Delivery Hero deal is the same playbook at 20× the scale.

The debate: $14.8B is a large check — roughly 10% of Uber's market cap — for a business that has historically burned cash, and European antitrust review will be probing given overlapping footprints (hence the pre-emptive SSW Partners divestiture). The bull offset: Uber is buying with a >$10B TTM FCF engine, the deal is EPS-accretive by management's math, and Prosus's committed 17% plus the board recommendation make a rival bid unlikely. Regulatory delay, not deal break, is the realistic bear case — and the stock arguably already discounts it.

6. Balance Sheet, Cash Flow & Capital Allocation

Uber's financial character has inverted from its cash-burning past. TTM free cash flow above $10B, $2.9B of quarterly operating cash flow, and $5.4B of unrestricted cash give management genuine strategic optionality: fund the Delivery Hero equity consideration, keep investing in AV partnerships, and — per the CEO's Q2 remarks — continue reducing the share count. The balance sheet is no longer a constraint on the thesis; it is an enabler of it.

The open capital-allocation question is what happens to buyback pace while the Delivery Hero cash consideration is being staged. Management has not quantified a pause; watch the Q3 call for the buyback-vs-deal-funding split. With the stock at ~15–16× forward earnings, every dollar of repurchase at these levels is highly accretive — a point the CEO's personal $10M purchase underscores.

7. Competitive Position & the Autonomy Debate

The stock's −29% one-year decline and −14.8% YTD return are not an operating story — they are a terminal-value debate. The market is pricing the risk that robotaxi networks disintermediate the aggregator. The evidence, to date, cuts the other way:

  • Partnership, not displacement: Uber's Waymo partnership is expanding (Austin, Atlanta operating together), with management targeting AV partners live in up to 15 cities by year-end. Uber is positioning as the demand layer for autonomous supply — the asset-light side of the trade.
  • Share resilience: Bank of America estimates Uber retains ~73% of the US mobility market through 2028 despite Waymo and Tesla competition, and AV partnerships are not expected to scale meaningfully before 2028 — an 18-to-24-month window for the core business to keep compounding.
  • AV trips are immaterial today: robotaxi volumes remain a very small fraction of total trips; the P&L shows zero AV-driven degradation.

The honest bull/bear synthesis — articulated even by bullish analysts — is that nobody can yet handicap the 2028–2030 endgame. What can be said: at $69.62 the market is pricing substantial AV disruption while the core business grows bookings 20%+ and EPS ~30%. That asymmetry — paying a 15× forward multiple for a 30% EPS grower because of a risk that has not materialized — is the core of the BUY case.

Smart-money cross-checks: Khosrowshahi's $10M open-market buy (Sep 10, at $70.96 — above today's price) and Bill Ackman's reported double-digit-percentage position in his new fund both lean against the AV-bear consensus. Neither is dispositive; together they suggest sophisticated capital sees the current price as mispricing the risk.

8. Risks

  • Autonomous disruption (principal risk): if Waymo/Tesla/Zoox scale faster than the 2028 timeline, or bypass aggregators entirely, mobility take-rates and growth could compress structurally. This is the risk the stock is already pricing.
  • Delivery Hero execution: $14.8B is material; European antitrust could impose harsher remedies or delay close beyond H2 2027; integration of a historically loss-making asset could dilute the margin-expansion narrative.
  • Brazil / driver supply: the Moto driver shortage cost ~2pp of global trip growth in Q2; recurrence would show up directly in bookings.
  • Consumer cyclicality: a sharper US consumer slowdown (note the September Fed hike to 3.75–4.00%) would hit both mobility and delivery volumes; freight is already cyclically exposed.
  • FX: ~1pp headwind guided for Q3; a stronger dollar would widen the bookings-vs-revenue gap further.
  • Regulatory: driver-classification litigation remains a standing tail risk in the US and Europe.

The $105 target needs no new math: it is the initiation framework — ~24× forward-year consensus EPS — applied to estimates that have, if anything, drifted up (27 analysts raised 2026 EPS in the last 30 days vs 2 cuts). At $69.62 the stock trades at roughly 16× 2027E EPS of ~$4.38 — a multiple more typical of a low-teens grower than a 25–30% EPS compounder with a $10B FCF engine. If AV timelines compress (2027 scale instead of 2028+), the multiple framework breaks and the target must be revisited; that is the explicit tripwire.

10. Catalysts & What to Watch

  • Q3 2026 earnings (expected first week of November): the print must confirm bookings ≥$58.25B guide, Brazil/Moto normalization, and delivery margin trajectory. A beat-and-raise would directly challenge the AV-bear narrative.
  • Delivery Hero acceptance deadline — November 5: clearing the 50%+1 threshold de-risks the strategic leg; watch for the tender result announcement shortly after.
  • AV city launches: progress toward the "up to 15 cities by year-end" target; any Waymo-expansion headlines cut both ways (partnership validation vs. competition fear).
  • Capital allocation signal: Q3 call commentary on buyback pace alongside deal funding.
  • Estimate revisions: 2027 EPS revisions into the Q3 print — the 27-up/2-down skew over the last 30 days is the trend to watch continue.

B. Glossary

  • Gross bookings — total dollar value of trips and orders on the platform, before driver/courier payouts; Uber's primary demand metric.
  • MAPCs — monthly active platform consumers completing ≥1 mobility ride or delivery order in a month.
  • Adjusted EBITDA / non-GAAP EPS — company-defined profitability metrics excluding stock comp, legal/regulatory items, and investment revaluations.
  • Take rate — Uber's revenue as a percentage of gross bookings.
  • GMV — gross merchandise value; used for the combined Uber + Delivery Hero pro-forma scale.
  • VWAP — volume-weighted average price, the benchmark for the Delivery Hero offer premium.
  • cc — constant currency. pp — percentage points.
