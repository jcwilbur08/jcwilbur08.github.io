WEEKLY MARKET INTELLIGENCEWeek ended Sep. 25, 2026
Executive Call

A winning week with a narrower foundation than the headline indexes imply

The market’s central tension is now clear: AI earnings power and monetization remain strong enough to support large-cap technology even as long-term interest rates impose a higher valuation hurdle on the rest of the market. Monday’s AI surge pushed the Nasdaq to a record close as the 10-year Treasury briefly moved below 5%; by Friday, the 10-year had pushed above 5.1%, yet Microsoft and other AI-linked shares still carried the major indexes higher.13

AI demand regained market leadership.
Semiconductors and software led the advance. Monday’s Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 4.3%, and Friday’s Microsoft rally reinforced the view that AI spending is translating into product and revenue opportunities.13
Breadth remained weak.
QQQ gained roughly 3.2% on the week using IEX closes, versus about 1.3% for SPY and a 0.7% decline for IWM. Small-cap and rate-sensitive participation did not confirm the strength in mega-cap technology.
Rates remain the principal macro constraint.
The 10-year Treasury moved to a fresh 19-year high above 5.1% Friday, keeping pressure on real estate, utilities, towers and other duration-sensitive assets.4
Oil helped at the margin, but remains a risk variable.
Brent fell below $100 early in the week and eased again Friday. Lower crude reduced immediate inflation pressure, but geopolitical supply risk remained embedded in pricing.13
Investment posture: constructive on earnings-backed AI and high-quality secular growth, but valuation discipline remains essential. The market is rewarding companies that can show monetization now; it is not broadly rewarding duration, leverage or economic sensitivity while the long end remains above 5%.

MARKET REGIMEGrowth, inflation, policy and cross-asset confirmation
Macro & Cross-Asset

The long bond, not the Fed meeting, is now the immediate valuation gatekeeper

Growth remains adequate.
Friday’s durable-goods detail showed core capital-goods orders pointing to robust August business investment, reinforcing the idea that growth has slowed less than the bond market would need for an easy disinflation trade.3
Policy remains restrictive.
Fed officials continued to emphasize inflation risks during the week. The market therefore has limited room to count on rapid policy relief while demand remains resilient.
Oil is the swing inflation input.
Brent’s move below $100 early in the week was a welcome easing impulse, but the market continues to treat Middle East supply routes as a macro variable rather than a background headline.1
Risk appetite is bifurcated.
AI equities can rally with a 5%+ 10-year yield, but utilities, REITs and small caps are not confirming a broad easing in financial conditions.

Three market scenarios

1 — Controlled cooling

Best broadening setup.
JOLTS and payrolls cool without a labor break, PCE is contained and the 10-year drifts below 5%. Small caps and rate-sensitive holdings should participate more fully.

2 — Hot growth / sticky inflation

Current pressure regime persists.
Firm PCE and labor data push the 10-year toward or above 5.2%. AI leaders may still work, but portfolio breadth likely remains narrow and AMT/small caps stay constrained.

3 — Growth scare

Rates fall for the wrong reason.
Sharp labor deterioration lowers yields but raises earnings risk. High-quality secular growth may outperform, while financials, cyclicals and small caps could weaken further.

What to Watch

Four questions to carry into the next Portfolio Review

  1. Can the 10-year hold below 5.2%? A sustained break higher would be a materially different valuation regime than the one that supported the summer rally.
  2. Does AI leadership broaden beyond a handful of names? The portfolio benefited this week from QCOM, META, TSM, ASML, MSFT and SOXQ, but durable market health requires more than one factor.
  3. Do small caps begin to confirm resilient growth? Continued AVUV/VTWO and IWM underperformance would signal that financing conditions remain restrictive for the domestic economy.
  4. Does lower oil persist? A durable move below $100 Brent would improve the inflation mix; renewed supply stress would quickly reverse that benefit.
Bottom line: this was a constructive week for the portfolio’s strongest secular exposures, not a clean all-clear for the market. The earnings side of the AI thesis strengthened, while the discount-rate side of the valuation equation became more demanding. Next week’s PCE, JOLTS and payrolls will determine which side wins.
