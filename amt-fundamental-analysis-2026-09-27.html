1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

American Tower is the world's largest independent tower owner — over 148,000 communications sites plus the CoreSite data center platform — and the purest public play on the compounding of global mobile data demand. The second quarter (reported July 28, 2026) was a beat-and-raise: revenue of $2.75B (+4.7%, reported), AFFO per share of $2.71 (+4.2%, reported), record leasing at CoreSite, and the second full-year guidance raise of 2026, lifting 2026 AFFO per share to $11.00–$11.17.

And yet the stock is languishing. At $169.03, AMT sits about 5% above its 52-week low of $160.06, down roughly 13% over the past year, and trades at only ~15.2× the 2026 AFFO-per-share guidance midpoint (calculated) — a multiple normally reserved for towers with real problems, not for a business whose reported growth is merely being masked by one-time items. Strip out the one-time DISH churn and refinancing costs, and Q2 AFFO per share grew over 5% (reported by management), not ~1%; U.S. & Canada organic billings grew ~5% ex-DISH rather than the headline ~1%. The earnings quality is better than the chart suggests.

Bottom line: Initiate at BUY with a $225 12-month target — ~33% price upside from $169.03 plus a ~4.2% forward dividend yield for a ~37% total return (both calculated). The target is anchored on ~19× estimated 2027 AFFO per share of ~$11.85, a multiple consistent with AMT's historical range and still below the ~19× at which the weaker Crown Castle currently trades. The $225 level also matches Morningstar's $225 fair value estimate, a useful independent cross-check.
Portfolio note: At ~2.3% (~$39,201), AMT is Cam's dedicated digital-infrastructure position — a real-estate REIT paying a ~$1,660/year forward dividend stream (231.92 shares × $7.16 annualized, calculated) — and it is up ~31% (+$9,266) on a $129.06 average cost. This is an add candidate on the BUY call: the thesis below argues for building toward a 3% weight on weakness below ~$165, funded by the position's own dividend or fresh capital.

2. Business Overview

American Tower, founded in 1995 and structured as a REIT since 2012, is one of the largest global REITs. It owns, operates and develops multitenant communications real estate: macro cell towers, rooftop and small-cell sites, and the CoreSite portfolio of interconnection-rich data centers in the U.S. The business model is a toll booth — carriers (AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile as the anchor U.S. tenants) sign long-term, non-cancellable leases with contractual annual rent escalators, and incremental tenants on an existing tower drop through to cash flow at very high incremental margins because the steel is already in the ground.

The portfolio: over 148,000 communications sites (reported) across the U.S., Europe, Latin America, Africa and Asia-Pacific, plus CoreSite's U.S. data center footprint. Roughly 30 data center facilities sit in the CoreSite network, which delivered ~12% cash revenue growth in Q2 (reported) and record leasing — a second secular growth vector tied to cloud and AI workloads rather than wireless traffic.

REIT structure matters for how to read this company: GAAP earnings are distorted by large non-cash depreciation charges on the tower portfolio, so the industry's standard cash metrics are Funds From Operations (FFO) and Adjusted Funds From Operations (AFFO) — FFO less straight-line rent adjustments and recurring maintenance capex. The dividend is paid out of AFFO, and the payout ratio that matters is dividends divided by AFFO, not by GAAP EPS.

The headline that matters most for a REIT — AFFO per share — needs normalization to be read correctly. Excluding FX effects, attributable AFFO per share grew only ~1% (reported by management), but normalized for the one-time DISH churn and refinancing costs, AFFO per share grew over 5%. Management attributed the raise to currency tailwinds and data center strength — honest sourcing of the drivers, which is what you want to see.

The U.S. & Canada line deserves care because it is the most profitable segment and the one the market watches. The headline ~1% organic growth (reported) understates the underlying leasing: nearly all of the drag is the one-time DISH-related churn event. Ex-DISH, the segment is growing ~5% organically — new business commencements plus the contractual ~3% escalators on the installed base, the classic tower compounding engine, working as designed.

Internationally, Africa & APAC at ~11% organic growth (reported) is the growth engine, Europe at ~4% is dependable, and Latin America remains the problem child — declining over 2% on elevated Brazilian churn. The LatAm weakness is real but contained: it is churn-driven rather than a demand problem, and the region's lease economics still compound once the churn rolls off.

CoreSite is becoming material. Record leasing activity in Q2 and ~12% cash revenue growth (both reported) make the data center platform a genuine second growth vector — and one whose demand driver (AI and cloud workloads) is independent of the wireless capex cycle. The market still values AMT almost entirely as a tower company; any multiple expansion of the data center assets is free optionality.

5. The 5G Densification Cycle

The structural thesis for towers is unchanged: mobile data traffic grows relentlessly, and carriers must densify — adding equipment to existing towers, amending leases, and building new sites — to carry it. Every amendment and every new co-location on an existing tower carries near-100% incremental margins, which is why tower revenue compounds faster than the site count.

After the post-merger digestion of Sprint (T-Mobile) and the carrier capex pullback of 2023–24, the cycle is turning back toward deployment. CEO Steve Vondran cited "robust leasing demand across the company's global tower portfolio" (reported) in Q2, and new business commencements are running again — organic tenant billings rose $34M in the quarter (reported). Management expects total property revenue growth of ~5% for the full year on the raised guide. This is mid-cycle tower behavior, not a boom, which is fine: towers are a compounding machine, not a cyclical sprint.

The demand visibility is unusually good for an equity: the company has referenced roughly $54 billion of contracted non-cancellable revenue in its backlog. That backlog, plus escalators and churn visibility, is why AFFO per share is one of the most forecastable cash metrics in the REIT universe.

6. Escalators, Churn & the DISH Overhang

Two mechanical features of tower leases drive the model: escalators (contractual ~3% annual rent increases in the U.S.) and churn (leases not renewed or decommissioned). Organic tenant billings growth is essentially new business + escalators − churn, and 2026 is the year churn is hogging the spotlight.

The DISH situation is the overhang. DISH's financial distress produced a one-time churn event that management explicitly normalizes around — the ~4 percentage-point gap between reported (~1%) and normalized (~5%) U.S. & Canada organic growth, and the ~4-point gap between reported and normalized AFFO per share growth, are both attributable to DISH plus refinancing costs. The key analytical judgment: this is a one-time event, not a recurring leak. Once it annualizes out of the base (early 2027 on the calendar), reported growth converges back to the normalized ~5% AFFO per share run rate — the re-acceleration the 2027 estimates are built on.

Watch the characterization, not just the number. If DISH-related churn proves to be a multi-quarter bleed rather than a one-time event, the 2027 re-acceleration thesis breaks and the multiple the stock deserves is lower. Management's "one-time" framing is reported, not independently verified — track the Q3 and Q4 organic billings prints for confirmation that the churn is contained.

7. Balance Sheet & Leverage

American Tower ended Q2 with leverage of 4.9× net debt to EBITDA (reported), inside its 3–5× target range but at the high end of it — and this is the single biggest fundamental knock on the equity. Towers are a debt-financed business model (the spread between lease yields and borrowing costs is the arbitrage), but 4.9× leaves limited headroom, and the market's fixation on it is a large part of why the stock trades at 15× AFFO.

Management is actively managing the wall: on September 9, 2026, the company priced a $1.6B registered public offering of senior unsecured notes across multiple maturities (reported), with proceeds earmarked for refinancing near-term maturities and general corporate purposes. This is routine maturity management, not distress — but it is a reminder that the refinancing conveyor belt never stops, and in a 5%+ 10-year Treasury environment every turn of the belt reprices a slice of the debt stack higher.

The mitigants: investment-grade balance sheet, long-dated maturities, staggered refinancing, and leverage that has been declining as EBITDA grows. The target range tops out at 5×, so 4.9× is compliant — but there is no room for a large debt-funded acquisition without equity, and the rating is sensitive to the leverage path from here.

8. Dividend Growth & AFFO Coverage

The dividend is the other half of the total-return case, and it is in excellent health. On September 18, 2026, the board declared a quarterly distribution of $1.79 per share (reported), payable October 20 to holders of record September 30 — an annualized $7.16 (calculated) and a ~4.24% forward yield at the $169.03 reference price (calculated). The company has now raised the dividend for 12 consecutive years (reported).

Coverage is comfortable: $7.16 of annualized distributions against the $11.09 2026 AFFO-per-share guidance midpoint is a ~65% AFFO payout ratio (calculated) — right in the sweet spot for a tower REIT, retaining roughly a third of cash flow for reinvestment and deleveraging. (The ~98–100% payout ratios quoted on GAAP earnings are an artifact of depreciation charges, not an economic signal — this is exactly why AFFO is the right denominator.) Management guided to roughly 5% dividend growth for 2026, consistent with the $1.79 quarterly rate.

For Cam's position, the dividend math: 231.92 shares × $7.16 = ~$1,660 of annual income (calculated), paid quarterly in the Feb/Apr/Jul/Oct cycle. At a 4.24% yield on market value — against a 10-year Treasury around 5.18% — the equity yield is below the risk-free rate, which is the honest bear point on the income thesis; the bull point is the 12-year record of growing the payout through every rate regime.

The pricing anomaly is visible in the table: AMT, the best operator of the three by scale, diversification and balance sheet, trades at the lowest forward AFFO multiple of the U.S.-centric pair — ~15.2× versus ~19× for Crown Castle (multiple estimates reported/derived from consensus data). 10. Risks

Interest rates. The dominant valuation risk. At a 10-year Treasury around 5.18% (per the September 25 weekly close), AMT's 4.24% dividend yield offers no spread to the risk-free rate, and every 100 bps of higher-for-longer reprices the refinancing stack and compresses the multiple the market will pay for a 3–5% AFFO grower. The September FOMC hiked to 3.75–4.00% with more tightening signaled — the opposite of what tower multiples want to see. This is the main reason the stock is at 15× AFFO instead of 20×.

Carrier consolidation and churn. The tower industry's history is a series of carrier mergers that destroyed lease revenue — Sprint/T-Mobile, and now the DISH event. Three national carriers is the stable end-state, and the FCC is unlikely to bless a duopoly, but any renewed consolidation or a second DISH-scale default would hit the installed base directly.

FX and emerging markets. Roughly half the tower portfolio sits outside the U.S., and Q2's GAAP earnings were materially moved by unrealized FX gains — the same channel cuts the other way. Brazil churn, LatAm legal disputes (management has disclosed ~$30M of revenue reserves tied to LatAm disputes), and currency volatility in Africa/APAC are persistent drags on reported numbers even when the local-currency business is healthy.

Leverage. At 4.9×, inside the target range but with no cushion: a debt-funded acquisition, a ratings action, or a sustained EBITDA miss would force the conversation the bulls don't want to have. The dividend is safe at a 65% AFFO payout, but deleveraging competes with dividend growth for the retained third of cash flow.

Data center execution. CoreSite is now material enough to matter — record leasing is great, but data center development is more capital-intensive and competitive than towers, and a misstep in capacity planning would show up in capex before it shows up in revenue.

The two estimated inputs, defended in turn. 2027E AFFO per share of ~$11.85 (~7% growth): management's normalized Q2 run rate was "over 5%" AFFO per share growth ex-DISH and refinancing costs; as the one-time DISH churn annualizes out of the base in early 2027, reported growth converges to that normalized rate, with CoreSite's ~12% growth and Africa/APAC's ~11% organic billings providing the extra point or two. The 19.0× multiple: AMT has historically traded in the high-teens to low-20s on forward AFFO in normal rate environments; the current ~15.2× (calculated: $169.03 ÷ $11.09) reflects peak rate anxiety and DISH noise. Nineteen times is not a return to the old 25–30× glory days — it is simply parity with where Crown Castle, the weaker operator, trades today (~19×), and it sits below AMT's own 5-year average. It is a normalization multiple, not an expansion multiple. Downside math: at the 52-week low of $160.06 the current multiple would be ~14.4× 2026E AFFO — the valuation floor is close, which is part of the call.

12. Catalysts & What to Watch

  • Q3 2026 earnings (late October 2026): the print that confirms or breaks the "one-time DISH churn" framing. Watch U.S. & Canada organic billings ex-DISH and whether reported AFFO per share growth starts converging toward the normalized 5%+ run rate.
  • Rate path: the October 27–28 FOMC meeting is the next decision point. Any dovish surprise re-prices the entire tower complex; the September hike is why the entry point exists.
  • CoreSite leasing: a third consecutive quarter of record leasing would start forcing the market to value the data center platform separately — the free option in the thesis.
  • Dividend declaration cadence: the $1.79 quarterly rate implies ~5% annual growth; the Q1 2027 declaration (typically February) is the next read on whether that pace holds.
  • Carrier capex budgets: AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile 2027 capex guidance (early 2027) sets the ceiling for new business commencements; densification commentary has been constructive.
  • Leverage trajectory: any quarter printing below 4.9× net debt/EBITDA de-risks the balance-sheet knock and supports multiple normalization.

13. Portfolio Fit & Sizing

AMT occupies a distinct sleeve in Cam's book: it is the portfolio's digital-infrastructure holding — a real-estate REIT whose economics (contracted escalators, 65% AFFO payout, 12-year dividend growth streak) behave nothing like the portfolio's equity positions. At 231.92 shares and ~$39,201 (~2.3% of the ~$1.70M portfolio, calculated), it is a full starter position: large enough to matter to income (~$1,660/year forward dividends, calculated), small enough that the 4.9× leverage and rate sensitivity don't threaten the whole book.

The position is up ~31% (+$9,266) on a $129.06 average cost (both calculated) — a winner, but a winner trading 5% above its 52-week low while the fundamentals are inflecting. That combination is exactly when adding is hardest and most rewarded.

Recommendation (advisory only — not a trade instruction): Add toward a 3% weight on weakness below ~$165 — roughly 45–50 additional shares (~$7,500–$8,000 at current prices, calculated), which would bring the position to ~$47K and ~2.8% of the portfolio. The $165 level is ~2.4% below the reference price and near the top of the recent support zone; it keeps the add inside the valuation floor identified in Section 11. Funding can come from the position's own quarterly distributions or fresh capital — either way, the dividend compounds the add while you wait. Cap the sleeve at ~3.5%: the leverage profile and customer concentration merit a measured allocation despite the business quality, and the rate risk is real. No action is needed above $180 — at that point the easy part of the re-rating is done.

Income role: the ~$1,660/year forward stream (calculated) is the portfolio's single largest REIT income contributor, paid quarterly in the Feb/Apr/Jul/Oct cycle, and it has grown every year for 12 years. In a retirement portfolio, that compounding income stream is the point of the position as much as the price target is.
