§0 — The one-paragraph version

ASML is the sole global supplier of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems — the machines TSMC, Samsung, Intel, and every leading-edge memory maker need to print the smallest chip features in the world, with no second source. The stock nearly tripled between April 2025 and its June 2026 high ($1,999.96) as the market woke up to just how AI-driven capex was accelerating fab-equipment demand — and it has since given back 16%, mostly on tightening US/China export-restriction news rather than any change in the underlying business. Full-year 2026 guidance was raised twice this year — first to €36-40B, then to €43-45B — implying 34.6% revenue growth, and while some of that headline number flatters a soft FY24 comparison ("digestion year," only 2.5% growth), the clean two-year stacked growth rate of 24.7%/yr is still excellent and represents genuine re-acceleration. The real, quantifiable risk is China: its share of ASML's revenue is guided down from 33% (FY25) to roughly 20% (FY26E), by policy design rather than weak demand — a real headwind, but one the company is already managing and disclosing, not concealing. Set against the specific decision on the table — moving capital out of QCOM (facing a real, structural Apple-modem revenue cliff worth an estimated $5.7-5.9B/year) into TSM or NVDA (already rated Hold in this repo) — ASML is the strongest of the three reallocation candidates: a widening monopoly moat, an accelerating growth trajectory, and a price that, while not cheap, does not require anything historically rare to deliver a solid return.

1. History, business model, and revenue mix

Founded in 1984 and headquartered in Veldhoven, the Netherlands, ASML designs, manufactures, and services the photolithography systems used to print circuit patterns onto silicon wafers — the single most technically demanding step in chip manufacturing. Its product line spans Deep Ultraviolet (DUV) systems (the mature-node workhorse, also the tools most exposed to China export restrictions), Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) systems (required for leading-edge logic and advanced memory), and the newest High-NA EUV systems (the next generation, now shipping). Revenue also includes a large and growing Installed Base Management (service, upgrades, spare parts) stream tied to its enormous global fleet of already-installed tools.

2. Moat and competitive landscape

This is as close to a textbook monopoly as exists in public markets: ASML is the sole commercial supplier of EUV lithography systems, a position built over roughly two decades and billions of euros of R&D that no competitor — not Nikon, not Canon, not any Chinese domestic effort — has been able to replicate. High-NA EUV, ASML's next-generation platform now shipping to Intel with TSMC and Samsung committed, extends this exclusivity into the next several process-node generations. TSMC and Samsung's commitments alone lock in demand visibility through 2027.

Where the moat is thinner than the bulls admit — The DUV (non-EUV) side of the business is more contestable, and this is exactly where the China risk lives: a July 2026 report noted China is mass-producing a DUV-class tool domestically, a genuine (if likely years-behind-the-frontier) crack in what was previously an unchallenged position even in mature lithography. The EUV monopoly itself remains intact and is not what's under pressure — but investors treating "ASML's moat" as one undifferentiated thing are missing that its two product lines face very different competitive futures.

3. Growth drivers

  • AI-driven fab capex, still under-appreciated even after two guidance raises. Big Tech AI infrastructure spend (estimated over $400B in 2026) is flowing through to leading-edge Logic and advanced Memory capacity additions, both of which are more lithography-intensive per wafer than prior generations.
  • High-NA EUV ramp. ASML is targeting roughly 30% EUV capacity growth for two consecutive years (2026-2027) and at least 80 low-NA EUV units by 2027, with High-NA now formally in commercial production at Intel.
  • Installed Base Management outperformance. Q2 FY26's beat versus guidance was driven primarily by stronger-than-expected service/upgrade revenue on the existing fleet — a high-margin, less-cyclical growth lever independent of new-system order timing.
  • Capacity expansion commitments from TSMC and Samsung lock in demand through 2027, providing a longer visibility window than the headline loss of quarterly bookings disclosure might suggest (see §6).

4. Capital allocation

ASML runs a genuinely shareholder-friendly capital return program alongside its growth reinvestment: a 2025 total dividend of €7.50/share (+17% YoY) and a new €12B share buyback program running 2026-2028, with €1.1B already executed in Q1 2026 alone. The dividend yield (~0.46%) is immaterial to the investment case and should be ignored as a reason to own the stock — the real capital-allocation story is a company generating enough free cash flow, even while ramping High-NA EUV manufacturing capacity, to run a large buyback and grow the dividend simultaneously.

Watch this — As High-NA EUV capex requirements scale (each system reportedly costs on the order of €350-380M and requires significant customer prepayments and ASML-side tooling investment), watch whether the buyback pace holds up against reinvestment needs — a slowdown in repurchases would be a signal, not necessarily a red flag, about where management sees the better use of capital.

5. Financial deep dive — strip the noise

Unlike several other companies in this report series, ASML's Q2 FY26 results did not contain an obvious one-time gain inflating GAAP earnings — no unusual tax item, no asset-mark gain, no legal settlement. The "noise" here is different: it's in how the growth-rate headline is framed.

Finding — FY26's guided 34.6% YoY revenue growth is genuinely impressive, but it is compared against FY24's unusually soft 2.5% growth — an easy base. The clean two-year stacked CAGR (FY24 → FY26E) is 24.7%/yr — still excellent, still a real acceleration versus FY23-24's pace, but meaningfully below the single-year "34.6%" headline that dominates the coverage of this stock. Some, not all, of the "explosive re-acceleration" story is an easy-comp effect.

A second, non-earnings item worth flagging: starting Q1 FY26, ASML stopped disclosing quarterly net bookings, moving order-backlog visibility to an annual-only cadence. The last hard figure was Q4 FY25's €13.2B of net bookings and a ~€38.8B year-end backlog. Management's stated rationale — that one or two large orders landing in a single quarter distorts the bookings print more than it informs — is reasonable, but the practical effect is that investors can no longer sanity-check demand in real time and must instead infer it from guidance, shipment cadence, and management commentary. This is a real reduction in transparency, not a hidden problem with the business, but it raises the bar for how much weight to put on any single quarter's narrative.

The one red flag that deserves its own callout — The loss of quarterly bookings disclosure is a bigger deal than it sounds. ASML's own stated reason (order lumpiness) is legitimate, but the practical result is that the single best real-time signal of whether AI-driven demand is actually converting into firm orders — as opposed to just capex announcements and press releases from hyperscalers — is no longer available to outside investors in anything faster than an annual cadence. That makes this stock more reliant on trusting management's guidance and commentary, and less checkable in real time, than it used to be.

Bear case vs. bull case

Bull case

  • High-NA EUV becomes the standard for all leading-edge logic and advanced memory nodes, extending the monopoly's revenue-per-wafer for a full technology generation.
  • AI-driven fab capex keeps surprising to the upside, as it has in every guidance revision this year.
  • China risk resolves better than feared — restrictions stay contained to the most advanced tools, preserving the larger mature-node DUV franchise.
  • Continued large-scale buybacks (€12B program) compound per-share value on top of underlying earnings growth.

Bear case

  • US-China tensions escalate to a full DUV export and servicing ban, cutting China revenue further and faster than the already-guided decline.
  • The AI-capex supercycle digests (as it briefly did in FY24), and a second "2.5%-growth year" resets sentiment hard against a now-higher valuation base.
  • Chinese domestic lithography progress, even years behind the frontier, erodes the DUV franchise's long-term pricing power faster than expected.
  • Reduced bookings transparency means the market is slower to detect a real slowdown when one eventually arrives, risking a sharper re-rating once it does.

What's priced in

At $1,679.92, clearing a 10%/yr return requires 25.2%/yr revenue growth if the multiple stays stuck at 22x, 17.6%/yr at a 30x exit multiple, or just 8.5%/yr at a 45x exit multiple close to today's forward level. Given ASML's own two-year clean growth rate is already running at 24.7%/yr, even the more demanding rows of this table are not asking for anything historically unprecedented for this specific company — a meaningfully different picture than PLTR's reverse check in this report series, where the required growth rate was closer to the edge of software history.

QCOM is not a broken company — its automotive and IoT diversification is real and growing fast — but it is trading its most profitable legacy revenue stream for a still-smaller new one, on a knowable timeline, which is a genuine reason to have reduced conviction in the name right now. Of the three reallocation targets, ASML has the cleanest story: an intact, arguably widening monopoly, real and disclosed (not hidden) geopolitical risk, and a valuation that — while the highest of the three on a trailing basis — is supported by the highest and most durable growth rate of the group. TSM was not analyzed in this session and would need its own full report before a genuine three-way comparison is complete; it is a reasonable candidate worth covering next given its own AI-driven growth and closer functional overlap with ASML as "the other half of the leading-edge chip supply chain." NVDA, already covered in this repo, carries a specific counterparty-concentration risk (OpenAI) that neither ASML nor QCOM shares.

Portfolio fit

Reallocating from QCOM into ASML would reduce direct exposure to the Apple-modem-cliff and China-smartphone risks specific to QCOM, while increasing exposure to the broader AI-infrastructure capex theme already represented by AVGO, MSFT, and SOXQ in this portfolio. That is a real trade-off, not a free upgrade: it swaps a company-specific structural risk for a sector-concentration one. Given the 10-year horizon and growth-oriented mandate, and given that ASML's risk (geopolitical, largely disclosed) is more transparent than QCOM's slow-motion Apple-revenue erosion, this report's view is that the trade is a reasonable one to make — but sizing should account for the fact that this adds to, rather than diversifies away from, the AI-capex concentration already in the book.

Three things to watch

  • Any further US legislative action on China DUV export/servicing restrictions — the single largest identified risk to the base case.
  • Q3 FY26 earnings (October 14, 2026) — the next hard data point on whether the two guidance raises this year are holding, and any updated color on 2027 demand now that quarterly bookings are no longer disclosed.
  • High-NA EUV adoption pace beyond Intel — specifically whether TSMC and Samsung's commitments convert into confirmed orders and shipments on the timeline implied by the 2027 capacity targets.

Bottom line

Add — the strongest of the three reallocation candidates. ASML's core business did not deteriorate; if anything, it re-accelerated, and the pullback reflects real but already-disclosed geopolitical risk rather than a fundamentals surprise. Against the specific alternative of continuing to hold QCOM through a real structural revenue cliff, or moving into an unanalyzed TSM or an already-Hold-rated NVDA, ASML offers the cleanest combination of moat quality, growth durability, and transparently-priced risk in this comparison set — at a price that, per the reverse check above, does not require anything historically unusual from the business to work out.
