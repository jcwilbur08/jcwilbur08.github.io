Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Nothing in the last eight days changes the fundamental picture — and that is the point. GE Vernova is executing: gas-turbine orders keep coming, the backlog is marching toward $200B, data-center demand is real and signed, and the company just declared its dividend. But at roughly 61 times 2026 consensus earnings and 26 times 2027 EBITDA, the stock already assumes the flawless version of this story. The GLJ Research Sell initiation at $470 is the articulated version of the risk: turbines delivering in 2027 were priced in 2024, and if 2027 margins disappoint, the multiple has a long way to fall.

Research posture: HOLD | $1,100 target maintained | Add gate: $875

At $949.50, the stock sits below both its 50-day ($965.86) and 200-day ($991.18) moving averages with the October 28 Q3 print four weeks out. This is a hold-the-core, add-on-weakness setup — not a chase. The recommended add gate is $875, the September 14 selloff low, roughly 8% below current levels. A retest of that level on no new fundamental information would be the accumulation opportunity; strength toward $1,050+ into the print would be the time to reassess, not add.

01 | News Flow

What changed since September 22

Eight days, five company announcements, and one loud bear call. The news flow was net positive on fundamentals and net negative on sentiment — which is why the stock went nowhere.

Sep 20 — Offshore wind overhang cleared

GE Vernova resolved its legal dispute with Vineyard Wind over a prior blade failure. The truce removes a legal overhang on the offshore wind business, though it does not fix the segment's operating losses.

Sep 23 — Egypt services agreement

The company signed rotor life-extension solutions for five 9F gas turbines (about 1,250 MW combined) at the Kureimat, Nubaria, and Cairo North plants with affiliates of state-owned EEHC, extending operating life 13–15 years with execution from 2028–2035. The order was booked in Q2; no dollar value was disclosed. Small in dollars, large in signal: the services flywheel — higher-margin, decades-long revenue — keeps turning.

Sep 29 — BWRX-300 construction permit

The U.S. NRC issued a construction permit to TVA for a BWRX-300 small modular reactor at Clinch River, Tennessee — the first BWRX-300 construction permit issued in the United States. This is optionality, not earnings: no near-term revenue, but it de-risks the SMR narrative the market occasionally ascribes value to.

Sep 29 — Crusoe data-center turbine order

AI infrastructure company Crusoe ordered 29 GE Vernova 35 MW aeroderivative gas turbines for behind-the-meter generation at U.S. data centers, including 10 units for its Abilene, Texas campus. This is the demand story in signed form: data-center electrification orders already exceeded $5B in the first half of 2026, more than double all of 2025.

Sep 30 — Dividend declared

The board declared a $0.50 per share quarterly dividend, payable November 24 to holders of record October 27 — roughly a 0.21% forward yield. Not a thesis driver; a signal of balance-sheet confidence alongside $3.9B returned to shareholders year-to-date.

The bear call: GLJ Research, Sell / $470 (Sep 14)

Gordon Johnson's GLJ Research initiated with a Sell rating and a $470 target — more than $300 below the next-lowest target on the Street — and the stock fell 8.6% that day to $874.66. The thesis deserves engagement, not dismissal: GLJ argues GEV is a cyclical gas-turbine manufacturer priced as a secular compounder at roughly 39 times forward EV/EBITDA at the time. Its core claim is about margin vintage — turbines delivering in 2027 were ordered in 2024, before the recent price increases, so GLJ estimates only about 3 points of backlog margin on the 2027 vintage versus 10–11 points on the 2025 vintage shipping in 2028. GLJ's 2027 EBITDA estimate of $7.42B sits 22% below consensus of roughly $9.5B, and it values the stock at 14 times its 2027 EBITDA, near the S&P 500 multiple.

Why the bear call matters even if you disagree. Whether or not GLJ's numbers prove right, the margin-vintage question is now the central debate into the October 28 print. Management's commentary on 2027 delivery margins, slot-reservation conversion, and pricing on 2031 slots will be read directly against this thesis. A confident answer is a catalyst; an evasive one is a de-rating event.

Sell-side scoreboard

Consensus remains constructive: 35 analysts tracked by one provider break down to 2 Strong Buy, 26 Buy, 5 Hold, and 2 Sell — a Moderate Buy — with an average target of about $1,172, roughly 23% above the current price. Recent actions cut both ways: Bernstein reaffirmed Outperform at $1,298 and Goldman upgraded to outperform in mid-September, while Morgan Stanley sits at Underweight (August) and BNP Paribas cut its target to $1,090 on a Neutral rating. My $1,100 target sits below the consensus average — deliberately, because the HOLD rating reflects a full multiple more than a broken thesis.

The second quarter itself was strong: orders of $24.2B (up 88% organically), revenue of $11.1B (up 22%), adjusted EBITDA of $1.25B at an 11.3% margin, and free cash flow of $5.1B in the quarter alone. Cash ended the quarter at $13.1B.

The backlog engine

Remaining performance obligations stood at $176B at quarter-end, up $13B sequentially, and management told investors in mid-September it expects backlog to surpass $200B in early 2027, with $100B of cumulative future services revenue by the end of 2027. Services are more than 55% of the backlog — the high-margin, recurring layer of the business.

In gas turbines specifically, the company has 116 GW under contract (53 GW firm backlog plus 63 GW of slot reservation agreements), added 20 GW in Q2, converted about 10 GW of reservations to firm orders, and is targeting at least 125 GW by year-end 2026 while already reserving 2031–2032 delivery slots. Heavy-duty gas equipment orders quadrupled in Q2 with 52 units including 15 HA-class machines.

Wind remains the swing factor. A $275M quarterly EBITDA loss against a ~$400M full-year guide leaves little room for slippage. The Vineyard Wind truce removes legal risk, not operating risk — U.S. onshore demand softness, permitting delays, and tariffs are the binding constraints. Any Q3 commentary suggesting the loss guide is at risk would pressure the stock regardless of Power strength.

Tariffs: an unresolved discrepancy

Two company-sourced figures do not obviously reconcile: Reuters' Q2 reporting put 2026 tariff costs at roughly $100–200M, while the CFO guided to a $250–350M net tariff impact for 2026 on the Q1 call, described as fully built into the outlook. The difference may be definitional or a Q2 revision — either way, the October 28 call needs to settle it.

Why $875 and not $925. The $921–926 zone is minor support that has held once; buying every 3% dip in a 61x-multiple stock ahead of a binary earnings event is how small losses compound. The $875 level is the market's own verdict on the bear case — it held after the worst one-day drop since December 2025. Adding where the bear thesis was fully priced in gives the best risk/reward in the structure.

Risks and catalysts into year-end

  • 2027 margin vintage. If 2027 deliveries were priced in 2024 at thin margins, consensus EBITDA is 20%+ too high — the GLJ thesis.
  • Wind losses. $275M Q2 EBITDA loss vs. ~$400M full-year guide leaves no room for slippage.

Catalysts
  • Q3 beat + 2027 margin rebuttal — the single biggest upside event.

  • BWRX-300 commercialization — post-NRC-permit optionality; longer-dated.
  • Buybacks — $3.9B returned YTD; continued support under the stock.
