Investment thesis

The upgrade: what changed

We upgrade Alphabet to Buy, conviction 6/10, with a $430 12-month target (from Hold / $410 on September 27). Three things moved since our last note: the antitrust tail risk was structurally defused, the Cloud/TPU story acquired a second, monetizable engine, and this week’s AI-infrastructure selloff left the group’s best cash generator 17% below its high while the cash engine itself was untouched.

Alphabet is the cycle’s cleanest way to own the AI buildout because it sells into it rather than financing it. Second-quarter operating income of $40.77B at a 34% margin is the strongest cash engine in the sector, and it funds a $195–205B capex program that the market currently treats as a liability. Our view: at ~23–24x forward earnings — in line with Microsoft and Amazon, for faster Cloud growth and a backlog of $514B — the market is paying a utility multiple for a platform whose second growth engine (external TPU systems) has barely begun to print revenue.

  • Cloud is the debate, and the bar just moved. The published Q3 consensus (EPS ~$3.02, revenue ~$127.7B) is not the real hurdle. The whisper is Google Cloud growth near or above +100% y/y — JPMorgan raised its Cloud estimate to +100% on October 15 — against +82% in Q2. That is a high bar, and it is the main reason conviction sits at 6 rather than 7 into the print.
  • TPU external sales are the upside surprise vector. First recognized in Q2 at small dollars, external TPU-system sales ramp through year-end with the significant majority of deal revenue landing in 2027. Sell-side estimates for that ramp diverge wildly — which means any hard disclosure on October 28 is unpriced optionality.
  • The breakup discount is gone. The September 2 ad-tech remedies ruling rejected a forced AdX sale; there is no Chrome divestiture in any current order on either side of the Atlantic. What remains is fines, behavioral terms, and compliance overhead — a sentiment cap, not a structural discount.
  • The risks are real and named. Q2 free cash flow was −$5.9B, the 2026 capex guide is $195–205B, and a $40B at-the-market equity program that began in Q3 is a steady technical overhang on the share price. Section 5 lays out both sides.

Desk view

Buy into the print at $336.91 for a $430 target (+27.6%). The upgrade rests on valuation and the defused antitrust overhang, not on a heroic Q3 call — the Cloud whisper bar is genuinely demanding, and a capex guide raised beyond $205B without matching revenue would be a derating event. Conviction 6/10 reflects that asymmetry honestly: the downside case is a guide disappointment, the upside case is a second engine the model doesn’t yet carry.

Market context

The week: relative strength in a sector unwind

GOOG fell just 2.3% over the five sessions to October 16 — and that was the story. The semiconductor complex unwound violently: the SOXX ETF fell for eight straight sessions, its longest losing streak in 16 years, closing nearly 14% off its October high. CoreWeave lost roughly a quarter of its value in five sessions as its bond yields spiked above 15%; Dell dropped about 20% from its early-October peak after a downgrade questioned enterprise AI demand; Intel, Micron, and Oracle all took double-digit or near-double-digit hits.

The drivers were financing questions, not demand questions. An FT report on October 8 pegged OpenAI’s revenue run-rate near $50B against a widely reported ~$70B; September CPI printed hot at 3.7% y/y with PPI up 1.3% m/m, leaving markets pricing roughly 92% odds of another Fed hike in 2026; and Broadcom CEO Hock Tan’s October 12 “casino” characterization of AI investing stung the whole complex. TSMC, notably, still printed a 68.7% jump in Q3 profit — end demand is not the issue. The debate is who pays for the buildout, and at what rate of return.

Alphabet sits on the right side of that debate. It sells compute into the buildout through Cloud and TPUs, carries the sector’s strongest operating cash generation, and drew fresh Street support mid-week — target raises or reiterations from JPMorgan, Cantor Fitzgerald, HSBC, and Evercore ISI all landed during the selloff. Investors are paying for a highly profitable advertising core that generates substantial cash even in volatile tape; October 28 is where that resilience gets tested against a much higher Cloud bar.

Q3 2026 preview · October 28, after market close

The October 28 bar: consensus is not the hurdle — Cloud is

Published consensus looks undemanding: EPS of ~$3.02 (+5.2% y/y) on revenue of ~$127.67B (+24.7% y/y). The number that will actually move the stock is Google Cloud’s growth rate. Post-Q2, the sell side raised Q3 Cloud revenue estimates by ~11% and Cloud operating-income estimates by ~18%; JPMorgan now models Cloud at +100% y/y and TD Cowen expects it to more than double, against +82% delivered in Q2. Management must also address the 2026 capex guide ($195–205B, with $80.6B spent in H1), any quantification of the 2027 trajectory — flagged by analysts as a significant increase, though the company has not guided it — and the pace of external TPU-system revenue.

AI positioning

The second engine: TPU external sales

Alphabet’s TPU franchise is transitioning from internal advantage to external product. Google Cloud’s TPU accelerator business is, by management’s account, more than twice the size of the next hyperscaler’s, with in-house silicon delivering payback in roughly half the time of a GPU server; network utilization has run above 91% since January and demand exceeds manufacturing capacity. The eighth-generation parts (v8t for training, v8i for inference) launched in April, with the inference part claiming ~80% better performance per dollar than its predecessor. First external TPU-system revenue — full systems delivered into customer data centers — was recognized in Q2.

Anthropic is the largest external TPU customer; Meta rents capacity for select inference workloads, and Salesforce, Midjourney, and Replit run production inference on the current generation. The commercial shape matters: management says system sales ramp through the end of 2026 with the significant majority of revenue from existing agreements falling in 2027 — meaning 2026 prints will understate the run-rate being built.

External TPU-system revenue — sell-side estimates

Estimates diverge by an order of magnitude in the out-years; treat the spread itself as the information. Wells Fargo’s quarterly figures shown for 2026.

Gemini monetization is running in parallel rather than waiting on hardware: AI Overviews are driving incremental query volume rather than cannibalizing the core — Q2 Search & other grew 17% to $63.3B and total advertising revenue rose 14% to $81.6B — AI Mode passed one billion monthly users after its October global expansion, and roughly 90% of the Fortune 100 now use Gemini Enterprise. The honest caveat: a University study this month found sponsored placements in only ~2% of AI-Overview surfaces, so the monetization density of the new formats is still early. That is headroom, but it is unproven headroom.

The key debate

Capex vs. cash flow: investment phase or value leak?

This is the argument that will decide the multiple. Alphabet’s 2026 capex guide of $195–205B — nearly all of it data centers — now exceeds what the business currently converts to free cash flow: Q2 FCF was −$5.9B, a quarterly turn negative that rattled holders who own Alphabet precisely for its cash generation. The buildout is being part-funded with equity: a ~$80B June package comprising a $40B at-the-market program (which began issuing in Q3 — a steady supply overhang), $15B of mandatory convertible preferred, $15B of common stock, and a $10B Berkshire Hathaway private placement.

The debate

Bears see a company spending ~$200B a year against unproven AI returns, diluting holders through an ATM program to do it, with management itself flagging a further significant capex increase in 2027. Bulls see demand evidence everywhere the bears aren’t looking: a $514B Cloud backlog, TPU utilization above 91% with demand exceeding manufacturing capacity, Cloud operating income that tripled in Q2 to $8.8B, and a funding mix anchored by a $10B Berkshire check. Goldman Sachs estimates hyperscaler capex at ~$800B in 2026 rising to ~$1.1T in 2027 — Alphabet is spending in line with the industry it is supplying, from the strongest balance-sheet position in it.

Our read: the spend is demand-backed today, but the burden of proof resets every quarter. The October 28 print must show Cloud revenue converting backlog at something close to the whisper rate. If it does, the capex reads as capacity sold forward; if Cloud decelerates materially while the guide rises, the bear framing wins and the multiple compresses toward the low-20s on slower earnings — which is roughly where our downside protection sits anyway.

One open item we flag plainly: Alphabet’s Q3 buyback pace could not be verified from available sources ahead of the print. With the ATM issuing shares in the same quarter, net share-count direction is a genuine unknown going in — and a legitimate question for the call.

Other bets

Waymo: funded optionality, not the thesis

Waymo is scaling on schedule and, as of this month, is funded to keep doing so without leaning on the parent. Paid rides exceed 500,000 per week — double the pace of under a year ago — across 15 US cities with a fleet of more than 4,000 vehicles, and management targets one million paid rides per week by the end of 2026. San Antonio, San Diego, and Tampa opened to all riders this week. In October Waymo closed its first debt financing, a $5B term loan led by PIMCO, Blackstone, and Sixth Street, on top of February’s $16B equity round at a $126B valuation — roughly 3% of Alphabet’s market value.

The competitive picture is real but subscale: Tesla operates 300+ purpose-built Cybercabs in Austin with Dallas next, and Amazon’s Zoox runs paid rides in Las Vegas; Uber remains the platform-scale counterweight and partner. Alphabet’s 2027 test, as framed by its own CEO, is to roughly double ride volume, halve cost per mile, and visibly shrink the Other Bets operating loss (Q2: $382M of revenue against a $1.8B loss). We treat Waymo as option value inside the target, not a driver of it: at ~3% of market cap, it does not need to carry the Buy case, and we note that published rationales behind the Street’s $420+ targets center on Cloud, TPU, and Search rather than itemized robotaxi value.

Regulatory

Antitrust: the overhang has eased

The single largest non-fundamental change since our September note: structural breakup risk has effectively left the story. On September 2, the ad-tech remedies ruling rejected a forced sale of AdX, imposing instead a six-year behavioral term — bid-data sharing with publishers and interoperability requirements — with a single internal compliance monitor; Google will appeal the underlying liability finding, and the DOJ may cross-appeal. In the search case, both sides are now in appeals in the D.C. Circuit, with no Chrome divestiture in force. In Europe, the Commission confirmed on October 14 that it is “in dialogue” with Google over July’s €460M and €430M fines; the €4.67B Android fine became legally final in July. A September 30 US ruling separately closed the publisher-litigation route over AI-search traffic, with the court directing the issue to Congress.

What remains is manageable: fines, behavioral constraints, an EU probe into AI Overviews, and a UK publisher opt-out regime. That is a compliance cost and a sentiment cap — not the breakup discount the stock carried for two years. Removing it is one of the three legs of this upgrade.

Valuation

Priced like a utility, growing like a platform

Ignore the trailing multiple: GOOG’s ~17.4x trailing P/E is depressed by a ~$98B one-time equity-investment gain that inflated Q2 GAAP EPS to $9.11 against a $2.89 consensus. The clean lens is forward earnings, where Alphabet trades at ~23–24x — essentially in line with Microsoft (~23x) and Amazon (~24x), above Meta (~21x), and at a PEG of roughly 1.0. (One data provider’s forward figure runs higher, ~25.6x; we carry the discrepancy openly rather than split it.) For that in-line multiple, the buyer gets the fastest-growing hyperscale cloud, the sector’s largest disclosed backlog, and a TPU hardware business the consensus does not yet model as a segment.

Forward path

Catalysts, risks & the gate decision

Catalysts. The October 28 print itself — Cloud growth against the ~+100% whisper, any hard TPU-system revenue disclosure, and backlog progression from $514B. The FOMC decision lands the same day, and the surrounding hyperscaler prints (Meta October 21; Microsoft October 28; Amazon October 29) will set the capex narrative Alphabet reports into. Beyond the quarter: Anthropic’s reported mid-November IPO (target valuation reports range from ~$1.5T to ~$2T — flagged as unconfirmed) is a direct TPU-demand read-through, and the Waymo one-million-rides-per-week milestone chase runs into year-end.

Risks. A 2026 capex guide raised beyond $205B — or a large quantified 2027 step-up — without matching revenue; Cloud decelerating against a triple-digit whisper bar; regulatory friction migrating into the AI products themselves (the EU’s AI Overviews probe, the UK publisher opt-out); competitive ad pressure from AI-native rivals; the macro tape (3.7% CPI, ~92% priced odds of another 2026 hike) that just demonstrated how fast the complex can derate; and continued ATM share supply capping rallies.

Gate decision — none installed

No GOOG price gate is currently installed, and the desk recommends keeping it that way until after October 28. The print carries a genuinely binary Cloud/capex read; installing a buy gate into it would be trading the event rather than the thesis. Revisit the gate decision on the post-print update: a clean print with Cloud at or near the whisper and capex inside the guide argues for a shallow-pullback gate; a guide raise beyond $205B argues for patience at a lower level.
