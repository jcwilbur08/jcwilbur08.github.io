1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Atlanta Braves Holdings is a Liberty Media tracking stock for two assets: the Atlanta Braves franchise and The Battery Atlanta, the mixed-use real estate development around Truist Park. The team just delivered a 94-win season, the NL East title, and a return to the playoffs after a one-year absence — and the stock is up ~39% year-to-date, near its 52-week high of $59.20. The reported $4 billion price tag on a possible Angels sale validates scarcity value for premium baseball franchises.

The problem is that validation is already in the price. At $59.03, the company's enterprise value of roughly $4.15B (calculated: $3.48B market cap plus ~$0.68B net debt) already captures nearly all of the reported $4 billion franchise comp. Meanwhile Q2 showed how lumpy the baseball economics are: six fewer home games turned $52M of year-ago baseball adjusted OIBDA into a $6M loss, the company carries $793M of GAAP debt with a $112.5M construction loan maturing this December, and baseball's labor agreement expires December 1 with players having rejected a salary-cap proposal. Morningstar's fair value of $53.77 sits below the current price.

Bottom line: Initiate at HOLD with a $63 12-month target — ~6.7% upside, no dividend. The target is anchored on an enterprise value of ~$4.45B (a ~$3.9B franchise valuation in line with reported private-market comps, plus a ~$0.55B premium for the growing Battery platform) less ~$0.68B of net debt. This is a keep-and-watch position: the asset is genuinely scarce and the playoffs starting September 29 are a real catalyst, but the run-up has left little margin of safety against the 2027 labor and refinancing risks.
Portfolio note: At 0.09% (~$1,476) this is a watch-list position, not a commitment — it exists to give the portfolio a toehold in a scarce sports asset with real-estate upside. No action is required at HOLD. Standing discipline: add only on a meaningful pullback toward the low $50s (near Morningstar's $53.77 fair value), and keep it capped at ~0.25% — this name should never be a driver of portfolio outcomes.

2. Business Overview

Atlanta Braves Holdings, Inc. was split off from Liberty Media and tracks the economic performance of the Braves Group. Two segments:

The share structure is a tracking-stock design: Class A (BATRA, one vote per share), Class B (10 votes), and Class C (BATRK, one-tenth vote, non-voting economics). All three track the same Braves Group economics; BATRA currently trades at a ~9% premium to BATRK ($59.03 vs $54.26), an inversion of the usual pattern that likely reflects retail preference for the voting Class A. There is no dividend and no buyback.

4. Key Drivers

  • The playoff run — starting September 29. The Braves (94-67) won the NL East — their first division title since 2023 — and host a wild-card series beginning September 29 at Truist Park. Playoff games are the highest-margin revenue in baseball (incremental tickets, concessions, sponsorships on a largely fixed cost base), and a deep October run is the single biggest near-term earnings lever.
  • The Battery compounds. Mixed-use revenue is growing 14–26% with ~$21M of quarterly adjusted OIBDA at ~74% margins — roughly $84M annualized of steady real-estate earnings inside a $305M-revenue company. Management calls it a long-term diversification platform; the numbers support the claim.
  • Scarcity value is real. A reported $4 billion price on a potential Angels sale sets a private-market comp for a flagship franchise — and the Braves, with a new-entrant stadium district and a perennially contending team, arguably deserve a premium to that comp. This is the floor under the long-term thesis.
  • The 2025 rebound. The team improved from 76-86 in 2025 to 94-67, returning to the postseason and restoring attendance and pricing power after a down year. Brian Snitker retired into an advisory role after the 2025 season; the new regime's first year is a success.

5. Risks

  • 2027 labor / lockout risk. Baseball's collective bargaining agreement expires December 1, 2026; players have rejected a salary-cap proposal, and short interest reached ~6.8% of tradable shares after the rejection. A 2027 lockout would cut games, ticket revenue, and media inventory — the revenue lines that fund everything else.
  • Refinancing wall. The $112.5M construction loan (SOFR + 1.80%, ~5.45% at June 30) matures December 2026, and the $95M mixed-use term loan (extended to May 2027, with one more extension option) follows. Refinancing in a 3.75–4.00% Fed-funds world will not get cheaper.
  • Debt load. $793M of GAAP debt against $116M of cash and $527M of equity — leverage is meaningful for a business whose flagship segment just printed negative quarterly OIBDA. Net debt of ~$677M is ~19% of market cap.
  • Baseball operating leverage cuts both ways. Six fewer home games swung baseball OIBDA by ~$58M year-over-year. Weather, scheduling, and playoff qualification all move the needle disproportionately.
  • Tracking-stock and liquidity discount. Thin Class A trading can magnify daily moves; tracking stocks historically trade at a discount to equivalent standalone value, and there is no dividend or buyback to support the price.
  • Media-rights timing. BravesVision and national digital-rights arrangements drove an $8.2M quarterly media-revenue decline on recognition timing — a reminder that the "recurring" media line has its own lumpiness.

6. Valuation

Sports assets are valued on enterprise value against franchise transaction comps, not earnings multiples — GAAP earnings are near zero by design. The framework:

12-month target$63Rounded; ~6.7% above $59.03
Target derivation — $63: The ~$4.45B target enterprise value assumes the franchise holds its reported ~$4B private-market comp level and the Battery contributes a ~$0.55B premium (roughly 6.5× its ~$84M annualized adjusted OIBDA — a conservative real-estate multiple given the debt it carries). Subtracting ~$0.68B of net debt gives ~$3.77B of target equity; divided by ~58.9M implied shares outstanding, that is ~$64, rounded to $63. Sanity checks: the target sits between Morningstar's $53.77 fair value and the $73 analyst target on the Class C shares, and implies an EV of ~$4.4B — a ~6% premium to today's ~$4.15B, earned through Battery growth and a clean refinancing of the December maturity. Upside case (~$73): a deep playoff run plus a renewed CBA at favorable terms. Downside case (~$45): a 2027 lockout plus refinancing stress — roughly a 25% haircut to the franchise comp.
