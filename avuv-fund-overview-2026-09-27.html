1. Executive Summary & Verdict

AVUV is not an index fund wearing a costume. It is a systematically managed, actively run small-cap value ETF from Avantis Investors that buys roughly 800 U.S. small-cap stocks screened for low valuations and high profitability, then weights them toward the strongest combinations of value and quality. The Russell 2000 Value Index is its comparison benchmark, not its mandate — the managers are free to deviate from it, and that freedom has paid off.

The track record is the argument. Over the trailing five years AVUV has compounded at ~11.7% annualized (reported) versus roughly half that for a plain Russell 2000 tracker, ranks in the top quintile of its Morningstar Small Value category over three years and the top decile over five, and carries a 4-star fund rating (reported). That is what a 0.25% fee is supposed to buy, and here it has delivered. It is the only one of Cam's three small-cap funds with a genuine factor thesis attached.

Bottom line: KEEP. AVUV earns its place as the portfolio's small-cap value anchor. The active systematic process (value plus profitability screens) has beaten its benchmark with top-decile category performance, the 0.25% expense ratio is modest for the strategy, and at ~1.83% it is a sensibly sized factor tilt — large enough to matter when value runs, small enough to ignore when it doesn't. No price target applies to funds; the discipline here is hold the position and judge it over 3–5 year cycles, not quarters. Nothing in this note proposes selling or adding shares.

2. Objective & Index/Methodology

AVUV seeks long-term capital appreciation. Under normal conditions it invests at least 80% of net assets in equity securities of U.S. small-capitalization companies. The strategy is deliberately index-like in structure but active in stock selection:

  • Universe: U.S. small-cap stocks, with the Russell 2000 Value Index as the benchmark for comparison (the fund does not seek to replicate it).
  • Value screen: emphasizes companies trading at lower valuations, defined mainly by an adjusted book-to-price ratio (reported).
  • Profitability screen: emphasizes higher profitability, defined mainly by an adjusted cash-from-operations to book-value ratio (reported) — this is what separates AVUV from a naive value index that would load up on cheap-but-dying companies.
  • Size tilt: within the universe, overweights smaller market caps versus larger ones.
  • Implementation: patient, low-turnover trading that pursues index-like diversification and transparency while using current prices to add value — the Avantis/Dimensional-family playbook. The fund may lend securities to earn income.

Inception: September 24, 2019 (reported). Issuer: Avantis Investors (American Century). Listing: NYSE Arca.

What it is not: AVUV is not a closet index. Its sector profile (Financials ~28%, Energy ~17%, Healthcare under 5% — reported) looks nothing like the Russell 2000's (~21% Healthcare, ~14% Technology — reported). Expect meaningful tracking difference versus any small-cap index in both directions; the outperformance documented below came with the same active risk.

AVUV vs VTWO — related, not redundant

The overlap is real: most of AVUV's ~800 names also sit inside VTWO's ~2,000, since both fish the same small-cap pond. But the factor loading is genuinely different. AVUV is a filtered, reweighted subset — the profitability screen throws out the unprofitable biotech and speculative growth names that make up roughly a fifth of the Russell 2000 (VTWO's Healthcare weight is ~21% versus AVUV's ~5%; AVUV's Financials are ~28% versus VTWO's ~18% — all reported). One independent comparison puts AVUV's correlation with a plain Russell 2000 tracker at a moderate ~0.58 (reported) — far from lockstep. In plain terms: AVUV owns profitable cheap companies; VTWO owns everything, including the story stocks. Those are different bets with different return drivers, and the last five years (~11.7% vs ~6.4% annualized — reported) show the difference is material.

VTWO vs FSSNX — this is the actual duplication

VTWO and FSSNX both track the same Russell 2000 Index with passive replication. Same benchmark, same blend exposure, same ~1.1% trailing yield profile. The differences are wrapper (ETF vs mutual fund) and a few basis points of fee. Holding both is paying twice for one exposure — the redundancy in this sleeve lives here, not between AVUV and VTWO.

The genuine answer: two small-cap exposures are defensible — one factor-tilted (AVUV), one core blend. Three tickers are one too many. KEEP AVUV as the differentiated value anchor at ~1.83%. For the blend sleeve, consolidate around a single Russell 2000 tracker: the recommendation is to keep FSSNX (cheapest at ~0.025%, already the larger anchor of the sleeve, no bid-ask spread or premium/discount) and fold VTWO's ~$14,349 into it, redeploying proceeds through the portfolio's standing staged-add discipline. One caveat before executing: VTWO shows a large unrealized gain on the recorded cost basis (+307% — calculated), so if it sits in a taxable account the consolidation realizes gains and needs a tax check first; in a retirement account there is no friction. Either way, this is advisory — no trades are presented as completed.

Combined, the three-fund small-cap sleeve is ~$76,571, about 4.5% of the ~$1.70M portfolio (calculated) — a reasonable satellite weight in a large-cap-dominated book. After the recommended consolidation it would be two holdings at the same ~4.5% weight.

8. Risks / What Could Change the Verdict

  • Factor winter. Value can underperform growth for years (see 2024's middling rank). The KEEP verdict assumes a multi-year holding period; over any single year AVUV can lag badly.
  • Process decay at scale. At ~$31B the fund is far larger than when the track record was built. Small-cap value capacity is finite — if future excess returns compress, the fee justification weakens.
  • Manager/process change. The edge is systematic, which is more durable than star-manager risk, but any dilution of the value/profitability discipline would be grounds to re-underwrite.
  • Small-cap regime risk. ~32% of Russell 2000 companies carry floating-rate debt (reported, Goldman Sachs via press); the September FOMC hike and any further tightening are a structural headwind for the whole asset class, AVUV included.
  • Rating/fee competition. If the Morningstar rating slips persistently or a cheaper vehicle replicates the process, revisit.

Appendix B — Glossary

ETF — exchange-traded fund. Expense ratio — annual fund operating costs as a percentage of assets. Small-cap value — smaller U.S. companies trading at low valuations. Profitability screen — overweighting companies with stronger cash-flow/profitability metrics. Russell 2000 / Russell 2000 Value — indexes of ~2,000 U.S. small-cap stocks (blend / value subset). Full replication — index fund holds all index constituents at index weights. Tracking difference — fund return minus index return. Factor tilt — deliberate overweight to a return driver (value, profitability, size). bps — basis points (1 bp = 0.01%).
