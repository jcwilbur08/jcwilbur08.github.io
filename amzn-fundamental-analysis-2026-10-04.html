Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Amazon remains the portfolio's highest-conviction AI-infrastructure anchor, and the seven days since our initiation have added evidence on both sides of the ledger. AWS signed a multi-year, billion-dollar-plus custom-silicon agreement with Synopsys, Rosenblatt raised its target to $360 calling AI-shopping-agent fears overblown, and the Q3 consensus has crept to the top of management's guidance range — all consistent with demand-led investment. Against that: the stock still sits ~12% below its August high, the AI-agent debate is a genuine (if early) question for the advertising flywheel, and the October 29 print is the near-term hinge on AWS growth sustainability and the 2027 capex trajectory.

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

AWS is reaccelerating at a scale where that should be impossible

Five consecutive quarters of acceleration to +37%, on a $42.2B base — a $169B annualized run rate that alone would rank near the top 25 of the Fortune 500. The segment added roughly $4.6B sequentially, about 80% more than its prior largest quarterly jump. At this scale, growth normally plateaus; instead it is steepening, with a $496B backlog growing triple digits as the forward anchor.

2

The retail business has inverted from margin drag to margin engine

North America operating income of $9.1B at a 7.9% margin, International profitable at 4.1%, and advertising at $19.8B (+26%) running ~$77B annualized. The flywheel — Prime density, fulfillment utilization, delivery speed, conversion, advertising inventory — is compounding, and Project Mercury's $6.8B same-day expansion extends it to 1,000+ fulfillment centers by 2031.

3

Custom silicon is a durable cost-structure moat

Trainium gives AWS a compute-cost edge neither Azure nor GCP can replicate one-for-one. The new multi-year Synopsys agreement (reported at over $1B) deepens in-house chip-design capability, and OpenAI's 2 GW Trainium commitment is the external proof point. If inference economics decide the AI cloud race, owning the silicon is the decisive advantage.

4

The capex is demand-led — but the proof compounds quarterly

Raising 2026 capex to ~$220B while free cash flow runs negative is the market's central objection, and it is a fair one. The rebuttal is arithmetic: you raise capex because the backlog is growing triple digits and AWS margins are expanding 650bps, not in hope of it. Each quarter the backlog grows or converts is another receipt; each quarter it stalls, the overbuild thesis gains weight.

5

Valuation leaves a genuine margin of safety against the consensus

At $251.52 the stock trades roughly 25% below the ~$329 street consensus and ~16% below Morningstar's $300 fair value, with zero sell ratings. On cleaned earnings (excluding the Anthropic mark), the forward multiple is in the low-20s — hardly demanding for 40%+ operating-income growth with a triple-digit backlog.

Variant perception. The market is pricing Amazon as a capex story with an advertising question mark (AI agents) and a soft Q3 guide. The differentiated view is that the AWS reacceleration plus the backlog makes this a demand story, the ad concerns are at least a year premature (per Rosenblatt's math), and the Q3 guide's softness is a Prime Day calendar shift, not a demand signal.

Q3 consensus creeps to the top of the guide. Street consensus now sits at ~$202.0B revenue (+12.1%) and $2.01 EPS (+3.1%) for the October 29 print — at the top of management's $197–202B guidance range. The bar is a beat, not a meet.

Prime Big Deal Days: Oct 6–7. The fall shopping event kicks off the holiday season; Adobe forecasts a record ~$275B U.S. holiday season. An early read on Q4 retail momentum lands before earnings.

Earnings-quality note

Do not annualize the $62.6B. Roughly $50.5B of Q2 net income is a non-cash Anthropic investment revaluation — about twice the quarter's entire operating profit. On a cleaned basis, trailing EPS is approximately $7.82, not the reported $12.43. Judge Amazon on operating income and cash conversion, never on GAAP net income in a quarter like this. The Anthropic mark flatters the P/E; it is a portfolio gain, not operating performance.
