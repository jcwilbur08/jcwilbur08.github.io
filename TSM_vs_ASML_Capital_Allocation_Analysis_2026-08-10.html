1. Executive Investment Decision

Bottom line: If capital is genuinely limited and only one security can be prioritized today, TSMC (TSM) is the better new-money investment at current prices. ASML is arguably the superior moat, but TSM currently offers a materially better combination of earnings momentum, forward valuation, and 12-18 month expected return. The trade-off is that TSM carries a far more severe geopolitical tail risk because the core manufacturing base remains concentrated in Taiwan.

3. Where Each Company Sits in the AI Value Chain

TSMC monetizes chip complexity at the manufacturing layer. Fabless designers and hyperscalers can change winners and architectures, but the most advanced designs still require leading-edge manufacturing, yield learning, and advanced packaging. This lets TSMC benefit from competition among NVIDIA, AMD, Apple, Broadcom/custom ASIC programs and other designers.
ASML monetizes complexity one layer further upstream. Every leading foundry needs lithography. As node geometries shrink and patterning becomes more complex, ASML captures value through EUV, immersion DUV, software, metrology and recurring service. It is the purer technological bottleneck.

Investment implication: ASML's moat is wider, but TSM's earnings are closer to the current AI demand pulse. With limited capital, that difference matters because valuation is also more favorable at TSM.

TSM: what must keep working

  • AI/HPC demand must remain durable enough to absorb 2nm, 3nm, advanced packaging and overseas capacity.
  • 2nm yield and pricing must offset the expected 3-4 percentage-point H2 gross-margin dilution from ramp costs.
  • Arizona and other overseas fabs must approach acceptable economics without permanently eroding consolidated margins.
  • Customer concentration must remain an advantage through scale, not become a bargaining-power problem.

ASML: what must keep working

  • Leading-edge logic and HBM/DRAM investment must sustain EUV and immersion-DUV demand into 2027-30.
  • High-NA must deliver credible production economics, even if adoption is more gradual than early enthusiasm implied.
  • China restrictions and domestic DUV substitution must not erode more of the addressable market than demand relocation can replace.
  • Installed-base revenue and pricing must support mid/high-50% long-run gross-margin economics.

What is priced in?

TSM: The market already knows AI is strong. The stock needs the AI/HPC cycle to remain durable into 2027-29, 2nm to scale well, and overseas-fab dilution to remain manageable. The upside no longer comes from discovering the AI thesis; it comes from earnings duration.
ASML: The market is already paying for a sustained EUV monopoly, strong AI capex, rising lithography intensity and successful capacity expansion. More of the expected return now depends on estimates continuing to rise rather than the multiple expanding further.

11. Final Recommendation

TSM is the better stock to deploy limited new capital into today. ASML is the better pure moat, but its current valuation offers too little incremental return relative to the execution already priced into the shares. At approximately $421, TSM's base-case value of $505 and probability-weighted value near $496 create a materially better expected-return cushion. The ranking is TSM first, ASML second.
TSM Investment Stance
ADD

WATCH

Capital-allocation conclusion: For an investor with constrained incremental capital, the mistake would be to split money evenly merely for diversification. The more disciplined approach is to put the first dollars into the security with the superior current risk/reward - TSM - while retaining ASML as a high-priority watchlist name. If the Taiwan geopolitical tail risk is personally unacceptable, then ASML becomes the preferred security despite its lower modeled return, because position quality is irrelevant if the investor cannot tolerate the principal risk.
