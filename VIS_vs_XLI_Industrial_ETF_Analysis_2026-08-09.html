Executive conclusion

VIS is the stronger strategic long-term choice for most investors who want a dedicated industrials allocation. It owns the same large industrial leaders that dominate XLI but adds meaningful mid- and small-cap exposure, reducing single-name concentration and broadening participation in reshoring, automation, infrastructure, logistics, electrical equipment, and specialized manufacturing. XLI is the superior tactical trading vehicle because it is substantially larger, trades far more heavily, has a slightly lower expense ratio, and delivers a purer large-cap S&P 500 industrials exposure.

2. Portfolio Breadth, Concentration, and Holdings

VIS: broader industrial participation

At June 30, 2026, VIS held 396 stocks. Vanguard reported a market-cap mix of 40.0% large cap, 11.3% medium/large, 25.8% medium, 10.3% medium/small, and 12.7% small cap. That makes VIS materially more diversified by company size than XLI.

Its top ten represented 31.1% of net assets, leaving almost 69% spread across hundreds of additional companies.

XLI: concentrated large-cap sector beta

XLI held 81 stocks at June 30 and 83 by August 6. Its June 30 top ten represented approximately 41.2% of assets. The fund is therefore more dependent on a smaller group of mega- and large-cap industrial leaders.

The tradeoff is precision: XLI closely mirrors the industrial segment of the S&P 500, making it useful for tactical sector allocation.

6. Liquidity and Trading Efficiency

VIS

Vanguard reported approximately $8.48 billion of ETF net assets as of June 30. It is fully adequate for long-term investors and normal position sizes, but trading volume is materially lower than XLI.

Best practice: use limit orders, especially near the open/close or during volatile sessions.

XLI

State Street reported approximately $34.5 billion in assets under management as of August 6, 2026, a 30-day median bid/ask spread of 0.01%, and heavy exchange volume.

XLI is the clear winner for tactical allocation, large block trades, options, and short-horizon implementation.

7. Structural Tailwinds for Industrials

Secular drivers

  • Power and grid investment: rising electricity demand, data-center buildout, grid resilience, and electrification support equipment suppliers and power infrastructure companies.
  • Aerospace cycle: commercial aviation production and aftermarket demand remain important earnings engines for aerospace suppliers.
  • Defense modernization: elevated geopolitical risk supports long-duration defense budgets and replenishment cycles.
  • Reshoring and automation: domestic manufacturing investment benefits machinery, controls, robotics, engineering, and distribution.
  • Infrastructure renewal: transport, construction, and public infrastructure spending provide a multi-year demand backdrop.

Cyclical sensitivities

  • Manufacturing PMIs and capital-expenditure budgets.
  • Nonresidential construction and private infrastructure spending.
  • Freight volumes and transportation pricing.
  • Interest rates and financing conditions.
  • Commodity and labor input costs.
  • Tariffs, trade restrictions, and supply-chain disruptions.

9. Portfolio Overlap and Whether Owning Both Makes Sense

Owning both ETFs usually adds complexity without adding much true diversification. Their largest positions overlap heavily, and the funds respond to the same macro drivers. A blended VIS/XLI position largely changes weights rather than introducing a distinct return stream.

Practical rule: choose VIS if the goal is a long-term industrial allocation with broad capitalization exposure. Choose XLI if the goal is a liquid tactical overweight to the S&P 500 industrial sector. Holding both is most defensible only if an investor deliberately wants a VIS core plus an additional large-cap industrial tilt.

12. Final Recommendation

Preferred fund: VIS for a strategic industrials allocation

Recommendation: VIS - ADD / preferred long-term vehicle. The case rests on broader diversification, lower concentration, meaningful participation in mid- and small-cap industrial companies, and a performance record that has matched or modestly exceeded XLI across common trailing periods through June 30, 2026. The additional 0.01% expense ratio is not meaningful enough to offset those structural advantages.

XLI - HOLD / preferred tactical vehicle. XLI remains an excellent ETF. It is preferable when liquidity, options access, block trading, or precise S&P 500 industrial-sector exposure is the priority. For investors who specifically want to emphasize the largest industrial franchises, XLI may be the cleaner implementation.

Valuation discipline: neither ETF should be viewed as a bargain at current sector multiples. A phased entry or rebalancing approach is preferable to treating recent strong returns as a reason to chase the sector.
