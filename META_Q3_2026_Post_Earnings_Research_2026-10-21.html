What changed since the October 20 underwriting?

The thesis is improved on near-term demand, not yet on cash conversion or governance.

04 / The Class C "split": what investors actually receive

Not the conventional two-for-one split into identical shares. The board approved a one-time stock dividend of one non-voting Class C share for each existing Class A or Class B share. Meta intends for the Class C stock to trade separately on Nasdaq. The distribution should cause the Class A price to reflect an economically equivalent 2-for-1 split, but the new shares need not trade at precisely the same price as Class A. [S1]

When?Amended corporate charter expected effective January 19, 2027. Stock-dividend record date, payment date, and separate Class C ticker were not announced at release. [S1]
Does this create new wealth?No. It partitions the economic claim into two traded lines; market value and stock prices adjust. Future relative prices may differ because governance rights differ.
What happens to votes?Existing voting power is not changed by the distribution. Zuckerberg's control is effectively insulated against future issuance of Class C shares. [S1]
Why create Class C?Meta cites AI infrastructure funding, acquisitions, employee compensation and flexibility for general corporate purposes without further diluting founder voting control. [S1]
Is the split itself financing?No cash is raised when shareholders receive the stock dividend. Cash may be raised only through later issuance/sale or other transactions involving Class C stock.
Tax and operations?Final tax treatment, trading ticker, option adjustment, record/payment dates and brokerage presentation require confirmed corporate-action documentation; no individualized tax advice is assumed.
Governance judgement: strategically useful, minority-shareholder negative. The structure resembles Alphabet's separation of voting and non-voting stock in spirit, but the economics of META Class A versus C should be monitored after listing. Longer-term economic dilution remains possible from acquisitions, share issuance and employee equity while Zuckerberg's voting power persists. Treat that as an explicit valuation/governance discount, not a bullish split catalyst.

05 / Free cash flow, balance sheet and capital allocation

Meta generated $32.79B of Q3 operating cash flow but spent $34.91B on reported capex including finance-lease principal, producing −$2.12B of company-defined free cash flow. Year-earlier positive $10.63B means deterioration of ~$12.75B. Combined Q2/Q3 company-defined FCF totaled approximately −$1.34B. [S1,S3]

As of September 30, reported cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities were $82.45B; long-term debt was around $80.92B from current company statements/reporting. The resulting near-parity between liquid assets and long-term debt is not a comprehensive net-debt bridge (excludes finance leases, short-term liabilities and legal commitments). A company with strong cash generation has substantial financing flexibility, but long-run AI capex plus litigation can reshape it. [S1]

Real cash drain versus accounting: AI build costs are largely capitalized rather than expensed immediately; current earnings can look far better than near-term FCF. Later depreciation, power, inference and replacement investment decide whether 2026 capex genuinely earns acceptable economic returns. For valuation, normalized owner FCF is more relevant than legal-adjusted GAAP EPS alone.

08 / Read-through and contrary evidence

Semiconductors and data centers: Meta's $135–145B 2026 capex band supports chip, networking, optical, and power demand and is directionally constructive for NVDA, AVGO, TSM, ASML and grid supply chains. It is not a supplier-specific order, and tighter cash generation could eventually constrain downstream budgets. Ad competitors: stronger price/ad implies Meta's monetization continues to contest Google, TikTok/ByteDance and retail media for performance spend. AI agents: Muse commerce adoption raises competitive stakes for OpenAI, Google and Anthropic, but third-party access/privacy restrictions may inhibit enterprise uptake. These are analyst interpretations, not contracts or guidance.

Strongest contrary evidence: even after removing the named legal charge, Q3 operating margin was about 35%, below the year-prior 40%; Q3 FCF crossed into negative territory amid rising capex; 2027 capex has no quantified ceiling; a new share class lowers minority voting influence relative to possible economic issuance. Fresh litigation penalties could meaningfully affect cash flow, and currently reported app sign-ups do not prove monetization. All of those matter more for permanent capital than split-related retail enthusiasm.

09 / Research conclusion

Maintain META at current size and demand 2027 cash-economics evidence before adding. The advertising engine and product execution warrant a premium to ordinary consumer internet platforms, but current capital intensity is atypical even for a platform of Meta's scale. Revenue and normalized operating profit support a modestly better thesis after Q3; FCF, litigation and governance keep valuation conviction moderate. This is Equity Research HOLD, 7/10 — not a change to any CIO-controlled authoritative recommendation.

Open questions: What fraction of Muse's 10M sign-ups are 30/90-day active users? How many pay or transact, what is the net take rate, and what is the inference cost? What are 2027 capex, depreciation and modeled after-tax AI returns? How and when will Class C be used for stock-based pay or acquisitions? Will 2027 core ad growth sustain both price and impression momentum?
