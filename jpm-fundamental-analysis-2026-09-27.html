1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

JPMorgan Chase is the best-run large bank in the world, and the second quarter proved it again: record net income of $21.2B (+41% y/y), EPS of $7.70 (+47%), record revenue in every line of business, a 23% return on tangible common equity, and a raised full-year net interest income guide. The fortress balance sheet (14.1% CET1), the $6.2B quarterly buyback, and a dividend just raised to $1.65 per quarter all confirm a compounder operating at the top of its game.

The problem is that the market knows. At $343.06, the stock trades at ~3.0× tangible book value — a multiple that prices in the excellence rather than discounting it — with only ~5% upside to the $360.58 street consensus and Morningstar's fair value ($326) sitting below the current price. Jamie Dimon himself called the environment "as good as it gets." For a bank, that sentence is both a compliment and a warning: peak-cycle earnings deserve peak-cycle multiples only if the cycle extends.

Bottom line: Initiate at HOLD with a $360 12-month target — ~4.9% price upside plus a ~1.9% forward dividend yield for a ~6.8% total return. The target is anchored on ~2.9× estimated September-2027 tangible book value per share of ~$125, a mild normalization from today's 3.0× trailing multiple. This is a keep-and-collect-the-dividend position, not an add — the time to press was any of the last five years, when Cam's $57.64 average cost was being built.
Portfolio note: At 7.21% (~$122,764) JPM is the portfolio's largest holding and its financial-services anchor, up 495% ($102,138) on cost. No action is required at HOLD: the position earns its weight through earnings compounding and a ~$2,362/year forward dividend stream. The standing discipline is to trim only on strength above ~$365 (the $366.50 52-week high) or if the weight drifts above 8% — harvesting into euphoria, not selling quality.

4. Segment Analysis

Commercial & Investment Bank — the quarter's hero

CIB revenue of $24.85B (+27%) and net income of $9.68B (+46%) at a 22% ROE carried the quarter. Investment-banking fees hit $3.28B (+30%) — advisory $1.0B (+20%, financial-institutions/tech/diversified deals), debt underwriting $1.4B (+19%, IG and leveraged), equity underwriting $829M (+78%, large IPOs and converts). Markets revenue of $12.1B (+35%) was the standout: Equity Markets $6.03B (+86%) on strong performance across products and regions, Fixed Income $6.05B (+6%, credit/currencies/rates offsetting soft commodities). Payments added $5.3B (+12%) and Securities Services $1.66B (+17%). The honest caveat: equity-trading and IB-fee levels this strong are cyclical peaks, not run-rates — underwrite the forward case on mid-cycle Markets, not Q2's print.

Consumer & Community Banking — the franchise moat

CCB revenue of $20.27B (+8%) and net income of $5.31B (+3%) look pedestrian next to CIB, but this is the durable engine: card sales volume $535.8B (+10%), active mobile customers 63.7M (+6%), average deposits $2.69T firm-wide (+7%). The deposit franchise — sticky, low-cost, enormous — is what funds the entire balance sheet and what competitors cannot replicate at this scale. Card NII benefited from higher revolving balances; wealth-management fees added a steady kicker.

Asset & Wealth Management — quiet compounding

Revenue $6.85B (+19%), net income ~$1.96B (+33%), AUM $5.1T (+18%) with $50B of long-term net inflows and total client assets of $7.7T (+19%). A record 44,000 first-time wealth investors in the quarter signals the funnel is widening. This is JPM's highest-multiple earnings stream and its best structural grower — every point of market appreciation and every inflow compounds fee revenue without credit risk.

5. Net Interest Income & the Rate Environment

NII is the stock's fundamental anchor — roughly half of revenue over five years — and the trajectory just improved. Management raised full-year 2026 NII guidance to ~$105.5B (from ~$103B), with NII ex-Markets at ~$96.5B (from ~$95B). Q2 NII was $25.51B (+10% y/y; ex-Markets $23.68B, +4%), powered by 10% average loan growth ($1.52T) and 7% deposit growth ($2.69T).

The September 16 FOMC hike to 3.75–4.00% — the first increase since July 2023, with the dots median pointing higher — is a net positive for JPM's NII arithmetic: higher-for-longer extends the window of elevated net yields. But two yellow flags from the Q2 data deserve attention: the net yield on interest-earning assets slipped to 2.40% (from 2.43%) as funding costs crept up, and noninterest-bearing deposit growth has decelerated to ~6% — the structural deposit advantage is eroding at the margin. NII can still grow from balance-sheet expansion, but margin expansion is largely done. Underwrite NII growth, not NIM expansion.

6. Credit Quality

Credit remains benign — the single most important confirmation in a late-cycle, higher-rate environment. The provision for credit losses fell 12% y/y to $2.52B; net charge-offs were $2.4B (down $44M); the reserve build was a token $149M, mostly wholesale. In Card Services, the net charge-off rate improved to 3.34% (from 3.47% in Q1), and management cut the full-year Card NCO guide to ~3.2% (from 3.4%).

The watch items: Card NCO dollars are still trending up ~4% y/y (~$2.0B) — early normalization, not deterioration — and management flagged continued pressure on lower-income households from elevated living costs and rates. Wholesale credit (CIB's $356M provision) bears watching if commercial real estate stress resurfaces. For now, JPM is being paid to take credit risk and experiencing almost none of it.

8. Leadership & Succession

The June 25, 2026 reshuffle settled the succession question into a clear two-horse race: Doug Petno (CEO of the commercial & investment bank) and Troy Rohrbaugh (CEO of consumer & community banking) were named co-presidents, while longtime heir-apparent Marianne Lake retired after 25+ years. Dimon plans to stay as CEO for about three more years before transitioning to executive chairman — the board is keen to keep him — and both contenders received $30M retention RSUs vesting through 2028 tied to a ≥12% ROTCE target (Jennifer Piepszak and Mary Erdoes got $20M each).

For the investment case this is net positive: three more years of Dimon removes near-term key-man risk, the retention structure aligns the contenders with shareholders, and Rohrbaugh's rotation into consumer banking gives him the breadth the role demands. The residual risk is the one markets always price around successions — whether the next CEO sustains the culture and the through-cycle discipline. That is a 2029 problem, not a 2026 one.

9. Competitive Position

JPM competes with everyone and is beaten by no one at scale. Against the money-center peers (Bank of America, Citigroup, Wells Fargo) it earns structurally higher returns — 23% ROTCE versus mid-teens for the peer group — because of three compounding advantages: (1) the #1 deposit franchise, which funds the balance sheet more cheaply than any rival; (2) the #1 investment bank and markets franchise, which monetizes volatility and deal flow; (3) scale in technology spend (~$18B+/year), which widens the digital moat every year. Against Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley it offers the same Wall Street earnings plus a consumer bank and a fortress balance sheet — at a lower multiple than the pure-play investment banks currently command.

The premium is the point of contention: the market already pays for this superiority. JPM's valuation premium to peers is the largest in large-cap banking — deserved, in our view, but it leaves less room for multiple expansion and more downside if returns normalize.

10. Risks

  • Cyclical peak risk. Record Markets revenue (+86% in equities trading), record IB fees, and Dimon's own "as good as it gets" — the base case must assume normalization, not extrapolation. A capital-markets slowdown would hit the highest-beta earnings first.
  • Credit normalization. Card NCOs are rising in dollars; lower-income consumer stress is acknowledged by management. A recession would turn the 3.2% Card NCO guide into 5%+ and the provision from tailwind to headwind.
  • Rate-path reversal. The NII upgrade assumes the higher-for-longer path. Aggressive Fed easing would compress NII even as it helped credit — JPM is asset-sensitive enough for this to matter.
  • Expense creep. Adjusted 2026 expense guide of ~$107.5B (+15% y/y in Q2) is growing faster than core revenue; the bank is investing through the cycle, but operating leverage is negative until revenue catches up.
  • Regulatory / capital. Basel endgame calibration and any future stress-test surprises could raise required capital and slow buybacks. Political scrutiny of large-bank fees and overdraft practices is a perennial headline risk.
  • Succession execution. Low probability, high impact — the 2029 transition must preserve the risk culture. The retention RSUs mitigate but do not eliminate this.
  • Valuation. At 3.0× tangible book, the stock has the least margin of safety of any point in the last five years. Quality does not immunize against multiple compression.
