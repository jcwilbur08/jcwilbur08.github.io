Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

American Tower is the world's largest independent tower owner, and it is priced like a problem credit. At $162.20 — $2.14 above its 52-week low and down 7.6% year to date — the stock trades at roughly 14.6x the midpoint of 2026 AFFO-per-share guidance. That is a multiple normally reserved for towers with real problems, not for a business whose reported growth is merely being masked by a one-time DISH churn event that annualizes out of the base in early 2027. Management's own CFO now says 2026 is the trough year for organic tenant billings growth, with acceleration expected in 2027.

02 | Business

Business quality and competitive position

American Tower, founded in 1995 and structured as a REIT since 2012, is one of the largest global REITs by market capitalization (~$75.6B). It owns, operates and develops multitenant communications real estate: macro cell towers, rooftop and small-cell sites across 24 countries, plus the CoreSite portfolio of interconnection-rich U.S. data centers. The business model is a toll booth — carriers (AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile as anchor U.S. tenants) sign long-term, non-cancellable leases with contractual annual rent escalators, and incremental tenants on an existing tower drop through at very high incremental margins because the steel is already in the ground.

The portfolio spans over 148,000 communications sites plus roughly 30 CoreSite data center facilities. CoreSite delivered ~12% cash revenue growth in Q2 with record leasing — a second secular growth vector tied to cloud and AI workloads rather than the wireless capex cycle. The company references roughly $54B of contracted non-cancellable revenue in its backlog, which is why AFFO per share is among the most forecastable cash metrics in the REIT universe.

REIT structure shapes how to read the numbers: GAAP earnings are distorted by large non-cash depreciation on the tower portfolio, so the industry's standard cash metrics are Funds From Operations (FFO) and Adjusted Funds From Operations (AFFO) — FFO less straight-line rent adjustments and recurring maintenance capex. The dividend is paid from AFFO; the payout ratio that matters is distributions divided by AFFO, not by GAAP EPS.

03 | Operating Detail

Leasing, the DISH overhang, and the 2027 re-acceleration

Organic tenant billings growth — new business commencements plus contractual escalators minus churn — is the cleanest same-tower revenue read. The Q2 regional picture (all reported): U.S. & Canada ~1% (~5% excluding DISH churn), Africa & APAC ~11%, Europe ~4%, Latin America declining over 2% on elevated Brazilian churn, and CoreSite cash revenue up ~12% with record leasing.

The DISH situation remains the overhang and the hinge. DISH's financial distress produced a one-time churn event that management explicitly normalizes around — the ~4 percentage-point gap between reported (~1%) and normalized (~5%) U.S. organic growth, and the parallel gap between reported and normalized AFFO per share growth, are both attributable to DISH plus refinancing costs. The analytical judgment stands: this is a one-time event, not a recurring leak. Once it annualizes out of the base in early 2027, reported growth converges back to the normalized ~5% run rate — the re-acceleration the 2027 estimates are built on. The CFO's trough-year framing at the Citi event is the first time management has characterized the cycle this explicitly in public.

CoreSite is becoming material. Record leasing in Q2 and ~12% cash revenue growth make the data center platform a genuine second growth vector — and one whose demand driver (AI and cloud workloads) is independent of the wireless capex cycle. Barclays' new edge-computing thesis (stranded power, deterministic latency at tower sites) points the same direction. The market still values AMT almost entirely as a tower company; any separate valuation of the data center assets is free optionality.

Balance sheet and leverage

Leverage ended Q2 at 4.9x net debt to EBITDA (reported), inside the 3–5x target range but at the top of it — the single biggest fundamental knock on the equity. On September 9, the company priced a $1.6B registered offering of senior unsecured notes (2031, 2033 and 2036 tranches; ~$1.58B net proceeds), earmarked to repay $600M of 2026 notes, pay down the revolver and fund general corporate purposes. This is routine maturity management, not distress — but in a 5%+ 10-year environment every turn of the refinancing belt reprices a slice of the debt stack higher. The mitigants: investment-grade rating, long-dated staggered maturities, and leverage that declines as EBITDA grows. There is no room for a large debt-funded acquisition without equity.

Dividend and AFFO coverage

The dividend is in excellent health. The board declared a $1.79 quarterly distribution (reported September 18), payable October 20 to holders of record September 30 — $7.16 annualized (calculated), a ~4.4% forward yield at the $162.20 reference price (calculated), and the 12th consecutive annual raise (reported). Coverage is comfortable: $7.16 against the $11.09 AFFO guidance midpoint is a ~65% AFFO payout ratio (calculated) — the sweet spot for a tower REIT, retaining roughly a third of cash flow for reinvestment and deleveraging. (Near-100% payout ratios quoted on GAAP earnings are a depreciation artifact, not an economic signal.) The honest bear point: at a 10-year near 5.2%, the equity yield offers no spread to the risk-free rate; the bull point is the 12-year record of growing the payout through every rate regime.

05 | Risks

Risks

Rates

The dominant valuation risk. At a 10-year near 5.2%, the 4.4% dividend yield offers no spread to risk-free, and every 100 bps of higher-for-longer reprices the refinancing stack and compresses the multiple for a 3–5% AFFO grower. This is the main reason the stock is at 14.6x AFFO instead of 20x.

DISH characterization

If DISH-related churn proves a multi-quarter bleed rather than one-time, the 2027 re-acceleration thesis breaks and the deserved multiple is lower. Management's framing is reported, not independently verified — the October 27 print must confirm containment.

Leverage

At 4.9x, inside the target range but with no cushion: a debt-funded acquisition, a ratings action, or a sustained EBITDA miss forces an unwanted conversation. Deleveraging competes with dividend growth for the retained third of cash flow.

FX & emerging markets

Roughly half the portfolio sits outside the U.S. Brazil churn, LatAm legal disputes (~$30M of revenue reserves disclosed), and currency volatility in Africa/APAC persistently drag reported numbers even when local-currency operations are healthy.
