The one-paragraph version. The fundamentals have improved again since our August 28 note — FY28 consensus EPS is up to $15.70 (42 upward revisions, zero down in 30 days), management guided FY28 revenue +~70% and called it supply-constrained, and the board added a record $150B to the buyback ($235B remaining through FY28). Our probability-weighted value moves up modestly to $328. But the stock moved too: at $233.99 it now sits essentially on our 40%-upside gate ($234.46), and on the screen-quoted "decade-low 29× trailing" multiple it is flattered — clean trailing EPS is $7.01, not $7.91, so the true trailing multiple is ~33×. We keep ADD, but tighten: the core accumulation band is now $215–$235, $235–$250 becomes conditional on the November print clearing specific gates, and we cut conviction to 8/10 because the demand-quality risk we flagged in August grew faster than the earnings did — NVIDIA's equity stakes in its own ecosystem nearly tripled to $94B in six months, alongside a guarantee structure capped at $105B and $279B of supply obligations. Practical read: the position is under-sized at 0.44% NAV; build it toward ~1.0% in three gated tranches rather than chasing the 52-week high, and keep the VXUS/AVUV sequencing intact.

2. Fundamentals: the quarter, the guide, and the margin trough

Q2 FY27 (July quarter) revenue was $96.2B, +106% y/y and +18% q/q, with Data Center at $89.0B (+117%) and gross margin at 75.0% GAAP and non-GAAP. Non-GAAP EPS of $2.22 beat $2.09 consensus. Q3 is guided to $108B ±2% at 74.0% gross margin, excluding any China data-center compute revenue, and management signalled a 71–72% gross-margin trough in Q4 as Vera Rubin ramps and memory costs bite. The headline that moved the stock was the call: FY28 revenue growth of ~70% versus a street that had modelled ~44%, with management saying customer forecasts point to growth closer to doubling and that the guide reflects supply, not demand.

Sources: NVIDIA 8-Ks and Robinhood reported financials. Q3E is the company guide midpoint; Q4E is the Yahoo Finance consensus ($124.3B) with our 71.5% margin placeholder at the guided trough. Q1 FY26's 60.5% margin includes the ~$4.5B H20 China inventory charge — a one-off, not a trend.

3. Strip the noise

The "decade-low ~28–29× trailing P/E" being quoted this week is a GAAP number inflated by marks on customer stakes. NVIDIA booked $23.7B of net equity-securities gains in H1 FY27 ($15.9B in Q1, $7.8B in Q2), which is why Q2 GAAP EPS ($2.46) printed above non-GAAP ($2.22) — an unusual inversion. On a trailing basis, GAAP EPS of ~$7.91 drops to ~$7.01 clean, lifting the true trailing multiple from ~29.6× to ~33.4×. NVIDIA's own non-GAAP excludes these gains, so here the non-GAAP figure is the conservative one. Forward multiples on consensus (25.1× FY27E, 14.9× FY28E) are already on a clean basis and are the ones that matter.

TTM = Q3 FY26–Q2 FY27. GAAP: $1.30 / $1.76 / $2.39 / $2.46. Non-GAAP: $1.30 / $1.62 / $1.87 / $2.22. Q4 FY26 GAAP is our calculation from reported net income and ~24.4B diluted shares. Price $233.99.

Two further adjustments worth stating. First, a definitional change: from Q1 FY27 NVIDIA's non-GAAP measures include stock-based compensation; prior-year non-GAAP excluded it. That makes the current basis more conservative, but it also means reported y/y non-GAAP growth slightly understates the operating improvement — not a flattering change, and we note it only so the comparison is like-for-like. Second, the buyback framing: management explicitly cited the strong performance of its startup investment portfolio as one rationale for the $150B authorization. Returning capital funded partly by unrealized marks on stakes in its own customers is a circularity we would not credit as operating cash generation.

4. Moat, competition, and why conviction is 8/10 rather than 9/10

Where the moat is still real. NVIDIA sells the full stack — GPU, networking (Spectrum-X Ethernet revenue +2.6× y/y), systems, and CUDA software — and management's revenue-per-gigawatt framing (roughly $18B on Hopper, $25B on Blackwell, ~$40B on Vera Rubin) explains why it is capturing a rising share of each data-center dollar. Fourteen consecutive quarters above its own outlook is a track record, not a forecast.

Where it is thinner than the bulls admit.

  • Relative performance is telling you something. NVDA is up only ~22% YTD while AMD is up ~184% and has crossed a $1T market cap, and the semiconductor ETF's 2026 return is roughly three times NVIDIA's. The market is pricing share leakage at the margin — to AMD's merchant GPUs and to custom ASICs.
  • The custom-silicon threat is one you already own. Broadcom (2.42% NAV) is the principal ASIC beneficiary. That partially hedges NVDA-specific share loss inside the AI cluster — but it does not hedge the cycle.
  • Vendor-financed demand is growing. This is the reason for the conviction cut, covered in the red-flag callout in §8.
Why it is still an ADD. Earnings power is rising faster than the share price: FY28 consensus EPS went from ~$12.67 to $15.70 in 90 days (+24%) while the stock rose low single digits from our last note. That is the opposite of a re-rating story — the multiple is compressing as estimates catch up.

7. Portfolio fit

Mandate. Yield (0.43% on the raised $0.25 quarterly dividend) is irrelevant given the $80K pension floor; this is a total-return holding. The 10-year horizon is an advantage here: a cyclical air-pocket in FY29 is survivable for you in a way it is not for a trader, which is why the bear case lowers conviction but does not change the rating.

Concentration. The defined AI-infrastructure cluster (NVDA, AVGO, TSM, ASML, GRID, GEV, SOXQ, VIS) is 7.4% of NAV; adding the hyperscalers (AMZN, MSFT, GOOG, META) takes AI-linked exposure to ~24%. NVDA is the cluster's smallest single-name weight despite being its central node — TSM manufactures its chips, ASML equips TSM, AVGO competes with it on custom silicon, and the hyperscalers are its customers. Building NVDA to ~1% is a rebalancing within the theme more than an addition to it, but it still deepens the same cycle.

Sequencing — stated plainly. The house rule is that the VXUS/AVUV gap (~$170K: VXUS ~$119K, AVUV ~$51K) closes before new individual-equity additions. The full NVDA build is ~$9K, small against that gap, but it is still an exception to the rule. We recommend pairing each tranche with a VXUS/AVUV contribution from idle cash rather than letting single-name adds quietly displace the sequence — the same discipline issue flagged on the ASML, UBER and GEHC adds.

Tax location. Low-yield growth equity; the taxable account is fine. Not tax advice.

Red flag: NVIDIA is increasingly financing its own demand. Equity and non-marketable stakes rose from $35.1B to $93.9B in six months (roughly $50B into frontier labs), the 10-Q discloses guarantees capped at $105B on an AI campus structure, and supply obligations reached $279B — together comparable to trailing revenue of ~$303B. None of this is hidden, and some of it is rational (securing scarce memory, seeding customers). But vendor-enabled demand is lower-quality than independent demand, the marks on those stakes are now flowing through GAAP EPS, and management cited that portfolio as part of the buyback rationale. This is the single reason conviction moves to 8/10: the risk we flagged in August grew faster than earnings did. G4 is the tripwire.

9. Counter-arguments

CaseArgument
BullAt ~15× FY28 consensus for a business guiding +70% growth with 74–75% gross margins, the market is paying a cyclical multiple for what may be a multi-year platform. Estimates have risen ~24% in 90 days with zero cuts; the buyback can retire ~2–3% of shares a year; and the guide is supply-limited, so the next leg is capacity-driven, not demand-dependent. Waiting for $215 may simply mean missing the re-basing of the zones when FY28 EPS moves to $17.
BearFifteen times peak earnings is not cheap if they are peak earnings. Some of today's demand is financed by NVIDIA itself, margins are guided down, AMD and custom silicon are visibly winning at the margin, and the stock is at a 52-week high. The "decade-low P/E" headline is a GAAP artefact. A 2027 capex digestion would take EPS and the multiple down together — the $175 bear case is ~25% below today's price.

10. What to monitor next

  1. Late-October hyperscaler prints (MSFT, GOOG, AMZN, META) — 2027 capex commentary is the first read on G3 before NVIDIA reports. All four are in your book.
  2. NVIDIA Q3 FY27 print (expected mid/late November; date to confirm) — revenue vs. $106B floor, GM vs. 73.5%, Q4 guide vs. 71%, FY28 +70% reaffirmation. This opens or closes G2/G3 and determines T2.
  3. Q3 10-Q balance sheet — equity stakes vs. ~$110B, any new guarantee or financing structures (G4).
  4. FY28 consensus trajectory — if it reaches $16.50+, re-base the zones upward (~$21 per $1 of EPS); if it stalls while price rises, the margin of safety is eroding.
  5. Rubin ramp and memory pricing — the drivers of whether the Q4 margin trough is one quarter or several.

Bottom line

NVIDIA remains an ADD, but $234 is the top of the range, not the middle of it. The earnings revision cycle continues to outrun the stock, and the reverse check shows the market pricing a post-FY28 plateau — a setup that has historically favoured patient buyers with long horizons like yours. Against that, the quality of a growing slice of demand is weaker than the headline numbers suggest, and the trailing multiple being quoted is flattered. We would build the position toward ~1% NAV in three gated tranches — T1 on a pullback to ≤$232, T2 after the November print confirms margin and demand, T3 at ≤$215 or after the Q4 trough is visibly behind it — and pair each with VXUS/AVUV contributions. We are lowering conviction to 8/10 and narrowing the core band to $215–$235; both changes reverse if the Q3 10-Q shows the ecosystem-financing build slowing while estimates keep rising.
