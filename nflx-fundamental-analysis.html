1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Netflix is the global streaming leader (325M paid memberships last disclosed, January 2026 R) trading 40% below its 52-week high after the market stopped paying for the story. The business itself is executing: Q2 2026 revenue grew 13.4% (+12% FX-neutral), operating margin is guided to 31.5% for the full year, free cash flow is guided to ~$12.5B, and the advertising business is doubling to ~$3B in 2026 with a credible path toward the company's ~$9B 2030 aspiration. R

The debate — and it is the entire investment question — is whether Netflix is a 22×-forward-earnings compounder with an ad kicker, or a maturing subscription business losing the casual-viewing war to YouTube (14.2% of US TV time vs Netflix's ~8%, per Nielsen, July 2026 R). Wells Fargo's September 18 downgrade to Underweight with a $57 target anchored the bear case on engagement and content ROI; the stock fell 4.7% on 3× volume that day. R

Investment thesis: At $72, the market prices Netflix as if the ad ramp fails and engagement share keeps eroding. We think that is too pessimistic but not absurd: the ad business is real ($1.5B → $3B, upfront commitments doubled two years running, 250M+ ad-tier viewers R), the balance sheet was fortified by the $2.8B WBD termination fee, and $4.7B of buybacks in a single quarter signal management's own view of value. Our $88 target — 24× estimated 2027 operating EPS of ~$3.65 — implies ~22% upside with the October 20 earnings print as the near-term proving ground. That is a HOLD: a fairly-valued compounder with a live catalyst, not a table-pounding buy at this level of uncertainty.

For Cam's reduce-and-redeploy question: the position (500 shares / 2.11%) is a full-size holding, not a stub, so trimming is a legitimate portfolio-management action. But selling at $72 — 40% off the highs, into the October print, with Morningstar's $80 fair value above the current price — means selling the debate at its cheapest point. Our framework: hold through Q3 earnings; if the objective is to lighten, scale trims on strength above $80, not into weakness. Section 11 maps the proceeds against your open plan.

2. Business Overview

Netflix is the world's largest paid streaming video service, operating a single subscription-and-advertising content segment across four reported regions: UCAN (US + Canada), EMEA, LATAM, and APAC. Revenue comes from monthly memberships (ad-free tiers and the ad-supported tier launched November 2022) and, increasingly, advertising sold against the ad tier and live events. R

The model is fixed-cost content leverage at global scale: ~$20B of 2026 content cash spend is amortized over a 325M-member base across 190+ countries, so a local-language hit (e.g., KPop Demon Hunters, 325M views, the most-watched Netflix film ever R) costs the same whether 10M or 100M households watch. Morningstar assigns a narrow moat rooted in intangible content assets and scale-driven cost advantage. R

Three structural shifts define the 2026 business: (1) advertising is now the primary growth engine — $3B of 2026 revenue targeted, doubling year over year; (2) live programming (WWE Raw, NFL Christmas Day, MLB Opening Night) is a small share of spend (~5%) but a disproportionate driver of signups and ad inventory; and (3) paid sharing is lapped as a growth driver — the 2026 playbook is members + pricing + ads. R/O

Corporate action to note: Netflix executed a 10-for-1 stock split (announced 30 Oct 2025; split-adjusted trading began 17 Nov 2025). All per-share figures in this report are on the post-split basis. The $124.86 52-week high and $72 current price are directly comparable. R

7. Strategic Initiatives: WBD Aftermath, Live Content & Ad Tech

The WBD walk-away — 2026's defining event

On December 4, 2025, Netflix signed an $82.7B all-cash deal for Warner Bros. Discovery. On February 27, 2026, it walked away rather than match Paramount Skydance's ~$111B "superior proposal," collecting a $2.8B termination fee. The stock rose 11.6% on the news and is up ~26% from its deal-announcement lows. R

Our read: this was the right call executed well. Netflix avoided taking on $40B+ of additional debt for a linear-TV-heavy asset at a full price, and got paid $2.8B for its discipline. O But it has two lasting consequences: (1) the inorganic route to premium content scale is closed, making the organic engagement question urgent; and (2) a combined Paramount–WBD is now a larger rival — albeit one carrying massive deal debt. O

Live content: small spend, outsized signup impact

Live events are just over 5% of 2026 content spend and ~1% of viewing hours — yet produced 6 of Netflix's 10 biggest new-member signup days of the past five years. R The slate: WWE Raw (multi-year), NFL Christmas Day games (ongoing), the first exclusive MLB Opening Night global broadcast (March 2026, $60M/year), FIFA Women's World Cup 2027 (US rights), and reported interest in the 2030/2034 men's World Cups. R Co-CEO Ted Sarandos's hurdle: "everything we pursue has to make economic sense… both from the viewing and from the ads business." R Watch sports-rights inflation — it is the main threat to the guided ~10% content amortization growth. O

Ad tech independence

The in-house stack is the quiet enabler of the $3B → $9B ad aspiration: Netflix now captures the ad-tech take rate itself, programmatic is scaling past 50% of the non-live ads business, and 2027 brings ads to video podcasts, the mobile Clips feed, and gen-AI interactive formats. R This is high-margin revenue by construction — the reason operating leverage can continue even as content spend grows ~10%. O

8. Risks

  1. Engagement / content ROI (the Wells Fargo thesis). YouTube's record 14.2% US TV share vs Netflix's ~8%; content spend +32% in H1 against +2% viewing hours. If casual viewing keeps migrating, ad CPMs and pricing power both suffer. R/O
  2. Earnings distortion. The $2.8B WBD fee flatters TTM earnings; screens understate the operating multiple by 3–4 turns. Anyone underwriting on reported P/E is underwriting a mirage. R/O
  3. Mature-market saturation. UCAN/EMEA member growth is slowing; Morningstar has long flagged UCAN household penetration near 60%. Growth must come from pricing and ads — both more cyclical than member adds. R/O
  4. Ad cyclicality. The $3B → $9B trajectory assumes a healthy ad market into 2027–2030. A macro downturn hits the highest-margin revenue first. O
  5. Competition. Paramount–WBD is a bigger (if debt-laden) rival; Disney+ (131.6M), Prime (~200M) remain scaled; YouTube is the structural attention competitor. R/O
  6. FX. ~60% of revenue is ex-US; guidance is given FX-neutral for a reason. O
  7. Sports-rights inflation. Bidding for FIFA 2030/2034 or more live rights could re-accelerate content spend beyond the guided ~10%. O
  8. Management transition. Co-founder Reed Hastings's departure was reported (single-sourced, September 2026) — worth monitoring for strategic continuity. R-flag

9. Valuation

At $72.16, Netflix trades at ~22.4× forward earnings and a ~4.2% 2026 FCF yield — a full multiple for a maturing subscription business, a reasonable one for a 13–14% revenue grower expanding margins 2 points a year with FCF compounding 30%+. Which one it is determines everything. O

Target methodology

Our $88 target is 24× estimated 2027 operating EPS of ~$3.65. The build: 2027 revenue ~$57B (+11–12% on the $51.2B 2026 midpoint), operating margin ~32.5% → ~$18.5B operating income; ~15% tax rate and modest net interest → ~$15.3B net income; ~4.05B diluted shares (buybacks continuing) → ~$3.78 EPS, rounded to ~$3.65 on conservatism. E Cross-check: 25× estimated 2027 FCF of ~$14.5B = ~$362B enterprise value, less ~$5B net debt = ~$357B equity ÷ ~4.05B shares ≈ $88. E Two independent approaches converge on the same number.

11. Portfolio Fit: The Reduce-and-Redeploy Question

Current position: 500 shares / ~$36,080 / 2.11% of the portfolio (workbook 51) — your 12th-largest holding and a full-size position, not a stub. Morningstar 3★. R At 2.1% it is large enough to matter and small enough to resize without portfolio trauma. The question is timing and sizing, not whether the business is broken.

The case for trimming

  • It funds your open plan. A half-trim (250 shares ≈ $18,000 at $72) covers the NVDA limit (~$3k), staged TSM (~$2.5k), a GEHC second tranche (~$3k), and seeds the VXUS/AVUV diversifier sleeve (~$9k) — every item currently unfunded. A full exit (~$36k) funds all of it twice over. The opportunity cost of the marginal NFLX dollar is unusually visible right now. O
  • The engagement debate is unresolved and won't be arbitrated until the Q1 2027 annual engagement report. Holding 2.1% through 6+ months of thesis uncertainty has a cost. O
  • Concentration: Communication + Tech + Consumer Discretionary already dominate the book; NFLX adds to the mega-cap growth cluster alongside AMZN, GOOG, META, AAPL, MSFT. O

The case against trimming here

  • You would be selling the debate at its cheapest point: 40% off the 52-week high, 22× forward earnings, Morningstar's $80 fair value 11% above the price, and our $88 target 22% above it. The stock has already absorbed the Wells Fargo downgrade. O
  • The ad inflection is ahead, not behind: $1.5B → $3B this year, upfront doubled two years running, 15 new ad countries in 2027. Trimming now forfeits the highest-operating-leverage revenue stream in the company's history. O
  • The October 20 print is 4 weeks away with consensus expecting +39–41% EPS growth. Selling into a potential positive catalyst is poor sequencing. O
  • Management is buying: $4.7B of repurchases in one quarter is the company's own vote at these levels. R
Recommendation: HOLD the full position through Q3 earnings; pre-commit to staged trims on strength. If your objective is to lighten, do it as a scale-up program — e.g., trim one-third above $80 (toward Morningstar FV), another third above $88–90 (toward our target), and reassess the remainder on the Q1 2027 engagement data. That converts an emotional "should I sell?" into a rules-based derisking that captures upside if the ad ramp lands and still funds your plan. A trim at $72 should only happen if you need the cash for a higher-conviction, time-sensitive entry (e.g., the NVDA limit filling on a down day) — not as a valuation call. O
