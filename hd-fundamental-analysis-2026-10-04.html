2. The quarter: acceleration with guidance reaffirmed

Q2 FY2026 — reported August 18, 2026

Home Depot's second quarter was the cleanest print in the sector. Revenue of $47.86 billion rose 5.7% and beat consensus of $47.27 billion. Comparable sales of +1.7% total (+1.3% US) beat the ~0.9% consensus and marked the strongest quarter since Q3 FY2022, with average ticket up 2.8% more than offsetting a 1.0% decline in transactions. Adjusted EPS of $4.92 rose 5.1% and beat the $4.71–$4.73 consensus range. Gross margin of 33.7% expanded, though it was flattered by roughly $685 million of IEEPA tariff refunds; adjusted operating margin of 14.7% slipped just 10 basis points. Online sales grew 11% for the fifth straight double-digit quarter, now 16.6% of net sales.

Crucially, management reaffirmed full-year guidance across the board: sales growth of 2.5–4.5%, comps flat to +2%, adjusted operating margin of 12.8–13.0%, and EPS growth of flat to 4% (about $14.69–$15.28). In a tape where Lowe's cut to the low end of every range, the reaffirmation is the tell — HD's demand is holding while the category softens. The stock rose 1.9% on the print, a quiet endorsement.

3. Business mix: the Pro fortress

Pro >50% of sales · 8 straight positive Pro comp quarters · 1,340 SRS locations

Home Depot's moat is the professional contractor, and it keeps getting deeper. Pro customers are now more than half of sales, with eight consecutive quarters of positive Pro comps — a streak Lowe's cannot match. The $18.25 billion SRS Distribution acquisition (June 2024) brought roofing and landscaping supply at contractor scale, and the September 2025 GMS acquisition added $5.5 billion of drywall and steel-framing revenue across ~1,200 locations. Over 1,340 SRS locations now operate, roughly 90% of HD stores have completed a sale through SRS in the past year, and management expects $400 million of cross-sell revenue in 2026, scaling in 2027.

The strategic logic is sound: distribution-led Pro exposure tilts the mix toward repair, replace, and remodel — resilient, non-discretionary demand — and away from new construction. Near-term wholesale distribution carries lower margins, but the wallet-share capture with complex-Pro contractors (roofers, landscapers, drywallers) is structural. DIY remains core in-store but is growing slower; the future of this company is the job site, not the weekend warrior.

4. Capital allocation: 3.3% yield, paused buyback, deleveraging

$9.32 annualized dividend · 16-year streak · ~2.4x leverage

The dividend is now a genuine attraction: $2.33 quarterly ($9.32 annualized) yields about 3.3% at the current price — above Lowe's 2.8%, a reversal of the historical pattern. The 16-year raise streak is intact, but the trajectory bears watching: raises have decelerated from +15% (2022) to +10% to +7.7% to +2.2% to just +1.3% in February 2026, and the payout ratio sits at ~62–66%. The next declaration (with Q4 results, February 2027) needs to show the raise re-accelerating, or the market will read the dividend story as maturing.

Buybacks remain paused since the SRS deal — first-half FY2026 cash went to $4.6 billion of dividends, $3.0 billion of debt repayment, and capex — and pro forma net debt/EBITDA of ~2.4x post-GMS keeps the pause in place. Deleveraging is progressing ($3.0 billion repaid in H1), and buyback resumption is the latent EPS lever when it returns.

8. Risks and catalysts into year-end

Housing is the swing factor

Risks: (1) Housing turnover — management itself frames recovery as "more of a 2027+ event"; if 7%+ mortgages persist, large-ticket discretionary stays soft and the comp acceleration stalls. (2) Margin optics — Q2's $685 million of IEEPA tariff refunds flattered gross margin; the underlying ~10bp operating-margin slip is the cleaner read, and H2 carries residual tariff absorption. (3) Leverage — ~$51 billion of net debt from SRS/GMS constrains buybacks and dividend growth until deleveraging advances. (4) Valuation — 19.8x trailing still embeds a premium; a market de-rating would hit HD before LOW.

Catalysts: Q3 FY2026 earnings on November 17 (company-confirmed via IR calendar); the $400 million SRS cross-sell ramp through 2026; any turn in mortgage rates or existing-home sales — HD's December 2025 "market recovery case" (5–6% sales growth, 4–5% comps) is the re-rating blueprint if turnover inflects.
