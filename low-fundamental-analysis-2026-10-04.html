The honest summary: the initiation's numbers all still hold. The re-rating below is a translation of rulers, not a change of mind.

2. The quarter: beat on EPS, cut on guidance

Q2 FY2026 — reported August 19, 2026

Lowe's second quarter told two stories. Revenue of $25.96 billion rose 8.3% year over year, helped by the newly closed FBM and ADG acquisitions, but missed consensus of $26.13 billion. Comparable sales grew just 0.2% — the fifth straight positive quarter, but decelerating from 1.3% in Q4 and 0.6% in Q1, with transactions down 2.1% offset by a 2.3% higher average ticket. Adjusted EPS of $4.40 beat the $4.22 consensus, though the beat leaned on a $0.11 benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds. Gross margin of 33.04% compressed roughly 80 basis points on acquisition mix dilution, and adjusted operating margin of about 14.0% slipped 62 basis points.

The stock fell about 4% on the report — not because of the EPS beat, but because management cut FY2026 guidance to the low end of every prior range: sales of roughly $92.0 billion, flat comparable sales, adjusted operating margin of about 11.6%, and adjusted EPS of about $12.25. Management also warned that Q3 adjusted EPS would run about 7% below the prior-year quarter. The message was honest — demand trends, not execution, are the constraint — and the market chose to shoot first.

3. Business mix: the Pro pivot is working, slowly

Pro >30% of sales · DIY >60% · Online +15.7%

The strategic story at Lowe's is the Pro pivot. Professional customers now account for more than 30% of sales, up from under 20% seven years ago, and Pro comps have been positive for five consecutive quarters. The $8.8 billion Foundation Building Materials acquisition (closed October 2025) and the $1.3 billion Artisan Design Group deal added roughly $1.94 billion of quarterly sales in Q2 versus $138 million a year earlier — real contractor distribution, concentrated in interior Pro and new construction, deliberately counter-positioned against Home Depot's dominance in complex exterior Pro like roofing and landscaping.

The offset is DIY: still more than 60% of sales, and DIY is where a 7.28% mortgage rate and 14-month-low existing-home sales bite hardest. Big-ticket discretionary projects remain soft across the industry. Online is a genuine bright spot at +15.7% in Q2, contributing nearly 200 basis points to comps. Rural assortments now reach about 500 of the company's 1,761 stores. The mix is moving the right direction; it is just moving slower than the housing cycle needs.

4. Capital allocation: Aristocrat dividend, paused buyback, elevated leverage

$5.00 annualized dividend · 50+ year raise streak · 3.0x leverage

Lowe's remains one of the market's premier dividend compounders: a Dividend Aristocrat with more than 50 consecutive years of increases, most recently $1.20 to $1.25 quarterly in May 2026 (+4.2%), for a $5.00 annualized payout yielding about 2.8% at the current price on a comfortable ~42% payout ratio. The five-year dividend CAGR near 15% is among the best in large-cap retail. The next $1.25 quarterly dividend is payable November 4 to holders of record October 21.

The balance sheet is the constraint. Adjusted debt/EBITDA stands at 3.0x after the FBM/ADG deals, against a stated target of 2.75x by mid-2027, and buybacks are effectively paused — $366 million in the first half of FY2026 against a $10.8 billion remaining authorization. Deleveraging, not repurchases, is the near-term capital story. That is the right priority, but it removes a historical source of EPS support until mid-2027.

5. Valuation and target framework

15x forward · 6.9% FCF yield · 41% upside to Morningstar FVE

At $180.80, Lowe's trades at about 15.3x trailing earnings and 15.6–16.7x forward estimates, with EV/EBITDA near 10.3x and a trailing free-cash-flow yield around 6.9% — cheaper than the stock has been on earnings multiples in years, and cheap on every metric versus Home Depot. The PEG near 2.4–2.5 is unremarkable only because near-term growth is muted; the multiple itself is the opportunity.

$225 twelve-month target (about 24% upside). Framework: 17.3x FY2027 consensus EPS of $13.03 — a modest re-rating from today's ~16x toward the historical mid-teens-to-high-teens range as Pro mix and deleveraging progress, with earnings doing the rest. Cross-checks: Morningstar's $255 fair value estimate implies ~41% upside; the sell-side average target of $250–$256 implies mid-30s percent upside. Our target sits deliberately below both — we underwrite the multiple, not the multiple expansion, and leave room for the housing cycle to stay soft.

7. Portfolio fit and recommended gate

New position · initiate toward ~1% on weakness

Neither Lowe's nor Home Depot is currently held — both sit on the watchlist. Home improvement is a new sector exposure for the portfolio — economically sensitive, tied to the housing cycle, but anchored by two wide-moat duopolists with 50+ year operating histories. A starter position toward ~1% of NAV (about $17,000, or roughly 100 shares at the gate) adds cyclical balance to a portfolio concentrated in tech, financials, and healthcare.

Recommended gate: LOW ADD at or below $165. At $165 the forward multiple drops to ~13.5x, the yield rises to 3.0%, and the stock would sit ~35% below Morningstar fair value — a genuine margin of safety for a Dividend Aristocrat. Size the starter toward ~1% of NAV (about 100 shares / ~$16,500 at the gate). Break below $150 on fundamental news means reassess, not buy. The stock at $180.80 is about 9.6% above the gate — no reason to chase; let weakness come to the level. Gate recommendation only — not installed in the dashboard panel pending CIO approval.

8. Risks and catalysts into year-end

Housing is the swing factor

Risks: (1) Housing turnover — 30-year mortgages at 7.28%, existing-home sales at a 14-month low, and 4.9 months of supply pressure both DIY tickets and big-ticket financing; a further leg up in rates hits LOW harder than HD given the DIY skew. (2) Tariffs — China sourcing disclosures conflict (under 10% per one analysis, ~20% per another); H2 2026 price increases are planned and carry elasticity risk. (3) Competition — Home Depot is widening the comps gap (+1.7% vs +0.2%) and dominates complex Pro. (4) Leverage — 3.0x debt/EBITDA delays buyback resumption to mid-2027.

Catalysts: Q3 FY2026 earnings around November 18 (provider projection, not company-confirmed); the $1.25 quarterly dividend payable November 4 (record October 21); any H2 tariff-refund collections (excluded from guidance, so pure upside); and the macro swing factor — a sustained break in mortgage rates below 7% would re-rate the whole sector.
