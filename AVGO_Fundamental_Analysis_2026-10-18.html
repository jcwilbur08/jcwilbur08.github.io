Executive summary

Broadcom’s operating thesis is stronger than it was at the prior underwriting: Q3 FY2026 delivered record revenue, $13.7B of free cash flow, approximately $16.7B of AI semiconductor revenue and a visible next-quarter AI ramp. The stock’s decline into the high-$320s therefore looks more like a valuation and financing-risk reset than a demonstrated loss of demand.

The counterpoint is important. Management’s $58B FY2026, $115B FY2027 and $230B FY2028 AI framework is an expectation, not contracted backlog; future growth depends on a concentrated group of hyperscalers and emerging AI customers; and a credit-market or capex-cycle shock could compress both estimates and the multiple. My conclusion is ADD, 8/10 conviction, but in staged increments and with a hard ceiling near 3% of the portfolio until the next print confirms conversion from demand visibility to durable cash earnings.

Business quality and moat

Semiconductor Solutions

Broadcom owns mission-critical positions in Ethernet switching, optical interconnect, DSP/SerDes, custom accelerators, storage connectivity, broadband and wireless. The AI opportunity is not simply “selling a chip”: it is the combination of custom XPU design, high-speed networking and the interconnect layer required to scale distributed compute.

Moat: deep IP, long qualification cycles, customer co-design, manufacturing coordination and a portfolio that spans the data-center architecture.

Infrastructure Software

VMware Cloud Foundation, mainframe, cybersecurity, AIOps, application delivery and enterprise automation add recurring revenue and switching costs. VMware expands the private-cloud and hybrid-cloud control point, although integration, customer retention and pricing discipline remain critical.

Moat: embedded workflows, compliance requirements, installed-base familiarity and high cost of operational failure.

Valuation: DCF, reverse DCF and market cross-check

Base DCF: $425 per share

Normalized starting FCF: $40B; five-year FCF CAGR: 25%; WACC: 8.5%; terminal growth: 3.0%; diluted shares: approximately 4.95B. The base case assumes AI revenue compounds rapidly but decelerates from the initial surge and software remains a durable cash contributor.

$425 is approximately 29% above the $328.75 reference price.

Reverse DCF: what price implies

At $328.75, with $40B normalized FCF, 8.5% WACC and 3.0% terminal growth, the market is broadly pricing a high-teens to low-20s five-year FCF CAGR. That is demanding, but not irrational if the $115B FY2027 AI framework becomes revenue and cash rather than only customer intent.

The reverse DCF is a sensitivity exercise, not a forecast or a claim that management guidance is contracted backlog.

Bull / base / bear scenarios

Bull — $650 · 25% probability

AI semiconductor revenue reaches or exceeds the $115B FY2027 framework, networking remains a large share of AI revenue, software retention and pricing are strong, and normalized FCF exceeds $55B. The market accepts Broadcom as a second platform in AI infrastructure and maintains a premium multiple.

Base — $425 · 55% probability

Custom silicon and networking continue to compound, but growth normalizes as hyperscaler capex becomes more selective. Software contributes recurring cash flow, FCF grows at a high-teens to mid-20s rate for several years, and the multiple remains elevated but does not expand materially.

Bear — $210 · 20% probability

AI financing stress delays or cancels deployments, customer concentration becomes visible, custom silicon competition rises, or software integration weakens. FCF growth falls toward low teens and the market rerates AVGO toward a more ordinary semiconductor/software multiple.

Probability-weighted value: approximately $422. The expected value is close to the base DCF because the bull case is powerful but the bear case is severe. This asymmetry supports staged buying, not a full-size allocation based on the workbook fair-value number alone.

Upcoming checkpoints

  1. Next earnings: revenue and AI semiconductor revenue versus the implied ramp; require cash-flow conversion, not only revenue guidance.
  2. AI customer quality: evidence of firm orders, shipment timing, recognized revenue and acceptable margins from hyperscaler and AI-lab customers.
  3. Infrastructure software: VMware retention, bookings, private-cloud adoption, pricing, recurring revenue and operating leverage.
  4. Capital intensity and balance sheet: capex, working capital, diluted share count, acquisition spending and the relationship between GAAP and non-GAAP FCF.
  5. Market structure: credit spreads, hyperscaler capex plans, AI customer funding and whether capital availability becomes the binding constraint described by management.

Risks and explicit thesis-break conditions

Key risks

  • AI capex financing and customer solvency.
  • Concentration in a small number of hyperscalers and emerging AI labs.
  • Custom silicon competition, including internal designs and Marvell.
  • VMware integration, retention and price elasticity.
  • Non-GAAP FCF quality, SBC and acquisition amortization.
  • Export controls, supply constraints and geopolitics.

Thesis-break conditions

Re-underwrite AVGO if: (1) AI semiconductor revenue or guide declines for two consecutive quarters without a clear timing explanation; (2) major customer orders are delayed, cancelled or financing-dependent; (3) normalized FCF margins fall materially while revenue growth slows; (4) infrastructure software retention or pricing deteriorates for two quarters; (5) custom-silicon share is lost to internal or competing designs; or (6) leverage rises because capital returns or acquisitions outrun durable cash flow.

A single earnings miss, sector selloff or AI-financing headline is a review trigger—not an automatic exit.

Conclusion

Broadcom remains one of the portfolio’s strongest long-duration growth franchises. The operating evidence supports the thesis; the market’s concern is whether the next phase of AI infrastructure can be financed and monetized at the pace investors now expect. At $328.75, the risk/reward is favorable enough for a staged ADD toward approximately 3%, with 8/10 conviction, but the next underwriting should be driven by cash realization, customer quality and software durability—not by the headline AI revenue framework alone.
