Investment conclusion

Eli Lilly remains the single best growth asset in healthcare: Q2 revenue up 48% to $22.97 billion (a $2.3 billion beat), full-year guidance raised to $85–87 billion revenue and $35.50–36.50 non-GAAP EPS, the first non-peptide oral GLP-1 (Foundayo) approved and launching, retatrutide's 28.3% weight loss heading for a Q1 2027 BLA, and manufacturing supply finally normalized after years as the constraint. The incremental news since October 3 is confirmatory, not thesis-changing: Foundayo post-hoc data strengthened the oral story (57% modeled reduction in 10-year diabetes risk), cross-trial comparisons favored both Foundayo and Zepbound, and JPMorgan raised its target to $1,500. But $1,143 still pays 31x forward earnings for that excellence, and the risks — Novo's oral Wegovy, Pfizer's $10 billion Metsera bet, Amgen's once-monthly MariTide, and baked-in global pricing declines — are unchanged. Buy with a $1,300 twelve-month target (27.9x FY2027 consensus EPS of $46.65). This is staged accumulation on weakness, not a name to chase into the October 29 print. 1. What changed in 24 hours

Incremental news, October 2–4 · confirmatory, not thesis-changing

One session produced no fundamental developments and a quiet tape: Lilly closed Friday at $1,142.85, down 0.61% ($7.00) on no company-specific driver. The incremental items, all verified, point the same direction as the existing thesis:

  • Foundayo post-hoc data (October 1). Lilly reported ATTAIN-1 post-hoc analyses: at 72 weeks, the 17.2 mg dose was associated with an estimated 57% reduction in predicted 10-year type 2 diabetes risk versus placebo (45% and 50% at the 5.5 mg and 9 mg doses), plus an estimated 18% reduction in predicted cardiovascular disease risk. These are modeled risk estimates, not observed cardiovascular outcomes — useful evidence for prescribers and payers, but the commercial value depends on how regulators and payers interpret modeled data. Directionally positive for the oral ramp.
  • Cross-trial comparisons (September 29). Lilly presented analyses showing Foundayo 17.2 mg with 1.50% greater weight loss and 0.30% greater A1C reduction than oral semaglutide 25 mg at 52 weeks (ACHIEVE-3 versus PIONEER PLUS data — a cross-trial comparison, not a head-to-head), and Zepbound 15 mg with 4.50% greater weight loss than Wegovy HD at 72 weeks (SURMOUNT-5: 20.2% versus 13.7%). Cross-trial comparisons carry the usual caveats, but both favor Lilly's molecules.
  • Sell-side: JPMorgan to $1,500 (September 28). JPMorgan raised its price target from $1,400 to $1,500 and maintained Overweight — the highest major target in the comp set and a sign the Street's incretin growth conviction is intact. Consensus averages roughly $1,312.
  • Competitive noise: Viking Therapeutics. Viking's maintenance-dosing data drew attention, but Lilly's stock barely moved — correctly so. Lilly already owns the maintenance answer: Foundayo held weight off after Zepbound or Wegovy in its own Phase 3 (Wegovy-switch patients regained only ~0.9 kg over 52 weeks), and lower-dose Zepbound maintained most weight loss in a separate study. Viking still needs longer, larger trials.
  • Access initiative (September 28). Lilly launched the "Change the Course Commitment," aiming for parity in patients treated with its diabetes and obesity medicines between resource-limited settings and high-income countries by 2040 — reputationally useful, immaterial to 2026–27 math.

Net: nothing in the last 24 hours alters the rating, target, conviction, or gate. The next real test is Q3 earnings around October 29.

2. The quarter: +48% revenue and a raised guide

Q2 2026 — reported August 5 · rev $22.97B · non-GAAP EPS $8.38

Lilly's second quarter was a genuine blowout: revenue of $22.974 billion rose 48% year over year and beat consensus by roughly $2.3 billion; non-GAAP EPS of $8.38 (+33%) crushed the ~$6.40 estimate even after $3.03 of acquired IPR&D charges; gross margin printed at 85.8% — software-like economics on molecules. Management raised full-year guidance to $85–87 billion of revenue and $35.50–36.50 non-GAAP EPS. Mounjaro did $9.943 billion (+91%) and Zepbound $4.928 billion (+46%) — the incretin duo is ~65% of company revenue and still accelerating. When two drugs are two-thirds of a trillion-dollar company and both are growing 46–91%, the quarter speaks for itself.

3. The GLP-1 franchise: injectables, orals, and what's next

Foundayo approved Apr 1 · retatrutide 28.3% · amylin next

Foundayo (orforglipron) is the strategic unlock: FDA-approved April 1, 2026 as the first non-peptide oral GLP-1 — no food or water restrictions — at $25/month commercial and $149 self-pay, with 12.4% weight loss (27 lbs) in ATTAIN-1 and EU approval in September 2026. Early uptake is mixed (about a third of new oral starts; ~3,700 scripts in week two) and Q2 sales weren't separately disclosed — the ramp, not the approval, is the 2026 story. A Type 2 diabetes filing is submitted, and October post-hoc data showed a 57% modeled reduction in diabetes risk. The October 1 EASD symposium walked through the full ACHIEVE program for the diabetes indication.

The pipeline compounds the lead. Retatrutide delivered 28.3% weight loss over 80 weeks in Phase 3 — a number no competitor has touched — with a BLA planned for Q1 2027 across obesity, sleep apnea, and knee osteoarthritis. The amylin eloralintide hit up to 20.1% in Phase 2, with Phase 3 enrolling and a Zepbound combination reaching 23% (reported September 30). Lilly isn't defending a franchise; it's stacking three successive waves — injectable, oral, triple-agonist/amylin — each aimed at a different patient preference.

4. Beyond obesity: the rest of the portfolio

Verzenio, Jardiance, Ebglyss, Kisunla all contributing

The non-obesity business is quietly strong: Verzenio $1.474 billion (flat, a $5B+ anchor), Jardiance $1.23 billion (+79%), Ebglyss $201 million (+131%), Omvoh $102 million (+36%), Jaypirca $192 million (+56%), Kisunla $167 million (Alzheimer's, building quarter over quarter), and Trulicity $1.22 billion (+12%) still throwing off cash. The Inluriyo/Verzenio combination won FDA approval in late September 2026, and Onswik (once-weekly basal insulin) and an expanded Olumiant label added to the non-incretin news flow. Obesity gets the multiple; this diversified base is what makes the multiple survivable if incretin growth ever pauses.

5. Manufacturing: the constraint becomes the moat

>$27B US commitments · supply normalized · +60% volume

For two years Lilly's growth was supply-constrained; that era is ending. The company added $4.5 billion of Indiana commitments in Q2 (total US commitments above $27 billion), opened the Lebanon, Indiana genetic-medicine facility, produced the first commercial batch in Limerick, Ireland, and has $6.5 billion of Houston capacity under construction. Volume rose 60%, and $1.5 billion of Foundayo inventory was pre-built ahead of launch. Manufacturing at this scale — tens of billions of deployed capital — is now a competitive moat: no rival can replicate the capacity in under three years, which is exactly the window Lilly's pipeline needs.

6. Capital allocation

$6.92 dividend · 11 straight raises · $47.8B debt vs $9B cash

The $1.73 quarterly dividend ($6.92 annualized, 0.60% yield) has risen 11 straight years at ~15% CAGR on a modest ~22–23% payout — income is not the thesis, compounding is. Q2 saw $1.6 billion of buybacks against $1.5 billion of dividends; the balance sheet carries $47.8 billion of long-term debt against $9 billion cash after an acquisition-heavy stretch (over $13 billion of H1 2026 acquisition/IPR&D spend). Leverage is elevated for Lilly but serviceable at these margins — the 85.8% gross margin is the shock absorber. Management has also signaled appetite for larger-scale acquisitions following the Centessa deal.

8. Portfolio fit and recommended gate

New position · the growth anchor for the healthcare sleeve

Lilly is not currently held. In the healthcare sleeve taking shape — VHT as core, BMY/UNH/GEHC as single names — Lilly is the growth anchor: the one name with genuine 40%+ revenue growth and a multi-wave pipeline. But 31x forward with Q3 earnings around October 29 is not an entry to chase; the stock is 12% off its high and the competitive headlines (Novo, Metsera, MariTide) will keep coming.

Recommended gate: LLY ADD at or below $1,000

9. Risks and catalysts

Competition · pricing · Q3 around Oct 29

Risks: (1) Competition — Novo's oral Wegovy, Pfizer's $10B Metsera bet (Phase 3 late 2026), Amgen's once-monthly MariTide (~20% Phase 2 loss), plus Roche/Viking/AstraZeneca/Regeneron. Note Novo's CagriSema just failed non-inferiority vs tirzepatide (23% vs 25.5%) — the moat is holding, but the siege is real. (2) Pricing — guidance already bakes in low-to-mid-teens global pricing declines; IRA/MFN/TrumpRx pressure can always get worse. (3) Multiple — 31x forward compresses hard on any growth disappointment; Foundayo's ramp is the near-term tripwire.

Catalysts: Q3 earnings around October 29 (provider projection, not company-confirmed) — the print that decides whether conviction moves toward 8 or back to 6; orforglipron Type 2 diabetes decision timeline; eloralintide Phase 3/combo data; retatrutide BLA in Q1 2027; EMBER-4 in 2027.
