Investment conclusion

Johnson & Johnson is the highest-quality healthcare compounder in the market — 64 consecutive dividend increases, 0.8x net leverage, and franchises like Darzalex (+18.9%), Tremfya (+72.5% to its first $2 billion quarter), and Spravato (+40%) growing at biotech rates. But quality is fully priced: at $256 the stock trades at ~23x forward earnings on guidance management cut on July 29, and two overhangs remain unresolved — the $5.5–7 billion talc settlement framework (still needs 95% claimant acceptance) and the Stelara biosimilar cliff (down 55%, a 760-basis-point drag on pharma growth). With Q3 earnings landing October 13, this is a name to own on weakness, not to chase. Initiate HOLD with a $275 twelve-month target (21.4x FY2027 consensus EPS of $12.83). 1. The quarter and the cut

Q2 2026 — reported July 15 · guidance cut July 29

The second quarter itself was a beat: revenue of $25.31 billion rose 6.6% (5.6% operational), ahead of consensus, and adjusted EPS of $2.90 beat the $2.84 estimate. Innovative Medicine grew 6.8% operationally to $16.38 billion; MedTech grew 3.6% to $8.93 billion; the US was 57.4% of sales. Management initially raised full-year guidance — then, two weeks later on July 29, cut adjusted EPS guidance to $10.96–$11.11 from $11.60–$11.75, entirely due to deal costs: the $1 billion Firefly Bio acquisition and the Sail Biomedicines partnership shaved $0.64 off 2026, with a further $1.36 impact guided for 2027. Revenue guidance was maintained at $100.8–$101.4 billion. The market read it correctly: the quarter was fine, but the earnings trajectory just got $2 more expensive to reach.

2. Innovative Medicine: growth engines at biotech rates

Darzalex +18.9% · Tremfya +72.5% · Spravato +40%

Underneath the Stelara hole, J&J's pharma engine is firing. Darzalex did $4.21 billion (+18.9%) and remains the dominant multiple-myeloma therapy; oncology segment sales rose 17.3%. Tremfya posted its first $2 billion quarter at $2.05 billion (+72.5%) and just won a label expansion for inhibiting joint damage in psoriatic arthritis — it is the designated post-Stelara anchor, with management targeting ~$10 billion peak. Spravato reached $584 million (+40%), with Jefferies seeing $5 billion-plus peak potential. The cell-therapy trio (Carvykti, Tecvayli, Talvey) all grew 48–63%. And IcotYde — the first oral peptide IL-23 inhibitor, FDA-approved in March 2026 — just got its peak estimate raised to $4.5 billion by BofA. This is a genuine growth pipeline, not a melting ice cube.

3. MedTech: the steady compounder

$8.93B quarterly · EP, Shockwave, wound closure leading

MedTech (+3.6% operational in Q2) is the ballast: electrophysiology (VARIPULSE rollout) and Shockwave in cardiovascular, wound closure and biosurgery in surgery, Acuvue in vision, trauma in orthopedics. Full-year 2025 MedTech sales were ~$34 billion (+5.4% operational). The watch item is the potential DePuy Synthes orthopedics separation (~mid-2027) — a sum-of-the-parts unlock if executed well — and the ~$500 million of 2026 tariff costs concentrated here on China retaliatory duties.

4. The two overhangs: talc and Stelara

$5.5–7B talc framework · Stelara −55%

Talc: the July 2026 framework would pay ~$5.5 billion (uncapped, possibly $6–7 billion) to settle ~76,000 ovarian-cancer claims — 99.75% of the US docket — with ~$3 billion payable in each of 2027 and 2028. But it needs 95% claimant acceptance and is working through federal MDL approval; the UK case (~7,000 claimants) is excluded. A signed deal removes the last existential headline; a failed acceptance restarts the saga. Stelara: biosimilar erosion took sales down 55.2% to $740 million in Q2 — a 760-basis-point drag on Innovative Medicine growth. Tremfya and IcotYde must cover this hole through 2027. On the positive side, Stelara and Xarelto exit IRA price negotiation on January 1, 2027 via biosimilar/generic competition, capping the policy damage.

5. Capital allocation: Dividend King, fortress balance sheet

64 straight raises · 0.8x leverage · ~$21B FCF guide

The dividend is sacred: $1.34 quarterly ($5.36 annualized, ~2.1% yield), raised for the 64th consecutive year in April 2026, covered ~1.7x by free cash flow against ~$21 billion of 2026 FCF guidance. Net debt/EBITDA of ~0.8x is fortress-grade and funds the M&A machine: Intra-Cellular ($14.6B, Caplyta), Atraverse, Firefly Bio ($1B), and the Sail Biomedicines partnership ($785M upfront plus a $2.58B buyout option). Buybacks ($4.0B in Q1) are routine rather than aggressive — the cash goes to the pipeline first. That is the right priority at this multiple, but it means shareholders are underwriting R&D, not repurchases.

6. Valuation and target framework

~23x forward on cut guide · consensus PT $274 · Morningstar $190 (dated)

At $256.03, J&J trades at ~30x trailing and ~23.2x the (cut) FY2026 guidance midpoint — a full multiple by any historical standard for this name, reflecting the market's willingness to pay for quality in a soft tape. The sell-side averages $274 (range $260–$320, Moderate Buy); Morningstar's $190 fair value is five months stale and safely ignored as a timing artifact. EV/EBITDA near 20x and a 2.7% FCF yield confirm: this is priced as a compounder, not a value.

$275 twelve-month target (about 7% upside). Framework: 21.4x FY2027 consensus EPS of $12.83 — a quality multiple on cleaner earnings once the 2026 deal-cost noise passes, roughly in line with the sell-side average. The upside is modest because the multiple is already full; the return case from here is earnings compounding plus the 2.1% dividend, not re-rating. A talc settlement signing or a Q3 beat-and-raise would be the upside surprise; either justifies revisiting.

8. Risks and catalysts
