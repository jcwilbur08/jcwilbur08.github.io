Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

The Braves did exactly what the September 27 thesis needed them to do — and the stock barely noticed. Atlanta won its wild-card series 2–1 over Philadelphia on October 1, the franchise's first postseason series victory since the 2021 World Series, in front of a sellout 41,731 at Truist Park. The shares closed the week at $57.62, down 2.4% from the prior report's reference price and 4.7% below the fresh $60.48 all-time high printed September 29. That divergence — real on-field success met with a shrug by the market — is the entire story of this update: at $57.62 the quote sits essentially on top of Morningstar's $57.92 fair value, leaving no margin of safety against the one risk that got worse this week, a now-near-certain December lockout.

Research posture: Hold  |  Conviction 5/10  |  12-month target $60

Under the new rating scale effective October 3, the September 27 legacy HOLD translates to Hold: conviction 5/10 with ~4% upside to the $60 target clears neither the Buy bar (6–7 conviction and at least 10% upside) nor the Strong Buy bar. The $60 target is the same ~$4.45B enterprise-value framework as September 27 — a ~$3.9B franchise value against the reported ~$4B private-market comp plus a ~$0.55B premium for the compounding Battery platform, less ~$0.68B of net debt — now divided by an updated ~63.0M share count, which mechanically trims the prior $63 target. This remains a keep-and-watch tracking position: genuinely scarce asset, fairly priced, with a binary labor outcome 58 days out.

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

Scarcity is the floor

Premium baseball franchises almost never trade. The reported ~$4B price on a potential Angels sale sets the private-market comp, and the Braves — perennial contender, new-entrant stadium district, top-tier attendance — deserve to sit at or near that level. That comp is the valuation anchor; everything else is a premium or discount to it.

2

The Battery is the only part of the business that compounds

Mixed-use revenue grew 14% in Q2 and 26% in the first half, producing ~$21M of quarterly adjusted OIBDA at ~74% margins — roughly $84M annualized of steady real-estate earnings inside a company whose baseball segment just printed a quarterly OIBDA loss. The development diversifies the revenue base beyond the 81-game home schedule and is the credible source of the ~$0.55B premium in the target enterprise value.

3

Playoff baseball is high-margin, and October 2026 is delivering it

Postseason games carry incremental ticket, concession, and sponsorship revenue on a largely fixed cost base. The wild-card series win banked three home games (30,127 / 37,150 / 41,731 attendance); a longer NLDS run would add more. This is real, but it is also lumpy and largely non-recurring — it supports the thesis without changing the valuation framework.

4

The price already reflects all of the above

At $57.62, up ~36% year-to-date and within 5% of the all-time high, the quote captures the playoff run, the scarcity comp, and the Battery growth. Morningstar's $57.92 fair value — essentially the market price — confirms the fairly-valued read. There is no margin of safety here, which caps conviction at 5/10 regardless of asset quality.

5

The December 1 CBA expiry is a genuine binary

Owners have now circulated a concrete proposal — a $245.3M team spending cap and $171.2M floor with a ~50/50 revenue split — and the players' association has rejected any hard cap outright. A lockout is the base case; the open question is duration. A 2022-style brief stoppage is absorbable. A prolonged stoppage that erases a large part of the 2027 season would cut the revenue lines that fund everything else. This is the downside case, and it is no longer remote.

Variant perception. The market treats BATRA as a momentum play on a contending team. The differentiated view is that this is a fairly-priced NAV asset with a compounding real-estate kicker and a binary labor outcome — a hold-and-watch position to be added only on dislocation, not on playoff excitement. The variant bet is patience: the real entry is a lockout-driven washout, not this week's headlines.
02 | Business & Structure

What the tracking stock actually owns

Atlanta Braves Holdings tracks the economic performance of the Braves Group: the Atlanta Braves franchise (Truist Park event revenue, media rights including the BravesVision regional venture, sponsorships, retail and licensing) and The Battery Atlanta, the mixed-use real-estate development around the ballpark. The share structure is a Liberty Media legacy: Class A (BATRA, one vote), Class B (ten votes), and Class C (BATRK, fractional vote) all track the same economics. There is no dividend and no buyback; Class A trading is thin and can magnify daily moves.

Why a tracking stock matters for sizing

Tracking stocks historically trade at a discount to equivalent standalone value, offer no shareholder distributions, and BATRA's thin Class A float adds volatility around events like the December CBA expiry. The structure is fine for a satellite tracking position; it is a poor vehicle for a core holding. Size accordingly.

Balance sheet

GAAP debt stood at ~$793M against ~$116M of cash at June 30 (net debt ~$677M, roughly 19% of market cap). The $112.5M construction loan (SOFR + 1.80%) matures December 2026 — the same month as the CBA expiry — and a $95M mixed-use term loan was extended to May 2027. First-half operating cash flow turned negative (−$1.6M vs +$87.6M a year ago). Refinancing in a ~5.2% 10-year world will not get cheaper; watch the December maturity execution.

Quality read. The earnings lumpiness is a feature of the asset, not a flaw in the quarter — but the balance sheet moved the wrong way while it happened. Debt rose on facility and TeamCo revolver borrowings, and the company now faces a refinancing and a labor negotiation in the same month. Neither is fatal alone; together they are the reason conviction stays at 5/10.

06 | Risks

What can go wrong

Elevated

2027 lockout

Concrete $245.3M cap proposal vs. an outright union rejection. A prolonged stoppage cuts games — the revenue lines that fund everything else — and would likely compress the franchise comp that anchors the valuation.

Elevated

December refinancing

$112.5M construction loan matures the same month as the CBA expiry; the $95M term loan follows in May 2027. Two binary events in one month is poor sequencing for a levered tracking stock.

Moderate

Baseball operating leverage

Six fewer home games swung baseball OIBDA ~$58M year-over-year in Q2. Playoff qualification, weather, and scheduling move the needle disproportionately — in both directions.

Moderate

Tracking-stock discount & liquidity

Thin Class A trading magnifies moves; tracking stocks historically trade at a discount to standalone value; no dividend or buyback supports the price. The Sep 30 EVP sale adds near-term overhang optics.

Moderate

Media-rights lumpiness

BravesVision and national digital-rights timing drove an $8.2M quarterly media decline — the "recurring" line has its own calendar risk.

Mitigant

Battery diversification

~$84M annualized mixed-use OIBDA at ~74% margins is contractually steadier than baseball and grows regardless of the standings or the CBA calendar.

Glossary

Adjusted OIBDA — operating income before depreciation and amortization, adjusted for stock-based compensation and other items; the company's primary non-GAAP operating metric. Tracking stock — a class of common stock whose value tracks a specific business unit of the issuer. BATRA / BATRK / BATRV — Atlanta Braves Holdings Class A (1 vote), Class C (fractional vote), Class B (10 votes). CBA — collective bargaining agreement. BravesVision — the team's regional sports broadcast venture. NAV — net asset value; here, franchise value plus real-estate premium less net debt. Rule 10b5-1 — SEC provision allowing pre-planned insider sales.
