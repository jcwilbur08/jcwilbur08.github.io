Section 01 · The Verdict

A record beat-and-raise, sold before it landed

Q3 FY2027 was a demolition on every bar the desk tracks. Revenue of $54.937B (+103%) cleared the company's own $49.0B guide by $5.94B; non-GAAP EPS of $9.45 (+265%) cleared the $6.50 guide by 45.4%; AI-server revenue of $22.031B (+291%) built a record $132.3B backlog; guidance was raised on every line; and free cash flow of $3.474B (+587%) reversed the Q2 flag that anchored the October downgrade. The flagged failure mode — a revenue and AI beat bought with margin compression — did not occur: non-GAAP gross margin rose to 21.2% and ISG operating margin expanded 460 basis points sequentially to 17.0%.

And yet the stock fell 11.83% to $295.28 in the regular session before the print — an AI-hardware selloff day, not a reaction to results — and traded lower again on November 25. The reference close sits $4.72 below the $300 line and $54.86 below the 200-day average. That split is the whole underwrite: the print does not invalidate the thesis, so no rating or target change is triggered — but the suspension cannot lift while both technical legs fail and the $300 line is breached.

Desk decision

Buy 6/10, $580 maintained. Suspension: KEEP. The $300 level was the "re-opens the thesis" line, and this print is the re-examination: it clears the thesis on every fundamental prong — beat on every bar, AI revenue $22.031B, FCF $3.474B against the $1B line, guidance raised on every line, margins expanded. A price-only invalidation on the day of a record beat-and-raise would let the tape overrule the fundamentals. Section 04 · Segments & Margins

The failure mode that didn't happen

The desk's flagged failure mode for this print was precise: a revenue and AI beat delivered with margin compression — the AI mix diluting the P&L faster than scale could offset it. It did not occur. Gross margin rose year over year on both measures, and ISG operating margin expanded from 15.0% in Q2 to 17.0% — helped by record traditional-server (+235%) and storage (+24%, margin-rich Dell IP) performance alongside AI, not by AI alone.

Two margin caveats, carried plainly

AI-server margin itself is still thin and undisclosed. Dell does not publish an AI-server-only margin line; on the call, the business was characterised qualitatively as operating "in the mid-single digits." The 17.0% ISG figure is a blend. Q4 ISG margin is guided to step down sequentially — North America mix normalising — described only qualitatively ("above the high end of our range," with sequential moderation); no numeric Q4 ISG margin was given. That guided step-down is one reason conviction stays at 6.

CSG is the soft spot, and management named the mechanism: in a softer PC unit market, customers are "prioritizing their budgets near term to servers and storage, electing to defer some PC-related refresh." Dell is prioritising ISG over CSG for constrained supply — PC wafer capacity reallocated to servers. The mix shift is structural in management's telling: ISG was 38% of revenue in FY2024; over the last four quarters that rose to 68%, and ISG's share of operating income from 54% to 83% — "and I expect the trend to continue into our next fiscal year."

Section 05 · Cash Flow

The Q2 flag, answered decisively

The October downgrade was anchored on cash conversion: Q2 free cash flow of $986M, down 47%, with roughly 88% of Q2's adjusted FCF made of add-backs rather than operating cash. Q3 reversed it on the clean measure. GAAP free cash flow — operating cash flow less capex and capitalised software, Dell's own definition in both quarters — rose to $3,474M, +587% year over year and 3.5× the desk's $1B invalidation line. The adjusted measure is far cleaner this quarter too: add-backs of $814M are ~19% of adjusted FCF, against $7,163M (~88%) in Q2.

Sequential read: GAAP FCF $986M (Q2) → $3,474M (Q3). Adjusted FCF fell sequentially, $8,149M → $4,288M, only because Q2's add-backs were extraordinary; the GAAP measure is the clean comparison. The desk's Q3 watch metric — operating cash flow well above Q2's $2,225M — was also cleared: it doubled.

Management's explanation was thin: operating cash flow was attributed to "sequential revenue growth and higher profitability," with no working-capital bridge offered and no analyst pressing the definition. Section 07 · The Call

The reported 25–45% price increases: not confirmed

A November 22 press report said Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro were seeking 25–45% increases on selected server transactions after contracts had been signed, citing memory and circuit-board costs — and that XTX had sued Dell's Irish unit in June 2026 alleging a fixed $549M price, with Dell then seeking an additional $62–70M. None of that is confirmed. Dell never mentioned the report, the 25–45% figures, post-signature repricing, or XTX anywhere in the release or on the call, and no analyst asked about it directly. The figures remain reported, not confirmed — they are not Dell facts and are not used anywhere in this underwrite.

What Dell did confirm is directional: prices to customers are rising with component inflation, and the memory crunch runs into 2027.

"Not as inflationary is still a cost increase to our customers in the form of price… there's nothing that suggests that we're going to see price declines in enterprise NAND and DRAM for servers. The demand for those categories of memory continues to be way ahead of the supply… We think that crunch continues clearly into '27."— Jeff Clarke, on DRAM/NAND pricing, Q3 FY2027 call
"Do I think the rate of increase slows? Yes. Do I still think that a significant price implication to our customers? Yes."— Jeff Clarke, Q3 FY2027 call

On the forward book, Vera Rubin systems are "priced with the expected delivery date" — priced for future delivery, not described as a retroactive change to signed contracts. On the P&L, the operating-income-rate gain was attributed to "scale and pricing discipline despite the impact of record AI mix," with operating expenses at 7.0% of revenue, "the lowest in 42 years." Customer acceptance of higher prices in general is evidenced indirectly — $60.2B of orders in the quarter and "I don't know of a customer that's on the sideline." Acceptance of a 25–45% retroactive hike specifically is not established: the nearest analyst question, on who bears "inventory and repricing risk" if build-outs are delayed, was answered as a vetting and credit question, not a pricing one. One analyst floated that Dell "passed on perhaps more than the cost increase"; the premise was neither confirmed nor denied. This question stays open, and it is one of the three reasons conviction is held at 6 rather than raised.

Section 10 · Valuation

$580 stands — the earnings leg did the work

The $580 target was set on October 16 as a multiples target: ~22× the then-consensus FY27 EPS of $26.09 — precisely 22.2×. No discounted-cash-flow valuation exists in the October report or the November refresh, and none is manufactured here. Since November 12, FY27 consensus EPS has risen 15.1% to $29.84 — just $0.16 above management's own raised $29.68 guide, meaning the Street has essentially converged onto the guide — while the Street's average target fell 10.6%. The entire target decline across the Street is multiple compression, not estimate cuts: the Street's implied multiple on FY27 earnings fell from ~22.8× to ~17.7×, and the market's own multiple at the reference close is 9.89× (it was 14.32× at $371.17 on November 12).

Why $580 is neither raised nor cut

Not raised to $662: the multiple that arithmetic assumes no longer exists. The market pays ~9.9×, the Street's own implied multiple has compressed to ~17.7×, and the post-print broker actions cut targets almost to a firm — ten cuts against a single trivial raise — into a record beat. Raising the desk target 14% on the same print that cut the stock 12% in-session would repeat the September error — targets of $600+ set at 23–24× immediately before a 40–50% drawdown. $580 is already demanding: it now implies 19.4× FY27E and 18.0× FY28E — roughly a doubling of the market's current multiple — and it already sits +9.9% above the Street average ($527.68) and +6.4% above the median ($545). On November 12 it sat 1.7% below the Street average; the desk did not move, the Street moved under it. The print strengthening the earnings leg is precisely why $580 now costs fewer multiple turns than when it was set — not a reason to spend the gain by raising it.

Not cut: the print answers the cash-conversion question the October downgrade was anchored on — operating cash flow $4,459m against $2,225m in Q2, free cash flow $3,474m against $986m — and guidance was raised on every line. Cutting the target into a print that beat every bar by 40%+ on EPS would be a price-chasing cut, not an analytical one.

Rating: Buy 6/10 maintained. The print strengthens the fundamentals and passes every invalidation prong, but the technical legs fail, the tape is still falling (November 25 intraday ~$272 at 10:28 a.m. ET, context only), Q4 ISG margin is guided to step down sequentially, and the reported-repricing question is unresolved. No upgrade case is made; no downgrade case is made — no fundamental invalidation trigger fired. One data caveat: the FY27E consensus of $29.84 currently rests on 13 analysts, down from 24 on November 12 — the poll is mid-update as post-print estimates flow in, and the figure may move. FY27E consensus revenue is $202.83B against the $202.0B guide; FY28E EPS is $32.26.
