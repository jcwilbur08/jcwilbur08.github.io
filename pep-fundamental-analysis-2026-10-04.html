01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

The multiple prices the bear case, not the business

At roughly 15.4x forward core earnings, PepsiCo trades at the low end of its ten-year range, down from about 26x at the 2021 peak. The de-rating reflects real problems — North America volumes, failed pricing experiments, margin contraction — but it now embeds an assumption that the problems are permanent. For a company that generated nearly $94B of 2025 revenue across 200+ countries with a DSD network no competitor can replicate, that is a strong assumption. Mean reversion of the multiple toward the high teens does not require a growth miracle; it requires stabilization.

2

The dividend funds the patience this turnaround demands

A ~4.6% forward yield on the 54th consecutive annual increase means the position pays roughly 1.15% per quarter while the thesis plays out. In a retirement portfolio, that cash flow is not decorative: it converts a potentially dead-money waiting period into a compensated one. The payout is well covered by ~$8.55–8.71 of guided 2026 core EPS, and management has shown no inclination to touch the streak even through the volume trough.

3

Elliott is forcing the exact medicine the business needs

Elliott Investment Management disclosed a ~$4B stake in September and, after weeks of engagement, PepsiCo committed to a sweeping reset: roughly 20% of U.S. SKUs eliminated by early next year, manufacturing-line closures, layoffs across North America, and a full review of the North America supply chain with an update due late 2026. Elliott gets no board seats and no proxy fight — this is a negotiated operating plan, not a governance war. The restructuring addresses the cost base that has been deleveraging as volumes softened.

4

International growth is the engine the market ignores

Strip out North America and PepsiCo is a growth company: EMEA core operating profit rose 29% in Q2, Asia Pacific foods grew 35% with volume up 10%, Latin America remains a steady scale platform, and global convenient-foods organic volume ran at +3% — the best rate since 2022. International is large enough that sustained high-single-digit organic growth there can carry consolidated results while North America repairs itself.

5

North America volumes are the swing factor — and Q3 is the verdict

The February affordability program cut wholesale prices by up to 15%; Frito-Lay volume stayed flat and revenue fell 2%. In late September, PepsiCo told Reuters it would raise some chip prices by a low- to mid-single-digit percentage — still below pre-cut levels. Read honestly, this is an admission that the elasticity experiment failed and a test of whether any pricing power remains. Thursday's print will show whether volumes have stabilized, improved, or deteriorated further. The rating, the target, and the position size all hinge on that answer.

Variant perception. The street sees a melting staples icon with broken pricing power and a Hold consensus to prove it. The differentiated view is narrower: the international business is already compounding, the cost base is being forcibly reset, and the market is valuing the consolidated company as if North America's trough is permanent. If volumes merely stabilize — not surge — the combination of a 15.4x entry multiple and a 4.6% yield produces a mid-20s total return without heroics.
02 | Business & Industry

Business quality and competitive position

PepsiCo, headquartered in Purchase, New York, is the world's largest convenient-foods-and-beverages company by sales, with products consumed more than a billion times a day in over 200 countries and territories. The portfolio spans salty snacks (Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Tostitos), beverages (Pepsi, Mountain Dew, Gatorade, Starry, poppi), and foods (Quaker). Convenient foods have historically contributed the majority of operating profit, which is precisely why the North America volume problem matters so much.

Moat assessment

Direct-store-delivery network
Strong

The Frito-Lay DSD system is the shelf-space machine behind U.S. salty-snack leadership; no competitor can replicate its route density and in-store execution.

Brand portfolio
Strong

Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Pepsi, Gatorade and Mountain Dew are category-defining brands with decades of equity, though health trends are eroding some categories at the margin.

Scale & procurement
Strong

Nearly $94B of revenue provides procurement, manufacturing and advertising scale advantages across 200+ markets.

Pricing power
Weakened

The failed 2026 elasticity experiment is the central concern: once consumers defected to private label at 47–63% discounts, list-price cuts did not bring them back. The September price-increase reversal is the test of what power remains.

Competitive landscape

Coca-Cola remains the cleanest comp and the painful one: KO is up roughly 26% year-to-date while PepsiCo is down ~11%, a 37-point divergence between two businesses that once traded as a pair. Coke's pure-play beverage model avoided the snacks-volume problem entirely. Within salty snacks, private label is the share thief — not a branded competitor — which makes the problem harder to solve with innovation alone. In beverages, healthier alternatives and energy drinks (where PepsiCo holds a Celsius stake) continue to fragment the category.

The private-label problem is structural, not cyclical. When grocery prices crossed affordability thresholds, consumers permanently shifted to store brands priced nearly half below branded snacks. Promotional pricing has not reversed the trial. This is why the SKU rationalization and simpler-ingredients pivot (Doritos Protein, Simply NKD lines, prebiotic cola) matter more than the next round of price adjustments: the company must give consumers a reason to trade back up, not just a lower price.

Dividend and capital returns

PepsiCo is a Dividend King with 54 consecutive annual increases, and the current annualized dividend of about $5.81 per share yields roughly 4.6% at the $125.89 reference price. On the $8.55–8.71 core EPS guide, the payout ratio sits near 67% — elevated for a growth company, comfortable for a mature staples compounder with stable cash generation. The dividend is the ballast of this thesis: it pays shareholders to wait through the volume trough and the restructuring. Share repurchases continue but are secondary to the dividend in the capital-return hierarchy.

04 | Management & Capital Allocation

Elliott forced the plan; execution is now the only variable

CEO Ramon Laguarta has run PepsiCo since 2018, overseeing the portfolio's pivot toward healthier options and the international expansion that now carries growth. The defining governance event of 2026 is the September engagement with Elliott Investment Management, which disclosed a ~$4B stake and pushed for strategic clarity, refranchising of bottling operations, and divestiture of non-core food assets. After weeks of discussions, PepsiCo announced a negotiated operating plan — no board seats, no proxy contest — that Elliott publicly endorsed.

Positive

Elliott's plan is the right medicine

~20% U.S. SKU reduction by early next year, manufacturing-line closures, layoffs across North America, and a full NA supply-chain review with an update due late 2026. The cost base gets reset while volumes are soft — exactly when restructuring dollars go furthest.

Positive

Collaborative, not hostile

Laguarta acknowledged many of Elliott's proposals were already in the strategic roadmap. A negotiated plan with continued engagement beats a proxy fight: management owns the execution instead of resisting it.

Watch

Bottling refranchising unresolved

Elliott's push to refranchise or spin the North American bottling network remains the open item — Laguarta has not given a definitive answer. This is the highest-leverage remaining lever.

Watch

Execution during contraction

Plant closures (Maryland, 143 jobs) and layoffs in early October signal seriousness, but restructuring during volume declines risks cutting muscle with fat. Watch for the late-2026 supply-chain review to quantify savings.

Innovation pivot: simpler ingredients, functional benefits

Alongside cost action, PepsiCo is accelerating products with simpler and more functional profiles — Doritos Protein, Simply NKD Cheetos and Doritos (no artificial flavors or colors), a prebiotic cola — and expanding experiential distribution, including a September multi-year Live Nation partnership covering 110+ U.S. concert venues. The strategy is coherent: give consumers a reason to trade back up from private label rather than competing on price alone.

Why this management setup earns patience. The classic activist failure mode is a plan announced and then slow-walked. Here the sequencing is unusually fast: stake disclosed in September, restructuring announced within weeks, layoffs underway by early October, SKU cuts targeted for early next year, supply-chain review due late 2026. Speed of execution is the best early signal that this engagement will be different.

Buy — contrarian, paid-to-wait, with a dated checkpoint

PepsiCo is investable because the market has priced a permanent impairment into a temporary — and actively remediated — North America volume problem, while the dividend pays shareholders to wait for the proof.

The attractive part of the setup is the asymmetry: ~21% price upside to $152 plus a 4.6% yield, against a 52-week-low entry where expectations are washed out and an activist is forcing cost action. The international business is already growing at high-single digits, which means the consolidated company does not need a North America miracle — it needs stabilization.

The caution is equally important, and it is why this is a 6/10 Buy rather than a Strong Buy. The February pricing experiment failed, the September price-increase reversal is ambiguous evidence at best, JPMorgan just downgraded into the print, and the full-year guide requires a second-half acceleration that has not yet appeared. Thursday's quarter is a genuine binary: it either validates the stabilization thesis or breaks it.
