01 · Desk call

Maintain Buy—but recognize that this is now a credit story, too.

The $220 target and 6/10 conviction hold. The upside case improved as Starship crossed its first orbital milestone and AI contracts began turning into cash receipts. The offset is a proposed debt load large enough to change the stock’s risk profile.

The October 5 initiation treated SPCX as a three-engine growth platform: launch, Starlink connectivity, and AI compute. All three engines advanced in ten days. Flight 14 reached orbit and deployed 26 next-generation Starlink satellites. Starlink moved closer to a standalone US mobile proposition. Google’s $920 million monthly compute payments began in October.

Yet the stock fell roughly 9% in a week, even as the news improved. That is not irrational. The reported $40 billion Apollo-led financing would take debt above $79 billion, while 1.31 billion shares become eligible for sale shortly after Q3 earnings. The market is forcing the equity story to absorb both credit risk and supply risk at once.

Bottom line

Thesis intact, but riskier. Maintain Buy, 6/10, and the $220 target. Do not chase the pullback above the entry gate. A roughly 4.9 beta, negative free cash flow and unresolved financing terms justify staged sizing only.

What 6/10 means here

The return potential is strong enough for Buy, but conviction is capped by leverage, share-supply and execution risk. The rating is not a forecast that every catalyst lands; it is a judgment that the target remains achievable if contracted AI demand, Starlink volume and Starship cadence convert without a balance-sheet break.

02 · Since October 5

More proof on growth. More pressure on funding.

For the better

  • Starship de-risked a major step. Flight 14 completed the program’s first orbital flight and deployed 26 V3 Starlink satellites.
  • AI cash flow began. Google’s $920 million monthly payments started in October under a roughly $30 billion agreement through June 2029; Anthropic contracted for up to $84.5 billion through May 2029.
  • Starlink distribution widened. Subscribers reached roughly 13 million; Q3 revenue is estimated near $4.8 billion, and American Airlines expanded deployment to 1,030+ aircraft.
  • Sell-side support strengthened. Goldman and Pivotal moved to $230, Barclays initiated at $254, and Morgan Stanley maintained $300.

For the worse

  • Leverage became the dominant risk. The FT-reported $40 billion financing would add roughly $30 billion of bonds and $10 billion of loans.
  • Credit contagion is live. CoreWeave’s bonds yielding 15% became the immediate template for repricing debt-funded AI infrastructure.
  • Supply is intensifying. Another 1.31 billion shares are due to unlock on the second trading day after Q3 results.
  • Starlink’s carrier path got messier. Ukraine headlines weighed on sentiment, while T-Mobile’s public positioning cooled.

The key synthesis

The growth evidence has improved enough to preserve the target. The financing evidence has worsened enough to prevent a conviction upgrade. SPCX now needs operating delivery and capital discipline simultaneously.

04 · Operating engine

Three growth platforms, each with a different proof burden.

Space

Flight 14 reached orbit and deployed 26 V3 satellites despite one post-separation engine failure. Flight 15, expected around mid-November, is intended to attempt a ship catch. An operational KSC Starship launch is targeted for October 30.

Connectivity

Starlink is near 13 million subscribers. Q3 revenue is estimated around $4.8 billion, up roughly 12% from Q2. Subscriber growth is strong, but geographic mix drove consumer ARPU down to $66 from $85 year over year.

AI

Google payments began at $920 million per month, and Anthropic committed up to $84.5 billion through May 2029. Both contracts carry 90-day mutual cancellation clauses, so “contracted” must not be read as guaranteed.

Starlink Mobile gets strategic

The October 8 Grain Management agreement adds up to 14 MHz of paired 800 MHz low-band spectrum, subject to FCC approval. Combined with earlier spectrum and the planned direct-to-device constellation, it creates a credible path toward a fourth US carrier. The price was not disclosed by Grain; a widely reported $8 billion figure remains secondary.

Strategic does not mean near-term

The network build still requires satellites, regulatory approvals and potentially $50–130 billion of terrestrial capital under Bernstein’s estimate. Observers do not expect full nationwide service before 2035. T-Mobile’s cooling posture adds distribution uncertainty.

05 · Capital & credit

The financing plan can fund the upside—and transfer value away from equity.

The proposed $40 billion debt package is the most consequential change since initiation. It could secure the compute needed for Starmind, but it would also deepen fixed claims on a business already spending far ahead of internally generated cash.

What would break the equity case

A financing that raises the cost of capital faster than contracted AI revenue raises durable cash generation. The five-year CDS spread already reached a record. Until pricing, covenants and maturity structure are known, target upside must be discounted by capital-structure uncertainty.

07 · Risk register

What can invalidate the call.

High impact

Debt outruns durable cash generation

The reported financing would push total debt above $79 billion while capex already dwarfs operating cash flow. Credit-spread widening can compress the equity multiple before any operational miss.

High impact

Contracted AI demand proves cancellable

Google and Anthropic both carry 90-day mutual cancellation clauses. Capacity built against those commitments could become stranded or underutilized.

High impact

Share unlock becomes persistent supply

Another 1.31 billion shares are due to unlock shortly after Q3 results. The stock’s roughly 9% weekly decline despite positive news suggests supply is already shaping the tape.

Medium–high

Starship recovery slips

Flight 14’s orbital success was meaningful, but reusable economics still depend on catching and reflying both ship and booster. Flight 15 is the next technical gate.

Medium–high

Starlink Mobile becomes capital-intensive telecom

Spectrum improves strategic control, but satellite deployment, FCC approval and a potentially massive terrestrial build could consume more cash before the service scales.

Medium

Political and partner friction compounds

Ukraine-related service headlines and T-Mobile’s cooling posture can affect government trust, carrier distribution and investor sentiment even without an immediate revenue hit.
