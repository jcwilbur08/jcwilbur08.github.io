Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

IJH is the default mid-cap index vehicle for a reason. It tracks the S&P MidCap 400 — the only major U.S. mid-cap index whose members must pass a profitability test (positive trailing four-quarter earnings and a positive most recent quarter) before a committee ever admits them. That screen systematically keeps the unprofitable tail out of the portfolio, which is the structural reason the S&P 400 has historically compounded at a premium to the purely rules-based Russell Midcap alternative. At a 0.05% expense ratio, roughly $122 billion in assets, and a 26-year operating history since May 2000, IJH is the largest and most liquid mid-cap ETF in existence.

Research posture: HOLD | Conviction 6/10

At $73.39, IJH sits about 6.8% below its August 52-week high and is up roughly 12% year-to-date on a NAV total-return basis. Nothing fundamental changed in seven days — the price slipped about one percent and the ruler changed. For funds, "upside" is the honest expected excess return versus the benchmark: by construction that number is approximately zero, so the rating anchors on vehicle conviction. The vehicle deserves a genuine 6 — but no more, because an index fund cannot beat its own index by enough to earn a Buy. Action: none. Hold the 271.576-share position as the mid-cap vehicle in its account.

Re-rating box: KEEP → Hold (new conviction-anchored scale). The September 27 legacy KEEP translates to the new scale's Hold via the scale's second prong — higher conviction with insufficient upside. Conviction on the vehicle is a real 6/10: the profitability screen is a genuine structural edge, 0.05% is among the cheapest fees in the category, tracking is surgical, and the position has compounded 329% on Cam's $17.11 average cost. But the second prong of the new scale — expected upside — cannot be met by an index fund: its job is to match its benchmark, not beat it. Buy requires 6–7 conviction and at least 10% expected upside; Reduce requires conviction 3. Neither describes this fund. This is the canonical "hold-and-collect" case the new scale was written to describe. No change in fundamental view — only the ruler changed.

01 | Objective

Objective & index tracked

IJH seeks investment results that correspond generally, before fees and expenses, to the price and yield performance of the S&P MidCap 400 Index (reported) — the mid-capitalization tier of the U.S. equity market.

Index ruleDetailBasis
Size / scope~400 U.S. mid-cap equities; the fund holds ~416 namesreported
SelectionCommittee-selected for liquidity, industry-group representation, and financial viability — not purely rules-basedreported
Profitability requirementPositive sum of trailing four quarters' GAAP earnings and positive most recent quarter's earnings — the key quality screenreported
WeightingFloat-adjusted market-cap weightedreported
ReplicationFund invests at least 90% of assets in index securities (hybrid sampling)reported
InceptionMay 22, 2000 (fund); first trade May 26, 2000reported
Why the profitability screen matters. The Russell Midcap (tracked by Cam's FSMDX) is purely rules-based and will admit unprofitable companies; the S&P 400 will not. Over full cycles that filter has produced a persistent quality premium — the index owns fewer story stocks and fewer blowups. It is the single most important difference between the two mid-cap funds in Cam's book, and the reason each account keeps its own vehicle rather than being consolidated.

What the record says

The quality screen has historically compounded at a premium to the Russell Midcap alternative over full cycles — fewer unprofitable members means fewer blowups. Tracking is surgical: multi-year NAV returns sit within basis points of the index, so every dollar of gross return reaches the shareholder minus 5 bp.

The honest discount

Mid-caps have trailed large-cap growth for years — 10-year IJH is ~3 points a year behind the S&P 500. That gap is the diversification price, not a defect: it is precisely why a retirement book holds mid-caps at all. Anyone expecting IJH to beat the large-cap book is underwriting the wrong fund.

September context. Mid-caps came under pressure in September as the Fed hiked 25 bps (first since 2023) and the 10-year pushed toward 5.3% — small and mid-caps carry more floating-rate debt than the S&P 500, so the rate path matters more here than it does for Cam's large-cap book. The fund weathered it; the YTD total return stayed positive and the tracking stayed tight.

07 | Risks

Risks / what could change the verdict

Could push conviction down
  • Index-methodology change: if S&P removed the profitability screen, IJH's one structural edge disappears — re-underwrite immediately
  • Fee hike or tracking deterioration: either would violate the terms of the hold; a sustained multi-year tracking error above ~15 bp would force a switch to a competitor
  • Account restructuring: if the two retirement accounts were ever combined, the mid-cap duplication (IJH vs FSMDX) would need a genuine winner — on construction, IJH would be the survivor
Could push conviction up
  • Mid-cap regime change: a sustained mid-cap earnings cycle (cheaper valuations + the Fed easing path resuming) would make the sleeve's diversifying role more valuable — but it would not change the rating, because an index fund still cannot clear the Buy upside prong
  • Peer degradation: FSMDX adopting a worse index or raising fees would widen IJH's relative edge

Note the discipline of the new scale here: even a glowing regime-change scenario does not flip this to Buy, because the upside prong measures expected excess return — which an index fund cannot promise. The rating stays honest about what an index fund is.

Glossary

S&P MidCap 400 — float-adjusted market-cap-weighted index of ~400 U.S. mid-cap equities with a committee profitability requirement. Russell Midcap — purely rules-based index of the 800 smallest companies in the Russell 1000. Tracking difference — the gap between fund and index total returns, annualized. Hybrid sampling — the fund holds a representative sample of index securities rather than all of them. Creation/redemption — the in-kind ETF share mechanism that keeps turnover and tax drag low.
