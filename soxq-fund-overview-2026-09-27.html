1. Executive Summary & Verdict

SOXQ is the cheapest broad semiconductor benchmark vehicle available: a 0.19% expense ratio (reported) tracking the industry-standard PHLX Semiconductor Sector Index — the 30 largest US-listed semiconductor companies — against 0.35% for both direct rivals SOXX and SMH (reported). It carries a 5-star Morningstar fund rating (reported) and a top Zacks ETF timeliness rank. The 1-year total return is ~99% (reported), and the fund has roughly tripled since inception in June 2021.

The catch is sizing, not quality: 28 shares, ~$2,791, 0.16% of the portfolio (calculated). At this weight the fund is decorative — it cannot move the book. And it duplicates Cam's ~$92,400 direct chip sleeve (NVDA, AVGO, TSM, QCOM, ASML — calculated), with roughly a third of the ETF's assets sitting in those same five names.

Bottom line — KEEP. This is the right fund for semiconductor breadth at the lowest fee in its class. The verdict is on the vehicle, not the size: the honest prescription is to either build it into a real satellite (~1% of the book) or fold it into the direct chip names — keeping a 0.16% placeholder adds complexity without impact. Do not replace it with SOXX or SMH: both charge nearly double the fee for a similar 30-stock exposure.
Portfolio note: The duplication concern is real but bounded. About two-thirds of SOXQ (estimated) is in names Cam does not own directly — Micron, Intel, Marvell, Texas Instruments, Analog Devices, the equipment makers (Applied Materials, Lam, KLA) — which is exactly the diversification a 5-stock direct chip sleeve lacks. If the position were folded into NVDA/AVGO, Cam would lose that equipment-and-memory tail. The fund earns its place as breadth; it just needs a size that matters.

2. Objective & Index Tracked

The fund's objective is to track the investment results of the PHLX Semiconductor Sector Index (SOX) before fees and expenses (reported, per Invesco). The index is designed to measure the performance of the 30 largest US-listed securities of companies engaged in the semiconductor business — design, distribution, manufacture, and sale of semiconductors, including memory, microprocessors, integrated circuits, and related equipment (reported).

Key index mechanics (reported): the fund normally invests at least 90% of assets in index constituents; the index is reconstituted annually in September (most recently September 18, 2026) and rebalanced quarterly (March, June, September, December). The weighting is tiered/modified market-cap — the largest names are capped, which is why NVDA's ~14% weight here is well below its ~19% weight in the market-cap-heavier SMH. The fund is classified non-diversified (reported).

8. Risks / What Could Change the Verdict

  • Pure sector concentration. 100% Technology, beta 1.85, non-diversified — in a semiconductor downturn this sleeve falls hardest (the 2022 drawdown and the ~14% pullback from the 52-week high are the template).
  • NVDA concentration. At ~14% post-reconstitution, the ETF increasingly mirrors Cam's direct NVDA — the diversification benefit shrinks as the top weight grows.
  • Cyclicality. Memory pricing, foundry utilization, and capex cycles drive the non-AI two-thirds of the index; an AI-capex pause would hit both the direct names and this fund simultaneously.
  • SOXX's performance edge. If the ICE-index rival keeps outperforming the PHLX index by double digits, the "cheapest fee" argument weakens — fees are certain, tracking differences compound.
  • Small-fund liquidity. $3.2B AUM vs $48B+ for the rivals; adequate at current size, but spreads could widen in a stress event.

A REPLACE trigger would be: a cheaper PHLX-SOX tracker launching, or SOXX/SMH cutting fees below 0.19% — neither has happened. A TRIM/fold trigger is simpler and nearer: if Cam decides the five direct names are sufficient chip exposure, folding this $2,791 into NVDA/AVGO loses only the memory/analog/equipment tail — a defensible simplification. No trigger is firing today.

Appendix B — Glossary
