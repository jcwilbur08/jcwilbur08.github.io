02 | Why the CRUSH rule matters

Confirmed regulatory proposal

  • CMS issued CMS-6098-P on November 16, 2026.
  • For Medicare Advantage risk adjustment, CMS proposes to exclude a fee-for-service adjuster, set sampling methodology, and extrapolate audit findings starting with payment year 2018.
  • CMS estimates approximately $9 billion in MA overpayment recoveries per payment year, across the industry, subject to methodology and implementation.
  • The broader rule package cites nearly $90B federal savings over 2027–2036 and requires comments by Jan. 19, 2027.

Why equity holders care

  • One-time liability: historical reimbursements already recognized could face cash recoveries or litigation contingencies.
  • Recurring economics: stricter diagnosis support can lower risk-adjusted revenue, increase compliance cost, and dampen cash earnings.
  • Uncertain enforcement: actual audit samples, appeals, documentation quality, timing and company-level share are not disclosed.
  • Litigation path: prior RADV policy has been contested; the proposed replacement may still be modified or challenged.
Critical guardrail: CMS's approximately $9B per payment year is an agency estimate for all Medicare Advantage plans, not a $9B bill to UNH and not a currently accrued UNH loss. It would be incorrect to multiply that figure by nine years and treat the result as an adjudicated liability. There is no sourced company-level RADV liability quantification in this report.

What the market is pricing

The equity decline likely reflects a combination of retroactive clawback concern, a higher discount rate to uncertain Medicare earnings and a lower willingness to assign UNH its historic premium multiple. Conversely, rigorous enforcement could also reduce illegitimate coding-based competition and eventually improve system confidence. Both possible outcomes matter; the legal and operating path remains unproven.

06 | Catalysts, contrary case and thesis-break tests

Near-term checkpoints

  • Immediately: comments by CMS, insurer trade groups, management and legal experts on retroactive recoveries and legal basis.
  • Dec. 7, 2026: MA annual enrollment window closes; watch product mix, county exits, pricing and retention.
  • Jan. 19, 2027: CMS CRUSH comment deadline, with possible revisions thereafter.
  • January 2027 (date unconfirmed): Q4 print and first formal FY27 outlook; compare cash economics with reserve-neutral MCR.
  • Q1 2027: test 2027 MA membership, Optum profit quality, and any disclosed audit accounting.

Strongest contrary case

  • Risk-adjustment revenues and historical payments may be less defensible than investors previously thought.
  • Bad outcomes may include both historical cash recovery and recurring reimbursement compression.
  • Increased medical utilization and smaller member base may mean EPS consensus is too high.
  • Prior regulatory reviews and company-sponsored audits do not resolve final government determinations.
  • At depressed prices, severe legal uncertainty can overwhelm nominal forward P/E cheapness.

Explicit thesis-break / mandatory-review conditions

Proposed, not adjudicated. Trigger immediate re-underwriting if: (1) management quantifies a company-specific incremental RADV cash exposure or contingent liability incompatible with the base case (illustrative present value >$12B); (2) two comparable quarters show reserve-adjusted MCR ≥89.5% despite 2027 repricing; (3) 2027 adjusted EPS is guided below $21 or cut ≥10% for sustained underwriting reasons; (4) Optum Health margin stays ≤1% for two comparable quarters after nonrecurring effects; or (5) material 2027 enrollment attrition occurs without a measurable margin offset. A proposed rule alone is not a realized loss.

Questions the next disclosure must answer

  1. Which years, contracts, and coding methodologies are exposed to the proposed extrapolation, and how would legal rights apply?
  2. What prospective price/benefit adjustments can mitigate recurring risk-adjustment pressure?
  3. How much of the company's recent MCR improvement survives removal of favorable reserve development?
  4. Can consolidated cash flow and capital returns withstand an adverse audit scenario?
  5. How quickly can audited medical-documentation support be shown, independently, across the MA book?
