EPS-quality bridge: the market should prioritize operating income

Q3 2026 GAAP EPS was $1.89 against $1.95 a year earlier, but Q3 2025 contained $9.5B in pretax Anthropic investment gains. Current quarter non-operating income (expense) was a net −$1.2B, versus +$10.7B a year earlier. The difference is approximately $12B pretax and overwhelms the $10.7B year-over-year growth in operating profit. The apparent EPS contraction is not evidence of deteriorating core operations. A fully tax-normalized quarterly EPS bridge is not defensible without item-by-item tax treatment. 2026 YTD net income also includes unusual investment effects, so trailing P/E is a poor standalone valuation anchor. 03 / Capital allocation, funding and the FCF problem

$168.3B+29% year-over-year
$210.7B+82% year-over-year
$114.2BCash + marketable securities
$173.1Bversus $65.6B at Dec 2025

Uncomfortable fact: Amazon's operating cash generation is rising, but its capital program has outrun it. Company-defined trailing FCF was −$42.36B, worsening from −$7.60B one quarter earlier. The cash outflow is growth investment, not the same as operating losses, but it still consumes funding and is sensitive to demand forecasts, financing costs and future depreciation. [IR]

Funding discipline checkpoint. Balance-sheet cash and marketable securities were ~$114.2B and long-term debt ~$173.1B at September 30. Simple net debt for our model is ~$58.9B; it excludes $98.8B long-term lease liabilities and other obligations. We do not present this simplified bridge as comprehensive net leverage. The increase in gross debt and investment-related assets warrants separate work on financing terms and the accounting treatment of investment marks. [IR]

Cash capital expenditures expected at ~$220B during 2026 are an extraordinary burden even for Amazon's scale. A sustainable return case requires more than bookings: deployable megawatts, actual paid utilization, pricing, operating margin after depreciation, renewals, and a credible future reduction in capex as a share of revenue.

Reverse DCF: what today's stock price requires

Keeping the base-case 2027–30 FCF path (−$20B, +$20B, +$65B, +$120B), 8.7% discount rate and 3.5% terminal growth, the $243.79 regular-session price requires approximately $190B of 2031 annual FCF, versus our $185B base-case assumption. This is roughly 2.6% above our terminal-year base forecast. The after-hours mark implies a lower hurdle; both depend on the same strong terminal assumptions.

DCF mechanics and important limitations

Enterprise value = Σ(FY2027–31 free cash flows / (1+WACC)^t) + [FY2031 FCF × (1+terminal growth) / (WACC−terminal growth)] / (1+WACC)^5. Equity value subtracts approximately $58.9B net debt (reported long-term debt less cash and marketable securities) and divides by 11.034B shares including stock-based awards outstanding. The FCF series uses cash-from-operations less net PP&E spend as a pragmatic firm-level cash proxy, with no explicit reconciliation of interest, leases, working capital or debt maturity schedule. Valuation confidence: low to moderate, intentionally shown as a broad range rather than a precise target. Market cap/EV multiples to peers are not directly comparable without updated consensus metrics and investment-adjusted cash flows.

07 / Catalysts, falsifiers, sector read-through and open questions

Next checkpoints

  • Q4 2026 results (date not yet verified): sales vs $240–247B, operating income vs $28.5–33.5B, and high-teens advertising-growth expectation.
  • 2027 management capital plan: cash capex run rate, data-center delivery, leases/debt funding and depreciation trajectory.
  • AWS conversion: backlog growth and realization, reserve-to-revenue lag, actual AI inference monetization and 2027–28 >40% management growth claim.
  • Each quarter: paid unit growth and 3P seller mix; retail margins; gross/lease-adjusted leverage; TTM FCF trend.

Explicit thesis-break conditions

  • AWS below 20% growth for two consecutive quarters while cloud market growth is substantially faster and share loss is verified.
  • AWS margin below 25% in two quarters without credible ramp/depreciation explanation.
  • FCF materially negative through FY2027 with no financeable normalization path or materially underutilized AI infrastructure.
  • Financing costs or dilution undermine the economics of growth; worsening leverage at unchanged cash generation.
  • Retail margin below 4% alongside sustained ad/merchant monetization erosion.

Cross-sector implications

Cloud competitors (MSFT, GOOGL): AWS acceleration and a large reported backlog suggest the AI infrastructure demand pool is still expanding, but this does not independently prove share wins. Chip/foundry (NVDA, AVGO, TSM, ASML): Amazon's huge infrastructure budget is a supportive demand signal, tempered by possible custom silicon substitution and eventual capex digestion. Power, equipment and data-center infrastructure: capacity reservations and energy supply constraints make power delivery and grid infrastructure critical to realized cloud revenue. These are interpretations of Amazon's disclosed spending and demand, not measured revenue forecasts for individual peers.

Outstanding diligence questions

  • Full October 29 call transcript with named analyst questions, executive responses, and any capex nuance that changes $220B expectations.
  • Reported $578B backlog: accounting definition, cancellation terms, geographic commitments, deployment schedule, and cash-yield evidence.
  • Why balance-sheet investment assets and other liabilities increased sharply, and normalized EPS after fair-value changes.
  • Independent post-print FY2027 and FY2028 estimates; model should be refreshed after sell-side revisions stabilize.
  • Detailed 2027–31 cash-capex / operating-cash / depreciation / lease-adjusted FCFF bridge, rather than the proxy DCF above.
