Executive Summary

Johnson & Johnson is emerging from the STELARA patent cliff with better underlying growth than the headline erosion suggests. Q2 2026 sales reached $25.31 billion (+6.6% reported; +5.6% operational), while management raised full-year guidance to approximately $101.1 billion of reported sales and $11.68 of adjusted EPS at the midpoint. Innovative Medicine remains the primary growth engine, led by DARZALEX, CARVYKTI, RYBREVANT/LAZCLUZE, TREMFYA, SPRAVATO and CAPLYTA; MedTech adds diversification but is currently growing more slowly. At the August 7 close of $259.24, JNJ trades at roughly 22.2x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance and yields about 2.1% on the $5.36 annualized dividend. The company is high quality, but the rerating has reduced the margin of safety.

Recommendation & Conviction

HOLD / WATCH FOR ENTRY — Conviction 7/10. The fundamental thesis is constructive: JNJ has a durable healthcare franchise, improving pipeline breadth, strong cash generation and one of the market's most reliable dividend records. However, at roughly 22x current-year adjusted EPS, the market is already assigning meaningful credit for successful post-STELARA replacement growth and partial normalization of litigation risk. A more attractive risk/reward would emerge on a valuation reset or if earnings growth accelerates enough to grow into the multiple.

Business & Industry Position

Following the Kenvue separation, JNJ is a focused healthcare company with two segments. 2025 Innovative Medicine sales were $60.4 billion and MedTech sales were $33.8 billion. Innovative Medicine concentrates on oncology, immunology and neuroscience, while MedTech spans cardiovascular, surgery, orthopaedics and vision. The combination creates unusual diversification across pharmaceuticals and medical devices, while still retaining significant exposure to product cycles, reimbursement, patent expirations and regulatory outcomes.

Moat & Competitive Advantages

JNJ's moat rests on scale in R&D, global commercialization, regulatory expertise, physician and hospital relationships, intellectual property, manufacturing know-how and a broad portfolio that can absorb individual product failures. In pharmaceuticals, the key test is whether pipeline productivity consistently offsets loss of exclusivity. In MedTech, installed bases, clinician familiarity and procedure ecosystems create switching frictions. The moat is strong, but not static: biosimilars, drug-pricing pressure and device innovation require continuous reinvestment.

Financial Quality

2025 segment sales totaled $94.2 billion, with Innovative Medicine generating $22.3 billion of segment income before tax and MedTech $4.1 billion. In the first half of 2026, companywide sales were $49.37 billion, up 8.2% reported. Q2 adjusted net earnings were $7.08 billion and adjusted EPS was $2.90. Estimated first-half free cash flow was approximately $8.7 billion. Management's July guidance implies 2026 reported sales growth of about 7.3% and adjusted EPS growth of about 8.2% at the midpoint.

Recent Earnings — Q2 2026

Q2 was a solid confirmation quarter. Innovative Medicine sales were $16.38 billion (+6.8% operational), despite a roughly 760-basis-point STELARA drag. Growth came from DARZALEX, CARVYKTI, TECVAYLI, RYBREVANT/LAZCLUZE, TREMFYA, SPRAVATO and CAPLYTA. MedTech sales were $8.93 billion (+3.6% operational), a respectable but less compelling pace. The raised outlook is important because it suggests the portfolio is absorbing the largest patent headwind while maintaining mid-single-digit-plus operating growth.

Pipeline & Catalysts

The 2026 pipeline calendar includes additional opportunities for TREMFYA, icotrokinra, nipocalimab, DARZALEX, TECVAYLI, ERLEADA and bleximenib. Near-term upside depends on continued oncology and immunology momentum, successful CAPLYTA integration and expansion, acceleration in MedTech, and execution on robotic surgery and cardiovascular platforms. A sustained pattern of 5–7% operational sales growth after STELARA would materially strengthen the long-duration thesis.

Dividend & Capital Allocation

JNJ raised its quarterly dividend 3.1% in April 2026 to $1.34, or $5.36 annualized, marking its 64th consecutive annual increase. At $259.24, the indicated yield is approximately 2.1%. The dividend is a major quality signal but current income is not unusually high at today's valuation. Capital allocation must balance the dividend, R&D, acquisitions, MedTech investment, litigation payments and balance-sheet flexibility.

Litigation

The talc overhang has improved but is not fully extinguished. In July, JNJ agreed to an estimated $5.5 billion settlement covering roughly 76,000 ovarian-cancer claims, subject to 95% claimant participation. The agreement could ultimately cost more depending on participation and does not cover future claims. A recent federal-court development also raised the evidentiary bar for many plaintiffs. The proposed settlement reduces a major uncertainty discount, but investors should not model litigation risk at zero.

Valuation

At the August 7 close of $259.24 and the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($11.68), JNJ trades at approximately 22.2x 2026 adjusted EPS. That is a premium valuation for a company currently targeting high-single-digit adjusted EPS growth. The premium can be justified by business quality, durability, dividend consistency and improving pipeline visibility, but it limits upside if growth merely meets guidance. Valuation is the primary reason the stance is Hold rather than Add.

Bull / Base / Bear Framework

BULL: post-STELARA growth accelerates as TREMFYA, oncology and neuroscience outperform; MedTech improves; litigation uncertainty fades. A durable high-single-digit EPS CAGR supports a premium multiple. BASE: JNJ compounds revenue in the mid-single digits and EPS in the mid-to-high single digits, with dividend growth adding modest income; returns increasingly depend on earnings growth rather than multiple expansion. BEAR: pipeline launches disappoint, MedTech stays sluggish, pricing/patent pressure intensifies or litigation costs re-expand; a valuation compression compounds weaker earnings growth.

What Is Working

• Innovative Medicine is growing through a major STELARA headwind.

• Oncology, immunology and neuroscience franchises provide multiple independent growth drivers.

• 2026 sales and EPS guidance were raised after Q2.

• The dividend record and cash generation remain exceptional.

• The proposed talc settlement could materially reduce a long-standing uncertainty discount.

What Is Weakening / Watch Items

• MedTech operational growth of 3.6% in Q2 trails the pharmaceutical segment.

• STELARA erosion remains significant even as the portfolio offsets it.

• The share-price rerating has pushed the earnings multiple to roughly 22x 2026 guidance.

• Talc risk is reduced, not eliminated; the settlement has participation conditions and excludes future claims.

• Sustaining premium valuation requires consistent pipeline conversion and margin discipline.

Thesis-Break Conditions

The thesis would materially weaken if: (1) organic/operational growth settles below roughly 4% after the STELARA transition; (2) multiple major pipeline assets fail or leading growth franchises decelerate simultaneously; (3) MedTech remains structurally low-growth despite investment and acquisitions; (4) litigation liabilities materially exceed current expectations; (5) adjusted EPS growth persistently lags revenue growth without a credible investment-driven explanation; or (6) capital allocation meaningfully weakens the balance sheet or dividend trajectory.

Investment Thesis

JNJ is a high-quality healthcare compounder transitioning from dependence on legacy blockbusters toward a broader set of oncology, immunology, neuroscience and MedTech growth platforms. Q2 2026 provides evidence that the transition is working: operational sales rose 5.6% despite substantial STELARA erosion and management raised guidance. The central investment debate is no longer whether JNJ can survive the patent cliff, but whether its new growth engines can sustain enough high-single-digit earnings growth to justify a low-20s earnings multiple. The answer is plausible, but at $259.24 the valuation leaves less room for execution mistakes. Therefore the appropriate posture is HOLD / WATCH FOR ENTRY rather than aggressive accumulation.

Portfolio Fit

JNJ fits best as a defensive, lower-beta healthcare compounder and dividend-growth anchor rather than a high-growth return engine. It can improve sector diversification and earnings resilience in a growth-oriented retirement portfolio. The current valuation argues for disciplined sizing and patience rather than treating historical brand quality alone as a reason to buy at any price.
