Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

The thesis got better and the price got cheaper. Since September 22, Broadcom's FY2027 AI revenue outlook was reaffirmed at $115B (raised from "over $100B"), CEO Hock Tan publicly held the line on a combined ~$345B FY2027+FY2028 AI trajectory against the AI-slowdown narrative, and the Singapore substrate plant opened to attack the supply bottlenecks constraining accelerator shipments. Meanwhile the stock drifted 3.5% lower to $351.19, now 29% below its June high and trading below both major moving averages. The one genuinely new risk is China's SASAC reviewing Broadcom switch usage in state-controlled data centers — stock-taking for now, but a real overhang to monitor.

Research posture: BUY | $535 target (raised from $505) | Add gate: $340

The target raise reflects the raised FY2027 AI outlook: $535 = ~27.5x FY2027 consensus EPS of $19.50, a modest multiple discount to today's ~34x FY2026 multiple as earnings compound ~88%. That implies ~52% upside from $351.19. The add gate is $340, just below the late-September lows — a staged add toward ~3% portfolio weight on weakness without thesis damage. A break below $320 on fundamental news (a Q4 guide-down, China escalation, or AI-capex crack) is a reassess, not a buy.

01 | News Flow

What changed since September 22

The news flow was operationally positive and sentimentally mixed — new demand signals and supply relief on one side, a new China overhang and weak technicals on the other.

Sep 21 — Bernstein reaffirms

Bernstein reiterated its Buy rating with a $575 target — the latest sell-side action found in the research window. No rating or target changes were found strictly within September 22–30.

Sep 23 — China SASAC review

China's SASAC examined Broadcom switch usage in state-controlled data centers as part of a domestic-semiconductor push; preliminary findings suggest Broadcom switches may be present in up to 90% of SOEs. Described as a stock-taking exercise, not a ban — but informal scale-back recommendations could follow. This is the single most important new risk item. Networking is roughly 40% of AI semiconductor revenue, and China exposure was not previously a live overhang.

Sep 23 — Samueli 10b5-1 sale

Co-founder and director Henry Samueli sold 702,190 shares (~$250M) under a Rule 10b5-1 plan adopted in December 2025 — about 0.84% of his 83.4M-share base. Scheduled selling; read as noise, not a fundamental signal.

Sep 23–24 — VMware SMB mea culpa

Broadcom executives publicly acknowledged missteps with small and mid-size customers around subscription bundling and the VCF push, promising an updated vSphere Standard with details at VMware Explore Frankfurt in October. Honest, and necessary — the channel relationship is the VMware franchise's soft underbelly.

Sep 24–29 — Channel shock

Broadcom stripped Pinnacle partner Insight Enterprises of VMware license-resale and Broadcom-funded VCF services authorization in North America (Insight serves clients through October 31). Partners called the move rare and surprising — more evidence the VMware go-to-market is still being rebuilt.

Sep 28–29 — Singapore substrate plant

Broadcom and TOPPAN opened their joint-venture FC-BGA substrate factory in Singapore (~95,000 sqm, mass production by end of 2026, 300+ staff). Substrates are a named bottleneck for AI accelerators and networking switches — this is supply relief with a direct line to shipment capacity.

Sep 29 — JPMorgan AI-trade note

A JPMorgan note arguing the AI trade could recover from its pullback sparked a 3.2% intraday spike to $360.53; the stock closed up 1.6% at $355.10 — then gave it back the next day in a weak tape.

Week of Sep 21 — Slowdown narrative rebutted

Anthropic's CEO pushed an AI-"slowdown" narrative; Hock Tan publicly held firm on the ~$345B combined FY2027+FY2028 AI revenue outlook. When the CEO of your largest 2027–28 XPU customer questions the cycle and your CEO doesn't blink, that is a data point worth weighting.

Net assessment. Nothing in the window undermines the AI revenue trajectory; the China review is the one item that could, if it escalates. The VMware channel noise is real but second-order — infrastructure software is 30% of revenue growing at 29% with ~93% gross margins, and the franchise risk is reputational, not yet financial.

Networking: the other half of the AI story

AI networking — Tomahawk 6 switches, Jericho 4 Ethernet fabric routers, co-packaged optics — is roughly 40% of AI semiconductor revenue. This is the business the China SASAC review touches, which is why the review matters more than its "stock-taking" label suggests. Management has named the execution bottlenecks explicitly: HBM, substrates, leading-edge wafers, data-center readiness, power. The Singapore substrate plant is a direct answer to one of them.

The bear case on the ramp, stated fairly. Custom-silicon competition is real: Marvell signed a ~$120B Google agreement with a 59M-share warrant kicker, MediaTek won a Google training chip, and OpenAI disclosed a Samsung partnership. The $115B→$230B trajectory assumes Broadcom holds share while hyperscalers actively diversify suppliers. Execution — not demand — is the risk, and it is concentrated in exactly the customers the bears name.

VMware: accelerating, but the channel is strained

The numbers accelerated sharply; the relationships did not. Both are true, and the numbers matter more — for now.

The in-window developments are all on the relationship side: the Insight Enterprises deauthorization, the public SMB mea culpa, and a Gartner assessment noting that the VMware deal has reinvigorated server-virtualization competition, with enterprises reassessing long-term commitments. None of it has shown up in the financials yet — but a channel in open revolt is a leading indicator worth tracking into the Frankfurt Explore event in October, where the vSphere Standard refresh lands.

Framing. VMware is ~30% of revenue at ~93% gross margin — it is the profit stabilizer while AI hardware scales. The bull case needs it to keep growing at double digits; the bear case needs the partner backlash to turn into churn. Watch the October Frankfurt announcements and the Q4 software growth rate for the tiebreaker.

07 | Risks & Catalysts

Risks and catalysts into year-end

Key risks
  • China SASAC review. The new one: escalation from stock-taking to SOE scale-back would hit networking (~40% of AI revenue).
  • AI-capex durability. A digestion pause after the 2024–26 buildout would undercut the $115B→$230B ramp.
  • Customer concentration. Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta — four names carry the trajectory.
  • Custom-silicon share. Marvell's Google deal and MediaTek's training-chip win show the moat is contested.
  • Margin dilution. Gross margin guided 75% → 73% on XPU mix; op leverage must keep offsetting it.
  • VMware channel. Partner revolt is a leading indicator; watch Frankfurt and Q4 software growth.
Catalysts
  • FQ4 earnings ~Dec 9 — the key event: $21.7B AI quarter and FY2027 ramp confirmation.
  • Meta MTIA production shipments in Q4 — first volume for a new XPU customer.
  • VMware Explore Frankfurt (Oct) — vSphere Standard refresh; channel temperature check.
  • Hyperscaler Q3 capex prints — durability read-through for the whole AI complex.
  • December dividend declaration — potential 16th consecutive annual raise.
  • $10B buyback renewal — authorization expires Dec 31.
