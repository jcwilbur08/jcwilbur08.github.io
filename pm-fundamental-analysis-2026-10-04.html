Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Philip Morris is compounding like a consumer-growth franchise while the market still prices it like a tobacco annuity. Seven days after initiation, nothing fundamental has changed: the reference price slipped 1.6% to $187.46, almost entirely the October 2 ex-dividend effect on the newly raised $1.60 quarterly payout. The Q2 print — first quarter ever above $11 billion in revenue, smoke-free at ~42% of sales growing 11.8% organically, adjusted EPS of $2.20 beating by $0.15 — stands unchallenged. The next quantified checkpoint is Q3 earnings, expected the week of October 20.

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

The mix shift is the entire investment case

Smoke-free was 41.5% of full-year 2025 net revenue and ~42% in Q2 2026, growing 11.8% organically against a combustible base growing ~6% on pricing. At these rates the crossover to a majority-smoke-free revenue base is a matter of a few years. Each point of mix shift is margin-accretive: international smoke-free gross margin is 70.1%, the highest in the portfolio.

2

Two under-monetized U.S. options are priced as optionality, not as value

ZYN ULTRA (FDA-authorized August 2026, 20+ SKUs) is ramping in the world’s most profitable nicotine market, and IQOS ILUMA awaits its FDA PMTA decision with management “launch-ready.” The $210 target requires neither to succeed — they are free call options on 2028–2030 earnings power.

3

The cash machine funds everything at once

Guided 2026 operating cash flow of ~$13.5 billion covers the ~$10 billion annualized dividend bill (calculated on ~1.56 billion shares) with room to spare, while simultaneously funding the Swedish Match deleveraging. The dividend raise to $6.40 annualized was the board’s vote of confidence in that arithmetic.

4

The market still applies a tobacco discount to a growth business

Consensus “Moderate Buy” with a ~$207 average target implies the street sees roughly fair value; the differentiated view is that a company approaching majority smoke-free revenue with double-digit organic growth in its growth segment deserves to hold a premium-staples multiple rather than drift back toward one. That multiple defense — not expansion — is the base case.

Why this is Buy, not Strong Buy. Strong Buy requires conviction 8–10 AND at least 15% upside. This clears neither prong: conviction is 6 and price upside is ~12%. The business quality argues for more, but a 22x entry multiple, a ~77% payout ratio, recurring FX trims to guidance, and two near-term binaries (the October 20 print, ILUMA timing) keep this a disciplined Buy — sized as a compounder, not chased as a momentum name.

03 | Segments

Portfolio: smoke-free, combustibles, and the U.S.

International smoke-free — the crown jewel

Revenue of $3.88 billion (+14.2%, +11.8% organic) on 8.0% volume growth, with a 70.1% gross profit margin — software-like margins on a third of total revenue. IQOS heated-tobacco shipments grew 7.6%, holding roughly three-quarters volume share of the global category, with European momentum offsetting drags from Japan’s excise increase and Poland’s flavor restrictions.

International combustibles — the funded decline

Revenue of $6.46 billion (+9.8% reported, +6.4% organic) — growing revenue in a declining-volume business, the defining trick of tobacco economics. Cigarette volumes are guided down 2–3% for the full year; pricing and share gains (Marlboro; Turkey, Indonesia, Egypt) more than offset it. A duration asset, not a growth asset: multi-year, high-margin cash that funds the transition and the dividend.

United States — ZYN carries, IQOS waits

U.S. revenue of $856 million (−0.7%) — the decline entirely in legacy cigars and wellness products. The asset that matters, ZYN, grew shipments 1.8% to 2.9 billion pouches against tough comparisons. PMI runs IQOS in the U.S. directly since the Altria license ended in April 2024 and has invested heavily in American manufacturing and distribution. Management’s framing stands: the U.S. is the largest unaddressed growth opportunity in the portfolio.

ZYN: the U.S. growth engine

ZYN holds roughly 70% of the U.S. nicotine-pouch category and was the first pouch family ever to receive FDA modified-risk authorization (20 variants). The 2026 playbook is premiumization and share defense: ZYN ULTRA launched with FDA marketing authorization in August 2026 — 20+ SKUs at higher nicotine strengths (9mg, 11mg) in a moist format, moving to 20-pouch cans — narrowing ZYN’s historical price premium versus competitors while expanding the premium tier. ZYN is now in 60 markets internationally.

What to watch on ZYN: competition from BAT’s Velo and Altria’s on! is intensifying on price, and U.S. flavor-restriction proposals are perennial headline risk. The ULTRA repositioning is management’s answer — but if pouch-share losses accelerate despite it, the U.S. growth leg weakens. Q3 U.S. shipment data is the checkpoint.

IQOS and the American heated-tobacco opportunity

The FDA reauthorized IQOS as a modified-risk tobacco product in April 2026; devices and consumables hold first-ever marketing authorizations in the category. The pending decision is the PMTA for IQOS ILUMA (the induction-heating generation) — management expects a decision “sooner than later” and is prepared to launch immediately. Heated tobacco carries roughly twice the gross profit per unit of combustibles: a U.S. rollout is a structural margin event, not just a revenue event. The target does not require it to succeed — it is priced as optionality.

06 | Risks

Risks

  • Regulatory and excise. The most regulated consumer category on earth. EU excise trajectory, flavor restrictions (Poland already), and any U.S. federal action on pouches or flavors can move volumes faster than pricing compensates. The permanent discount on the multiple.
  • ZYN share defense. The ULTRA repositioning must work. Sustained share loss to Velo or on! on price would weaken the U.S. growth leg.
  • Currency. PMI reports in dollars and earns almost everywhere else — the guide has been trimmed for FX three times this year.
  • ILUMA timing. Priced as upside, but a prolonged FDA delay or adverse PMTA decision removes a free call option the market is beginning to count.
  • Leverage. ~2.7x debt/EBITDA with negative book equity is aggressive for staples; refinancing-rate spikes would raise deleveraging costs.
  • Dividend coverage optics. ~77% of guided earnings leaves little room for error in a bad year.
  • Valuation. At ~22x forward earnings versus ~15x for staples peers, the stock is priced for the smoke-free story to keep delivering. Any deceleration compresses the multiple.
  • Litigation and ESG exclusion. Tobacco litigation tail risk plus ESG-mandate exclusion caps the institutional buyer base — a structural headwind to multiple expansion.
