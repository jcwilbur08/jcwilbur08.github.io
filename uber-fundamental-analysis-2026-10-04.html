01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

The platform is compounding on both the top line and the margin line

Gross bookings of $58.0B grew 24% year over year in Q2, the fourth straight quarter above 20%. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 4.9% of bookings from 4.5% a year ago, non-GAAP operating income rose 40%, and trailing free cash flow exceeded $10B. This is no longer a growth-at-any-cost marketplace; it is a scaled platform with operating leverage, and the compounding is broad-based across mobility, delivery, and freight.

2

Delivery is the underappreciated growth engine - and Delivery Hero doubles its scale

Delivery bookings grew 26%, the fastest of the three segments, and the Costco partnership expanded from 17 to 47 states covering nearly 600 warehouses. The $14.8B Delivery Hero acquisition would create the largest food-delivery group outside China, with $236B of pro-forma 2025 GMV across 99 markets, and is expected to be non-GAAP EPS accretive from close, reaching high-single-digit accretion by year three. The deal is strategically coherent even before the synergy math.

3

Uber is the demand layer for autonomy - the asset-light side of the trade

Uber shut its own AV program in 2020 and now aggregates third-party autonomous supply: Waymo, WeRide, Wayve, Nuro, Zoox, Pony AI, Avride, and others. The platform targets AV partners live in up to 15 cities by year-end - six in the US and nine internationally - while AV trips remain a small fraction of the ~300 million weekly trips on the platform. Spreading technology risk across many partners while owning dispatch, payments, and the rider relationship is the right capital structure for an uncertain AV timeline.

4

The multiple has decoupled from the fundamentals

At $68.11 the stock trades at roughly 15.6x consensus 2027 non-GAAP EPS of about $4.38 and about 23x forward earnings - a multiple more appropriate for a mid-teens grower than for a business guiding 20%-ish bookings growth with 25-30% EPS growth. The gap between the market multiple and a fair 24x growth multiple on a proven compounder is the opportunity; the AV debate is the reason it exists, and the AV risk has not materialized in any operating metric.

5

Insiders and sophisticated capital are leaning against the consensus

CEO Dara Khosrowshahi bought approximately $10M of stock in the open market at $70.96 on September 10 - above today's price, with his own money. The stock is down 17% year to date and 29% over the trailing year while estimates have drifted up, not down. When the CEO buys above the current quote into a sentiment washout, the burden of proof shifts to the bears.

Variant perception. The market sees a rideshare aggregator facing existential robotaxi displacement and a $14.8B European delivery acquisition to digest. The differentiated view is that Uber is becoming the universal demand aggregator for autonomous miles - owning the rider relationship across human and autonomous supply - while the core marketplace compounds bookings at 20%+ with expanding margins. The valuation prices substantial AV disruption; the operating results price none of it. That asymmetry is the Strong Buy case.
02 | Business & Industry

Business quality and competitive position

Uber operates the world's largest mobility-and-delivery platform: on-demand rides, food and grocery delivery, and freight brokerage, unified by a single membership layer (Uber One), a single payments stack, and a single rider app. The flywheel is cross-platform adoption - a mobility rider who tries Eats spends more across the platform, and delivery frequency deepens the habit loop. In Q2 the platform completed 3.87B trips (+18%) for 208M monthly active consumers (+16%), with trips per MAPC up about 2%.

The autonomy ecosystem - Uber as aggregator

Uber's AV strategy is deliberately asset-light: partner with every credible autonomy stack, integrate them into the same dispatch app, and let partners bear vehicle and technology risk. The partner roster now spans Waymo (Austin, Atlanta), WeRide (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Riyadh, Madrid), Wayve (London), Nuro (Bay Area, Houston), Zoox (Las Vegas, Los Angeles), Pony AI and Verne (Zagreb), Avride (Texas testing), and Baidu's Apollo Go in select markets. Management reported being live in 7 AV cities on the Q2 call and targets up to 15 by year-end - six in the US, nine internationally.

September AV milestones
3 launches

Spain's first national Level 4 permit (WeRide + Uber, Madrid, commercial ops targeted by end-2026); Wayve commercial launch in London - the capital's first robotaxi operation; Pony AI/Verne launch in Zagreb, Europe's first city booking AVs directly through the Uber app.

AV trip scale today
~0.1%

AV trips run in the hundreds of thousands per week versus roughly 300 million total weekly platform trips - strategically important, immaterial to the current P&L.

The Waymo tension is real but bounded. Waymo has notified Uber it intends to operate Austin and Atlanta independently from January 2028, when the contract permits, and the two companies are lobbying on opposite sides of robotaxi regulation (Uber favors mandatory hybrid human-AV networks; Waymo favors direct operation). Against that: Waymo still scales through the Uber app in both cities today, Waymo runs roughly 500,000 rides per week globally against Uber's ~300 million, and independent analysts estimate Uber retains roughly 73% US mobility share through 2028. The partnership is fraying at the edges while the competitive threat remains 18-24 months from material scale.

Moat assessment

Network liquidity
Strong

208M MAPCs and the deepest driver/courier pool in most markets create wait-time and reliability advantages that are self-reinforcing.

Cross-platform data
Strong

Rides, delivery, freight, and membership data feed dispatch, pricing, and fraud systems; Uber One ties the ecosystem together and raises switching costs.

Aggregator position in AV
Moderate-strong

Owning dispatch, payments, and the rider relationship across many AV partners is a genuine moat - unless regulators or partners route around the aggregator.

Pricing power
Moderate

Take rates are constrained by driver/courier economics and competition (Lyft, DoorDash, regional players); leverage comes from mix and efficiency, not price extraction.

Competitive landscape

In mobility, Lyft remains the US challenger and regional players (Grab, Didi, Bolt, inDrive) contest international markets; in delivery, DoorDash - fresh off its $3.87B Deliveroo purchase - and Prosus-owned Just Eat Takeaway are the global counterparts. The emerging competitive axis is autonomous: Waymo (Alphabet), Tesla's Cybercab (launched in the US in late September), and Zoox all aspire to own the rider relationship directly. Uber's answer is to be the universal aggregator - the iOS of mobility - hosting every AV stack the way the App Store hosts apps.

Quality of growth is the story. Bookings growth (+24%) is running at roughly double revenue growth (+12%) because of accounting presentation changes in certain markets - the economics accrue to Uber either way, but the P&L narrative confuses casual observers. Meanwhile margin expansion (adj. EBITDA +33% on +24% bookings) and the $10B FCF milestone confirm that growth is profitable growth. The stock's 17% YTD decline is a sentiment repricing, not an earnings reset - estimates have drifted up, not down.

Balance sheet and capital intensity

At June 30, 2026, Uber held $5.4B of unrestricted cash and short-term investments against about $14.7B of total debt. On approximately 2.04B shares outstanding, the $68.11 reference price implies a market capitalization of about $139B and an enterprise value near $154B. The balance sheet is a strategic asset: it funds the Delivery Hero consideration, the AV partnership commitments, and ongoing buybacks without strain. Net leverage is modest for a platform of this scale, and the $10B+ TTM free-cash-flow engine de-risks the $14.8B Delivery Hero check considerably.

Capital allocation

Management's framework is straightforward: invest in the platform and AV partnerships, fund the Delivery Hero acquisition, and reduce the share count. The open question is buyback pacing while the Delivery Hero cash consideration is staged - management has not quantified a pause, and the Q3 call should address the buyback-vs-deal-funding split. With the stock near 15-16x 2027 earnings, every repurchase dollar at these levels is highly accretive. The CEO's personal $10M open-market purchase at $70.96 in September is the strongest possible signal of management's view on current valuation.

04 | Management & Capital Allocation

Operator-led, capital-disciplined, and aligned

CEO Dara Khosrowshahi has run Uber since 2017, presiding over the pivot from cash-burning growth to profitable compounding: the company crossed GAAP operating profitability, built a $10B+ FCF engine, and is now deploying that cash into the largest strategic acquisition in its history. The September $10M open-market purchase - 141,000 shares at $70.96, above the current price - is material insider alignment, not token buying.

Positive

Capital discipline

The Delivery Hero deal is large but structured: a pre-emptive 14-market divestiture to SSW Partners (~€1.4B) de-risks antitrust, Prosus's 17% is irrevocably committed, and the ~25% existing stake plus derivatives reduce the incremental outlay. Management expects non-GAAP EPS accretion from close.

Positive

AV strategy coherence

Rather than re-building an AV stack after selling ATG in 2020, Uber is assembling the broadest AV partner network in the industry - an asset-light approach that spreads technology risk and preserves balance-sheet flexibility.

Watch

Deal integration

Delivery Hero has historically burned cash; H1 2026 showed progress (€348M FCF before special items vs -€8M prior year), but integrating a €7.8B-revenue European operator while maintaining Uber's margin trajectory is a genuine execution test.

Watch

Regulatory posture

Uber is lobbying for mandatory hybrid human-AV networks (New Jersey, DC) - a defensible moat play, but one that concedes AVs could otherwise bypass the aggregator. Driver-classification litigation remains a standing tail risk in the US and Europe.

09 | Final Recommendation

Strong Buy - the asymmetry is the thesis

Rating
Strong Buy
Conviction
8 / 10
12-mo target

Uber is a 20%-plus bookings compounder with a $10B free-cash-flow engine trading at 15.6x forward earnings because the market is pricing an autonomous-vehicle disruption that has not shown up in a single operating metric. That is a mispricing, not a value trap - and the fired add gate is the entry.

The Strong Buy rests on three legs. First, the operating evidence is unambiguous: 24% bookings growth, expanding margins, $10B FCF, and a CEO buying $10M of stock with his own money. Second, the valuation gap is unusually wide for a name this large: 15.6x 2027 EPS versus a 24x fair multiple for 25-30% EPS growth implies 54% upside before any heroics. Third, the risk is genuinely understood and bounded: the AV timeline has 18-24 months of runway before it can matter to the P&L, and the explicit tripwire - material AV share gains or partner defection - is observable, not theoretical.

The reason conviction is 8 rather than 9 or 10 is the single-factor nature of the risk: the AV endgame is a genuine terminal-value debate that no amount of current operating strength can fully resolve, and the $14.8B Delivery Hero check adds real integration risk. That is why the position is staged, capped at 0.75% interim, and gated - not why it should be avoided.
