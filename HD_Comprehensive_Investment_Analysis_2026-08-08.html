Executive Summary

Home Depot (NYSE: HD) remains the highest-quality scaled franchise in U.S. home improvement, but the near-term earnings setup is still constrained by housing affordability, subdued turnover, and weak demand for large discretionary projects. The strategic offset is meaningful: Home Depot is transforming from a predominantly big-box home-improvement retailer into a broader professional contractor and specialty-distribution platform through SRS Distribution, GMS and the expansion into HVAC distribution. Our rating is HOLD / WATCH FOR ENTRY with a 7/10 conviction. At the August 6 closing price of $349.52, the shares trade at roughly 22.9x-23.8x management's FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance range, which is not distressed enough to fully compensate for a still-muted housing cycle, acquisition-related leverage and near-term margin pressure. The long-term franchise remains attractive, and a housing recovery could restore mid-to-high-single-digit EPS growth before capital returns. We would become more constructive either on evidence that comparable sales and large-project demand are inflecting sustainably or on a valuation reset toward the low-$300s.

Investment Thesis

Thesis: Home Depot should compound value over a long horizon because it combines a dominant retail network, powerful vendor relationships, an advantaged Pro ecosystem and significant exposure to an eventual normalization in U.S. housing activity. The central debate is timing and price: the company is investing through a depressed home-improvement cycle, while SRS/GMS broaden its addressable market and deepen its professional-customer moat. If housing turnover and larger renovation activity recover, Home Depot can layer cyclical upside onto structurally higher Pro wallet share. What the market may be underestimating: the strategic value of the specialty-distribution platform. Home Depot now operates 2,361 retail stores and more than 1,280 SRS locations, and management says its addressable market has expanded to approximately $1.2 trillion. This creates more ways to serve complex Pro projects beyond the store aisle. What the market may be correctly discounting: near-term organic growth remains modest. Q1 FY2026 comparable sales rose only 0.6%, U.S. comps rose 0.4%, transactions fell 1.3%, and management continues to describe larger discretionary projects as pressured.

Business & Competitive Position

Home Depot is the world's largest home-improvement specialty retailer. Its competitive advantages are scale purchasing, dense store coverage, brand recognition, inventory breadth, omnichannel fulfillment, contractor relationships and a growing specialty-distribution network. The retail stores also function as local fulfillment nodes, while SRS extends the company into roofing, building products, landscape, pool and now HVAC distribution. The principal competitor is Lowe's in big-box home improvement, but the strategic battleground increasingly includes specialty distributors and trade-focused suppliers. Home Depot's Pro strategy is important because professional customers generally purchase more frequently, undertake larger projects and value jobsite fulfillment, credit, assortment and reliability. This can improve customer lifetime value even when gross margins on distribution mix are lower than traditional retail.

Financial Quality

FY2025 sales were $164.7 billion, up 3.2%, while comparable sales increased only 0.3%. Net earnings were $14.2 billion and adjusted EPS was $14.69. Adjusted operating margin declined to 13.1% from 13.8%, reflecting a difficult demand environment and the changing business mix. Adjusted ROIC remained strong at 25.7%, although below 31.9% in the prior year. Cash generation remains a core strength. Home Depot generated $16.3 billion of operating cash flow in FY2025 and spent $3.7 billion on capital expenditures. However, acquisition activity has materially increased the capital intensity and leverage of the enterprise. At May 3, 2026, long-term debt excluding current installments was approximately $44.8 billion. The balance sheet is manageable for the franchise, but leverage reduces the flexibility that historically supported aggressive repurchases.

Latest Earnings & 2026 Outlook

Q1 FY2026 sales increased 4.8% to $41.8 billion, helped by acquisitions, while comparable sales rose 0.6%. Adjusted EPS declined 3.7% to $3.43. Nine of sixteen merchandising departments posted positive comps. Average ticket increased 2.2%, transactions declined 1.3%, and transactions above $1,000 rose 0.8%. Pro comps were positive and outperformed DIY, but larger discretionary projects remained pressured. Management reaffirmed FY2026 guidance: total sales growth of 2.5%-4.5%, comparable sales from flat to +2%, adjusted operating margin of 12.8%-13.0%, and adjusted EPS growth of 0%-4% from FY2025's $14.69. The next major checkpoint is Q2 earnings on August 18, 2026.

Moat, Management & Capital Allocation

The moat is wide but evolving. Scale, supplier economics, distribution density, customer data, Pro loyalty and fulfillment capabilities are difficult to replicate simultaneously. Management's strategic direction is coherent: defend the core store franchise while building an interconnected Pro ecosystem. The key execution risk is integrating acquisitions without permanently diluting margins or returns on invested capital. Capital allocation has shifted from a historically repurchase-heavy model toward acquisitions and investment. FY2025 cash uses included $9.2 billion of dividends, $3.7 billion of capex and the approximately $5.5 billion GMS acquisition. The quarterly dividend was raised 1.3% to $2.33, or $9.32 annualized. Valuation

At $349.52, Home Depot trades at approximately 22.9x the high end and 23.8x the low end of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance. That valuation is reasonable for a high-quality franchise but assumes at least partial normalization in growth over the next several years. Our preferred framework is normalized earnings power rather than a single-year depressed-cycle multiple. A base case of roughly $16.25-$16.75 normalized EPS and a 23x-24x multiple supports a value zone around $375-$400. A stronger housing recovery with EPS approaching $18 and a premium multiple can justify $430-$450+, while prolonged housing weakness, acquisition integration friction and multiple compression could push fair value toward $290-$315. Valuation discipline: below ~$320 we would view the risk/reward as increasingly attractive; $320-$365 is a reasonable accumulation/watch zone subject to fundamentals; above ~$410 requires clearer evidence of a housing and big-project recovery.

Bull / Base / Bear Scenarios

Bull case - $450 (25% probability): housing turnover and remodeling recover faster than expected, comps reach the 4%-5% market-recovery range, Pro initiatives gain share, operating leverage improves, and normalized EPS approaches $18. Base case - $390 (55% probability): comps improve gradually, Pro remains stronger than DIY, integration proceeds adequately, and normalized EPS reaches the mid-$16 range. This produces moderate price appreciation plus the dividend. Bear case - $300 (20% probability): mortgage rates and affordability keep housing activity depressed, big-ticket projects remain weak, acquisition mix pressures margins, leverage constrains buybacks, and the market de-rates the shares toward ~20x depressed earnings.

What Is Working / What Is Weakening

Working: positive Pro comps; broad category participation; growing specialty distribution; resilient average ticket; strong cash generation; high adjusted ROIC; durable dividend; expanding addressable market. Weakening or unresolved: negative transaction growth; continued pressure on larger discretionary projects; adjusted EPS still declining year over year in Q1; lower operating margin and ROIC versus prior periods; higher debt and acquisition complexity; continued dependence on a housing-cycle normalization for stronger organic growth.

Catalysts & Checkpoints

The most important near-term catalyst is Q2 FY2026 earnings on August 18. Watch U.S. comparable sales, transactions, big-ticket project trends, Pro versus DIY performance, gross and adjusted operating margins, SRS/GMS integration, debt reduction and any change to full-year guidance. Over the next 12-24 months, existing-home sales, mortgage rates, home-price stability and remodeling activity are the macro checkpoints most likely to determine the speed of earnings normalization.

Risks & Thesis-Break Conditions

Primary risks include a prolonged housing recession, weaker consumer confidence, persistent high mortgage rates, competitive pricing, wage and freight inflation, acquisition integration failures, lower returns on invested capital and excessive leverage. Thesis-break conditions: (1) sustained market-share losses in the core business; (2) Pro growth failing to exceed the market despite heavy investment; (3) adjusted operating margin structurally falling below ~12% without a credible recovery path; (4) ROIC deteriorating materially toward the cost of capital; (5) acquisition-related leverage remaining elevated while free cash flow weakens; or (6) evidence that the SRS/GMS strategy is destroying rather than creating economic value.
