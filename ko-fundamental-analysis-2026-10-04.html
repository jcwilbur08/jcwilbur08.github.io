Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Coca-Cola is doing everything right, and the market has noticed. The second quarter delivered the mix investors waited years for — real volume growth of 5% alongside disciplined pricing, margin expansion, an earnings beat, and a raised full-year guide. The company is deliberately pivoting from price-led to volume-led growth, winning share in its home market while its closest rival retrenches, and compounding a 64-year dividend streak. The problem is arithmetic, not execution: at roughly 26× trailing earnings and 7% below its 52-week high, the stock prices in the good news. The one-week move since the September 27 report — down 2.5% to $85.65 on no fundamental news — improves the setup only marginally.

Research posture: HOLD | Conviction 5/10 | 12-month target $94

At $85.65, the $94 target implies ~10% price upside (9.75% precisely) plus a 2.5% dividend yield for a ~12% total return. Under the new conviction-anchored rating scale, this is a Hold on both prongs: conviction is 5 — positive on the business, unconvinced on the price — and price upside sits just under the 10% Buy threshold. The rating rests on price upside, stated honestly; the dividend adds income but does not change the rating. This remains a hold-and-collect position: elite income, best-in-class execution, no reason to add at 26× earnings.

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

The concentrate model remains the best economic engine in consumer staples

Coca-Cola sells concentrates and syrups to independent bottlers, capturing the high-margin, asset-light portion of the value chain while bottlers handle manufacturing and distribution. Gross margin near 62% and comparable operating margin of 35.6% are the fingerprints of a brand owner, not a manufacturer. As the Africa bottling sale closes — expected late Q3 or Q4 2026 — the decade-long refranchising program is essentially complete, leaving a pure concentrate-and-brand owner.

2

The pivot from price-led to volume-led growth is working — so far

After years of pricing-led growth, Q2 balanced the mix: unit cases +5% with price/mix of only +2% (pricing +3, mix −1). Management is deliberately trading price for volume and household penetration, particularly in emerging markets. Trademark Coca-Cola volume grew 5% across all geographies — the strongest quarterly print in 17 years outside the COVID rebound. The honest caveat: the FIFA World Cup is a genuine one-off demand pull, and H2 must prove the trend without the tournament.

3

The dividend is the ballast of the position

The February 2026 increase marked the 64th consecutive annual raise — $2.12 annualized, ~2.5% forward yield, ~64% payout ratio, ~1.36× covered by guided free cash flow of ~$12.4B. For this portfolio, 293.487 shares generate roughly $622 per year of forward income. The yield will not drive the return from here — it sits below the stock's own five-year average of ~2.8% because the price has run — but it floors the investment case and compounds quietly through any multiple compression.

4

It is winning the beverage war outright

Q2 laid the competitive contrast bare: Coca-Cola North America volume +3% while PepsiCo's North American beverage volume fell 4%. Coca-Cola is taking value share in its home market as its closest rival retrenches. Versus Keurig Dr Pepper (a US-centric challenger) and Monster (energy optionality, not the thesis), the global system and away-from-home footprint remain unmatched.

5

The price already reflects the execution

At ~26× trailing earnings, ~8× sales, and a 2.5% yield below its own history, the stock is priced like the compounder it is — leaving no margin of safety for a volume disappointment. Morningstar's $75 fair value (2 stars) is ~12% below the market. This is why conviction is 5, not 7: maximum respect for the business, none for the entry price.

Variant perception. The street's Moderate Buy consensus and $96.18 average target imply the market expects the volume story to persist and the multiple to hold. Our more cautious read: the Q2 volume print deserves a proof quarter before the multiple is underwritten at 26×. The October 27 print is the referee — volume above 2–3% ex-World Cup confirms the pivot; reversion toward 1% re-prices the growth narrative.
02 | Business & Industry

Business quality and competitive position

Coca-Cola, selling since 1886, is the world's largest beverage company by value, with products in more than 200 countries and territories. The portfolio centers on Trademark Coca-Cola but spans sparkling (Sprite, Fanta), water, sports (Powerade), juice and dairy (Minute Maid, fairlife), tea, coffee, and energy. Henrique Braun became CEO on March 31, 2026, succeeding James Quincey (now Executive Chairman) — an internal-operator transition that has delivered continuity. On September 25, the company announced Rob Gehring — Monster Beverage's Americas CEO — will lead the North America operating unit from December 1, 2026, succeeding interim leader John Murphy, who remains president and CFO.

Competitive landscape

Versus PepsiCo, the two are increasingly different investments: Coca-Cola is a pure-play beverage compounder at 35.6% comparable operating margin; PepsiCo is a snacks-and-beverages conglomerate at ~16.8% core margin with a North American volume problem. In this portfolio they are complements — KO for beverage excellence and income reliability, PEP for snacks exposure and contrarian value — not substitutes. Keurig Dr Pepper is a distant US-centric third; Monster and energy remain optionality rather than thesis. Private label is a non-issue for the trademark portfolio.

GLP-1 and health trends are the slow-burn question. Appetite-suppressing drugs have coincided with roughly a 7% decline in sugary-drink consumption among users, but Coca-Cola is mitigating through its Zero Sugar portfolio, which grew volume ~13% in late 2025. Category volumes are not immune, but the mitigant is working.

Q3 2026 preview — October 27, before the open

The company confirmed on September 29 that third-quarter results publish Tuesday, October 27, before the NYSE opens, with an investor call at 8:30 a.m. ET. Consensus expects ~$0.87 comparable EPS (+6.1% y/y) on roughly $12.9B revenue. The stock has beaten EPS estimates in each of the last four quarters. What matters is not the penny beat — it is unit case volume ex-World Cup: anything above 2–3% confirms the volume-led pivot is real; a reversion toward 1% undermines the core of the bull case and invites multiple compression.

Africa bottling sale — the last refranchising domino. Coca-Cola HBC's pending ~$2.6B acquisition of a majority stake in Coca-Cola Beverages Africa is expected to close late in 2026. Completion removes the last company-owned bottling overhang and confirms the asset-light end state — but also means the decade-long margin tailwind from refranchising is fully harvested; future margin gains must come from mix and productivity.

Dividend and capital returns

The $0.53 quarterly dividend ($2.12 annualized) was paid October 1 to holders of record September 15 — the 64th consecutive annual increase. At the $85.65 reference price the forward yield is 2.47%, with a ~64% payout ratio and five-year average dividend growth near 4.5%. Buybacks are secondary; the dividend is the capital-return story. India — on track to become the company's third-largest market, with a local bottling stake divested to the Jubilant Bhartia Group — is the long-duration growth offset to the mature-market income profile.

04 | Management & Capital Allocation

Operator-led continuity, with a notable external hire

Henrique Braun's first full quarter as CEO (Q2) delivered a beat-and-raise — a strong start for the internal-operator transition from James Quincey, who remains Executive Chairman. The September 25 appointment of Rob Gehring — Monster Beverage's Americas CEO — to lead North America from December 1 is the more interesting signal: an external commercial hire into the company's most profitable market, succeeding interim leader John Murphy (who remains president and CFO). It reads as an offensive move on execution and innovation, not a repair job.

Positive

Capital discipline

Refranchising completion, a ~64% dividend payout, and buybacks funded from operations — no empire-building. The $101.9B returned since 2010 is the track record.

Positive

Incidence-based pricing

Taking price where consumers can absorb it and investing it where penetration is the prize — the opposite of the indiscriminate increases that broke PepsiCo's North American volume.

Watch

Africa sale execution

The close has already slipped once (now late Q3 or Q4 2026, pending regulatory approvals). A further delay keeps lower-margin bottling assets on the books into 2027.

Watch

IRS transfer-pricing dispute

The Eleventh Circuit appeal is the balance-sheet black swan: up to ~$18B liability if lost, against a $6B deposit already made in 2025. Some analysts consider it priced in; the tail is real.
