1   Executive summary & investment thesis

ASML remains the finest industrial monopoly in technology — sole supplier of EUV lithography, fresh off a Q2 2026 beat-and-raise that lifted full-year guidance to $50–52 bn. But the market knows it: the ADR is up ~60% year-to-date and ~95% over twelve months, and now trades at ~53× trailing earnings and ~38× EV/EBITDA. The business is exceptional; the price already assumes it stays that way.

Thesis in brief. (1) Fundamentals have never been stronger — Q2 sales of $10.8 bn beat guidance, FY2026 guidance was raised twice, and AI-driven demand has management expanding EUV and DUV capacity 30% into 2027. (2) The EUV monopoly is widening: High-NA is now qualified at Intel's 18A with TSMC and Samsung engaged. (3) But valuation is full — multiples sit well above historical medians after the run to June's all-time high of $1,987. (4) The binary risk is Washington: the MATCH Act could extend controls to DUV servicing, putting ~14–15% of revenue in play. (5) With Q3 earnings due 14 October, risk/reward is balanced — we see no reason to chase, and every reason to own the name on weakness.

We rate ASML a HOLD with a 12-month price target of $1,950, ~14% above current levels — roughly in line with the $1,970 street consensus. This is a core compounder to accumulate on pullbacks, not to chase at all-time highs.

5   Business overview

ASML designs and manufactures photolithography systems — the machines that print transistor patterns onto silicon wafers. Every advanced chip on earth passes through an ASML tool. The portfolio:

PlatformUse caseASP (indicative)Market share
EUV low-NA (NXE:3600D/3800E)7nm and below — leading-edge logic & DRAM$180–200m100%
EUV High-NA (EXE:5000/5200)2nm and below — single-exposure patterning$350–380m+100%
DUV immersion (NXT series)Mid-range & mature nodes, China capacity$60–80m~90%
DUV dry (KrF/ArF/i-line)Legacy nodes, broad industrial base$30–50m~85%

In Q2 2026, EUV was 57% of system sales and ArFi immersion 29%, with logic and memory roughly evenly split — a healthy balance. Regionally, shipments skewed to South Korea 43%, Taiwan 30%, China 14% (down from 27% a year earlier), reflecting where leading-edge capacity is being built. Installed Base Management contributes 25–30% of revenue at superior margins.

Customer concentration is structural. TSMC (in 2nm volume production since January 2026), Samsung and Intel dominate EUV demand; SK Hynix and Micron drive memory. Co-development — e.g., Intel's High-NA qualification on 18A — embeds ASML in roadmaps years before revenue.

6   Competitive moat — the widest in technology hardware

  • Absolute EUV monopoly. The only commercial EUV supplier; ~90–94% of the total lithography market. No credible EUV competitor is less than 5–10 years away.
  • Physics as a barrier. Three decades and $23 bn+ of cumulative R&D; High-NA extends the lead — Zeiss's optics (ASML owns 25% of Zeiss SMT) and system integration cannot be replicated on any investable horizon.
  • Faltering rivals. Nikon has no EUV answer and recently posted a record ~¥86 bn loss; Canon's nanoimprint remains far behind on overlay and throughput.
  • Installed-base flywheel. Services and upgrades (~30% of revenue) are recurring, software-led, and high-margin — Q2's IBM beat is the template.
  • China's gap. SMEE serves mature nodes; state-backed Shanghai Aishengna plans ~20 DUV tools in 2027 versus ASML's ~130 immersion shipments in 2026. EUV-class capability is many years away, if ever.
  • Pricing power: EUV ASPs near $200 mn; High-NA at $350 mn+ resets the price umbrella upward.

7   Demand drivers — a multi-year capex supercycle

  • AI datacenter build-out. BofA sees wafer-fab equipment spending rising 129% from $156 bn to $360 bn by 2030; ASML is named a key beneficiary, with its 2027 low-NA EUV capacity reportedly already spoken for.
  • TSMC's 2nm ramp. Volume production began January 2026; 2026 capex of $52–56 bn (70–80% to 2nm/A16); FY2026 revenue growth guided above 40%. Gate-all-around transistors are lithography-intensive.
  • Memory recovery. HBM-driven DRAM capex is back — Korea took 43% of Q2 shipments — and ASML guides memory-related lithography sales +75% in FY2026.
  • High-NA adoption. Intel has qualified High-NA on 18A — the first high-volume logic use — with TSMC and Samsung engaged; BofA models ~5 tools in 2026 rising to ~20 by 2030, each at nearly 2× low-NA ASP.
  • Geographic fab diversification. US, EU, Japanese and Korean subsidy programmes underwrite a decade of greenfield fabs — all ASML customers by necessity.

8   China & export controls — the binary risk

China was 14% of Q2 2026 sales (guided ~20% of FY2026, down from ~33% in 2025), almost entirely DUV. The regulatory picture is fluid:

  • EUV: fully restricted since 2019 — zero China exposure to lose.
  • DUV: advanced immersion tools require Dutch export licenses since 2023–24; mature DUV continues to ship.
  • MATCH Act (H.R. 8170): introduced April 2026 and cleared the House Foreign Affairs Committee — it would extend US controls to ASML's DUV immersion tools and servicing (installation, maintenance, remote updates) for named Chinese chipmakers, with a 150-day window for allies to align. Not yet law; the administration has taken no formal position.
  • June 2026 incident: the Commerce Secretary publicly alleged EUV-related components reached China; ASML vehemently denied it, no evidence was shown, but the stock fell 7.8% in a session — a preview of headline sensitivity.
  • Dutch pushback: PM Schoof raised ASML directly with the White House; the Netherlands resists US dictation of its export policy.
Impact framing. BofA models a full DUV-plus-services ban at ~14–15% of revenue and ~16–17% of gross profit. ASML states 2026 guidance already reflects expected control outcomes. This is the single largest downside scenario — and the reason we are not more constructive at 53× earnings.

10   Long-term outlook to 2030

The November 2024 Investor Day scenarios — $51–70 bn revenue at 56–60% gross margin by 2030 — remain unchanged, with the next update at the Capital Markets Day on 10 June 2027. Remarkably, FY2026 guidance of $50–52 bn already brushes the low end of the 2030 range four years early — the AI cycle is pulling the trajectory forward. The building blocks are credible: EUV layer-count growth at 2nm-class nodes, High-NA ASP uplift, a growing service annuity, and DUV resilience. We underwrite the base case; the upper half of the range is free optionality.

11   Catalysts, recommendation & target methodology

Near-term catalysts

  • Q3 2026 results (14 Oct 2026) — a guided record quarter ($12.8–13.9 bn sales); consensus expects $12.54 EPS (+96% y/y). The bar is high.
  • MATCH Act progression — any movement toward law is the key downside headline risk.
  • Memory capex acceleration — further DRAM/HBM order strength is pure upside.
  • Capital Markets Day (10 Jun 2027) — next formal update to 2030 scenarios.

Recommendation: HOLD — 12-month price target $1,950

Methodology. We apply a 35× multiple — ASML's through-cycle forward multiple — to our FY2027 EPS estimate of ~$55.70 (consensus FY2026 $44.10 grown ~26%): 35 × 55.70 = $1,950, implying ~14% upside from $1,711. The target sits just below the $1,970 street consensus — we see fair value, not a re-rating, from here.

Positioning guidance for the CIO. ASML is a core compounder holding with a 3–5 year horizon — but at 53× trailing earnings after a 60% YTD run, this is a moment for discipline, not accumulation. Hold the position; add on export-control headlines or a post-earnings washout (the June episode showed how quickly 8% dislocations appear); trim only if the MATCH Act becomes law or 2030 scenarios are cut. Revisit aggressively into any weakness toward $1,400.
