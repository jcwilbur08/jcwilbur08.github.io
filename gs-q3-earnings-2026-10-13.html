The desk call

Upgrade to Buy. Raise the target to $1,050.

This was a genuine franchise beat, not a trading blip. Goldman converted a broad capital-markets recovery into record advisory revenue, exceptional equity-financing economics and returns well above its own through-cycle framework.

The September 27 thesis carried a Hold and a $1,020 target. Q3 removes enough uncertainty around sustainable earnings power to warrant a Buy, while the stock’s post-print fade leaves nearly 18% upside to the revised target.

Conviction stops at 6/10. Goldman remains a cyclical capital-markets business, FICC came in soft, and the very strong Q2 comparison makes quarterly earnings look uneven even as the franchise strengthens.

What changed

Reported EPS of $17.01 beat LSEG consensus by about 30%. Revenue reached $18.68B, the second-highest quarter in firm history. Record advisory, record equity financing and a record asset base show multiple engines working at once.

Franchise evidence

Financing, advice and asset gathering are reinforcing one another.

Advisory leadership

Advisory fees reached a record $1.654B, up 18%. Goldman held the top M&A position on announced and completed volume, advising on $1.5T of announced transactions year to date with an approximately $430B lead over the nearest competitor.

Structural financing

Equities rose 72% to $6.44B as financing revenue nearly doubled to a record. Combined FICC and equities financing reached $12.7B year to date and now represents more than 40% of markets revenue.

Asset gathering

Asset & Wealth Management revenue grew 14% to $5.05B. Assets under supervision reached a record $4.1T, while management and other fees hit a record $3.6B.

Read the FICC miss correctly

FICC revenue of $3.47B came in below roughly $3.72B of directional street expectations as mortgages, rates and credit weakened. The softness was not isolated to Goldman: JPMorgan and Wells Fargo also printed soft fixed-income results. Financing still grew 14% to its second-best quarter.

Forward setup

The backlog is converting, fundraising is accelerating.

Goldman enters Q4 with several earnings bridges already in view: a robust banking pipeline, higher alternatives fundraising, elevated incentive fees and ample capital capacity.

Alternatives steps up

Management raised 2026 gross third-party alternatives fundraising expectations to about $150B from more than $125B. The firm raised $37B in Q3 and $122B year to date; alternative assets under supervision reached a record $491B, up 26%.

Capital remains an asset

The 12.4% CET1 ratio sits about 100 basis points above the requirement. Goldman returned $4.49B to common shareholders in Q3: $3.0B of repurchases and $1.5B of dividends. A $5.00-per-share dividend was declared for December 30.

Fee momentum continues

Management expects incentive fees to remain elevated in Q4. Wealth-management client assets reached a record $2.1T after $47B of long-term net inflows, and 2026 inflows are annualizing at 12% versus a 5% target.

Backlog requires monitoring

Management described investment-banking backlog as very robust after a strong conversion quarter. Separate coverage reported a sequential decline and a level slightly below year-end 2025. Both can be true, but Q4 conversion is the next proof point.

Revenue and EPS stepped down from Q2’s records, FICC missed, expenses ran above Visible Alpha expectations, and the banking backlog was reported lower sequentially. Those are enough reasons for a fast-money fade after an initial gap.

They are not enough to undo the upgrade. Return on equity was 18.7%, return on tangible equity was 20.5%, and core engines outside FICC all delivered evidence of scale and durability.

Succession overhang addressed

David Solomon said there is “no CEO succession transition or timeline underway.” The answer does not eliminate long-term governance debate, but it removes the immediate transition narrative from the earnings call.

What can break

Why conviction stays at 6/10.

Primary risk

Capital-markets cyclicality

Higher rates, geopolitical shocks or weaker risk appetite can delay M&A and issuance. Q3 began with a five-year-high banking backlog, but sponsor exits remain below historical averages and abrupt rate moves could slow conversion.

Execution risk

FICC and operating leverage

FICC missed directional expectations, while operating expense reached $11.31B, above Visible Alpha’s $11.0B estimate. A sustained expense ramp without matching revenue would weaken the upgrade case.

Earnings quality

Quarterly volatility

Investments revenue depends on monetization timing; incentive fees can swing; charitable contributions distort comparability. The $1,050 target requires judging the franchise through the cycle rather than annualizing one exceptional quarter.
