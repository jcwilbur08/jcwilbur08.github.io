01 | Executive Summary

02 | Business Overview

Four segments, one integrated machine

UnitedHealth pairs the largest U.S. health insurer (UnitedHealthcare) with Optum, a health-services platform spanning care delivery, pharmacy benefits, and data analytics. In Q2 2026 Optum contributed just over half of consolidated earnings from operations — the diversification that is supposed to make UNH more than a managed-care spread business.

04 | What Changed Since September 25

Nine days of noise, no operating signal

The September 25 report went out at $373.44; nine days later the stock is $371.90, essentially flat after a choppy week. Nothing in the operating picture changed. What changed is the headlines — and the tape.

Legal and regulatory headlines

1

CalPERS investor suit survives dismissal — core claims proceed

A federal judge denied UnitedHealth's motion to dismiss the core claims of the CalPERS-led securities lawsuit, which alleges the company manufactured roughly $3.3B in 2024 earnings through a home-visit diagnosis-upcoding scheme to mask a declining Medicare Advantage business. The judge dismissed several peripheral allegations as corporate "puffery." This is a civil investor suit, not a DOJ action — it produces no new factual findings and creates no new regulatory exposure. It does, however, keep the coding-practices narrative in the headlines and lengthen the uncertainty tail. Press-reported; all figures are the plaintiffs' allegations.

2

Two September HHS OIG audits estimate MA overpayments

The OIG's audit of UnitedHealthcare Benefits of Texas reviewed 230 sampled cases from 2020–2021 and found 172 where records did not support the diagnosis codes (or could not be located), estimating at least $24.4M in overpayments and recommending a refund. A separate Wisconsin plan audit estimated at least $46.9M. UnitedHealth disputes both sets of findings. Financially immaterial at this scale — but consistent with the broader audit wave: CMS announced it would audit all ~550 eligible MA contracts annually (up from ~60) and expand its medical-coder staff from ~40 to ~2,000. The audit environment is structurally tightening regardless of any single finding.

3

New chief administrative officer; AI investment theme continues

Jodee Kozlak joins September 28 as chief administrative officer, a newly created role covering people, real estate, procurement and corporate security — framed by the company as supporting enterprise modernization and cross-business alignment. Separately, the company expects roughly $1.5B in 2026 AI investment aimed at administrative cost reduction across claims, call centers and clinical documentation. Neutral to mildly constructive; execution, not announcements, is what counts.

Tape and analyst moves

  • Ex-dividend mechanics: the September 14 ex-date for the $2.32 quarterly payout (paid September 22) created dividend-capture selling pressure; the stock dropped 5.2% intraday from near $405 to a $378.08 low on converging ex-div, rate-hike concerns on strong August employment data, and valuation recalibration after the summer run.
  • Zacks Research downgraded Strong-Buy → Hold on September 14 — a quantitative-model downgrade, while fundamental analysts (Bernstein Outperform/$512, JPMorgan Overweight/$516, Goldman Buy/$490, Mizuho Outperform/$493, Cantor Overweight/$495, DA Davidson $512, BMO $512, Piper Overweight/$499, Leerink Outperform/$472, Argus Buy/$470, Deutsche Hold/$467, Barclays Overweight/$441, BofA Buy/$450) remain overwhelmingly constructive. Consensus sits near $481–$482 with a Moderate Buy rating across ~36 analysts.
  • Insider tape: an August 21 sale of 1,169 shares at $390 by an Optum executive (press-reported) trimmed ~7% of that holder's position — routine-size selling, worth noting, not worth over-reading.
  • MA 2027 positioning: press reports describe an expansion of 2027 Medicare Advantage offerings — consistent with the repricing strategy (margin over membership) ahead of the October 15–December 7 enrollment window.
Net assessment. The operating trajectory — MCR bending, guidance raised twice, buyback authorized at ≥$5B, operating cash flow guided to ~$24B — is intact. The nine days added headline risk (lawsuit, audits), a quantitative-model downgrade, and a better entry price. None of it touches the earnings math. The October 13 print is where the thesis gets re-tested, not the news cycle.

Three policy fronts

Medicare Advantage funding is tightening. The CY2026 final rate was +5.06% (~$25B); the CY2027 final rate is only +2.48% (~$13B), after a +0.09% proposal — and CMS excluded unlinked chart-review diagnoses from risk scores (~$7B in CMS savings), a direct shot at coding-driven margin. UnitedHealth itself says 2027 medical trend will run "meaningfully above" the 2.48% funding. Pricing discipline into 2027 bids is the single most important operating variable after the MCR.

PBM reform is now law. The Consolidated Appropriations Act of 2026, signed February 3, 2026, mandates Part D compensation delinking effective January 1, 2028, plus 100% commercial rebate pass-through, a spread-pricing ban, and Part D pharmacy nondiscrimination. Optum Rx is about 34% of UnitedHealth's 2026 outlook revenue (~$150.5B of ~$439B) — direct exposure to the 2028 reshaping of rebate and spread profit pools.

Medicaid is shrinking and getting cut. Enrollment is 73.5M (May 2026, KFF), down ~20M from the peak; states project flat FY2026 enrollment but +7.9% spending. Roughly $911B in federal Medicaid cuts are enacted, with work requirements by end of 2026 and 6-month redeterminations from January 2027. AM Best does not expect managed-Medicaid profitability to return until late 2026 or 2027 — and press reports put UnitedHealth's Community & State margin at −1% to −1.7% for 2026 (not company-disclosed).

Vertical-integration politics are loud, not yet legislative

The Warren–Hawley Break Up Big Medicine Act (February 2026) would bar simultaneous ownership of an insurer or PBM and a provider organization; Mark Cuban endorsed the concept in August, and midterm candidates are campaigning on "Big Medicine" breakups. To level-set: no new consequential laws have passed, and the integrated model continues — the $3.3B Amedisys acquisition closed with ~160 home-health and hospice divestitures required by DOJ. The company also released independent audits of its MA, utilization-management and pharmacy practices in December and opened headquarters access to reporters in June. Political rhetoric is a valuation overhang, not an earnings event — until it becomes law.

09 | Risks

What can break the trade

  1. DOJ overhang. Formal criminal and civil requests are confirmed in the July 24, 2025 10-Q; the company is complying. No charges have been filed and no DOJ press release has surfaced as of October 4, 2026. Everything about scope, timeline, and outcome beyond the filing language is press-reported and unconfirmed — which cuts both ways: resolution could come as a manageable settlement or as a prolonged overhang. This is the single largest component of the ~28% uncertainty haircut.
  2. CalPERS investor suit. Core claims now survive dismissal. As a civil securities suit it creates no new regulatory exposure, but a trial or settlement process would keep coding-practice allegations in headlines and could produce damaging discovery. Low probability of thesis damage, moderate probability of headline drag.
  3. Medical trend re-acceleration. The Q2 MCR improvement is partly timing ($860M of favorable prior-period development). Management itself frames 2026 as "pushing down an already high number." If underlying trend re-accelerates, the 88.1% full-year MCR outlook is at risk.
  4. Medicare Advantage funding squeeze. The CY2027 final rate of +2.48% sits well below the medical trend UnitedHealth expects ("meaningfully above" 2.48%), and the exclusion of unlinked chart-review diagnoses from risk scores removes ~$7B of industry coding margin. 2027 bids must be priced with unusual discipline.
  5. OIG/CMS audit wave. The September audit findings are immaterial in dollars, but the structural direction is one-way: CMS auditing all ~550 MA contracts annually and expanding coder staff ~50x. Sustained findings could pressure risk-score practices industry-wide.
  6. PBM delinking (January 1, 2028). Part D compensation delinking reshapes Optum Rx's rebate and spread profit pools — and Optum Rx is ~34% of revenue. The company has two years to reposition; the earnings mix impact is not yet quantified.
  7. Medicaid policy. ~$911B in federal cuts, work requirements by end of 2026, and 6-month redeterminations from January 2027 land on a book whose managed-Medicaid margins are already negative and may not recover until late 2026 or 2027.
  8. Deliberate membership shrinkage. Margin over membership is the right trade, but −1.2M members since year-end 2025 (including −880K in MA) shrinks the premium base that future margin must be earned on. Shrink too far and operating leverage works in reverse.
  9. Vertical-integration politics. Break-up rhetoric is loud but no consequential law has passed. The risk is a midterm outcome that turns rhetoric into legislation — a low-probability, high-impact tail.
