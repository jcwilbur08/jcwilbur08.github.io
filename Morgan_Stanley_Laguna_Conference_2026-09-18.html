Executive Investment View

Morgan Stanley Laguna 2026
Report date: Sep. 18, 2026

Morgan Stanley's 14th Annual Laguna Conference was held September 15-17 in Dana Point, California, covering industrials, machinery, aerospace & defense, airlines, freight transportation, autos/suppliers and materials in an unplugged fireside-chat format. [1] [2]

Portfolio conclusion: the conference strengthened the long-duration physical-infrastructure thesis behind GEV, GRID and VIS and provided favorable second-order evidence for the portfolio's semiconductor and hyperscaler exposure. The most meaningful incremental evidence came from GE Vernova; the clearest caution remained aerospace and transportation supply/cost execution rather than a collapse in end demand.

What changed versus the pre-conference view?

  • GEV: evidence moved from merely “strong demand” to stronger proof of duration, monetization and execution capacity.
  • AI infrastructure: Eaton, Trane and Rockwell provide independent confirmation that the buildout is broadening into power distribution, cooling, modular electrical systems and factory automation.
  • Aerospace: demand is not the limiting variable. The debate is increasingly about castings, forgings, engines, wings, skilled labor and factory throughput.
  • Transport: higher fuel costs are creating divergent economics: rail gains efficiency share while trucking absorbs lagged fuel and labor costs.

1. GE Vernova - Most Important Portfolio Session

GEV | Holding: 1.82%

GE Vernova CEO Scott Strazik described continued “strong and durable” end-market demand. The company exited Q2 with $176 billion of backlog and now expects to reach the previously discussed $200 billion milestone very early in 2027, based on the strength of expected Q3 orders. [3] [4]

Backlog duration
2032 deliveries now contracted
GEV has begun contracting 2032 gas-turbine deliveries; 2030-31 incremental capacity is seeing strong appetite and favorable economics.
Services flywheel
~$100B future HA service revenue
Management expects roughly $100B of future HA services revenue to be contracted by end-2027, based on the installed / contracted fleet.
Electrification
$45B backlog
Electrification backlog has risen from roughly $6B at the end of 2021 to $45B today.

Why the comments matter

The key improvement is not simply another large order number. GEV is increasingly demonstrating a full economic chain: scarce generation capacity → pricing power → long-dated backlog → factory productivity → recurring service revenue. Management said 2026 improvement in Power backlog margin should exceed the low-double-digit-point improvement achieved in 2025. [4]

On services, the company has about 130 HA machines operating and another 195 under contract; by the end of 2027 it expects at least 400 machines either running or contracted. Management estimates each gigawatt of HA capacity can generate roughly $500 million of high-margin service revenue over the first 20 years. [4]

GEV also highlighted the shift in data-center relevance from selling discrete equipment toward integrated power and grid solutions. Electrification backlog has reached $45 billion, and management emphasized new products such as its Energy Management System for integrating a data center with the grid or local generation source. [4]

Underwriting impact: this is thesis-strengthening evidence. It increases confidence in revenue visibility and future service economics. It does not remove the valuation risk: the stock increasingly discounts years of strong execution, so the correct response is greater fundamental confidence rather than automatic willingness to pay any price.

What could still go wrong?

  • Execution: a huge backlog only creates value if turbine, grid-equipment and service capacity can convert it into cash with acceptable working capital.
  • Cycle extrapolation: 2030-32 bookings are powerful evidence of duration, but unusually tight capacity and pricing can normalize later in the decade.
  • Wind: remains the comparatively weaker part of the portfolio and is not the driver of the current rerating.
  • Discount rates: the current >5% 10-year Treasury environment raises the required return on long-duration industrial growth.

2. AI Power, Electrical & Thermal Infrastructure

GRID / VIS / AI-stack read-through

The strongest cross-company message from Laguna was that the AI buildout is visible well beyond chips. Independent companies supplying electrical distribution, cooling and automation are reporting unusually strong pipelines, backlogs and capacity investment.

Eaton - electrical distribution demand remains exceptional

Eaton said its 2026 organic-growth outlook had progressed from 8% to 10% and then to 12%. Data-center orders were up about 85% and revenues about 65%; management said its active data-center negotiation pipeline was more than 130% higher and noted that many publicly announced customer projects are not yet in Eaton's quoting funnel. [5]

Management explicitly characterized its prior $31 billion 2030 Electrical revenue target as a “floor”, noting that the original framework assumed only 17% data-center growth versus eight consecutive quarters above 35%, including the most recent 65% growth rate. [5]

Trane - cooling is becoming part of a system-level architecture

Trane reported a record $12.1 billion backlog, more than 90% tied to applied systems, and said its service business has compounded at a double-digit rate since 2020. [6]

More important for your AI thesis, Trane described joint reference-design work with Eaton and a major chip manufacturer, plus similar work with hyperscalers. The architecture is being designed as one integrated system encompassing thermal management and electrical infrastructure rather than separate equipment purchases. [6]

Rockwell - broader North American capex remains constructive

Rockwell characterized underlying North American customer tone as distinctly positive, citing data centers, modernization, e-commerce/warehouse automation and semiconductor facilities among the areas supporting investment. Management also said recent third-party market-share reports support its view that the company is taking modest share in important areas. [7]

Portfolio read-through: this is the best conference evidence for GRID and VIS, and it is also important for NVDA, AVGO, TSM, ASML, MSFT, AMZN, GOOG and META. Hyperscaler capex is producing measurable demand for transformers, switchgear, electrical modules, cooling systems, factory equipment and services. The physical layer is not behaving like a speculative narrative detached from orders.

3. Aerospace & Defense

GE Aerospace / LMT research lens

GE Aerospace - demand strong; supply chain is the governor

GE Aerospace CFO Rahul Ghai highlighted roughly 1,800 new engine orders at Farnborough, including a 1,000-plus-engine LEAP-1A order from IndiGo, plus another 100-engine-and-services order from Korean Air. The company described total backlog of about $210 billion, including roughly $170 billion of commercial-services backlog. [8] [9]

Management expects free-cash-flow conversion to remain above 100% of net income for at least the next two to three years before normalizing over time. It also described approximately $5 billion of inventory added over recent years as a future cash-flow tailwind if the rate of inventory build slows. [9]

The proposed CPP acquisition is best understood as a targeted capacity and delivery solution for critical castings, not a broad move to vertically integrate the entire supply chain. GE separately said a redesigned GE9X mid-seal is shipping and should not delay the Boeing 777X entry-into-service timetable. [10] [11]

RTX and Honeywell - independent confirmation of the bottleneck

RTX cited a $289 billion backlog, up 22% year over year, with strength across commercial aftermarket, commercial OE and defense. Management said GTF MRO output was up 40% year over year in Q2 and described long-run aftermarket fundamentals as strong. [12]

Honeywell Aerospace likewise said castings and forgings remain difficult bottlenecks. Its team has placed skilled workers at supplier factories; management said output at those suppliers had improved roughly 30% year over year over the prior 30-45 days, though about 70 suppliers still missed quality or delivery targets. [13]

Boeing - the negative counter-evidence

Boeing CEO Kelly Ortberg said stabilizing 737 MAX production at 47 aircraft per month is taking longer than expected because wing production has not consistently supported the target. The 787 ramp to 10 per month is also taking longer, in part because of engine constraints and seat-certification timing. Boeing now expects roughly $2 billion of 2026 free cash flow rather than reaching the $3 billion upside case. [14]

Interpretation: the aerospace demand thesis remains strong, but investors should not confuse backlog with frictionless earnings conversion. The supply chain is still the dominant near-term risk. For GE Aerospace, slower OEM ramps can delay engine deliveries, while the large installed base and low retirements can simultaneously support aftermarket demand.

Lockheed Martin - a favorable research-universe update

Lockheed used Laguna to emphasize a shift toward mission integration, digital insertion and multi-year contracting. Management highlighted a new long-term agreement for the Joint Advanced Tactical Missile and discussed combining high-end platforms with counter-UAS, autonomous and commercial technologies. [15]

The core investment read-through is improved demand visibility: long-term agreements give both Lockheed and smaller suppliers greater confidence to add plant, equipment and labor. The key offset is execution discipline—production scaling must translate into margin and cash flow rather than simply higher backlog.

4. Autonomy, Freight & Macro Read-Through

UBER / XOM / economic signal

Aurora - relevant to UBER as ecosystem optionality

Aurora described the commercial transition from technology development toward industrialization. Its second-generation hardware is designed to support roughly 1,500 tractors. The company expects to transition during 2027 from a trucking-as-a-service model toward Driver-as-a-Service, with the majority of vehicles expected to be customer-owned and operated by the end of 2027. [16] [17]

Aurora's disclosed ecosystem includes Uber and Uber Freight alongside FedEx, PACCAR, Volvo, Ryder, Schneider and others. [16]

UBER read-through: constructive but not thesis-changing. The strategic idea that UBER can serve as a network / orchestration layer around multiple autonomous providers remains plausible. Laguna did not provide enough evidence to alter near-term UBER earnings assumptions.

Union Pacific vs. J.B. Hunt - same fuel shock, different economics

Union Pacific said current-quarter carloads were up about 5%, with strength not limited to intermodal. Management said the fuel-efficiency gap between rail and truck is beginning to drive additional conversions to rail and that customer order books and inventories remain supportive, while acknowledging the risk of eventual demand destruction if fuel remains elevated. [18] [19]

J.B. Hunt, by contrast, warned that rising fuel and driver-related costs were pressuring near-term earnings even while demand and hiring needs remain strong. The key message is not “freight recession”; it is cost shock and modal redistribution. [20]

5. Portfolio Manager Synthesis

Decision framework

Strongest thesis evidence gained

1. GEV
The conference materially improved visibility into backlog duration, pricing, factory conversion and the service annuity. The business case is stronger; valuation remains the gating variable.
2. GRID / VIS
Multiple suppliers independently validated that AI-related electrical, cooling and automation capex remains broad, physical and capacity constrained.
3. GE Aerospace
Orders and service backlog remain exceptional; CPP is a rational bottleneck response. The risk is still throughput, not lack of demand.
4. LMT
Multi-year procurement and mission integration should improve visibility and enable supply-chain investment, though execution/margin discipline is essential.

Most important contrary evidence

  • Boeing: OEM production stabilization is still taking longer than hoped, demonstrating how hard it is to turn demand into output.
  • Transport: fuel and labor costs can outrun pricing mechanisms, creating short-term earnings misses even with decent demand.
  • Valuation / rates: strong secular growth is less valuable when the 10-year Treasury is above 5%; this matters particularly for GEV and high-duration AI beneficiaries.
  • AI capex concentration: physical orders validate the spending cycle but do not yet prove that hyperscaler returns on invested capital will justify every dollar of future capex.

Bottom line: Laguna did not present evidence of an AI-infrastructure demand rollover. It instead showed a system struggling to build physical capacity fast enough—power generation, grid equipment, electrical distribution, cooling, automation and aerospace components. For your portfolio, that is fundamentally supportive. The next-dollar question remains primarily about valuation, concentration and entry point, not whether the underlying industrial demand exists.
