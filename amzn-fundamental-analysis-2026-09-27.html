1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Amazon is two world-class businesses wearing one ticker, and both just posted some of their best quarters in years. AWS reaccelerated to +37% — its fastest growth in 18 quarters — on $42.2B of revenue, with operating income up 64% at a 39.4% margin, and closed the quarter with a $496B backlog growing triple digits. The retail businesses, long derided as low-margin, now print expanding margins while advertising grew 26% to $19.8B. Total revenue crossed $200B for the first time (+20%), and operating income surged 43% to $27.5B — growing more than twice as fast as revenue.

The market's verdict so far: a selloff. The stock has fallen ~13% from its August high near $287 on one number — full-year 2026 capex guidance raised from ~$200B to ~$220B, which drove quarterly free cash flow to –$7.7B. Wall Street is 66 Buy / 4 Hold with a ~$329 consensus target, Morningstar says fair value is $300 (4 stars) — and the stock sits at $249.38, roughly 25% below consensus. The bear case is a story; the bull case is arithmetic. When a company growing operating income at 43% trades ~25% below what 70 analysts think it is worth, and the "problem" is that it is spending $220B to build the infrastructure its customers are contractually committed to filling, the skew favors the buyer.

Bottom line: Re-initiate at BUY with a $315 12-month target — ~26% price upside. The target is anchored on an estimated ~$10.20 forward clean EPS at 31×, consistent with Amazon's historical growth premium when AWS is reaccelerating. The $62.6B reported net income is a mirage — roughly $50.5B of it is a non-cash Anthropic revaluation — but stripping it reveals a cleaner, stronger earnings machine: the real story is the $27.5B of operating income growing at 43%.
Portfolio note: At 7.63% (~$129,678) Amazon is one of the portfolio's largest positions and its single greatest dollar winner — +$121,742 (+1,534%) on a $15.26 average cost. The BUY call supports the existing full weight; it does not demand adding into a 7.6% position. Standing discipline: accumulate on weakness below ~$225 toward an 8.5% cap; no chasing above ~$270 without a re-underwrite.

2. Business Overview

Amazon operates three reportable segments plus a fourth profit engine embedded inside them. AWS (~21% of revenue, ~61% of operating income) is the world's leading cloud platform. North America retail is the dominant US e-commerce franchise, increasingly a logistics-and-advertising platform. International retail covers the rest of the world at lower margins. And advertising (~$77B annualized run rate) is the highest-margin revenue stream, reported inside the retail segments. Supporting all of it: a logistics network built out under Project Mercury, custom silicon (Trainium), and stakes/partnerships in the frontier AI labs.

4. Segment Analysis

AWS — the engine room

AWS at $42.2B (+37%) delivered its fastest growth in 18 quarters and its fifth consecutive acceleration — a feat essentially unheard of at this scale, where growth normally plateaus. The segment added ~$4.6B quarter-over-quarter, ~80% more than its prior largest sequential jump: a demand step-change, not a trend line. At a $169B annualized run rate, AWS alone would rank roughly 24th on the Fortune 500. Operating income of $16.6B (+64%) at a 39.4% margin means AWS produced ~61% of Amazon's total operating income on ~21% of revenue. The $496B backlog, growing triple digits, is the forward-revenue anchor — enterprise AI commitments with contractual weight behind them.

North America retail — margin expansion in the "low-margin" business

North America revenue grew 16% with operating income of $9.1B at a 7.9% margin — a level the segment could only dream of a few years ago. The drivers are structural: advertising mix (ad dollars fall almost straight to operating income), same-day delivery density from the fulfillment build-out, and Project Mercury's $6.8B commitment to extend same-day to 1,000+ fulfillment centers by 2031, which trades near-term logistics spend for a permanent conversion-and-retention moat.

International retail — profitable and accelerating

International grew 15% with operating income of $1.7B at a 4.1% margin. Still thin, but positive, growing, and — critically — no longer a drag on consolidated margins. The segment is three to five years behind North America on the advertising and logistics-density curve, which is the margin roadmap.

Advertising — the stealth compounder

Advertising revenue of $19.8B (+26%), now running at ~$77B annualized, is Amazon's highest-margin major line and the quiet driver of retail margin expansion. Every point of ad-mix gain is worth roughly a full point of segment margin at this scale. This is also the least capex-intensive growth engine in the company — a valuable counterweight to the $220B infrastructure build.

5. AWS & the AI Capex Cycle

The $220B question is whether Amazon is building cathedrals or capacity. The evidence favors capacity: the AI business and the chips business each run at >$25B annualized run rates with triple-digit growth, and the $496B backlog is the demand receipt. The $20B guide increase is attributed substantially to higher memory-chip costs — a procurement-level signal that AI inference demand has reshaped the data-center bill of materials, not a scope expansion. Bears will note the demand-funding circularity: some of the "demand" comes from AI labs Amazon has itself invested in. That is a real disclosure to watch, but Anthropic's and OpenAI's compute commitments are backed by their own funded roadmaps, and enterprise cloud migration plus AI inference is a far broader base than any single lab.

The honest tension: quarterly free cash flow is now –$7.7B, and TTM FCF is negative for the first time since 2022. If AI demand disappoints against ~$220B/year of committed spend, Amazon owns a lot of very expensive servers. But AWS's reacceleration to +37% with margins expanding to 39.4% is precisely the pattern of demand-led, not supply-led, investment — you raise capex because the backlog is growing triple digits, not in hope of it.

6. Retail Margins & the Advertising Flywheel

The retail story has inverted: the segment once subsidized by AWS now contributes expanding margins of its own. North America's 7.9% operating margin reflects the flywheel working as designed — Prime density → fulfillment utilization → delivery speed → conversion → advertising inventory → higher-margin ad dollars → reinvestment in logistics. Project Mercury's $6.8B same-day expansion is the next turn of that wheel. With advertising at $77B annualized growing 26%, retail operating leverage has a multi-year runway: every 100bps of ad-mix shift moves consolidated margins by roughly 20–30bps, before any fulfillment efficiency gains.

7. The Anthropic & OpenAI Partnerships

Amazon completed its $50B OpenAI investment in July with a final $35B tranche, alongside an expanded AWS agreement worth $100B over eight years; OpenAI committed to 2 GW of Trainium capacity. Combined with the Anthropic position (the source of the Q2 revaluation gain), Amazon has embedded itself in both leading frontier labs — as compute provider, chip supplier, and investor. Strategically, this converts potential disruptors into AWS anchor tenants and validates Trainium as an alternative to merchant GPUs. The financial risk is concentration: a large share of AI compute demand is now tied to a small number of labs. The valuation note is cleaner: exclude the Anthropic mark from any earnings multiple — it is a portfolio gain, not operating performance.

8. Cash Flow & Capital Allocation

There is no dividend and no meaningful buyback — Amazon's capital allocation is a single bet: reinvest everything at high returns. With Q2 capex at $53.1B and the FY26 guide at ~$220B, the company is spending roughly its entire gross cash generation on infrastructure. The balance sheet absorbs it: this is a net-cash, investment-grade fortress choosing growth over payout. The right metric is not FCF this quarter but return on the incremental capex — AWS's 39.4% margins and triple-digit backlog growth argue the returns are there. The discipline question for the Q3 call (October 29) is whether the 2027 capex trajectory moderates as the current build completes, or whether management guides to a new, permanently higher plateau.

9. Competitive Position

Amazon holds the strongest position of the three hyperscalers on the dimension that matters most: demand visibility. AWS's +37% reacceleration and $496B backlog outpace the peer narrative, and the custom-silicon strategy (Trainium) gives it a cost-structure edge Microsoft and Google cannot match one-for-one. In retail, no competitor combines the logistics density, Prime membership base, and advertising platform — Walmart is the only credible challenger, and it competes on a different margin structure. In advertising, Amazon is the clear #3 behind Google and Meta, taking share from both as retail media grows faster than search or social. The partnerships with OpenAI and Anthropic add a layer competitors lack: the two leading labs are AWS customers, chip customers, and investees simultaneously.

10. Risks

  • AI capex overbuild. ~$220B/year of committed spend against AI demand that must keep growing triple digits. If enterprise AI adoption disappoints, utilization — and the AWS margin — compresses fast.
  • Demand circularity. A portion of AI compute demand traces back to labs Amazon has funded. If lab funding tightens, some "backlog" could prove softer than the headline suggests.
  • Regulatory. The FTC monopoly bench trial, rescheduled from October 13, 2026 to February 9, 2027, remains the central legal overhang; an adverse outcome could force structural or behavioral remedies in retail.
  • Q3 guidance conservatism. The $197–202B guide trails the ~$204.1B consensus — a soft Q3 print (October 29) could re-open the "is retail slowing?" debate.
  • Margin mix. The 39.4% AWS margin embeds favorable mix; aggressive price competition from Azure/GCP or Trainium ramp costs could compress it.
  • Valuation sensitivity. At ~34× clean trailing earnings, the multiple assumes the reacceleration sustains. Any AWS deceleration would be punished.
  • Memory-cost inflation. The guide raise was partly memory prices; persistent component inflation raises the capex floor without raising returns.

12. Catalysts & What to Watch

  • Q3 earnings — October 29, 2026 (32 days out): the immediate checkpoint. Watch AWS growth (does +37% hold?), the 2027 capex trajectory, and whether the soft $197–202B guide proves conservative.
  • AWS backlog conversion: each quarter the $496B backlog grows or converts into recognized revenue de-risks the capex thesis.
  • Memory-chip pricing: the driver of the guide raise — stabilization would signal margin relief on the build.
  • FTC trial — February 9, 2027: the date moved from October; monitor pre-trial rulings for settlement signals.
  • Trainium adoption: OpenAI's 2 GW commitment is the proof point — follow-on commitments from other labs would validate the silicon moat.
  • Holiday retail: Q4 North America margins — the read on whether Project Mercury spend is converting to share.
