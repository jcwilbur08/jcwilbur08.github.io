Investment conclusion

Eli Lilly is the single best growth asset in healthcare: Q2 revenue up 48% to $22.97 billion (a $2.3 billion beat), full-year guidance raised to $85–87 billion revenue and $35.50–36.50 non-GAAP EPS, the first non-peptide oral GLP-1 (Foundayo) approved and launching, retatrutide's 28.3% weight loss heading for a Q1 2027 BLA, and manufacturing supply finally normalized after years as the constraint. But $1,143 pays 31x forward earnings for that excellence, consensus upside is only ~15% to $1,312, and the risks are real: Novo's oral Wegovy, Pfizer's $10 billion Metsera bet, Amgen's once-monthly MariTide, and global pricing pressure already baked into guidance (low-to-mid-teens declines). This is the highest-conviction growth name in the sector and the fullest-priced — both can be true. Initiate ADD with a $1,300 twelve-month target (27.9x FY2027 consensus EPS of $46.65). Buy/add gate at or below $1,000.

1. The quarter: +48% revenue and a raised guide

Q2 2026 — reported August 5 · rev $22.97B · non-GAAP EPS $8.38

Lilly's second quarter was a genuine blowout: revenue of $22.974 billion rose 48% year over year and beat consensus by roughly $2.3 billion; non-GAAP EPS of $8.38 (+33%) crushed the ~$6.40 estimate even after $3.03 of acquired IPR&D charges; gross margin printed at 85.8% — software-like economics on molecules. Management raised full-year guidance to $85–87 billion of revenue and $35.50–$36.50 non-GAAP EPS. Mounjaro did $9.943 billion (+91%) and Zepbound $4.928 billion (+46%) — the incretin duo is ~65% of company revenue and still accelerating. When two drugs are two-thirds of a trillion-dollar company and both are growing 46–91%, the quarter speaks for itself.

2. The GLP-1 franchise: injectables, orals, and what's next

Foundayo approved Apr 1 · retatrutide 28.3% · amylin next

Foundayo (orforglipron) is the strategic unlock: FDA-approved April 1, 2026 as the first non-peptide oral GLP-1 — no food or water restrictions — at $25/month commercial and $149 self-pay, with 12.4% weight loss (27 lbs) in ATTAIN-1 and EU approval in September 2026. Early uptake is mixed (about a third of new oral starts per the CEO; ~3,700 scripts in week two) and Q2 sales weren't separately disclosed — the ramp, not the approval, is the 2026 story. A Type 2 diabetes filing is submitted, and October data showed 57% diabetes risk reduction.

The pipeline compounds the lead. Retatrutide delivered 28.3% weight loss over 80 weeks in Phase 3 — a number no competitor has touched — with a BLA planned for Q1 2027 across obesity, sleep apnea, and knee osteoarthritis. The amylin eloralintide hit up to 20.1% in Phase 2, with Phase 3 enrolling and a Zepbound combination reaching 23% (reported September 30). Lilly isn't defending a franchise; it's stacking three successive waves — injectable, oral, triple-agonist/amylin — each aimed at a different patient preference.

3. Beyond obesity: the rest of the portfolio

Verzenio, Jardiance, Ebglyss, Kisunla all contributing

The non-obesity business is quietly strong: Verzenio $1.474 billion (flat, a $5B+ anchor), Jardiance $1.23 billion (+79%), Ebglyss $201 million (+131%), Omvoh $102 million (+36%), Jaypirca $192 million (+56%), Kisunla $167 million (Alzheimer's, building quarter over quarter), and Trulicity $1.22 billion (+12%) still throwing off cash. The Inluriyo/Verzenio combination won FDA approval in late September 2026. Obesity gets the multiple; this diversified base is what makes the multiple survivable if incretin growth ever pauses.

4. Manufacturing: the constraint becomes the moat

>$27B US commitments · supply normalized · +60% volume

For two years Lilly's growth was supply-constrained; that era is ending. The company added $4.5 billion of Indiana commitments in Q2 (total US commitments above $27 billion), opened the Lebanon, Indiana genetic-medicine facility, produced the first commercial batch in Limerick, Ireland, and has $6.5 billion of Houston capacity under construction. Volume rose 60%, and $1.5 billion of Foundayo inventory was pre-built ahead of launch. Manufacturing at this scale — tens of billions of deployed capital — is now a competitive moat: no rival can replicate the capacity in under three years, which is exactly the window Lilly's pipeline needs.

5. Capital allocation

$6.92 dividend · 11 straight raises · $47.8B debt vs $9B cash

The $1.73 quarterly dividend ($6.92 annualized, 0.60% yield) has risen 11 straight years at ~15% CAGR on a modest ~22–23% payout — income is not the thesis, compounding is. Q2 saw $1.6 billion of buybacks against $1.5 billion of dividends; the balance sheet carries $47.8 billion of long-term debt against $9 billion cash after an acquisition-heavy stretch (over $13 billion of H1 2026 acquisition/IPR&D spend). Leverage is elevated for Lilly but serviceable at these margins — the 85.8% gross margin is the shock absorber.

6. Valuation and target framework

31.2x forward · PEG 1.40 · consensus $1,312 · Morningstar $900 (dated)

At $1,142.85, Lilly trades at 38–41x trailing and 31.2x forward earnings, with a PEG of 1.40 and a ~2% forward FCF yield. The PEG is the bull case in one number: 31x earnings for ~22%+ multi-year EPS growth is not cheap, but it isn't absurd either. Consensus averages $1,311.96 (Moderate Buy; UBS $1,425, JPM $1,400, high $1,600); Morningstar's $900 fair value is from May 2026 and predates the entire Foundayo/retatrutide data flow — directionally cautious, numerically stale.

$1,300 twelve-month target (about 14% upside). Framework: 27.9x FY2027 consensus EPS of $46.65 — a premium multiple for premium growth, below today's 31x to reflect the pricing and competitive risks crystallizing over the next year. The target sits just under consensus ($1,312): we underwrite the pipeline, not multiple expansion. Retatrutide's BLA (Q1 2027) and the Foundayo ramp are the upside options not in the number.

7. Portfolio fit and recommended gate

New position · the growth anchor for the healthcare sleeve

Lilly is not currently held. In the healthcare sleeve taking shape — VHT as core, BMY/UNH/GEHC as single names — Lilly is the growth anchor: the one name with genuine 40%+ revenue growth and a multi-wave pipeline. But 31x forward with Q3 earnings around October 29 is not an entry to chase; the stock is 12% off its high and the competitive headlines (Novo, Metsera, MariTide) will keep coming.

Recommended gate: LLY ADD at or below $1,000. At $1,000 the forward multiple drops to ~27x FY2026E (~21x FY2027E) — still a premium, but a premium with a margin of safety, ~$300 above Morningstar's (stale) $900 fair value. Size the starter toward ~1% of NAV (about 15 shares / ~$15,000 at the gate). Break below $900 on fundamental news means reassess, not buy. Gate recommendation only — not installed in the dashboard panel pending CIO approval.

8. Risks and catalysts

Competition · pricing · Q3 around Oct 29

Risks: (1) Competition — Novo's oral Wegovy, Pfizer's $10B Metsera bet (Phase 3 late 2026), Amgen's once-monthly MariTide (~20% Phase 2 loss), plus Roche/Viking/AstraZeneca/Regeneron. Note Novo's CagriSema just failed non-inferiority vs tirzepatide (23% vs 25.5%) — the moat is holding, but the siege is real. (2) Pricing — guidance already bakes in low-to-mid-teens global pricing declines; IRA/MFN/TrumpRx pressure can always get worse. (3) Multiple — 31x forward compresses hard on any growth disappointment; Foundayo's ramp is the near-term tripwire.

Catalysts: Q3 earnings around October 29; orforglipron Type 2 diabetes decision timeline; eloralintide Phase 3/combo data; retatrutide BLA in Q1 2027; EMBER-4 in 2027.
