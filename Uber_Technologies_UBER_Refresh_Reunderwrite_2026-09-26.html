2. Business quality and competitive position

Uber's core advantage is the density of a global, multi-product marketplace rather than any single mobility product. Q2 reached 208 million monthly active platform consumers and 3.9 billion trips, while Gross Bookings grew 24% reported and 22% in constant currency. The operating model is now showing durable leverage: adjusted EBITDA grew 33% to $2.8B and non-GAAP operating income grew 40% to $2.1B.

Mobility

Q2 Gross Bookings grew about 20% in prepared remarks, with segment operating-income margin reaching 7.6%. Premium offerings and Uber for Business are outgrowing the core product, while expansion into suburban and lower-density markets remains profitable.

Delivery

Delivery Gross Bookings grew about 25%, segment operating-income margin reached a record 3.8%, and Grocery & Retail reached a $15B annualized bookings run rate growing roughly 40% YoY. Uber One members now represent more than 70% of Delivery Gross Bookings.

Advertising / platform monetization

Advertising exceeded a $2.5B annualized revenue run rate in Q2 and was still growing roughly 50% YoY. This is important because advertising can grow revenue and profit without a proportional increase in trip or delivery fulfillment cost.

Local commerce expansion

September partnerships added nearly 600 Costco locations across 47 states and more than 375 Wakefern stores. These do not alter near-term earnings by themselves, but they reinforce the selection flywheel behind Grocery & Retail.

Moat assessment

  • Demand aggregation: a very large installed consumer base improves utilization and reduces customer-acquisition friction for new supply.
  • Cross-platform engagement: Mobility, Delivery, membership and advertising create more use cases and more monetizable consumer intent than a single-product ride-hailing network.
  • Supply density and local operations: marketplace liquidity, regulatory relationships, payments, insurance, routing and support are difficult to reproduce globally.
  • Capital-light economics - with an AV caveat: the historical model scales without owning the underlying vehicles; AV commitments create a new test of whether that return profile can be preserved.
Interpretation: UBER's quality has improved because the question is no longer whether it can produce durable free cash flow. The harder question is whether management can deploy that cash into Delivery Hero and autonomous mobility without lowering returns on invested capital.

4. Management, capital allocation and balance sheet

Management has earned credibility on cost discipline and cash conversion, but capital allocation has become more complex. At Q2, Uber had $5.4B of unrestricted cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments, more than $10B of trailing free cash flow, and approximately $16B of remaining share-repurchase authorization. Diluted share count has continued to fall.

Buybacks

~$16B

Authorization remaining at Q2. Continued share-count decline is an important check that SBC is not overwhelming repurchases.

Delivery Hero

$14.8B

Offer equity value; transaction expected to be non-GAAP EPS accretive on close and high-single-digit accretive by year three.

AV commitments

>$10B

Expected multi-year commitments across equity, infrastructure and vehicle offtake. Third-party financing is supposed to preserve flexibility.

The Delivery Hero offer is expected to be funded with existing cash plus new debt, with a committed bridge facility of about EUR14B and management targeting gross leverage below 2x while retaining investment-grade credit quality. That is reasonable given current free cash flow, but it changes the risk profile: buybacks, M&A and AV capital all compete for the same cash.

Capital-allocation risk: The biggest underappreciated downside is not a sudden collapse in ride demand; it is a gradual deterioration in returns if Uber simultaneously pays for global delivery consolidation and funds too much AV fleet/infrastructure exposure. The thesis requires external capital and partner economics to keep AV commercialization from turning Uber into an asset-heavy fleet owner.

5. Autonomous vehicles: improving proof, unresolved economics

AV execution has moved from press-release optionality toward real-world deployment. Uber and Wayve launched supervised rides in London on Sept. 3, while Uber's multi-partner model already includes deployments in other cities. In Q2 remarks, management said AVs were live in seven cities, targeted as many as 15 by year-end, and that partners had committed approximately 120,000 vehicles to the Uber network over coming years.

Why this helps the thesis

  • Each launch is evidence that AV developers see value in Uber's demand aggregation, marketplace dispatch, local operations and customer interface.
  • A multi-partner network reduces reliance on any single autonomous-driving stack.
  • Uber can potentially monetize utilization and distribution without needing to win the core autonomous-driving technology race.

Why the risk remains real

  • Wayve's new Mercedes partnership validates the technology but also demonstrates that AV suppliers can distribute through multiple OEM and platform relationships.
  • Waymo, Zoox, Bolt and others are building direct or alternative distribution networks. Uber cannot assume it will capture today's ride-hailing take rate on autonomous trips.
  • Uber expects more than $10B of multi-year AV commitments. If external financing does not materialize on attractive terms, the business can become more capital intensive.
Decision hinge: The critical question is not “Do robotaxis threaten Uber?” It is “Can Uber be the highest-utilization commercialization layer for multiple AV stacks while earning attractive returns on any capital it contributes?” Evidence is improving, but that question is not yet settled.

6. Delivery Hero: strategic upside with execution and leverage risk

Uber's EUR41.50-per-share tender offer values Delivery Hero at about $14.8B of equity value, or $13.7B adjusted for Uber's prior stake purchases. The combination would extend Uber's multi-product platform to 99 markets, with $236B of combined pro forma 2025 Gross Bookings. Delivery Hero's boards have recommended shareholders accept the offer.

7. Industry, regulatory and macro context

Competitive intensity

DoorDash remains a formidable delivery competitor and trades at a materially higher valuation. Lyft is cheaper but lacks Uber's global multi-product breadth. Grab offers a multi-service model in Southeast Asia, but at smaller scale and with less mature cash generation.

Labor and local regulation

NYC's Sept. 22 DoorDash enforcement action raises the salience of delivery-worker pay and app-design compliance. Reuters noted it was unclear whether Uber Eats and Grubhub were complying with the same laws. Treat this as a regulatory watch item rather than an Uber-specific loss estimate.

Rates

The Fed raised the policy target to 3.75%-4.00% on Sept. 16. The Treasury's Sept. 25 curve showed the 10-year at 5.17%, up from 4.96% on Sept. 21. Higher long rates raise the return hurdle for a growth equity even when operating estimates are rising.

Consumer sensitivity

Mobility is relatively resilient but not recession-proof; Delivery, premium rides and advertising have more discretionary exposure. Current guidance still indicates healthy demand.

9. Market expectations and what is priced in

S&P Global's analyst set shows 50 analysts, a consensus Buy rating and a $101.24 average target, with a low of $70 and a high of $150. More recent individual calls remain mixed around the edges: Bank of America maintained Buy with a $101 target on Sept. 21; Citizens maintained a $100 target on Sept. 25; Arete had downgraded to Hold earlier in September.

What the market appears to be pricing in: the current valuation suggests investors are skeptical that 20%+ bookings growth and 30%+ EPS growth can persist while Uber simultaneously funds Delivery Hero and autonomous mobility. In my view, some AV disruption and transaction risk are already discounted, but not a severe structural loss of marketplace relevance. That distinction matters because the bear case requires more than Waymo or other AV players growing; it requires those networks to materially bypass Uber and impair Uber's economics.

10. Bull case, bear case and contrary evidence

Bull evidence

  • Q2 bookings +22% CC and non-GAAP EPS +35%.
  • TTM FCF exceeded $10B and share count is declining.
  • Delivery margin reached a record while Grocery & Retail grew ~40%.
  • Advertising run rate exceeded $2.5B and grew ~50%.
  • AV partners are launching on Uber in multiple geographies, supporting the commercialization-layer thesis.
  • Forward P/E and P/FCF are well below 2024-25 levels.

Strongest contrary evidence

  • Uber plans >$10B of multi-year AV commitments, challenging the capital-light narrative.
  • AV partners can multi-home or build direct channels, limiting Uber's long-run take rate.
  • Delivery Hero is a large cross-border acquisition with leverage, regulatory and integration risk.
  • 5%+ long yields can keep growth-equity multiples compressed even if earnings meet estimates.
  • Local labor regulation and worker-pay enforcement remain structural cost risks.
  • The workbook's $76 fair value is much less optimistic than the sell-side consensus.

12. Catalysts and key checkpoints

  1. Q3 2026 earnings - estimated Nov. 3: Gross Bookings should remain within or above the 18%-22% CC guide and non-GAAP EPS should meet or exceed $0.84-$0.88.
  2. Delivery Hero tender - Nov. 5: acceptance and regulatory progress should materially reduce transaction uncertainty.
  3. Margin progression: Mobility and Delivery segment operating-income margins should continue expanding without a material resurgence in rider/driver incentives.
  4. FCF / share count: TTM FCF should remain near or above $10B and diluted shares should keep declining despite SBC and strategic investment.
  5. AV economics: more city launches should come with evidence of utilization, economics and third-party financing - not just vehicle-count announcements.
  6. Grocery, retail and advertising: selection partnerships should translate into sustained bookings growth and advertising should continue to outgrow the core platform.
  7. Balance-sheet discipline: Delivery Hero financing and AV commitments should not push leverage above management's stated framework or crowd out economically attractive buybacks.

13. Explicit thesis-break conditions

  • Two consecutive quarters of constant-currency Gross Bookings growth below roughly 15% without a clearly temporary external shock.
  • TTM free cash flow falling below roughly $8B because of recurring operating deterioration rather than working-capital timing.
  • Diluted share count beginning to rise year over year despite material repurchases, indicating SBC or acquisition dilution is overwhelming buybacks.
  • Major AV partners increasingly bypassing Uber while Uber must fund or own fleets at scale without a credible path to attractive returns on invested capital.
  • Delivery growth falling to low-teens levels while segment margins contract, undermining the second-engine thesis.
  • Delivery Hero financing or integration causing a sustained leverage or cash-return deterioration materially worse than the current below-2x gross-leverage framework.
  • Driver-classification, worker-pay or labor rulings in major markets creating a sustained material margin reset, supply contraction or demand-damaging price increase.

14. Research conclusion and Portfolio CIO handoff

Research conclusion: Favorable. Uber's business quality is improving faster than the current valuation implies: bookings growth remains around 20%, margins are expanding, TTM free cash flow exceeds $10B, and the stock trades at a significant discount to its own recent forward P/E and to DoorDash. The principal debate has shifted from “Can Uber become sustainably profitable?” to “Can Uber preserve high returns while consolidating delivery and funding AV commercialization?”

Valuation conclusion: Attractive at $69.62, but I would use a lower multiple than the Sept. 22 report because the 10-year Treasury is above 5% and the capital-allocation burden has increased. My updated base value is about $95 and scenario-weighted value about $91. The prior $105 target is still mathematically reachable if FY2027 EPS reaches $4.41 and the market assigns roughly 24x earnings, but that multiple is no longer my base assumption.

CIO review item: UBER's authoritative thesis status remains unclassified in agent state. Because the position has risen to 70 shares, the Portfolio CIO should formally decide whether to classify it as a Working thesis and establish a position-size framework tied to the Nov. 3 earnings checkpoint, Nov. 5 Delivery Hero tender milestone and ongoing AV capital-efficiency evidence.

Open questions requiring additional work

  • What portion of the >$10B AV commitment is true at-risk Uber capital versus externally financed or contingent offtake?
  • What is the most realistic steady-state take rate on autonomous trips if AV developers maintain both first-party and Uber distribution?
  • How much Delivery Hero synergy is already embedded in FY2027 consensus EPS, and how much incremental financing cost is reflected?
  • Will NYC's delivery-worker enforcement create a material Uber Eats cost or restitution exposure, or is it primarily a competitor-specific issue?
