§0 — The one-paragraph version, and a note on method

This report compares three reallocation candidates for capital currently held in Qualcomm (QCOM), which faces a real, structural headwind: Apple's in-house C2 modem is arriving faster than expected, and QCOM's Apple-related revenue (an estimated $5.7-5.9B/year) is guided to fall roughly 50% sequentially in the December 2026 quarter. Rather than analyze each candidate in isolation, this report runs the same five-year scenario methodology — bear/base/bull revenue growth, margin, and exit-multiple assumptions, each producing a 5-year IRR — across all three names, on the same pricing date (close, September 18, 2026), so the resulting numbers are genuinely comparable rather than three separate reports stitched together. NVDA was already given a full dedicated fundamental-analysis report in this repo on September 19, 2026 (rated Hold — add only on weakness); this report summarizes that analysis and folds its numbers into the head-to-head comparison rather than re-deriving them from scratch. ASML and TSM receive full parallel treatment here. The headline finding: TSM's numbers are the best of the three, but its Taiwan risk is the hardest of the three to actually price — which is itself the central judgment call this report asks you to make, not a detail to skim past.

1. Business models, side by side

ASML — the lithography monopoly

Sole global supplier of EUV lithography systems — the machines that print the smallest features on leading-edge and advanced-memory chips. No second source exists. Revenue: hardware sales plus a large, growing Installed Base Management (service/upgrade) stream. FY26E revenue ~€44B (~$50.6B), +34.6% guided.

TSM — the sole leading-edge foundry

The contract manufacturer that actually builds the chips designed by NVIDIA, Apple, AMD, Broadcom, and Qualcomm, among others, using (in part) ASML's machines. No competitor — not Samsung, not Intel Foundry — matches its leading-edge yield and capacity at scale. FY26E revenue ~$171B, +41% guided ("slightly above 40%").

NVDA — the AI-compute leader (already covered)

Sells the GPUs, networking, and CUDA software stack that AI labs and hyperscalers rent compute from — a toll on the entire AI capex cycle at 70%+ gross margin. FY26 revenue $215.9B, +65.5%. See the September 19, 2026 report in this repo for full detail; summarized throughout this comparison.

All three sit in the same physical supply chain, in a specific order: ASML's machines enable TSM's fabs to print the chips NVIDIA (and others) design. A dollar of AI-driven chip demand flows through all three companies, just captured at different points and different margins. That is the central reason this comparison matters — these are not three independent bets, they are three different points of leverage on the same underlying demand curve.

5. Financial deep dive — strip the noise, all three

Each of the three names in this comparison had a distinct, real, findable "noise" item in its most recent quarter — and none of them, once stripped out, changes the underlying conclusion that all three businesses are performing well. The value here is in seeing how different the noise looks company to company.

ASML — an easy-comp growth framing, not a fake number

FY26's guided 34.6% YoY growth compares against FY24's unusually soft 2.5% growth. The clean two-year stacked CAGR (FY24→FY26E) is 24.7%/yr — still excellent, still a real re-acceleration, just less dramatic than the single-year headline.

TSM — a clean, one-off asset-sale gain

Finding — Of Q2 FY26's NT$706.56B headline net income (+77.4% YoY), NT$63.2B (9%) was a one-time gain on partially selling down TSM's stake in Vanguard International Semiconductor (VIS). Excluding it, net income still grew ~62% YoY — an outstanding quarter either way, and the least ambiguous adjustment of the three companies in this report.

NVDA — a recurring equity-marks swing (already documented, Sep 19 report)

FY26 headline diluted EPS of $4.88 included roughly $0.28/share (5.6%) from a swing in "Other income, net" — largely gains on NVIDIA's growing book of equity stakes in AI-ecosystem companies. Unlike TSM's clean one-off, this is a recurring feature of NVIDIA's business model going forward as it continues taking stakes in customers, making it structurally harder to treat as a pure one-time item.

The one red flag that deserves its own callout, for this comparison specifically — It would be a mistake to read TSM's superior modeled IRRs (§7) as meaning TSM is simply the better risk-adjusted choice. A smooth scenario model is bad at pricing binary, low-frequency, high-severity events — which is exactly the shape of Taiwan-China risk. ASML's and NVDA's core risks, while serious, are more continuous and more observable in real time (export-license data, OpenAI revenue disclosures) than a geopolitical crisis that could move from "quarantine risk assessment" to "actual disruption" over a matter of weeks. Treat TSM's -9.5%/yr modeled bear case as a floor on the ordinary-business-risk scenario, not a ceiling on what could actually happen.

Portfolio fit and the reallocation verdict

Reallocating out of QCOM into any of the three removes a real, structural, company-specific problem (the Apple-modem cliff) in exchange for exposure to the broader AI-infrastructure capex theme already represented in this portfolio by AVGO, MSFT, and SOXQ. That trade-off is the same regardless of which name is chosen — the differentiator is which flavor of AI-capex exposure, and which risk, the account owner is most comfortable holding for a 10-year horizon.

This report's recommendation is to split the reallocation between TSM and ASML rather than concentrate it in one name — TSM for its superior modeled numbers and its uniquely broad exposure across the entire chip-design customer base (it does not depend on any single AI lab's solvency the way NVDA increasingly does), sized with the explicit understanding that its Taiwan risk is a real, unhedged tail the model above does not fully capture; ASML as the lower-beta, more transparently-risk-priced complement, whose monopoly is arguably even more durable than TSM's process leadership precisely because no competitor exists at all, not just none at comparable scale. NVDA should not be added fresh alongside this reallocation — it is already covered, already rated Hold, and already carries both the widest scenario spread and the most idiosyncratic single-counterparty risk of the four names discussed in this report; adding it now would mean taking on three AI-capex-adjacent names in one move without a differentiated reason to prefer it over the two better risk-adjusted alternatives already selected.

Three things to watch

  • Any concrete escalation or de-escalation in Taiwan-China tensions — the single highest-impact, least-modelable variable across this entire comparison.
  • ASML's Q3 FY26 earnings (October 14, 2026) and TSM's next quarterly print — both will show whether the string of 2026 guidance raises continues or finally pauses.
  • NVDA's OpenAI-related disclosures (per the Sep 19 report) — the earliest observable signal of whether that report's Hold rating should be revisited before any future decision to add it fresh.

Bottom line

Reduce QCOM. Split the proceeds between TSM and ASML. Leave NVDA as a hold, not a fresh add. All three candidates are better businesses, at better risk-adjusted prices, than continuing to hold QCOM through a known, structural, multi-quarter revenue cliff in its most profitable segment. TSM has the best numbers on paper; ASML has the most legible risk. Splitting between them captures most of the upside this comparison identifies while avoiding a single-name bet on how well a five-year financial model can really price a geopolitical tail.
