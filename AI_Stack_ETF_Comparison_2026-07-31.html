Executive verdict

GRID is the strongest standalone choice because it captures the physical bottleneck in the AI buildout—electrical equipment, transmission, grid modernization and power-management infrastructure—without simply recreating a mega-cap technology portfolio.

Recommended thematic sleeve

60% GRID / 40% SOXQ. GRID supplies the differentiated grid-to-rack exposure; SOXQ supplies the high-beta compute layer. The pairing is intentionally aggressive and should be sized as a satellite, not a core retirement allocation.

Why not POWR yet?

POWR's current U.S. power-infrastructure mandate began on October 29, 2025. Its reported 1-, 3-, 5- and 10-year records include the former global energy-producers strategy and are not valid evidence for the present mandate.

Fit, sizing and explicit decision rules

Preferred implementation

  • Use the theme as a satellite sleeve rather than a substitute for broad U.S. and international equity diversification.
  • Within that sleeve, use 60% GRID / 40% SOXQ.
  • Stage purchases because both funds have experienced rapid multiple expansion and sharp short-term moves.
  • Rebalance annually or when either fund moves more than 10 percentage points from target.

Why this fits the stated profile

  • A 10-year horizon can absorb cyclical drawdowns.
  • High risk tolerance supports a semiconductor component.
  • GRID reduces dependence on the same mega-cap software/platform names already common in broad indexes.
  • The pairing expresses two distinct bottlenecks rather than four partially redundant narratives.

Principal risks and additional factors

  • Valuation and expectations: GRID and SOXQ can fall despite earnings growth when multiples compress or AI-capex expectations reset.
  • Cycle mismatch: semiconductor demand moves faster than utility rate-base growth and transmission projects. The two layers may not peak together.
  • Policy and permitting: grid investment can be delayed by permitting, interconnection queues, rate cases, tariffs and local opposition.
  • Concentration: GRID's top five were about 42% and SOXQ's top five about 38% at the cited dates.
  • Geopolitics: AIQ and SOXQ have direct Taiwan, South Korea and Netherlands exposure; supply chains remain globally interdependent.
  • Tracking and methodology: theme definitions and rebalances can materially change exposure. POWR demonstrates why mandate continuity matters.
  • Income: none of the four is primarily an income vehicle; yields are low and AIQ's current SEC yield is negative.
