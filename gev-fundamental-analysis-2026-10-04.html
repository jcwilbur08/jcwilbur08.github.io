01 | News Flow

What changed since September 30

Four days, one corporate action, and a sentiment repricing. The news flow was modestly positive on fundamentals; the price action was bigger than the news.

Sep 30 — Dividend declared

The board declared a $0.50 per share quarterly dividend, payable November 24, 2026 to holders of record October 27, 2026 — roughly a 0.20% forward yield at the current price. Not a thesis driver; a signal of balance-sheet confidence alongside $3.9B returned to shareholders year-to-date.

Oct 1 — Market reaction

Shares rose 3.8% on the session, traded as high as $1,003.78, and closed near $986 on volume of about 2.94 million shares — roughly 11% above average. The dividend news gave the market a reason to re-rate what it already knew, and the stock reclaimed its 50-day moving average.

Oct 1 — Evercore reaffirmed Outperform, $1,350

Evercore restated its outperform rating with a $1,350 target — the second-highest on the Street after Guggenheim's $1,450. Bullish sell-side momentum continues: Bernstein ($1,298), Jefferies ($1,185, raised), and the September Goldman upgrade to market outperform all sit well above the current price.

Early Oct — Springfield City Utilities order

GE Vernova secured an order from Springfield City Utilities (Missouri) for three LM6000VELOX aeroderivative gas turbine packages to expand capacity at the 100-MW McCartney Generating Station. The units add about 150 MW of dual-fuel (natural gas with fuel-oil backup) capacity, expected online in 2027, driven by a sharply higher planning reserve margin requirement (36% winter vs. 15% today). Small in dollars, large in signal: municipal utilities are now buying fast-start aeroderivatives for reliability — another leg of the demand story.

Early Oct — Texas HA + BWRX-300 hybrid (industry reporting)

Industry reporting notes a Texas hybrid plant pairing HA-class gas turbines with BWRX-300 SMRs in modular builds to deliver fast baseload power for data centers. This is the gas-plus-nuclear optionality thesis in a single project: near-term revenue from turbines, long-dated option value from the SMR — and it follows the September 29 NRC construction permit at Clinch River.

The bear call stands: GLJ Research, Sell / $470 (Sep 14)

Nothing in the last four days addressed the margin-vintage thesis, so it stands as the articulated bear case: turbines delivering in 2027 were ordered in 2024, before recent price increases, implying roughly 3 points of backlog margin on the 2027 vintage versus 10–11 points on the 2028 vintage. GLJ's 2027 EBITDA estimate of $7.42B sits about 22% below the roughly $9.5B consensus, and it values the stock at 14 times its 2027 EBITDA. The Q2 EPS miss — $2.47 reported vs. $3.17 consensus, a 22% shortfall on a revenue beat — gives the vintage question more bite, not less. Management's commentary on 2027 delivery margins, slot-reservation conversion, and pricing on 2031 slots will be read directly against this thesis on October 28. A confident answer is a catalyst; an evasive one is a de-rating event.

Why the bear call matters even if you disagree. The GLJ thesis is now the central debate into the print, and the stock's 4% dividend bounce did nothing to resolve it. If anything, the higher price raises the bar for the rebuttal: at 64x 2026 earnings, the margin for an inconclusive answer is thinner than it was at $949.

Sell-side scoreboard

Consensus remains constructive: 35 analysts tracked by one provider break down to 2 Strong Buy, 26 Buy, 5 Hold, and 2 Sell — a Moderate Buy — with an average target of about $1,172, roughly 19% above the current price. My $1,100 target sits below the consensus average — deliberately, because the Buy rating reflects a full multiple more than a broken thesis. Evercore's October 1 reaffirmation at $1,350 keeps the bullish end of the range anchored; GLJ's $470 keeps the bearish end honest.

The second quarter itself was strong on the top line and soft on the bottom line: orders of $24.2B (up 88% organically), revenue of $11.1B (up 22%), adjusted EBITDA of $1.25B at an 11.3% margin, diluted EPS of $2.47 (a $0.70 miss), and free cash flow of $5.1B in the quarter alone. Cash ended the quarter at $13.1B.

The backlog engine

Remaining performance obligations stood at $176B at quarter-end, up $13B sequentially, and management told investors in mid-September it expects backlog to surpass $200B in early 2027, with $100B of cumulative future services revenue by the end of 2027. Services are more than 55% of the backlog — the high-margin, recurring layer of the business.

In gas turbines specifically, the company has 116 GW under contract (53 GW firm backlog plus 63 GW of slot reservation agreements), added 20 GW in Q2, converted about 10 GW of reservations to firm orders, and is targeting at least 125 GW by year-end 2026 while already reserving 2031–2032 delivery slots. Heavy-duty gas equipment orders quadrupled in Q2 with 52 units including 15 HA-class machines. The Springfield order adds another aeroderivative proof point: smaller, fast-start units for grid reliability are a distinct demand leg from the large-frame data-center build.

Wind remains the swing factor. A $275M quarterly EBITDA loss against a ~$400M full-year guide leaves little room for slippage. The Vineyard Wind truce removes legal risk, not operating risk — U.S. onshore demand softness, permitting delays, and tariffs are the binding constraints. Any Q3 commentary suggesting the loss guide is at risk would pressure the stock regardless of Power strength.

Tariffs: an unresolved discrepancy

Two company-sourced figures do not obviously reconcile: Reuters' Q2 reporting put 2026 tariff costs at roughly $100–200M, while the CFO guided to a $250–350M net tariff impact for 2026 on the Q1 call, described as fully built into the outlook. The difference may be definitional or a Q2 revision — either way, the October 28 call needs to settle it.

Risks and catalysts into year-end

Key risks
  • 2027 margin vintage. If 2027 deliveries were priced in 2024 at thin margins, consensus EBITDA is 20%+ too high — the GLJ thesis. Q2's EPS miss adds weight to the question.

Catalysts
  • Q3 beat + 2027 margin rebuttal — the single biggest upside event.

  • Backlog crossing $200B in early 2027 — a psychological milestone.

  • Buybacks — $3.9B returned YTD; continued support under the stock.
