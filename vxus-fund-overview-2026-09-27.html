1. Executive Summary & Verdict

VXUS is the closest thing to a perfect international-equity building block: one ticker, ~8,700 stocks across 40+ countries, the FTSE Global All Cap ex US Index tracked to within basis points, at an expense ratio of 0.05% — roughly one-sixteenth of what the average foreign large-cap fund charges. In a year when non-US equities have finally caught up — the fund is up 27.4% over the last twelve months through mid-2026 — it is doing exactly the job it was hired for: diluting Cam's overwhelming US concentration with the rest of the world's equity risk premium, in a vehicle cheap enough that fees are a non-factor.

The portfolio case for KEEP rests on exclusivity. Outside of the ~29.5% international sleeve inside the Nuveen Lifecycle Index 2045 fund and two individual non-US stocks (TSM, ASML), VXUS is Cam's only standalone international holding. Combined, his explicit international exposure totals roughly 5.6% of the portfolio — thin for a retirement book by any conventional yardstick. There is no redundancy to prune here and no cheaper substitute to switch to. The fund earns its 2.7% weight on cost, diversification, and tracking alone.

Bottom line — KEEP. VXUS is best-in-class on cost and tracking, fills a role nothing else in the book fills, and Cam's total international weight (~5.6% including the target-date sleeve) is modest, not bloated. No trim, no replacement. The open question is the opposite one: whether ~5.6% international is enough for a $1.7M retirement portfolio — a sizing discussion for Cam, not a verdict change.

2. Objective & Index Tracked

The fund employs an indexing approach designed to track the FTSE Global All Cap ex US Index — a float-adjusted, market-capitalization-weighted benchmark covering approximately 98% of the world's non-US investable equity markets, spanning developed and emerging markets across Europe, the Pacific, and the Americas. It uses full replication: the fund generally holds the same stocks as the index in approximately the same proportions.

3. Costs

Cost is VXUS's sharpest edge. The ETF share class charges 0.05% — Vanguard having cut it from 0.07% — against a foreign large-cap category average of about 0.81%. On Cam's ~$46,111 position that is roughly $23 per year in expenses, versus ~$373 per year at the category average — a ~$350 annual savings for identical market exposure. Portfolio turnover runs around 8%, and the ETF structure keeps capital-gain distributions rare, preserving tax efficiency in a taxable account.

5. Performance & Tracking

Tracking is exemplary. For the period ended June 30, 2026, the fund's NAV returns ran within a few basis points of the benchmark at every horizon — the residual difference is essentially the fee drag, which is the entire point of a 0.05% expense ratio. The standout is the trailing year: 27.42% vs 26.74% for the index, as a weaker dollar and reviving Chinese and European equities finally gave ex-US holders a payoff year after a long stretch of US dominance.

8. Risks / What Could Change the Verdict

  • Currency drag. The fund is unhedged; a renewed strong-dollar regime would suppress reported returns even if underlying companies perform — the same mechanism that punished ex-US holders for most of the 2010s.
  • Emerging-markets weight (~25%). China and Taiwan are top country exposures; a Taiwan Strait escalation or renewed China regulatory crackdown would hit the two largest single names (TSMC, Tencent) and the EM sleeve together.
  • Europe concentration (~38%). Sluggish European growth, energy costs, or political fragmentation can drag the whole fund even when Asia performs.
  • Tracking/structural. Vanguard's full-replication approach has delivered basis-point tracking; a methodology change or sustained premium/discount in the ETF share class would be the operational red flag — none is present.
  • What changes the verdict: a material fee increase, an index change that narrows the mandate, or a deliberate CIO decision to run a 100% US book (not recommended). Absent those, the KEEP stands.
