01 · Investment thesis

A flawless business. A merely fair investment.

The 5/10 conviction score is about the stock at $946.92—not the quality of Costco’s franchise. Membership economics, renewal behavior, merchandise velocity and cash generation remain best in class. The valuation already capitalizes much of that excellence.

The bull case is operational

Paid memberships reached 84.1 million, U.S./Canada renewal rose to 92.3%, executive membership grew 9.4%, and digitally enabled Q4 comparable sales advanced 19.5%. September sales then accelerated, with adjusted comparable sales up 7.6%.

The bear case is mathematical

At roughly 44–46× trailing earnings, Costco trades close to its own five-year average of 46.8× and far above retail peers. There is no historical valuation discount to compensate for execution, policy or multiple risk.

Desk verdict

Hold · 5/10 · $1,050 target.

The target implies 10.9% price upside. High-quality growth can support a premium, but the current premium leaves too little margin of safety for a higher rating.

Quality is not valuation protection

Morningstar’s last verified fair value was $650 with a 1-star rating—31% below the reference price, or the market price 46% above fair value. Even allowing for Morningstar’s structurally cautious stance on Costco, that gap is too large to dismiss.

02 · Business model

The membership flywheel is the moat.

Costco trades merchandise margin for trust, volume and renewal. A narrow assortment, bulk purchasing and low markups create savings that members can see—then annual fees monetize the loyalty.

Low-price discipline

Roughly 4,000 core SKUs and merchandise markups near 14–15% keep the value proposition legible and inventory turns fast.

Recurring fee economics

Gold Star costs $65; Executive costs $130 and offers a 2% reward. Fiscal 2026 membership fee income reached $5.907 billion.

Private-label leverage

Kirkland Signature represents an estimated 28% to roughly one-third of sales, typically priced 15–20% below national-brand equivalents.

Scale without format drift

Costco ended fiscal 2026 with 939 warehouses, including 647 in the U.S. and Puerto Rico and 115 in Canada. Management plans 33 openings in fiscal 2027, including five relocations, moving toward a long-run pace near 30 net additions annually.

Full-year operating momentum

Fiscal 2026 net sales rose 10.1% to $297.247 billion. Net income increased roughly 14% to $9.226 billion, while operating cash flow reached $15.825 billion.

September stayed strong

Five-week September sales rose 13.0% to $30.02 billion. Adjusted comparable sales were up 7.6%, with digital sales up 19.1% excluding FX. A later Labor Day added just over 50 bps.

04 · Outlook

Growth has multiple lanes, but no formal guide.

Management did not issue fiscal 2027 sales or margin targets. The operating agenda is still clear: add warehouses, deepen convenience, reinvest tariff refunds in price and keep the value gap visible.

  • 01
    Physical expansionThirty-three planned openings, including five relocations; fiscal 2027 capex of about $7.5 billion supports warehouses, supply chain and e-commerce.
  • 02
    Digitally enabled demandFiscal 2026 digitally enabled sales exceeded $33 billion and grew more than 20%. Nationwide Uber Eats and expanded DoorDash add younger, largely incremental delivery customers.
  • 03
    Ancillary growthPharmacy grew nearly 20% in fiscal 2026, travel sent more than 750,000 members on cruises, and gas reached record member-household penetration.
  • 04
    Value reinvestmentCostco received $184 million in Q4 IEEPA refunds and a similar amount in Q1 FY2027, with the majority of future refunds intended for member-value investment.
Important: tariff refunds are temporary and policy-dependent. They can help fund price reductions, but they should not be capitalized as recurring earnings.
05 · Valuation

The market is underwriting the franchise, not offering it.

Costco’s current multiple is not an outlier versus its own recent history. That is precisely the problem: investors are being asked to pay the full historical premium after a decade of extraordinary returns.

Why $1,050

The target sits near the lower end of the post-Q4 sell-side consensus range and credits Costco’s superior execution without assuming a rerating from an already premium multiple.

Reverse-DCF warning

One published reverse-DCF analysis estimated the market price requires roughly 23.6% annual free-cash-flow growth for ten years, versus a historical FCF CAGR near 14.1%. The exact result is model-dependent; the direction is not.

07 · Risk frame

Most roads lead back to the multiple.

Primary risk

Multiple compression

A premium that survives merely good news can still contract. Costco sold off after Q2 and Q3 FY2026 despite solid results, documenting the market’s sensitivity to an already high bar.

Policy risk

Tariffs and temporary refunds

Imported merchandise and freight remain exposed to policy and fuel costs. Refund benefits are non-recurring, and future recovery depends on policy outcomes.

Operating risk

Cost pressure at thin margins

Healthcare, liability, wage and LIFO costs can move faster than sales. The merchandise model intentionally runs with limited gross-margin room.

Concentration

North America and California

U.S. and Canada account for 86% of net sales and operating income; California contributes about 26% of U.S. sales. International growth also brings FX exposure.

Competition

Value retail is not uncontested

Walmart, BJ’s, Target and Amazon remain formidable. BJ’s is expanding membership economics in Costco’s core Northeast overlap, even if its scale and renewal moat are smaller.
