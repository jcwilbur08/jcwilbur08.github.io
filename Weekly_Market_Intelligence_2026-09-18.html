Executive call

The Fed tightened, oil stayed expensive, and leadership narrowed

+0.7%Nasdaq week
-1.7%Dow week
-1.5%Russell 2000 week

The Federal Reserve raised the target range 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%, citing solid activity and still-elevated inflation. The September projections show a 4.1% median year-end policy rate, 2.3% real GDP growth, 4.1% unemployment and 3.7% PCE inflation for 2026. Meanwhile the 10-year Treasury finished near 5% and Brent crude closed at $104.87. Despite those headwinds, the Nasdaq rose for the week as semiconductors recovered from Monday’s AI-safety-driven selloff.

What changed.
Policy moved from “watching inflation” to an actual hike. A second 2026 hike is now embedded in the Fed’s median rate projection.
What did not break.
Large-cap technology and semiconductors retained earnings support. The Nasdaq outperformed while the Dow and small caps weakened.
Cross-asset warning.
Oil above $100 and the 10-year around 5% create a simultaneous inflation and discount-rate squeeze.
Portfolio read-through.
XOM benefits from the energy backdrop; AMT and long-duration growth face a higher hurdle; AI infrastructure remains resilient but valuation-sensitive.
Regime: solid nominal growth + elevated inflation + restrictive rates + geopolitical energy premium. That argues for selective risk-taking, not broad beta chasing.

Fund flows and positioning

U.S. equity funds posted a fourth consecutive weekly outflow, while small-cap funds saw modest inflows. That divergence fits the tape: investors are reducing broad exposure while selectively rotating rather than abandoning risk altogether.

MARKET STRUCTURELeadership and breadth
Equity tape

Semiconductor resilience matters more than the index headline

Monday’s 5.9% decline in the PHLX semiconductor index showed that AI risk narratives can still create violent de-risking. By week-end, however, chip leadership had recovered enough for the Nasdaq to finish positive. That is important confirmation for portfolio exposures in AVGO, NVDA, TSM, ASML and SOXQ, but it also reinforces the need to distinguish operating momentum from valuation support.

AI infrastructure
Demand remains the market’s strongest secular growth engine. Watch customer capex commentary, power constraints, financing structures and any broadening of safety/regulatory friction.
Financials
Higher rates can support spread income, but capital-markets sensitivity and credit costs matter. JPM, GS and AXP should be judged on earnings durability rather than rate direction alone.
Industrials / power
GEV, GRID and VIS remain tied to multi-year power and grid capex. Higher discount rates reduce valuation tolerance but do not change the physical bottleneck thesis.

Portfolio concentration

The canonical portfolio is $1,679,572 with $43,000 of cash (2.6%). The largest positions remain TLXIX 9.8%, AMZN 7.9%, JPM 7.5% and AAPL 6.8%. In this rate regime, concentration control matters because multiple compression can overwhelm otherwise good company execution.
