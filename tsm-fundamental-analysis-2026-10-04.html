01 | Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Eight days after the prior update, the thesis is stronger and the price is higher. TSM closed October 2 at $472.78 — up 4.9% since the September 25 reference and roughly 1.3% below its 52-week high. The move was not speculative: August delivered the first NT$500B-plus month in company history (+53.3% year over year), UBS raised its 2027 capex forecast to $90 billion ahead of the October 15 print, and press reports indicate Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm and MediaTek collectively raised their 2nm reservations by 10–20%, accelerating TSMC's year-end capacity target to roughly 120,000 wafers per month. The $545 target — 25 times forward FY2027 consensus EPS — is unchanged, and at 15.3% upside with 8/10 conviction the name screens as a Strong Buy under the new rating scale.

Research posture: Strong Buy | Conviction 8/10 | 12-month target $545

The target framework is intentionally unchanged: 25x FY2027E consensus EPS of approximately $21.80. The stock is no longer below the $460 accumulation line — the staged-entry nuance now lives in gate language, not the rating.

02 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

TSMC is the toll road on AI compute, and the tolls are rising

High-performance computing — the platform that carries AI accelerators — was 66% of Q2 revenue and grew 20% quarter on quarter; UBS expects the HPC mix to reach 74%. Every leading-edge AI chip from NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom and Apple must pass through TSMC's leading nodes or its advanced packaging. Press reports put current leading-edge utilization above 100% with annual wafer ASP growth above 15%. That is pricing power, not just volume.

2

The foundry moat is widening, not narrowing

TSMC's Q2 2026 global foundry revenue share hit a record 72.5% while Samsung's share has roughly halved since early 2023. TSMC is the only foundry shipping 2nm at scale, with A16 in volume production targeted for late 2026/2027 and A14 tape-outs reportedly ahead of schedule. Samsung's leading-edge credibility is improving (Tesla AI5 prototype production, OpenAI second-sourcing), but its 1.4nm-class node has been postponed to 2029 — a roadmap concession that keeps the technology gap open for years.

3

Demand signals are strengthening into the Q3 print

August printed the first NT$500B month on record, July-plus-August already covers roughly two-thirds of the Q3 guide, and five major customers reportedly raised 2nm reservations 10–20% in late September. The high-frequency data all points the same direction: capacity, not demand, is the constraint. That makes the October 15 margin print — not revenue — the number that matters.

4

The balance sheet funds the build internally

Net cash reserves stood at approximately NT$2.49 trillion (~$79B) at mid-year. Even with 2026 capex guided at $60–64B and UBS projecting $90B for 2027, the company funds the cycle from operating cash flow without equity issuance. The dividend — payable October 8 — remains a minor but growing return of cash, not a constraint on investment.

5

The multiple is full in absolute terms but reasonable against growth

At 28.5x FY2026E compressing to 21.7x FY2027E on roughly 32% forward EPS growth, the PEG sits near 0.9. The market is paying for certainty of AI-compute demand and 2nm pricing power, not for a cyclical bounce. Downside protection on multiple compression is thin if AI capex pauses; the valuation argument is earnings delivery, not re-rating.

Variant perception. The consensus debate centers on whether AI capex is peaking. The differentiated view: TSMC's own capex intensity — raised twice this year, with third-party forecasts pointing to $90B in 2027 — is the most honest signal in the ecosystem, because it is the company spending its own money on the demand it can see. If hyperscaler capex were about to roll over, TSMC would not be accelerating five fabs simultaneously toward a 70% capacity CAGR through 2028.
03 | Business & Industry

Business quality and competitive position

Founded in 1987, TSMC pioneered the pure-play foundry model: it manufactures chips designed by others and competes with no customer's products. In 2025 it deployed 305 distinct process technologies and manufactured 12,682 products for 534 customers. That breadth — plus a four-decade supplier and talent ecosystem in Taiwan — is the structural moat.

Technology roadmap

2nm (N2)
Ramping

First revenue in Q2 2026 at 3% of wafer revenue. Press reports indicate Apple, NVIDIA, AMD, Qualcomm and MediaTek raised reservations 10–20% in September, accelerating the year-end monthly capacity target to roughly 120,000 wafers — the 2027 goal pulled forward.

A16 (1.6nm-class)
Late 2026

First backside-power (Super Power Rail) node; mass production targeted from Q4 2026 with meaningful volume in 2027. NVIDIA is reported to have booked A16 capacity for next-gen AI GPUs.

A14 (1.4nm-class)
2028

Customer tape-outs reportedly ahead of schedule; +10–15% performance or −25–30% power versus N2. Pre-production in 2027, volume in 2028.

High-NA EUV
2030+

TSMC and ASML announced a joint initiative on large-format 12-inch photomasks for High-NA EUV, targeting a pilot line by 2031 and high-volume adoption from 2030 — a long-dated productivity lever, not a 2026–27 earnings driver.

Manufacturing footprint

Arizona: Fab 1 in volume production (4nm); Fab 2 equipment move-in through 2026 with volume production pulled forward to H2 2027 (3nm); Fab 3 (N2/A16) under construction; first on-site advanced-packaging line being readied. Total US investment framework ~$265B, supported by $6.6B in CHIPS Act grants and up to $5B in federal loans plus a 25% investment tax credit. Japan: Kumamoto Fab 1 in volume production; Fab 2 construction started October 2025, reportedly upgraded toward 3nm with a 2028 target. Germany (ESMC Dresden): 28/22nm and 16/12nm, equipment move-in H2 2026, production targeted late 2027. Advanced packaging (CoWoS): industry estimates put end-2026 capacity at ~130,000 wafers/month with a ~260,000 target by end-2028; CoWoS demand was estimated at ~20% above supply in mid-2026, narrowing toward ~10% by year-end.

Texas is the new watch item. From late September through early October, multiple press reports described TSMC scouting a second US campus in Texas — several advanced-node fabs, each costing at least $20B, contingent on Congress extending a 35% manufacturing tax credit. There is no company confirmation and no disclosed site, node roadmap, or timeline; treat it as single-source reporting. The strategic logic is straightforward (proximity to NVIDIA, AMD and hyperscaler customers; tariff and Section 232 insurance), but incremental multi-tens-of-billions capex commitments would extend the capital-intensity debate well past 2027. Separately, reports of TSMC evaluating involvement in Elon Musk's Texas "Terafab" project are highly speculative and should not be modeled.

Competitive watch

Foundry share (TrendForce, Q2 2026): TSMC 72.5% (record), Samsung 5.9%, SMIC 5.4%, UMC 3.9%, GlobalFoundries 3.2%. Samsung's 2nm yields are reported to have recovered and Tesla's AI5 began prototype production at Samsung's Taylor, TX fab in mid-September, while OpenAI added Samsung Foundry as a second source — the first credible leading-edge share threat in two years, but with no impact on reported share data yet. Intel's 18A entered high-volume manufacturing in January 2026 with external foundry volume still exploratory. The moat remains effectively a leading-edge monopoly; the watch is on whether Samsung can convert yield recovery into sustained volume share.

There is no equity dilution risk and no leverage constraint on the build. The capital-intensity debate is about return on that capex, not about funding it.
05 | Guidance & Outlook

Guidance: the Q3 print and the $50B Q4 question

  • Q3 2026 (July 16): revenue US$44.6–45.8B; gross margin 65–67%; operating margin 56–58%; based on NT$32.0/US$. Management cites continued strong demand for leading-edge process technologies, including the steep ramp of 2nm. The company entered its quiet period on October 5 — there will be no reiteration or revision before the print.
  • Q3 tracking: July + August = NT$982.39B. Against guidance of NT$1,427.2–1,465.6B (at NT$32), September must print NT$444.8–483.2B — essentially flat versus July and roughly 10% below August's record. September revenue is expected around October 8–10. Anything above NT$483B puts Q3 above the top of guidance on this math.
  • FY2026 revenue: "slightly more than 40%" growth in USD terms implies ≥US$171.4B (versus US$122.42B in 2025). H1 delivered $76.1B; the Q3 guide midpoint adds $45.2B; the residual implies roughly $50B of Q4 revenue — about 11% above the Q3 guide. August already printed at the required monthly run-rate (~$16.3B), so the guide is achievable, but it demands the 2nm ramp and AI demand stay hot through year-end.
  • Capex: FY2026 guide US$60–64B (~70–80% advanced processes, ~10% specialty, 10–20% advanced packaging/testing/mask). H1 spend was $26.80B, implying an H2 run-rate of $33–37B. UBS now forecasts $90B in 2027 and $105B in 2028 — a third-party signal that the build has years to run.
  • Margin headwinds (company-guided): N2 ramp dilutes gross margin by 3–4pp in H2 2026; overseas fabs dilute by 2–3pp in early stages, widening to 3–4pp later. N3 gross margin is expected to reach the corporate average in H2 2026, a partial offset.
Consensus tension, updated. Sell-side FY2026 revenue consensus sits at roughly $167.1B (+36.5%) — still below the ~$171.4B implied by the guide. UBS's preview expects Q3 at the high end of guidance and full-year growth of 41%, which would converge estimates toward the guide. The October 15 call's treatment of the "slightly more than 40%" guide — maintained, raised, or quietly softened — is the single most important forward signal after the margin print.

06 | Management & Capital Allocation

Execution is the franchise; allocation is disciplined

Chairman and CEO C.C. Wei has run the company through the steepest technology transitions in its history — N3, N2, and now the angstrom-class roadmap — while holding gross margins in the mid-60s through a $60B-plus capex cycle. The July earnings call's AI-demand-through-2030 framing is not hedging; it is the spending justification for five simultaneous fab ramps.

Positive

Capex discipline with proof

Two upward capex revisions in 2026 ($52–56B to $60–64B) were demand-led, not speculative: utilization above 100% and customer reservation increases validate the spend. The net-cash balance sheet absorbs it without leverage or dilution.

Positive

Dividend growth intact

The quarterly dividend continues its steady climb (NT$7.00 for Q1 2026, payable October 8). Payout discipline is intact at roughly a 21% payout ratio — the dividend is safe through the capex cycle.

Watch

US build complexity

The Arizona program is executing well by management's account, but overseas fabs structurally dilute margins (company-stated) and will take years to reach Taiwan cost structure. A Texas campus would extend this dynamic — diversification at the cost of margin.

Watch

Succession and governance

Wei's open-market purchase is a modest positive. The deeper governance question — continuity of the trust model and technology leadership into the A14 era — remains the long-term management risk, with no near-term signal of change.

Capital allocation priorities, in order

  1. Capacity and technology: $60–64B of 2026 capex, ~70–80% into advanced processes; the 2nm and advanced-packaging ramps absorb the bulk of incremental spend.
  2. Dividend: steadily increasing cash dividend; next ADR ex-div December 10, 2026.
  3. Strategic optionality: domestic bond capacity (NT$60B approved) and the US$10B TSMC Global capital injection preserve flexibility for the US build.
  4. Buybacks: no program in place — retained balance-sheet strength is the correct priority during the build phase.

09 | Portfolio Fit

Portfolio fit: the portfolio's best AI-compute expression, still light

The sole portfolio baseline for this section is retirement-portfolio-54.xlsx. Cam holds 26 shares at an average cost of approximately $427.42 (basis $11,112.89). At the $472.78 reference price the position is worth $12,292.28, a gain of $1,179.39 (+10.6%), representing approximately 0.73% of the ~$1.687M combined portfolio.

Fit benefit

Pure-play AI infrastructure

TSM is the portfolio's only pure-play semiconductor foundry exposure and its principal pick-and-shovel holding on AI compute. It complements Apple on the device side and AVGO in the AI stack without duplicating their economics.

Fit trade-off

Same factor, concentrated
