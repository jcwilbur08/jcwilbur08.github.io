01 · Verdict

A stronger business. The same Hold.

The print strengthened the business and the entry got cheaper — but the bookings question and a forward multiple still near 97× leave conviction at 5. High conviction in the business, low conviction in the entry price, unchanged.

Third-quarter revenue of $2,313.4m grew 96% year on year and beat the consensus bar used for this verdict by roughly $133m; adjusted earnings per share of $0.44 beat by $0.0339. Guidance was raised on every line, for the fourth quarter and the full year. U.S. commercial revenue overtook U.S. government revenue for the first time. On the numbers that describe the business today, this was the strongest quarter in the company’s history.

On the numbers that describe tomorrow’s business, it was the softest in two years. Total contract value closed in the quarter rose just 6% year on year, against 49% in the second quarter, and U.S. commercial signings rose 11% against 153%. The stock’s path told the same story: an initial after-hours rise on the beat, then a fade through the call to $170.84, then a partial recovery. Nothing in the print broke the thesis; nothing in it retired the valuation question either. The Hold, the 5/10 conviction, and the $200 target are all maintained.

One correction to the October 30 preview, flagged up front: the consensus earnings-per-share bar used for the verdict in this report is $0.4061, not the $0.4072 the preview carried — $0.0011 lower. The revenue bar, $2.18bn, is unchanged, and the verdict is identical on either bar. The reconciliation sits with the consensus table in the print section.

The verdict — beat and raise, with one open question

Beat: revenue $2,313.4m vs a $2.18bn bar (+6.1%); adjusted EPS $0.44 vs $0.4061 (+8.3%). The setup trap was cleared, not fired: the consensus revenue bar sat $16m above the top of management’s own $2.160–2.164bn guide, so a print merely at guidance would have read as a miss. The actual cleared the guide top by $149m.

Raise: full-year revenue guidance moved to $8.530–8.534bn (91% growth), adjusted operating income to $5.245–5.249bn, and adjusted free cash flow to $4.8–5.0bn.

The question: bookings. Total contract value +6% year on year and U.S. commercial remaining deal value up only 4% sequentially, against U.S. commercial revenue up 29% sequentially. Management attributes the shape to shorter-duration Sovereign AI deals. That explanation is plausible and unproven; it is now the swing factor into the fourth quarter.

Entry discipline — revised in October, unchanged by this print (recommended only; no gate installed)

The $161–166 starter zone stands. No after-hours snapshot on November 2 entered it — the low was $170.84. The deep tranche at or below $150 is retained, as is the ~$136 valuation anchor. A reclaim of $186–187 would be confirmation, not an entry. No adds above the $172–178 zone into strength; the next add comes only in the $161–166 zone, or on a print that shows bookings re-accelerating. If you want the starter zone armed as a live gate, that takes your word — nothing here installs one.

Your position

You now hold 4 shares, added after hours on November 2 at around $172 — your own execution, a very small starter. Bookings, backlog, and a disclosure that narrowed again

The release itself discloses only one backlog metric: U.S. commercial remaining deal value, $6,517m, up 79% year on year but just 4% sequentially — against U.S. commercial revenue up 29% sequentially. The remaining backlog figures were given on the call only: total remaining deal value of $13.4bn, up 55% year on year and 2% sequentially; remaining performance obligation of $4.9bn, up 88% year on year and flat sequentially; net dollar retention of 165%, up 800 basis points from 157%; U.S. commercial customers of 686, up 29%; and average trailing-twelve-month revenue from the top 20 customers of $146m, up 76%.

Total customer count was not disclosed anywhere — not in the release, not on the call. It is not carried forward from the second quarter’s 1,049 in this report. One third-party tracker published a figure of 1,090 customers; it is that tracker’s own metric, not a company actual, and it is not used here.

Cash needs one flag of its own. The release headline is $10.4bn of cash, cash equivalents and U.S. Treasury securities, up from $9.2bn. But the balance sheet’s own lines — cash and equivalents of $3,619.4m plus marketable securities of $6,942.1m — sum to $10.56bn. The roughly $162m gap is not reconciled in the release. The likely explanation is that not all marketable securities are Treasuries, but the release does not say so, and this report does not assert it: $10.4bn is the company headline, and the gap is an open item for the quarterly filing.

04 · The call

Sovereign AI is the story. Bookings got one answer.

Prepared remarks ran about 28 minutes and Q&A took just three questions. The tone was expansive and combative — a civilisational framing of “Sovereign AI” — and the only defensive passage in the entire call was the explanation of the bookings shape.

Four voices carried the call: the chief executive, the chief technology officer, the chief financial officer, and the chief revenue officer. (No executive named Moore participated, despite the name appearing in pre-print briefings.) The five statements that matter:

“In this quarter, our commercial business eclipsed our government business. In fact, the market value of that probably justifies our current valuation, given our aggregate business growing at 96%.”Chief Executive — on the commercial crossover and valuation

On the AI labs, the same speaker was dismissive of any rivalry: the U.S. market is “insatiable,” the labs are symbiotic rather than competitive at this point, and they are “helping it explode.” No analyst asked about valuation; the justification line above was volunteered.

“We signed multiple eight-figure deals in the quarter that were shorter in duration than usual as customers rapidly adopted initial Sovereign AI capabilities… over time, we expect both the duration and size of these contracts to increase.”Chief Financial Officer — the bookings explanation

This is the sentence the fourth quarter will be judged against. The same remarks disclosed the call-only backlog figures — total remaining deal value, performance obligations, retention, and the U.S. commercial customer count — and attributed a 53% year-on-year rise in adjusted expense to technical hiring and AI-platform investment. No headcount figure was given.

“The conceit that the labs have wrong is the belief that the atomic unit of the enterprise is the individual knowledge worker. It is not. The fundamental unit of the enterprise is the workflow… We are the diffusion layer, not just of choice, but of necessity.”Chief Technology Officer — the anti-lab thesis

The supporting evidence offered: a 30-billion-parameter post-trained model deployed in a Pacific Command exercise with “superior task-specific performance” to a frontier model of three trillion parameters, and a major insurer’s underwriting workflow running inside the company’s platform, cited from that lab partner’s own regulatory filing.

“We don’t have even the bandwidth to serve that demand… the Sovereign AI revolution, we launched this last quarter… we’re at the very beginning of this.”Chief Revenue Officer — demand and capacity

On government: U.S. government growth of 96% ran “across civil and defense, with Maven as our foundational work” — Maven now exceeds 100,000 users — alongside a prime agreement for eight TITAN systems for the Army. No contract value was attached to TITAN, and no shutdown, funding, or backlog question was raised in Q&A.

The fifth thread was deterrence. Asked about the shareholder letter’s framing, the chief executive described the products as “built to avoid war, meaning the adversary knows that they will have such pain inflicted upon them that, in fact, it is not worth going to war,” and closed by thanking retail investors. The technology chief added that deterrence “is not something you accomplish by hope. You actually have to build it brick by brick.”

What Q&A covered — and what it never touched

Three questions in total: one on Sovereign AI bootcamps (Yorkville), one pressing the labs-competition question directly (D.A. Davidson), and one on the letter’s anti-war framing (Deutsche Bank). No analyst pressed the contract-value deceleration — the bookings question was answered in prepared remarks and never tested. There was no Q&A on valuation, none on buybacks or capital allocation, none on hiring numbers, and none on 2027. Capital allocation was not addressed anywhere on the call; a claim circulating in a podcast summary that the repurchase programme was terminated earlier this year is single-sourced and unverified, and is not treated as fact here.

Names and conversions

Customers named in prepared remarks, none with a contract value: Optum Serve, L3Harris (a fine-tuned open model beating a frontier model in under 48 hours at 95% lower cost), NVIDIA, AIG, McCarthy Building Companies (30,000 hours a year saved), Tampa General, the FAA, and the U.S. Army. The pilot-to-production conversions were given without names: an industrial materials company from a ~$3m pilot to $29m of annual contract value; a global athletic company from $1.6m to ~$21m; a home-appliance company from $1m to $3m to $15m; an apparel company from $2.5m to $11.5m. Those four conversions are the bull case for the bookings explanation — short first deals that expand fast.

Europe was explicitly ducked: “You see it in Europe, which I spent half my life in. At this point, we should kind of avoid talking about it because of what’s going on.” No European deal was disclosed, and international growth was not a Q&A topic. The “sovereign” deals named were U.S. customers and a Pacific Command exercise.

On the shareholder letter: the letter dated November 2, 2026 exists and is listed among the company’s published letters, but its full text could not be retrieved for this report. Everything attributed to it here comes through the release, the call, or press quotation of it — including the “most dramatic dichotomy between haves and have nots” line in the release and the anti-war passage put to management in Q&A. No letter-only disclosure is relied on anywhere in this report.

08 · Risks & what changed

The swing factor moved from revenue to bookings.

Against the October 30 update: momentum strengthened, margins held at a record level rather than expanding, cash margin thinned, concentration rose, dilution stayed contained — and bookings durability weakened. That last item is the new centre of the thesis.

  • Momentum — strengthened. Growth accelerated for a thirteenth straight quarter to a record 96%; U.S. commercial crossed U.S. government for the first time; commercial revenue, government revenue, and GAAP operating income each crossed $1bn in a quarter for the first time; the fourth-quarter guide implies a further 88% growth.
  • Margin trajectory — flat at a record level. GAAP 47% and adjusted 62% are unchanged sequentially; the Rule of 40’s +3 points came entirely from growth, against +10 points in the second quarter. Adjusted expense rose 53% year on year on hiring and platform investment — the cost line to watch.
  • Bookings durability — weakened; the key swing factor. Contract value +6% year on year, U.S. commercial contract value +11% against +153% in the second quarter, U.S. commercial remaining deal value +4% sequentially against revenue +29%, performance obligations flat sequentially. Shorter Sovereign AI durations explain part of it; the burden of proof for the fourth quarter is now on signings, not revenue.
  • Cash generation — strong dollars, thinner margin. Adjusted free cash flow of $1,216.1m is flat against the second quarter’s $1,220m, and its margin fell from 63% to 53%. The cash headline rose to $10.4bn — subject to the unreconciled balance-sheet gap flagged in the print section.
  • Concentration — up. The U.S. is 84% of revenue; international growth of 26% against 119% for the U.S. widens the question of how well the model travels, and Europe was not addressed on the call.
  • Dilution — contained this quarter. Stock-based compensation of $318.9m rose 85% in dollars but fell as a share of revenue, to 13.8%; the diluted share count rose just 0.1% year on year. The dilution story is compensation intensity, not share-count growth.

The bear case

Third-quarter optics may be peak optics: revenue is converting a backlog signed in a longer-duration era while new signing has stalled — contract value up 6%, U.S. commercial signings up 11%, remaining deal value up 4% sequentially and performance obligations flat, against 29% sequential U.S. commercial revenue growth — so growth must decelerate sharply unless Sovereign AI deals lengthen and enlarge as management promises. International growth of 26% suggests the model does not travel as easily as the U.S. narrative implies. At roughly 93–97× forward earnings depending on provider, about 40–42× forward sales, and a sub-1% trailing free-cash-flow yield, with compensation running at $319m a quarter, expenses up 53%, and the labs hiring the company’s forward-deployed engineers to replicate its application layer more cheaply, any fourth-quarter bookings disappointment — or a broader AI-multiple de-rating — leaves no valuation cushion. The fade to $170.84 on a clean beat-and-raise is the market pricing exactly that asymmetry.

The bull case
