Executive summary

The pullback repriced the AI complex; it did not change the theses we own. Three things drove the week: Broadcom's CEO likening AI investors to gamblers on Monday, a Gartner forecast on Wednesday that over $3.5 trillion of AI infrastructure spend could face impairment by 2030 (and that most neoclouds will not survive independently), and renewed worry about debt-funded data-center buildouts. Hot CPI and PPI added rate pressure early, though the 10-year actually eased to ~5.10% by Friday and hike odds for the Oct 27–28 FOMC fell to ~17%. The two AI names that reported, TSM and ASML, both beat and raised. The market is now separating AI self-funders from AI borrowers; this book owns the former and none of the latter.

What happened, and what it means for this book

The tape (Oct 9 → Oct 16)

  • S&P 500 −1.95% (worst week since June); Nasdaq −4.48%; Dow +0.67%; Russell 2000 −3.05%.
  • SOX −11.41% over eight straight down sessions; tech sector −7.7%. Staples +5.0%, energy +4.9%, healthcare +3.5%.
  • Listed neoclouds averaged −22%; CoreWeave fell ~12% Friday alone. NVDA −6.6% for the week.
  • WTI +10.4% to ~$101; Brent ~$110 on Hormuz tanker attacks. VIX ~20, highest since late July.
  • 10-year 5.24% → 5.10% despite CPI 3.7% / PPI 6.3% y/y; FOMC hike odds ~17% for October, ~89% by December.

Analyst read

  • Sentiment, not earnings. TSM (revenue +41%, Q4 guide $52–54B) and ASML (sales +55%, FY raised, 2027–28 EUV supply short of demand) both beat and raised and still fell 8–9%. The cash-flow evidence moved one way; the multiple moved the other.
  • Self-funders vs. borrowers. The selling concentrated where buildouts are debt-funded. Our hyperscalers (MSFT −1.1%, META −1.3%, GOOG −3.2%) held far better than our suppliers (AVGO −9.1%, TSM −9.0%, GEV −8.3%).
  • The real test is two weeks out. If the Oct 21–29 prints reaffirm capex funded from operating cash flow, the supplier discount is an entry. If capex guides are cut, TSM/ASML/NVDA/AVGO face a genuine estimate reset — the one thing this week did not deliver.
  • Strip-the-noise flag: NVDA holds ~10% of CoreWeave. Its Q3 FY27 GAAP EPS will likely carry an equity-securities loss — the mirror of the $15.9B gain we stripped from Q1. Value NVDA on operating earnings.

Risks and counterarguments

Against buying the dip

  • Gartner's impairment work is the first mainstream estimate that a meaningful share of AI capex earns no return. If hyperscalers agree, supplier estimates fall, not just multiples.
  • Oil above $100 plus 3.7% CPI keeps the Fed hawkish; ~89% December hike odds is a real headwind for long-duration growth.
  • Eight straight down sessions in semis can be capitulation — or the first leg. Staging, not lump-sum, is the answer to that uncertainty.

Against standing still

  • TSM at ~20% below PW value and ASML at ~27% below base came with beat-and-raise quarters — the cleanest fundamental/price divergence in the book this year.
  • The book is under-diversified exactly where this tape hurts: diversifiers are 4.4% vs. a 15% target. Funding them is low-regret in any scenario.
  • A 10-year horizon backed by an $80K pension floor means drawdowns are an input, not a threat — provided position limits hold.
Thesis-break watch: (1) any hyperscaler cuts FY27 capex guidance; (2) Taiwan Strait or export-control escalation (TSM, ASML); (3) the 10-year durably above ~5.3% with an October hike; (4) credit stress spreading from neoclouds to investment-grade AI issuers.
