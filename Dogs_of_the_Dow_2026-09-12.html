1. What the “Dogs of the Dow” actually means

The classic strategy buys equal dollar amounts of the 10 highest-yielding Dow Jones Industrial Average constituents at the end of the calendar year, holds them for 12 months, and then rebalances. The thesis is contrarian: a high yield among blue-chip companies can indicate temporary price weakness and a potentially attractive valuation. The official 2026 Dogs were therefore fixed by yields on December 31, 2025; they are not simply the ten highest-yielding Dow stocks today.[1]

The approach has a respectable historical pedigree, but it is mechanically backward-looking. S&P Dow Jones Indices recently highlighted that the classic method relies on trailing dividend yield and therefore does not directly incorporate forward dividend expectations or fundamental quality.[3] In my view, the Dogs list is best treated as a research funnel, not a complete portfolio-construction system.

2026 context matters: The Dogs basket now yields roughly 3.13%, while the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield was around 4.95% on September 10. That means investors are not being paid enough through current income alone; future returns must come from earnings growth, dividend growth and/or valuation expansion.[2][4]

4. Analyst commentary by company

VZVerizon
ADD

What is working: Verizon reported record 2Q26 results, 184,000 postpaid phone net adds, more than 550,000 total mobility/broadband net adds, and raised full-year guidance for a second consecutive quarter.[5]

Valuation: ~9.3x forward P/E, ~0.9 PEG and 5.6% current yield. Even after a 24% price gain, valuation remains unusually low for a stable cash-flow franchise.

My view: Best pure Dogs setup. The stock no longer looks distressed, but it still offers a high cash yield plus credible operating stabilization. I would treat debt reduction and free-cash-flow durability as the key checkpoints.

UNHUnitedHealth
ADD

What is working: 2Q26 revenue reached $112.0B, operating earnings were $8.0B, and management raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $19.50-$20.00.[10]

Valuation: ~17.5x forward P/E and ~1.1 PEG. That is not “cheap” in absolute terms, but it is attractive if earnings normalize toward historical quality levels.

My view: Highest upside among the Dogs if cost trends normalize. The trade-off is much greater event and regulatory risk than Verizon or the staples names. Position size should reflect that.

HDHome Depot
ADD ON WEAKNESS

What is working: Fiscal 2Q26 sales rose 5.7%, U.S. comps increased 1.3%, and guidance was reaffirmed.[11]

Valuation: ~20.1x forward P/E, ~3.0% yield. The stock is roughly 28% below its 52-week high and is down about 10% from year-end.[18]

My view: Attractive as a multi-year housing normalization trade, not because the current earnings trend is strong. I prefer staged buying rather than one-time entry while long rates remain elevated.

MRKMerck
ADD ON PULLBACK

What is working: Q2 sales rose 5% to $16.6B; Keytruda remained strong, Winrevair grew 75%, and Merck highlighted multiple regulatory/pipeline milestones.[7]

Valuation: ~15.2x forward P/E. Reported earnings are distorted by large 2026 acquisition charges, making normalized forward earnings more useful than trailing P/E.

My view: Stronger than the headline “patent cliff” narrative implies, but the stock has already rerated almost 37% this year. I would add on pullbacks rather than chase.

AMGNAmgen
HOLD / ADD

What is working: Q2 revenue increased 10% to $10.1B, product sales rose 9%, and six key growth drivers grew 26% and represented nearly 70% of product sales.[8]

Valuation: ~16x forward earnings and ~2.7% yield. Reasonable, but not distressed.

My view: The core business is stronger than the Dogs label suggests. Recent weakness tied to read-through concerns for olpasiran is a reminder that pipeline assumptions can move valuation quickly.[19]

CVXChevron
HOLD

What is working: Q2 earnings were $12.1B, worldwide production rose 20%, U.S. production hit a record, and refining utilization was strong.[6]

Valuation: ~16x forward P/E, ~0.9 PEG, ~3.3% yield. The valuation is reasonable only if elevated commodity pricing persists.

My view: Chevron is a high-quality operator, but after a ~40% year-to-date price gain and oil above pre-war levels, the entry point is less attractive. I would hold rather than initiate aggressively here.[20]

PGProcter & Gamble
HOLD

What is working: FY26 net sales rose 3% and the dividend remains highly dependable.

What is weak: FY26 organic sales grew only 1%; Q4 organic sales were flat and core EPS fell 3%.[9]

My view: The stock is a defensive quality asset, but ~20.5x forward earnings for low-single-digit growth is not compelling while risk-free yields are high. The 3% dividend is useful, not sufficient by itself.

JNJJohnson & Johnson
HOLD

What is working: Q2 sales grew 6.6% to $25.3B and J&J raised full-year revenue and adjusted EPS guidance; adjusted EPS guidance midpoint implies ~8% growth.[13]

Valuation: ~20.8x forward P/E, ~4.25 PEG, ~2.0% yield.

My view: Excellent company, weaker stock setup after a ~28% rally. I would rather own it than sell it, but new money should demand a better entry point.

KOCoca-Cola
HOLD

What is working: Q2 organic revenue grew 6%, comparable EPS rose 11%, margins expanded and management raised full-year guidance.[12]

Valuation: ~25x forward earnings and ~4.0 PEG with a 2.4% yield.

My view: Operational quality is outstanding. The problem is price: the stock now requires persistent execution to justify a premium multiple when bonds yield substantially more.

NKENike
WATCH

What is weak: FY26 revenue was essentially flat; currency-neutral sales fell 2%, Nike Direct fell 8%, digital sales fell 12%, and Greater China remained a drag.[14]

Valuation: ~21.7x forward P/E despite a 42% price decline. The current 4.46% dividend yield is visually attractive, but it is mostly a consequence of the collapsing share price.

My view: Classic “yield trap versus turnaround” debate. The brand still has major long-term value, but I want evidence of revenue reacceleration and cleaner inventory/channel trends before upgrading. 5. The official list versus the Dogs if screened today

  • The 2026 official Dogs measure the performance of a fixed annual strategy.

6. Portfolio-manager interpretation

The biggest mistake would be treating all ten Dogs as equally attractive today simply because the original strategy equal-weights them. The screen deliberately mixes different reasons for high yield: slow growth (VZ), cyclicality (CVX/HD), patent or pipeline uncertainty (MRK/AMGN), defensive multiple compression (PG/KO/JNJ), regulatory/earnings disruption (UNH), and genuine operating deterioration (NKE). Those are not the same risk.

I would divide the group into four actionable buckets:

Actively attractiveVZ, UNHCurrent valuation still compensates for company-specific risk; both have identifiable fundamental catalysts.
Buy selectively on weaknessHD, MRK, AMGNGood businesses with reasonable long-run cases, but entry price and event timing matter.
Quality, but reratedCVX, JNJ, KO2026 gains have materially reduced the original Dogs discount. Better to hold than chase.
Needs proofPG, NKEPG needs growth acceleration to justify its multiple; Nike needs a credible turnaround before its yield should be trusted as a buy signal.

7. Strategy risks and what would change the ranking

  • Interest rates: If Treasury yields remain near 5%, slow-growth dividend equities need unusually strong earnings visibility or deep valuation discounts to compete. A meaningful decline in long rates would disproportionately help HD and the defensive dividend names.
  • Oil/geopolitics: CVX's 2026 strength is partly tied to a much stronger commodity backdrop. De-escalation and lower oil would likely reduce near-term earnings power and could compress the stock's relative advantage.
  • Healthcare policy and utilization: UNH has the most event-sensitive earnings path in the group. Continued high medical-cost trends would be the clearest negative thesis signal.
  • Patent/pipeline execution: MRK and AMGN require successful portfolio transition. Pipeline disappointments can quickly change normalized earnings assumptions.
  • Consumer weakness: NKE and HD are the most exposed to discretionary demand and macro sensitivity; PG and KO are more defensive but also priced more expensively.

8. Bottom line

The Dogs of the Dow remain useful as a disciplined contrarian screen, but I would not implement the strategy mechanically at current levels. The basket's 2026 outperformance has already harvested much of the initial valuation mean-reversion in several names. The strongest opportunities now are concentrated rather than broad.

Preferred order: VZ, UNH, HD, MRK, AMGN, CVX, PG, JNJ, KO, NKE. For an investor seeking a blend of income and total return, Verizon is the cleanest current setup. For higher upside with higher risk, UnitedHealth is more compelling. Home Depot is the best cyclical recovery candidate. Nike is the stock I would most resist buying solely because it now carries a high dividend yield.
