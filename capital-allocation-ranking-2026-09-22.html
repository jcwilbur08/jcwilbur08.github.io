Reasoning for each

1. UBER — best tactical asymmetry

BUY, $105 target (~48% upside from ~$70). The expectations reset is the whole pitch: the stock sits ~30% below its 52-week high on AV fears while the core business accelerated (Q2: gross bookings $58.0B +24%, adj. EBITDA $2.82B +33%, first $10B+ TTM free-cash-flow year). The Delivery Hero takeover is priced as risk, not opportunity. At 0.08% of the portfolio it is a stub, not a position — ~$3,500 (≈50 shares) takes it from 0.08% toward ~0.3% without creating any concentration. Morningstar's $76 fair value is more conservative than our $105; we are underwriting the FCF inflection and AV optionality more aggressively. Key risk: AV capital intensity and disintermediation — size it as a starter, not a conviction bet.

2. NVDA — highest quality, gated by price

BUY, $328 target (~43% upside). Q2 FY2027 was a record on every dimension ($96.2B revenue +106%, Data Center $89.0B +117%, 75% gross margin, $21.3B FCF), Vera Rubin is in full production, and the Q3 guide assumes zero China data-center revenue — yet the stock trades at ~15× FY2028E EPS, priced as a cyclical at peak margins. The 0.40% weight is a stub relative to that conviction. The gate is your own discipline: under $220. At $220 our target implies ~49% upside; above it, the margin of safety compresses and patience is the cheaper option. ~$3,000 (≈13–14 shares at $220) lifts the position from 0.40% toward ~0.6%. Do not chase above the limit — the Q4 print and memory-cost headlines will create entries.

3. TSM — the preferred semiconductor compound

BUY, $530 ADR target (~19% upside from $447.06). The strongest blend of quality, growth, and valuation among the direct semiconductor choices: Q2 2026 beat (US$40.20B revenue +36% y/y in NT$, 67.7% gross margin, US$4.31 ADR EPS +77%), 2nm in its first commercial quarter, FY2026 guidance raised to "slightly more than 40%" USD revenue growth, and a record 72.5% foundry share. At ~25× FY2027E consensus EPS with ~28% forward EPS growth (PEG ~0.96), you are paying a fair multiple for the company that manufactures everyone else's AI — including NVDA's and AVGO's. The 0.58% weight (22 shares) leaves room to build toward a full ~1% position: ~$2,500 (≈5–6 ADRs near $450) gets it to ~0.7%. Morningstar 4★/$534 agrees with our $530 almost to the dollar. Binding risk is Taiwan geopolitics — non-diversifiable, so stage the entry rather than buying it all at once. Ranks ahead of GEHC on compounding quality and estimate momentum, behind NVDA only on raw upside.

4. GEHC — the valuation dislocation

BUY, $84 target (~27% upside from $66.27). Still one of the clearest valuation gaps in the researched universe: Q2 beat ($1.13 adj. EPS vs $1.04 consensus), +11.1% organic orders, 1.15× book-to-bill, $23.9B backlog, and the PDx franchise compounding at 14.6% organic with 29.6% margins — all at ~13.6× 2026E earnings, depressed by transient input-cost inflation. Healthcare exposure diversifies you away from pure tech cyclicality, and at 0.19% the position is small enough that portfolio fit is unambiguously favorable. ~$1,500 (≈22 shares) lifts it from 0.19% toward ~0.3% — a smaller first step that keeps room for a second tranche on PCS-review resolution. Gating items to watch: PCS strategic review outcome, cash conversion, and M&A discipline. Morningstar 4★/$88 is consistent with our view.

5. VXUS — the geographic diversifier

No single-name catalyst; this is portfolio construction. At 2.74% it is your only dedicated international equity exposure, and the rest of the book is overwhelmingly U.S. large-cap. VXUS is the cleanest broad diversifier on your list (Morningstar 3★). It ranks below the three BUY-rated singles because its expected return is a market return, not a dislocation — fund it from the next tranche or standing cash, not by crowding out UBER/NVDA/GEHC. Posture: systematic recurring tranches rather than a single large entry at $87.37.

6. AVUV — the factor diversifier

U.S. small-cap value/profitability exposure (1.85%, Morningstar 4★) that is structurally underrepresented in a mega-cap-growth-heavy book. Same logic as VXUS: portfolio-construction dollars, not a near-term catalyst. Ranks fifth because factor exposure is a slower, quieter payoff than the three dislocated singles, and because AVUV at $121.93 is not on sale — add on weakness, not into strength. If you fund the diversifier sleeve, consider pairing with AVDV (international small-value, not currently held) for balance — see watchlist.

7. ASML — quality, but gated

HOLD, $1,950 target (~14% upside). The lithography moat is unique and High-NA adoption is intact, but this is the lowest expected return on your list and the only HOLD-rated name. At ~$1,740 the stock is above your $1,700 entry level — honor the discipline. The 0.51% weight (5 shares) is supportive of adding eventually, and Morningstar's 4★/$2,050 is more constructive than our $1,950, but neither justifies chasing. Put it on a price alert at $1,700 and redeploy the cash into higher-utility dollars meanwhile. A pullback without estimate deterioration would move it up materially.

ADBE / VRTX / AVDV (watchlist)

ADBE (reset software valuation) and VRTX (differentiated healthcare growth) are legitimate selective initiations for a future tranche — flagged here so they are not forgotten, not funded from this $10.5k.

What could change the ranking quickly?

Move higher

  • NVDA fills under $220 — or drops toward ~$205 without a thesis break (bigger tranche).
  • ASML trades at or below $1,700 — becomes fundable.
  • UBER AV economics clarified positively (third-party financing, platform share gains in AV markets).
  • GEHC PCS review resolves favorably; cash conversion inflects.
  • VXUS/AVUV pullbacks — better entries for the diversifier sleeve.

Move lower

  • NVDA runs away from the $220 limit — do not chase; the dollar rolls to GEHC or waits.
  • UBER AV capital commitments accelerate while platform economics deteriorate.
  • GEHC PCS/FCF execution misses current checkpoints.
  • A 10-year yield spike back above ~5% — duration hurts the long-duration growth names (NVDA, ASML) first.
  • Further semiconductor concentration elsewhere in the book.
