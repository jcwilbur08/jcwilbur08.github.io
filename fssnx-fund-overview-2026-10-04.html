1. Executive Summary & Verdict

Re-rating box — legacy KEEP translates to new-scale HOLD. Under the new conviction-anchored rating system, a legacy KEEP on a passively managed index fund maps to Hold by construction. The conviction score on the vehicle is a genuine 7/10: FSSNX is among the cheapest, tightest-tracking small-cap index funds in existence, run by a long-tenured index desk with ~$34 billion of scale. But the second prong of the new scale — expected upside — is approximately zero by design: an index fund's job is to match its benchmark, not to beat it. This is the canonical "higher conviction with insufficient upside" case in the new scale's Hold definition. Nothing about the fund changed in seven days; only the ruler did.

FSSNX is a textbook index fund doing exactly what it promises: it tracks the Russell 2000 at an expense ratio of 0.025% — roughly one-fortieth of the typical actively managed small-cap fund — with tight index replication and a three-star overall rating on the shares held. The small-cap segment just had a brutal September (−5.3% on the Russell 2000, its steepest monthly drop in more than a year, as the 10-year Treasury pushed toward 5.3% and the Fed hiked to 3.75–4.00%), yet the fund remains up roughly 14% year-to-date. The verdict is on the vehicle, not the timing: this is the right wrapper for small-cap exposure whenever Cam wants it.

Bottom line: HOLD — conviction 7/10. Keep FSSNX as the small-cap core in its retirement account. It costs about $7.84 per year on the current position and has historically matched or fractionally beaten the index net of fees. The three-vehicle small-cap structure stands: FSSNX (broad beta) plus AVUV (active small-value tilt) in this book, and VTWO stays as the Russell 2000 vehicle in its own account — the two trackers cannot be merged across retirement accounts, so each account keeps one.

2. Objective & Index Tracked

FSSNX seeks investment results that correspond to the total return of US small-cap stocks. In practice it invests at least 80% of assets in securities of the Russell 2000 Index, a market-capitalization-weighted index of roughly 2,000 small-cap US companies. The strategy is replication — holding index securities in approximately index weights — though the fund may not hold every name, and it lends securities to earn income that helps offset costs.

Owning small-cap index is a cycle bet: it tends to lead when rates fall and the economy accelerates, and lag in mega-cap-driven rallies or rate-hike regimes. Cam holds it as a structural diversifier, not a momentum trade.

This is a total-return vehicle, not an income play: the trailing yield is roughly 1.1–1.3% (reported), reflecting the fact that many Russell 2000 constituents pay no dividend at all. The most recent distribution was paid in late June 2026 (reported); the portfolio export carries a per-share distribution figure of $0.388 for the fund. The December distribution is the larger of the two and will be the next income event.

Tax note: FSSNX's distributions are overwhelmingly qualified dividends and occasional small capital gains; the semi-annual schedule means most of the year's income arrives in December. The position's 2026 distributions to date are immaterial to the verdict.

AVUV vs FSSNX — different products, both defensible. AVUV is not a cheaper or more expensive version of the same thing; it is a different return stream. Its systematic screens tilt hard toward profitable, cheap small companies (financials ~28%, energy ~17%, consumer cyclical ~19% — vs FSSNX's healthcare-heavy profile), and the value factor has a long academic and lived track record of excess returns over full cycles. Cam is paying 0.25% for a genuine active tilt, and the 4-star rating says the execution has been good. Keeping FSSNX (broad beta) and AVUV (value tilt) is a coherent barbell — this is diversification of construction, not duplication.

VTWO vs FSSNX — pure duplication, but unfixable. Both funds track the identical Russell 2000 index. There is no factor difference, no style difference, no tax difference that matters at this position size, and VTWO costs 2.4× as much. In a single account this would be the one to consolidate — but VTWO and FSSNX sit in different retirement accounts, so cross-account consolidation is not available. Each account keeps its own Russell 2000 vehicle. That is the final answer on the duplication: noted, and correctly left alone.

Recommended small-cap structure (advisory): HOLD FSSNX as the broad small-cap core in its account at the current ~1.86% weight — no accumulation gate recommended for an index vehicle; if the sleeve drifts above ~3% on a small-cap run, direct any new small-cap dollars to AVUV's factor tilt rather than the index. Keep AVUV as the active small-value tilt. VTWO stays in its own account. Result: three small-cap vehicles across two accounts, each with a distinct job, at a blended fee well under 0.15%.

Sizing judgment: the combined ~4.6% small-cap weight is reasonable for a retirement book that is otherwise large-cap dominated (the mega-cap tech positions alone dwarf it). September's episode is the sizing argument in miniature — small caps are the most rate-sensitive equity segment, so in a hike regime this sleeve is a drag and in a cutting regime it is a kicker. The ~1.9% FSSNX weight is small enough that the cycle risk is contained, large enough to matter when small caps run.

8. Risks — What Could Change the Verdict

  • Small-cap cycle risk. The fundamental risk is not the fund — it is the segment. In a rising-rate, late-cycle environment, unprofitable small caps underperform and the index can lag large caps for years (as it did 2023–2024, and as September 2026 just demonstrated). A HOLD on the vehicle does not mean small caps will outperform; it means this is the right vehicle if Cam wants the exposure.
  • Rate sensitivity — elevated right now. With ~32% of index constituents on floating-rate debt, the Fed's September hike to 3.75–4.00% (and the 10-year near 5.3%) is a genuine earnings headwind for the index. The next FOMC is the checkpoint.
  • Tracking breakdown. Would matter only if the fund's tracking error widened materially (e.g., above ~0.5% annualized) — no sign of this; it runs at ~0.06%.
  • Fee increase or strategy change. Fidelity raising the 0.025% fee or changing the benchmark would reopen the case. Neither is remotely likely — Fidelity uses these funds as loss-leader franchise builders.
  • Concentration drift. The Russell 2000's top names rotate yearly with reconstitution; if a single speculative cohort grew to dominate, the index's risk profile would change. Today no equity position exceeds 0.5%.

Appendix B — Glossary

NAV — net asset value: a mutual fund's per-share price, set once daily after market close. Expense ratio — annual operating cost as a percentage of assets; 0.025% = $2.50 per $10,000 per year. Tracking error / tracking difference — how closely a fund follows its index; smaller is better. Russell 2000 — market-cap-weighted index of ~2,000 US small-cap stocks; the industry-standard small-cap benchmark. Securities lending — the fund lends portfolio stocks to short-sellers for a fee, which offsets expenses. Replication — holding index securities in index weights (vs. sampling). Reconstitution — the index's annual (Russell: June) refresh of membership, which index funds must trade through. FOMC — the Federal Open Market Committee, which sets the federal funds rate.
