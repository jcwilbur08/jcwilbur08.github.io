Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Qualcomm's diversification story just got its biggest proof point yet. The September 8 multi-generational agreement with Amazon Web Services — custom silicon and optical connectivity for AI data centers, tied to up to $60 billion in potential product purchases over a decade — is the single most credible validation of the $15 billion FY2029 data-center revenue target since the June Investor Day. Layer on the September 24 Apple patent-license renewal (effective April 2027), the raised ~$7 billion annualized automotive exit outlook, BMW's selection of Qualcomm as lead compute-silicon provider, and double-digit chip price increases effective September 1, and the fundamental backdrop is materially better than when we last wrote.

The catch is sequencing: the Apple modem step-down and the QCT margin trough both land before the diversification ramp pays. CEO Cristiano Amon said Apple product revenue drops roughly 50% from the September quarter to the December quarter, and QCT EBT margins are guided to just 23–25% for the quarter about to be reported. The stock has also run 46% in six months. That is why this is a Buy at 6/10 conviction, not a Strong Buy — the direction of travel improved, but the next two quarters are the proving ground, starting with the fiscal Q4 print expected around November 4.

Research posture: Buy · Conviction 6/10 · 12-month target $215

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

The AWS deal is the first demand-side proof of the data-center story

Until September, Qualcomm's $15 billion FY2029 data-center target rested on a Meta CPU agreement, a first hyperscaler custom-silicon engagement, and roadmap slides. The AWS agreement puts the world's largest cloud buyer behind custom silicon and optical networking with a purchase framework of up to $60 billion over a decade and a ~25-million-share warrant at $161.26 that aligns Amazon's incentives with Qualcomm's execution. Initial revenue begins in the December 2026 quarter. This does not make FY2027 — it de-risks FY2028–29.

2

Automotive is no longer a side business

Fiscal Q3 automotive revenue of $1.59 billion (+61%) was the 23rd consecutive quarter of double-digit growth, and management raised the fiscal 2026 exit annualized run-rate to ~$7 billion. BMW's selection of Qualcomm as lead compute-silicon provider for next-generation ADAS and digital cockpit — on the fifth-generation Snapdragon Digital Chassis now ramping — is the strongest OEM validation to date. With a reported ~$65 billion design-win pipeline, automotive is on a credible path to the $10 billion FY2029 target.

3

The licensing annuity just got safer

The September 24 renewal of the global Apple patent-license agreement, effective April 1, 2027, removes the single largest QTL uncertainty even as Apple insources modems. QTL's ~70% EBT margins on roughly $5.5 billion of annual revenue are the portfolio's highest-quality earnings stream, and the renewal protects them through the modem transition.

4

Pricing power is returning to the chip business

Double-digit chip price increases effective September 1 — explicitly framed by the CEO as passing through supplier cost inflation — are the first broad-based pricing action in recent memory. Combined with easing memory costs, this is the mechanism by which QCT EBT margins recover from the guided 23–25% trough toward the high-20s. The November 4 print is the first checkpoint.

5

Valuation leaves room for the transition without pricing perfection

At $184.87, Qualcomm trades at roughly 17× our FY2027E non-GAAP EPS estimate of ~$10.75 and about 19× trailing free cash flow — a cash-compounder multiple for a business whose non-handset revenue is guided to grow 60%+ in FY2027. The $215 target at 20× FY2027E embeds only a modest re-rating for AWS validation. The Apple cliff and margin trough are in the numbers; the data-center ramp is mostly option value.

Variant perception. The Street's Hold consensus and ~$194–204 average target price Qualcomm as a no-growth handset cash cow with an Apple problem. The differentiated view is that September's three events — AWS, the Apple license renewal, and the auto raise — collectively reframe the FY2027–29 earnings power, while the current price still reflects the handset-trough narrative. The long-duration nature of the AWS purchase framework means estimate revisions should broaden as FY2027 data-center revenue prints.
02 | Business & Industry

Business quality and competitive position

QUALCOMM Incorporated (San Diego) operates two reportable segments plus QSI strategic investments (excluded from non-GAAP results). QCT (Qualcomm CDMA Technologies) is the semiconductor business: Snapdragon application processors and modems for handsets, automotive (Snapdragon Digital Chassis: cockpit, connectivity, ADAS), IoT (consumer, industrial, networking, XR/PC/wearables), and now data center (Dragonfly portfolio: CPUs, AI accelerators, custom silicon, connectivity). QTL (Qualcomm Technology Licensing) is the patent-licensing annuity, structurally around 70% EBT margins, scaling with 4G/5G device units.

Competitive landscape

In handsets, Qualcomm competes with MediaTek at the mid-tier and Apple/Samsung in-house silicon at the premium tier; the Apple modem insourcing is the structural share headwind. In automotive, the Digital Chassis competes with Mobileye, NVIDIA Drive, and in-house OEM efforts — the BMW lead-compute win is a direct competitive statement. In data-center custom silicon and AI inference, the field is NVIDIA, Broadcom, and Marvell; the AWS agreement puts Qualcomm credibly in that conversation for the first time. In licensing, the model is perennially contested but legally entrenched.

Apple is both the largest headwind and a contained one. The modem transition is accelerating — the U.S. iPhone 18 Pro Max teardown (Snapdragon X80) shows Apple fulfilling its 2026 supply agreement at the contractual minimum, and Apple product revenue drops ~50% quarter-on-quarter into December. But the September 24 patent-license renewal through the transition protects the higher-margin QTL annuity, and management frames FY2027 non-handset growth as replacing total FY2026 Apple product revenues. No dollar figure for Apple product revenue has been disclosed; the shape, not the level, is what we underwrite.

What changed in the quarter

The quarter confirmed the two-sided story. Handset revenue fell 20% on industry-wide memory supply constraints and OEM inventory discipline, and combined input-cost inflation (wafers, assembly, test, advanced packaging, memory) compressed QCT EBT margins to 26% from 31% two quarters earlier. Offsetting it, automotive and IoT combined grew 28%, and management guided double-digit sequential handset growth for Q4 as China-OEM demand normalizes. GAAP results remain noisy from the offsetting ~$5.7 billion non-cash tax valuation-allowance items across FY2025–FY2026; non-GAAP EPS, operating income, and cash flow are the cleaner anchors.

The margin trough is now dated and guided. QCT EBT margin of 23–25% for the quarter about to be reported is the cyclical low on management's own numbers, with price increases (effective September 1) and easing memory costs as the recovery mechanism. The November 4 print either confirms the trough — which the stock can work with — or breaks below 23%, which would reopen the earnings debate.

Balance sheet and capital intensity

At June 28, 2026, Qualcomm held $4.53 billion of cash and $3.77 billion of marketable securities against $15.27 billion of total debt, for net debt of roughly $7.0 billion — comfortable for a business generating $10+ billion of annual free cash flow. Shares outstanding of 1,057 million are down from 1,074 million at FY2025 year-end on buybacks, with a $20 billion repurchase authorization (announced April 2026) still largely ahead. Inventories of $8.38 billion (+28% from September 2025) remain elevated, consistent with the memory/input-cost environment. The $3.68 annualized dividend (~2.0% yield) is well covered by free cash flow.

04 | Segments

Segment deep dive: the diversification engine

Automotive — the compounder

Q3 automotive revenue of $1.59 billion (+61%) extended the streak to 23 consecutive quarters of double-digit growth, and management raised the fiscal 2026 exit annualized run-rate to ~$7 billion (from above $6 billion). The fifth-generation Snapdragon Digital Chassis began ramping in September, and BMW's selection of Qualcomm as lead compute-silicon provider for next-generation ADAS and digital cockpit is the marquee OEM validation. A reported ~$65 billion design-win pipeline supports the $10 billion FY2029 target. This is no longer an option — it is a growth business approaching the scale of the handset trough it must offset.

IoT — steady, with AI PC and XR torque

Q3 IoT revenue of $1.83 billion (+9%) was driven by consumer and networking. Embedded in the segment are the AI PC ramp (Snapdragon X / Oryon-based platforms), XR (Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 powering Samsung/Google intelligent eyewear), and wearables. Growth is steadier than automotive but the segment is the natural home for on-device AI monetization beyond phones.

Data center — from slides to purchase orders

The Dragonfly portfolio (C1000 CPU in production 2H 2028, AI300/AI250 inference accelerators, High Bandwidth Compute memory architecture, connectivity, custom silicon) targets $15 billion+ of FY2029 revenue against a framed $1 trillion+ total 2029 TAM. The roadmap stages as: connectivity ramp began FY2026 → custom silicon Q1 FY2027 → AI accelerator 2H FY2027 → CPU 2H FY2028. What changed in September: the AWS multi-generational agreement (custom silicon for AI inference plus optical networking, up to $60 billion in product purchases over a decade, ~25M-share warrant at $161.26, revenue from the December 2026 quarter) plus the earlier Meta multi-generation CPU agreement and a first hyperscaler custom-silicon engagement shipping later in calendar 2026. FY2026E data-center revenue is only ~$0.3 billion and the FY2027 company opportunity is above $1 billion — the ramp is almost entirely ahead, which is both the opportunity and the risk.

Handsets — managing the trough

FY2025's record $27.8 billion (+12%) was followed by FY2026 payback: Q3 handsets of $5.09 billion (-20%) on memory-driven OEM cuts, with management expecting FY2026 Android handset revenue down ~20% versus FY2025 — a greater-than-$1.50 annual EPS headwind. Premium-tier positioning is intact (Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy; record Q1 handset revenue on flagship launches), and the CFO expects double-digit sequential handset growth in Q4 as China-OEM demand normalizes. September's double-digit price increases reassert economics on the volume that remains.

QTL — the annuity, de-risked

QTL revenue of $1.28 billion (-3%) at 69% EBT margins remains the highest-quality earnings in the portfolio. The September 24 Apple patent-license renewal (effective April 1, 2027) extends the global agreement through the modem transition — the licensing annuity is now more durable than the modem revenue it outlasts. Key license renewals and OEM royalty pressure remain evergreen watch items, but the Apple tail risk is off the table.

06 | Strategic Initiatives

September changed the story: AWS, Apple, and pricing

The AWS agreement — structure and significance

Announced September 8, the multi-generational collaboration covers custom silicon for AWS AI data centers (centered on AI inference) extending to optical networking products. The commercial structure — up to $60 billion in potential product purchases over a decade, plus a warrant for ~25 million shares at $161.26 (roughly $4 billion if fully exercised over ten years) — aligns Amazon's incentives with Qualcomm's delivery. Initial revenue is expected in the December 2026 quarter. Two honest caveats: the purchase figure is a ceiling, not a commitment, and warrant-linked customer deals introduce a circularity question — demand quality, not just demand size, becomes a tracking item, the same lens we apply to Broadcom's Anthropic financing. Still, landing the world's largest cloud buyer is the strongest possible validation of a data-center roadmap that, weeks earlier, was still mostly slides.

Apple: modems out, license renewed

The two Apple tracks diverged decisively in September. On modems, the iPhone 18 teardown confirmed the U.S. Pro Max — and only the U.S. Pro Max — carries the Snapdragon X80, with Apple C2 everywhere else: Apple is fulfilling its 2026 supply agreement at the contractual minimum, and the CEO said Apple product revenue falls ~50% from the September to the December quarter. On licensing, the September 24 global patent-license renewal (effective April 1, 2027) secures the annuity through the transition. Net: the low-margin hardware attrition accelerates while the high-margin licensing stream is protected — painful in the near term, accretive to mix over time.

Pricing power returns

Double-digit chip price increases effective September 1 — explicitly described as passing through supplier cost inflation that Qualcomm "cannot continue to absorb" — are the first broad-based pricing action in recent memory. With DRAM and storage costs having pressured the entire device chain (Apple itself raised Mac/iPad prices in June), Qualcomm is choosing margin over volume into a trough. If memory costs ease from here, the price increases drop disproportionately to the bottom line in FY2027.

07 | Risks

Key risks

High

Apple step-down pace

The modem transition is accelerating faster than the original 20% planning assumption. The open question is the pace of the step-down versus the FY2027 non-handset ramp — management asserts the latter replaces the former, but dollar figures on both sides are undisclosed.

High

QCT margin trough

QCT EBT margins compressed from 31% to 26% in two quarters on mix and input-cost inflation, with 23–25% guided for FQ4. A print below 23% would reopen the earnings debate the AWS deal just helped close.

Medium

Memory cycle

Industry-wide memory supply constraints drove the FY2026 handset trough (a greater-than-$1.50 annual EPS headwind). Normalization is the single biggest near-term EPS lever — and its timing is not in Qualcomm's control.

Medium

Data-center execution

The $15 billion FY2029 target and above-$18 non-GAAP EPS target embed a steep, back-half-loaded ramp from a ~$0.3 billion FY2026 base against entrenched incumbents. The AWS framework is a ceiling, not a commitment; custom-silicon concentration is validation and customer-concentration risk at once.

Medium

China exposure

Qualcomm's own risk disclosures flag significant China concentration compounded by U.S./China trade and national-security tensions. No quantified China revenue split was verified for this report.

Lower

Licensing disputes

Structurally high-margin but perennially contested; the Apple renewal removes the largest single overhang, leaving ordinary-course OEM royalty pressure.

On Morningstar's 2-star to 3-star restoration

Morningstar restored Qualcomm to 3 stars (from 2) in the October 2 export with fair value unchanged at $170 — this is a mechanical valuation move, not a fundamental upgrade. The stock's decline from ~$198 to ~$185 narrowed the price-to-fair-value gap; nothing in the fair value itself changed. Our $215 target sits above Morningstar's $170 fair value: the divergence reflects our crediting the September AWS agreement and the validated data-center framework, which a $170 fair value — roughly 16× our FY2027E estimate — does not appear to embed. If Morningstar refreshes its model for the AWS purchase framework, fair value should move toward our range; if it does not, the disagreement is an honest one about how much to pay for unproven data-center revenue.
