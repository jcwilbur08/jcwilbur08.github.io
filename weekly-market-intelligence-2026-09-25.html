1 · Executive Call

The Hike Landed. The Market Moved On — Selectively.

Nine days after the Fed resumed hiking, equities chose to look through it: renewed AI optimism pulled a record-size weekly inflow back into equity funds, and Friday belonged to Microsoft, the chip complex, and industrials. But the bond market refused the memo — yields climbed every day this week — and the tape underneath was two-tiered: mega-cap tech advanced while small caps and rate-sensitive income lagged.

Monetary policy

The hiking cycle is back — and priced for more

The September 16 FOMC raised 25 bp to 3.75–4.00%, unanimous 12–0, the first hike since July 2023. The dot-plot median sits at 4.1% for end-2026 with 16 of 18 participants seeing further tightening, and markets were pricing roughly 68% odds of another hike at the October 27–28 meeting during the week.345

Market action

Big tech carried; small caps didn’t

S&P 500 +1.2%, Nasdaq +2.1%, Dow +0.3% — but the Russell 2000 fell 0.8%, a 2-percentage-point underperformance. U.S. equity funds drew +$37.6B, the first inflow in five weeks, yet small-cap funds lost −$1.02B and financials bled −$2.53B. Friday’s leaders were Technology (+0.99%) and Industrials (+0.94%); Energy (−0.87%) lagged.167

Macro mix

Activity hot, consumers cold

September flash PMI composite hit 58.4 — the strongest since July 2021 — with input-cost inflation at a 41-month high, jobless claims held at 197K, and new-home sales beat (684K SAAR). But Michigan sentiment sank to 48.1, a four-month low, with one-year inflation expectations at 4.6%. August PCE and the Q2 GDP final — both due Wednesday — will adjudicate.8910

Earnings & AI

AI capex intact; the bar is higher

Broadcom’s September 2 print was the template: AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7B (+221% YoY) and an FY2027 AI outlook of $115B — and the stock still sold off on margins. Semis rebounded Friday (SOXX/SMH +1.4% on NVDA/AVGO/MU), Costco beat on revenue and EPS but traded flat, and Darden missed and fell 3.6%. Micron reports Wednesday with $50B of revenue guidance on the line.111213

House view

Tactically cautious into the data gauntlet; constructive on the intermediate trend. The market has absorbed one hike and is pricing a decent chance of a second — the risk into Wednesday/Friday is a hot PCE or payrolls print that locks October in and punishes duration and small caps. The opportunity is the mirror image: in-line prints fade hike odds, relieve yields, and broaden leadership. No portfolio action is warranted ahead of the prints; the standing price gates (NVDA $220, TSM ≤$460, GRID $160s) remain the discipline.

4 · Micro & Sector Trends

The Market Pays for AI Revenue, Not AI Promises

Broadcom set the week’s template three weeks ago: $16.7B of AI semiconductor revenue, up 221%, guided to $115B in FY2027 — and the stock fell on margins. Friday’s semiconductor rebound (SOXX/SMH +1.4% on NVDA, AVGO, MU) shows demand is not the question. Delivery and profitability are.

AI semiconductors: demand proven, margins scrutinized Constructive

  • Broadcom (Sep 2): revenue $29.59B (+86% YoY); AI semi $16.7B (+221%); Q4 guide $34.8B with AI semi $21.7B; FY2026 AI outlook $58B, FY2027 $115B, FY2028 $230B — shares sold off on gross-margin softness.
  • Friday rebound: SOXX and SMH each +1.4%, led by Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron.
  • Nvidia pledged a $12.9B investment in Hugging Face (Sep 3) — vertical integration of the AI stack continues.
  • Next test: Micron reports Wednesday AMC guiding ~$50B revenue at ~86% gross margin; the bar is a beat and a clean margin story.

AI monetization: products landing, stocks choppy Mixed

  • Meta surged 4.5% Thursday on Connect AI product announcements — its “Muse agent” app topped U.S. download rankings — then gave back 3.5% Friday. Product momentum is real; multiple expansion is not automatic.
  • Microsoft rose ~4% Friday, among the S&P’s top movers — enterprise AI spend remains the steadier bid.
  • Akamai gained ~3% Friday on a reported $11.6B Anthropic investment — inference infrastructure is the next capex frontier.
  • Oracle invoked force majeure on its $4B “Project Jupiter” data-center build, warning of 6+ month delays — a reminder that the buildout has physical bottlenecks.

Consumer: spending, but selectively Softening

  • Costco beat (revenue $95.723B vs $94.9B; EPS $6.75 vs $6.54, though $0.15 was a one-time tariff refund) — and the stock barely moved. Beats are table stakes now.
  • Darden missed narrowly ($2.05 vs $2.06; revenue $3.20B) and fell ~3.6% Friday despite FY2027 EPS guidance of $11.10–$11.35.
  • KB Home: revenue $1.30B in line but down 20% YoY; full-year guidance light — housing volume is being bought with price.
  • Nike — removed from the S&P 100 (replaced by Palo Alto Networks) — reports Thursday AMC; consensus $0.44 EPS. A consumer-health read-through.

Market structure: narrow leadership, sector rotation Narrowing

  • Friday: 8 of 11 S&P sectors rose, but leadership was Technology (+0.99%) and Industrials (+0.94%); Energy (−0.87%) and Communication Services (−0.65%) lagged.
  • Fund flows confirm the rotation: tech +$4.89B in, financials −$2.53B out — notable with Goldman Sachs (−1.40% Thursday) as a Dow drag.
  • UnitedHealth was a bright spot: +0.99% Thursday, among the Dow’s leaders.

What changed this week

The AI trade’s filter tightened: revenue growth no longer suffices — margin delivery and buildout execution decide the multiple. Broadcom’s post-print fade and Oracle’s force majeure are the cautionary tales; Microsoft’s Friday strength and the semi rebound are the counterweight. For the portfolio’s AI sleeve (AVGO/NVDA/TSM/QCOM), Micron’s Wednesday print is the next margin exam.

5 · Global & Thematic Context

Tightening Is a Global Story Again

Europe: equities up, politics heavy

  • STOXX 600 +0.5% — snapped a three-week losing streak; banks +1.3% led Friday.
  • German 10-year at 3.17%; eurozone yields rose a seventh straight week; markets price at least one more ECB hike in 2026.
  • French–German 10-yr spread ~109 bp, touching 114.06 intraday — widest since June 2012. France is the eurozone’s political risk premium.

Asia: BoJ hiked; China quiet

  • Bank of Japan raised to 1.25% (7–2, Sep 18); yen weakened beyond ¥157/$; September flash PMIs softened (composite 52.5).
  • China had a quiet week; August data (released Sep 15) showed factories firm (+5.2%) but retail soft (+0.4%) and investment negative.
  • U.S.–Canada: 50% tariffs on ~$20B of goods stand; new Canadian whey import curbs take effect Sep 29.

Energy & geopolitics

  • Brent settled Friday at $104.32 (−2.1%) and WTI at $92.41 (−2.3%) as reports of U.S.–Iran talks over a phased Strait of Hormuz reopening cooled the risk premium — with Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia still a live threat.
  • A Trump–Xi meeting was expected around September 24; no new U.S.–China tariff action surfaced during the week.

8 · Market-Reaction Playbook

Three Ways the Data Week Plays

Hot data · ~30%

October hike gets locked in

Core PCE runs hot and/or payrolls print well above +100K with firm wages. October hike odds jump toward certainty; the 10-year pushes through recent highs; small caps and rate-sensitive income lag hard; financials stay under outflow pressure.

  • Book: hold quality; no new duration; let the standing gates (NVDA $220, TSM ≤$460, GRID $160s) do the buying.
Base case · ~50%

In-line prints, odds drift lower

PCE cooperates (~0.2% MoM core) and payrolls land near +100K. October hike odds fade from ~68%; yields stabilize; breadth improves with a small-cap relief bid; the AI trade keeps its margin filter.

  • Book: a calmer tape is the backdrop for planned adds — TSM on weakness ≤$460, UNH toward ~2%, GRID only in the $160s.
Weak data · ~20%

Growth scare, defensive bid

Payrolls disappoint sharply (<+50K) alongside soft PCE. Hike odds collapse but recession chatter replaces them; defensives and long duration rally; cyclicals and small caps wobble on earnings risk.

  • Book: protect; don’t chase defensives at highs; cash ($43K) is optionality, not a problem.
