Executive investment view

American Express remains one of the highest-quality consumer-finance franchises in the portfolio. The combination of affluent customer demographics, closed-loop economics, premium fee growth, disciplined underwriting and strong capital returns is still producing double-digit revenue and EPS growth. The September policy tightening and 5%+ long-end Treasury environment raise the discount-rate hurdle and increase the probability of eventual credit normalization, but the evidence does not yet show a deterioration in AXP's customer base.

At the September 25 close of $308.89, the stock trades near 17.5x 2026 consensus EPS and 15.3x 2027 consensus EPS, well below Visa and materially below AXP's own 2025 valuation. A reliable third-party PEG estimate is approximately 1.28x. The stock therefore looks substantially more attractive than it did at the prior underwriting, provided the next earnings release confirms that credit remains contained and premium-product economics are not being overwhelmed by benefit and acquisition costs.

Decision hinge

Can AXP sustain low-teens EPS growth with credit losses still near current levels while higher rates and richer premium-card benefits pressure funding and operating costs?

The answer is currently leaning yes, but the October 23 earnings release is the most important near-term confirmation point.

Proposed CIO review

Consider a staged-add review rather than an aggressive increase. Research supports a constructive stance at current valuation, but the Portfolio CIO should decide whether incremental exposure is justified given already-elevated total financials exposure.

1. What has changed since the previous underwriting?

Changed for the better

  • Valuation reset: AXP closed at $308.89 on September 25 versus $312.43 on September 16, after touching $301.96 on September 23. The stock is roughly 20% below its 52-week high of $387.49.
  • Credit remains resilient: August U.S. Consumer 30+ day delinquency stayed at 1.1% and Small Business delinquency improved to 1.3% from 1.4% in June. August principal-only net write-offs were 1.7% for Consumer and 2.2% for Small Business.
  • Consensus still supports growth: 2026 EPS consensus is $17.67 and 2027 is $20.18, implying about 14% growth next year.

Changed for the worse

  • Fed policy tightened: the FOMC raised the funds target to 3.75%-4.00% on September 16, increasing the risk of further consumer and small-business pressure if tightening persists.
  • Long rates remain restrictive: the 10-year Treasury was roughly 5.06% on September 25, with the 20-year and 30-year above 5.4%. That raises valuation hurdles for all financial assets and can increase deposit competition.
  • Expense intensity is elevated: Q2 expenses rose 12% as AXP spent more on rewards, services, Platinum benefits and operating costs. Future upside increasingly depends on those investments earning attractive incremental returns.

2. Business quality and competitive position

AXP's moat is unusually broad for a card issuer because it combines issuer economics, network economics, merchant relationships, data, premium brand and loyalty. Its closed-loop system provides richer transaction data than open-loop issuers and enables coordinated pricing, underwriting and rewards. The customer base skews toward affluent consumers and higher-quality small businesses, which historically supports better credit outcomes and more durable spend.

Premium Membership Model

Net card fees grew 15% year over year in Q2, reflecting strong acquisition, retention and product-refresh activity. Fee-paying products and premium benefits reduce dependence on pure revolving credit economics.

Younger cohort strength

Management continues to emphasize Millennials and Gen-Z as the largest and fastest-growing cohort. This matters because AXP is replacing older cardholders with customers who may have decades of lifetime value ahead.

International runway

International Card Services billed business grew 13% in Q2 (12% FX-adjusted), extending a long run of double-digit growth and providing a second engine beyond U.S. premium consumer.

5. Management, capital allocation and balance-sheet quality

Capital strength

AXP's CET1 ratio was 10.4% at June 30 versus a 7.0% effective minimum, while the Tier 1 leverage ratio was 9.6%. This provides a solid cushion and supports continued capital return.

Customer deposits were $157.0B, up from $152.5B at year-end 2025. The direct deposit franchise is strategically important because it diversifies funding beyond wholesale markets.

Shareholder returns

Diluted shares fell about 3% year over year in Q2. The current dividend is approximately $3.80 per share, a yield near 1.2%, while buybacks are the larger capital-return lever. This is attractive so long as repurchases are executed below reasonable intrinsic value.

Q2 annualized ROE was 36.4%, highlighting the franchise's strong earnings power, although financial-company ROE should not be compared directly with asset-light network ROIC.

6. Industry and macro context

The macro environment is mixed for AXP. Higher short rates can support asset yields, but the September Fed hike also increases funding costs and raises the probability that consumer and small-business credit eventually normalize. Long Treasury yields near 5% are a separate valuation headwind because they raise the opportunity cost of owning equities.

AXP is better positioned than many lenders because its economics are not purely spread-driven: discount revenue, net card fees and premium membership monetization are large and growing. Conversely, Visa and Mastercard remain structurally less credit-sensitive because they do not carry the same loan book. That distinction explains why AXP deserves a lower multiple than the networks even when its near-term growth is similar.

9. Catalysts, risks and contrary evidence

Catalysts

  • October 23 Q3 earnings confirm 10% revenue growth guidance and the $17.30-$17.90 EPS range.
  • Continued mid-teens net card fee growth validates premium-product pricing power.
  • Platinum refresh continues to accelerate spend while retention remains high.
  • Millennial/Gen-Z acquisition translates into durable fee revenue and lifetime spend growth.
  • Credit remains better than industry averages even as rates stay restrictive.

Risks / contrary case

  • Higher rates eventually weaken premium consumer and small-business credit with a lag.
  • Rewards, lounge, services and acquisition costs grow faster than monetization.
  • Regulation caps card rates, fees or merchant economics.
  • Merchant steering/surcharging reduces transaction share or discount economics.
  • Visa/Mastercard plus bank issuers intensify premium-card competition, raising acquisition costs.
  • Recession or geopolitical shock hits travel and entertainment spend disproportionately.

12. Key checkpoints and thesis-break conditions

Upcoming checkpoints

  • October 23, 2026: Q3 earnings; verify revenue, EPS guidance, spend growth, premium card fees and expense discipline.
  • Monthly: U.S. Consumer and Small Business 30+ day delinquency and principal-only net write-offs.
  • Platinum economics: retention after fee increases, spend acceleration and benefit utilization.
  • Funding: deposit growth and deposit costs after the September Fed hike.
  • 2027 consensus: EPS revisions must remain around $20 or better for the current valuation case to hold.

Explicit thesis-break conditions

  • Consumer 30+ day delinquency sustains above roughly 1.6%-1.7% and continues rising across multiple months without a macro recovery.
  • Consumer principal-only net write-offs move sustainably above roughly 2.5%-3.0% while spend growth simultaneously decelerates.
  • Net card fee growth falls to mid-single digits without an intentional portfolio action or clear cyclical explanation.
  • 2027 EPS consensus falls materially below $18.50 while valuation remains above roughly 17x forward earnings.
  • Premium-card benefit and engagement costs remain structurally above revenue growth and erode pre-provision earnings power.
  • Regulatory action materially impairs card pricing, merchant economics or interest-rate economics.

13. Research conclusion

AXP's underlying thesis remains intact and the security has become more attractive. The stock now offers a better balance of franchise quality, growth and valuation than at the prior underwriting. The most important new fact is not a fresh operating deterioration; it is the higher rate regime. Thus far, August credit data argue that AXP's customer quality is absorbing the pressure.

The current valuation offers a reasonable margin for a modest credit normalization, but not for a severe downturn. Research therefore supports a constructive, staged-add posture for CIO review, with the October earnings print and monthly credit statistics acting as gating checkpoints. The position should not be expanded without considering aggregate financials exposure.
