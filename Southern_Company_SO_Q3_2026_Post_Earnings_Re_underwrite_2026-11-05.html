EPS quality: reported versus adjusted

The $1.94 adjusted quarter is $0.17 above GAAP EPS and adjusted net income is $196M above GAAP. The adjustments principally involve accelerated depreciation from wind repowering, debt-extinguishment and associated tax adjustments, plus Nicor Gas capital investment costs. These are not cash-neutral in all cases, and ongoing repowering costs continue into 2027. Do not value the company using adjusted EPS without understanding why adjustment categories recur. S1

What would distort extrapolation? One of the warmest Q3 periods in roughly 50 years boosted demand; weather-normal retail sales of +3.5%, particularly +7.3% commercial, provide stronger evidence of underlying growth than headline revenue +13%. This is a seasonally powerful quarter—not a run rate for Q4. S1, S2

03 / Structural demand and the capex opportunity

The strongest evidence in the call is conversion from announced projects into billed consumption. Actual Q3 data-center usage rose 59% year-on-year and 52% for the nine months. Management described 21 GW of signed large-load agreements across Alabama, Georgia and Mississippi, plus ~7 GW of late-stage negotiations. A broader 75 GW funnel includes projects that will not all proceed. Treat 21, 7 and 75 as distinct confidence buckets; none of these figures is equivalent to a company-owned generation order. S2

Competitive position

Southern's integrated regulated utilities offer available land, incumbent network rights, experience procuring generation and customer contracts, and a territorial advantage for large loads. The company also benefits from an established residential / commercial population base. The counterargument is that geographic concentration in the Southeast increases correlated weather, political, and growth-cycle risk. Regulatory revenue protection—not simply new gigawatts—is the ultimate economic moat.

04 / Georgia election, PSC and affordability risk

Two political developments warrant direct valuation attention. Keisha Lance Bottoms won Georgia's November 3 gubernatorial election, and Democrats secured a majority on the Georgia Public Service Commission. The new political balance could heighten examination of utility rates, data-center cost allocation, generation additions and future return assumptions. No formal new regulatory action was established on the November 5 call; this is an increased uncertainty premium, not a confirmed change to allowed ROE or existing contracts. S8, S9

The critical underwriting question

Do large-load minimum charges and security terms genuinely protect residential and small-business ratepayers if data-center projects underutilize, delay or close? Management stressed contractual minimum bills, large customers' full cost contribution and an estimated ~$3B in Georgia customer benefits. Those are company claims that must be verified in tariffs and subsequent regulatory decisions. S2

CEO Chris Womack said management had already discussed the framework with the governor-elect during the campaign and expected to explain the contracts further. He also acknowledged the affordability debate. Georgia Power expects to make a rate filing in 2028; the company characterized its residential base-rate arrangement as extending to at least 2029. These statements are forward-looking, and a different commission composition could alter the trajectory. S2

05 / Financial quality, funding and shareholder returns

Southern operates a capital-intensive, debt-financed regulated utility model. Heavy growth investment can make reported free cash flow materially negative even when projects earn future regulated returns. Therefore, standalone negative FCF is not automatically a distress signal; it remains vital to compare financed capex with allowed returns, construction risk, cash recoveries and equity issuance.

Explicit discounted cash flow: dividend-based equity DCF

Because construction outlays substantially exceed current operating cash flow, conventional unadjusted FCFF would conflate today's growth build with permanently negative economics. A transparent two-stage discounted dividend stream (equity DCF proxy) is therefore useful alongside P/E and rate-base underwriting. This is not a complete FCFF-to-enterprise valuation; it discounts actual shareholder distributions and assumes continuing access to financing.

Analyst assumptions only. Formula: Σ[t=1..10] dividend(0)×(1+g)^t/(1+Ke)^t + [dividend(10)×(1+g_terminal)/(Ke−g_terminal)]/(1+Ke)^10. Uses a constant annual compounding assumption and no explicit interim dividends, buybacks or tax adjustment. Terminal value makes the model highly rate-sensitive.

Reverse DCF — what is the market paying for? Keeping 5% annual ten-year dividend growth and 3.5% terminal growth, $93.34 implies only about 7.31% cost of equity. Alternatively at a 7.5% cost of equity and 3.5% terminal growth, the stock price implies approximately 5.59% dividend growth for ten years. The market is already giving Southern substantial credit for dividend durability and financing access. Small rate changes can move the value materially.

FCF yield and earnings multiples must not be blended indiscriminately: utilities may have negative FCF while regulated asset bases compound. The right ongoing cross-checks are regulated ROE, rate-base growth, FFO/debt and per-share earnings after dilution. The ~ $89 DCF base and $96 P/E base scenario are directionally consistent: modest value at current price, with a better margin of safety below $89.

08 / Research conclusion, milestones and falsifiers

Company quality

8 / 10. Scale, integrated regulated footprint, captive demand growth, record of dividend payments and access to federal financing. Main caveat: capital complexity and regulatory concentration.

Valuation attractiveness

5 / 10. Better-than-normal utility growth at a near-peer multiple, but DDM base ~$89 versus $93.34 and real policy/funding uncertainty.

Thesis-break / falsification conditions

  • A materially adverse PSC decision impairs cost recovery or allows large-load costs to migrate to existing retail customers without appropriate compensation.
  • Signed-load ramp and minimum-bill collection materially underperform at two sequential reporting checkpoints.
  • Equity issuance or interest costs consume a disproportionate share of incremental EPS from the capex program, or FFO/debt persistently trends away from management's ~17% 2029 goal.
  • Generation cost overruns, permitting or lead-time problems reduce approved rate-base returns or lead to material disallowance.

Research conclusion / open questions

Retain / watch for $82–$89. Southern has earned a stronger operating outlook after Q3; the stock has not yet provided the margin of safety that would justify aggressive new capital at $93.34. The investment debate moves from is load real? to how much incremental per-share cash flow and regulatory return will shareholders keep after financing?

Open confirmations: Q3 Form 10-Q line-by-line debt and cash-flow reconciliation; Southern's official Q3 deck; signed-contract customer security terms; after-call FY27 consensus revision; forecast FFO/debt sensitivity; independent proof of regulated cost allocation. Until sourced, these remain unanswered rather than silently assumed.
