1. What’s new since initiation: Armada and the November 2 print

Oct. 1–4 · sovereign AI infrastructure · Q3 earnings date confirmed

The one genuine development since the October 3 initiation arrived the day before it was written: on October 1, Palantir announced Armada as its inaugural Certified Modular Data Center Partner. The agreement pairs Palantir's Sovereign AI Operating System with Armada's Galleon modular data centers, Armada Platform, and Sovereign AI Grid — customers can run open-weight models on data, compute capacity, and hardware they control, including fully air-gapped deployments. Commercial terms were not disclosed. Strategically, this is the right-shaped deal for Palantir's map: it adds a physical infrastructure layer to a software platform already centered on sovereign deployments, where management says it is winning against frontier labs precisely on data-and-model-control grounds. It also opens a commercial channel in regions where "sovereign AI" is procurement language, not marketing language. That said, it is a partnership announcement with no disclosed economics — a directionally positive sign, not a numbers event.

Q3 earnings confirmed
Nov. 2, 2026

The quarterly update is on the calendar. The company guides Q3 revenue of $2.160–$2.164 billion (~80% growth); the street expects roughly 84% — above guidance, again. The bar for this name is always set above what management says.

2. The quarter: the best print in enterprise software

Q2 2026 — reported August 3, 2026

Palantir's second quarter was extraordinary on almost every line. Revenue of $1.935 billion grew 93% year over year (19% sequentially) and beat the ~$1.81 billion consensus. Both GAAP and adjusted diluted EPS were $0.41 — a substantial beat against consensus estimates that ranged from roughly $0.28 to $0.35 depending on the provider — and the company has now beaten expectations nine quarters running. GAAP income from operations was $912 million (a 47% margin); adjusted operating income was $1.194 billion (62%). Combined with 93% growth, the Rule-of-40 score hit 155%, up 10 points quarter over quarter.

Cash generation matched the P&L: adjusted free cash flow was $1.220 billion (63% margin) and cash from operations $1.216 billion. Management raised fiscal 2026 guidance across the board — the largest guidance raise in company history — to revenue of $8.150–$8.158 billion (~82% growth), U.S. commercial revenue above $3.424 billion (at least 134% growth), adjusted operating income of $4.889–$4.897 billion, and adjusted free cash flow of $4.5–$4.7 billion, with GAAP operating and net income expected in every quarter of 2026. Q3 guidance calls for revenue of $2.160–$2.164 billion (~80% growth). One caution on quality: unrealized gains on the company's SpaceX holding added $0.03 to GAAP EPS and $0.02 to adjusted EPS per the CFO — small, but worth knowing the beat wasn't 100% operational.

3. The AIP machine: bootcamps, land-and-expand, and the copycats

1,049 customers · 157% net dollar retention · $2.1B U.S. commercial TCV

The Artificial Intelligence Platform (AIP) is the growth engine, and its distribution model is the story. Palantir runs intensive multi-day bootcamps where prospects build working AI use cases on their own data, compressing multi-quarter enterprise sales cycles into days or weeks. Customer count reached 1,049 (+24% year over year), with 653 U.S. commercial customers (+35%), up from roughly 155 commercial customers before AIP launched in April 2023. Bootcamp conversion rates are not consistently disclosed and published figures conflict — a figure worth treating with skepticism rather than as fact.

The land-and-expand math is spectacular: net dollar retention of 157% (up 700 basis points quarter over quarter), total contract value of $3.373 billion (+49%), record U.S. commercial total contract value of $2.132 billion (+153%), total remaining deal value of $13.1 billion (+83%), and remaining performance obligations of $4.9 billion, roughly doubled year over year. The company closed 220 deals of at least $1 million in the quarter. Management says AIP is winning competitive deployments against frontier labs on AI-sovereignty grounds — customers keep data and model control on-premise rather than feeding third-party models — and points to a major Silicon Valley technology company that chose Palantir over frontier labs for a $10 million annual contract.

The copycats are coming. Oracle introduced an AI data platform that fully automates the creation of enterprise ontologies — explicitly crediting Palantir with pioneering the approach — Microsoft's Fabric IQ directly copies the ontology concept, and Snowflake and Databricks have added semantic layers. Morningstar still assigns only a narrow (not wide) moat, built on switching costs and intangible assets. The ontology framework is a real architectural edge today; it is not a permanent one.

4. Business quality: 86% gross margins and a narrowing map

Adj. gross margin 86% · top 20 customers $124M avg · international share shrinking

Unit economics are top-tier SaaS: adjusted gross margin of 86% in Q2, with Morningstar projecting 83–85% over the next decade. Customer concentration is a feature and a bug: the top 20 customers averaged $124 million of trailing revenue, up 67% year over year — deepening relationships with large accounts, but meaningful concentration all the same.

The geographic picture is the weak spot. Government was ~51% of Q2 revenue (roughly $990 million of $1.935 billion; fiscal 2025 was 54% government), and international revenue was only ~19% of the total — down from ~27% a year earlier — with international commercial growing just 26% against U.S. commercial's 149%. Europe is the hole: Germany is reportedly pulling back from Palantir contracts, Spain has asked state-owned enterprises to limit them, and 120+ European organizations signed a September open letter demanding EU contract termination. Management calls Europe an AI laggard but notes Arab states exploring AI defense solutions; Morningstar's read is blunt — Europe adopting would be a massive accelerant, and it is not doing so. The Armada partnership's sovereign-AI framing reads as a direct response to this channel.

5. Capital allocation: $9.2B cash, heavy SBC, no dividend

$9.2B cash, no debt · SBC $265M = 13.7% of revenue · no dividend

The balance sheet is pristine: $9.2 billion in cash and short-term Treasuries at quarter-end with no debt. (One aggregator cited $1.79 billion of total debt; two other sources say none — we treat the debt claim as unreliable.) Capital return, however, is a one-way street that favors employees over shareholders. Stock-based compensation was $265.2 million in Q2 — 13.7% of revenue — with first-half SBC of $466.8 million up 48% year over year, an annualized run-rate of roughly $1.06 billion. The $1 billion buyback authorization (August 2023) has seen no 2026 repurchase activity that could be found, and basic shares still rose 1.5% year over year: the program functions as dilution management, not net reduction. To be fair, GAAP profitability is genuine — $1.062 billion of GAAP net income after fully expensing SBC — but the SBC load is a persistent shareholder cost. Palantir has never paid a dividend and has given no indication it will.

Watch the insider tape. CEO Alex Karp sold roughly $54 million of stock in May 2026, co-founder Peter Thiel cashed out more than $1 billion in 2025 plus multi-tranche 2026 sales around $140–$146, and ARK Invest trimmed about $26 million in September 2026. Insider selling at these valuations is normal; it is also a supply overhang that bears price against.

6. Valuation and target framework

~149–164x trailing P/E · ~117x FY2026E · ~55x EV/sales · 1% FCF yield

At $188.75, Palantir trades at roughly 149–164x trailing GAAP earnings (sources differ on the basis, not the conclusion), ~117x fiscal 2026 consensus EPS of $1.61, ~81x fiscal 2027 consensus EPS of $2.33, and ~55x enterprise value to guided 2026 revenue — versus the S&P 500 at 21.9x trailing earnings and Snowflake at ~16–21x forward sales. The free-cash-flow yield is ~1% on the fiscal 2026 guidance midpoint. The only mitigating fact is the denominator's velocity: EV/EBITDA has compressed from 463x in fiscal 2025's third quarter to 96x in fiscal 2026's second — the multiple is shrinking as earnings outrun the price. But make no mistake: this valuation requires Palantir to become one of the most profitable software companies in history and stay there.

$200 twelve-month target (~6% upside) — maintained. Framework: ~86x fiscal 2027 consensus EPS of $2.33 — essentially the ~81–85x multiple the market already pays on fiscal 2027 earnings. The target assumes one year of earnings delivery with no multiple expansion, which is the honest way to underwrite a stock at 55x sales: Palantir must grow into the multiple it already has. Cross-checks: Morningstar fair value $153 (~19% downside, 3 stars); street average ~$195 (~3–4% upside); street median $204 (~8%); street high $255 (BofA); street low $80. Our target splits the street's optimism and Morningstar's discipline — a HOLD with upside capped by the multiple, not by the business.

7. The other side of the trade: what the bears see

The $85 DCF, the surveillance headlines, and the 51% government book

The bear case is not that Palantir is a bad company — it is that the stock is priced for a future the company has to execute flawlessly to reach. One published bear-case discounted cash flow put intrinsic value at $85–$110 against a then-$172 price; at $188.75 the gap is wider. The multiple leaves no room for the growth deceleration that eventually finds every enterprise software company, and ~51% of revenue still comes from government, exposed to defense-budget shifts, procurement politics, and a controversy cycle that is live right now.

That controversy deserves plain treatment. The September 28–October 1 "People vs Palantir" global week of action, a September 30 march of 1,000+ nurses in San Francisco protesting ICE/CBP ties (centered on an app allegedly built with improperly obtained Medicaid data), reporting that ICE logs observers and protesters in a Palantir-built database (~$126 million in contracts since 2022, including $30 million for "ImmigrationOS"), 120+ European organizations demanding EU contract termination, and the U.K.'s £330 million NHS Federated Data Platform contract facing a break clause triggerable in March 2027 — together they form a reputational overhang with a real commercial channel: international government and commercial expansion. Palantir calls the claims baseless, says it does not collect or store data and does not conduct surveillance, and the September protest cycle has produced no contract cancellations yet. The FAA's June award of its $875 million SMART air-traffic contract to a competitor shows Palantir does not win every government bake-off either.

Why we are not initiating an entry here. The business case is nearly flawless; the risk/reward is not. Downside to Morningstar's $153 fair value is ~19%; upside to our $200 target is ~6%. A HOLD at $188.75 is not a call on Palantir's quality — it is a call on its price. The entry we want is the one where the multiple, not the growth, does the work.

9. Risks and catalysts

Q3 November 2 · Army $10B ceiling · NHS break clause March 2027

Risks: (1) Valuation — the central risk; any growth deceleration meets a multiple with no cushion. (2) Government concentration — ~51% of revenue; defense budgets, procurement delays, and the live political cycle are real exposures. (3) SBC dilution — ~$1.06 billion annualized run-rate, net share growth ~1.5% per year. (4) Insider selling — Karp and Thiel sales plus ARK trimming are a steady supply source. (5) Competition — frontier labs, Oracle, Microsoft, and sovereign European alternatives are all copying the ontology playbook. (6) International stall — the shrinking ~19% international share must re-accelerate for the growth story to compound.

Catalysts: Q3 2026 earnings November 2 (company guides revenue of $2.160–$2.164 billion, ~80% growth; the street expects ~84% — above guidance); Army $10 billion ceiling task orders, Maven procurement, and TITAN awards; AIPCon 12 (AIPCon 11 in September drove a street-high target raise); the Armada modular-data-center partnership ramp (commercial terms pending); the PwC alliance from September 2026; and the NHS contract break-clause window in March 2027, which cuts both ways.
