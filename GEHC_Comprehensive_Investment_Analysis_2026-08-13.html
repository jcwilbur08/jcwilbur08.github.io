Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

GE HealthCare is becoming a better business faster than its 2026 margin profile suggests. The core Advanced Imaging Solutions and Pharmaceutical Diagnostics franchises are showing healthy demand, record orders and a rising backlog, while the company is shifting its mix toward software, cloud, AI and recurring revenue. The market is still appropriately discounting a weak Patient Care Solutions business, post-Intelerad leverage, China pressure and a medium-term margin target that remains materially above current performance.

Research posture: ADD | Conviction 7/10 | Base value $92

At $73.77, GEHC trades at roughly 15.1x the $4.90 midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance and at an approximate 4.8% guided free-cash-flow yield. The valuation does not require perfect execution. A credible return to 4%-5% organic growth, moderate margin recovery, and a solution for PCS can support a mid-to-high-teens earnings multiple and a low-$90s value. The key reason this is not a Strong Add is that the 2024 Investor Day target of high-teens to 20%+ adjusted EBIT margin remains substantially above the 15.4%-15.7% 2026 guide. [S1, S4]

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

Installed-base economics create a durable franchise

GEHC has roughly 5 million installed units globally and serves about 1 billion patients annually. Equipment placements create follow-on service, upgrade, software and consumables opportunities. In 2025, services generated $6.96B, about one-third of revenue, and the company spent $1.26B on R&D. This combination of installed hardware, service infrastructure, regulatory know-how and clinical workflows creates meaningful switching costs even in a competitive market. [S3, S7]

2

Demand is stronger than the current earnings narrative

Q2 organic revenue growth was only 3.5%, but organic orders increased 11.1%, book-to-bill reached 1.15x and backlog rose to a record $23.9B. AIS organic revenue grew 5.0% and PDx 14.6%. This matters because a large imaging order book can take time to convert to revenue; orders and backlog therefore provide a more forward-looking signal than near-term reported sales alone. [S1]

3

Portfolio mix is shifting toward recurring software and high-margin diagnostics

The $2.3B Intelerad acquisition adds a cloud-first enterprise imaging platform with estimated first-full-year revenue of ~$270M, roughly 90% recurring, and expected adjusted EBITDA margin above 30%. PDx already produces close to 30% segment EBIT margins and is growing rapidly. Over time, software, radiopharmaceuticals and enterprise solutions can improve recurring revenue, growth durability and mix. [S5]

4

PCS is a problem - and a potential source of upside if management acts decisively

PCS Q2 revenue declined 13.5% organically and segment EBIT margin was -3.8%. Management is reviewing strategic options to maximize long-term value. A recovery, divestiture or other restructuring could reduce earnings volatility and make the quality of the remaining AIS/PDx/software portfolio more visible. The thesis does not assume an immediate sale; it assumes the current level of PCS losses is not permanent. [S1]

5

Current valuation offers room for execution without requiring a heroic multiple

At the $73.77 reference price, GEHC trades at ~15.1x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance and at a ~4.8% FCF yield using the ~$1.6B guide. Approximate market capitalization is $33.3B and enterprise value is $41.3B using June 30 cash/debt and issued less treasury shares. The valuation is attractive if the company can move earnings growth back toward high-single digits and restore confidence in margin expansion.

Variant perception. The market is focused on a messy spin-off transition, PCS weakness, tariff/inflation pressure, China and higher leverage. The differentiated view is that AIS/PDx demand and the order book are healthier than the consolidated margin profile implies, while software and a potential PCS strategic action can improve the business mix. The valuation offers a reasonable margin of safety if that improvement is gradual rather than perfect.
02 | Business & Industry

Business quality and competitive position

GE HealthCare is a global medical-technology platform spanning diagnostic imaging, image-guided procedures, ultrasound, enterprise imaging software, pharmaceutical diagnostics and patient monitoring. Following the April 2026 reorganization, the company reports three segments: Advanced Imaging Solutions, Pharmaceutical Diagnostics and Patient Care Solutions. AIS combines the former Imaging and Advanced Visualization Solutions businesses into a $14.6B 2025 revenue platform. [S6]

What changed in 2026

Q1 showed the downside of operating leverage and supply inflation. The company reduced its initial 2026 adjusted EBIT-margin guide from 15.8%-16.1% to 15.4%-15.7%, adjusted EPS from $4.95-$5.15 to $4.80-$5.00, and FCF from ~$1.7B to ~$1.6B, while keeping organic revenue growth at 3%-4%. In Q2, management reaffirmed the revised outlook after stronger orders and better-than-feared demand. [S1, S8]

Quality of Q2 demand was better than quality of Q2 margin. Q2 revenue rose 5.7% and adjusted EPS rose 6.6%, but adjusted EBIT margin declined 40 bps to 14.2%. The quarter included tariff refunds while still absorbing memory-chip, oil and freight inflation. The earnings path is therefore less about demand rescue and more about converting demand into normalized profitability. [S1]

Balance sheet and capital intensity

At June 30, 2026, GEHC held $2.1B of cash and ~$10.1B of debt. On a share count of approximately 451.7M (issued less treasury shares), the $73.77 reference price implies a market capitalization of about $33.3B and enterprise value of roughly $41.3B. Net debt is approximately $8.0B. Intelerad was funded with cash and debt, increasing the importance of disciplined integration and deleveraging. [S1, S5]

Dividend and capital returns

The quarterly dividend is $0.035 per share, or $0.14 annualized, for a yield of only about 0.19% at the reference price. GEHC is therefore a total-return and business-improvement thesis, not an income thesis. The company also repurchased $200M of shares in Q2 and had $500M remaining under its authorized program at quarter-end. [S1]

04 | Management & Capital Allocation

Execution is improving, but governance and M&A require monitoring

CEO Peter Arduini combines prior GE Healthcare experience with a decade as CEO of Integra LifeSciences. Since the spin, management has pursued a strategy centered on precision care, product innovation, lean/Heartbeat productivity, cloud/AI, radiopharmaceuticals, enterprise partnerships and selective M&A. [S4, S7]

Positive

Portfolio evolution

The AIS reorganization simplifies accountability and better integrates devices, image-guided care and enterprise imaging. The willingness to review strategic alternatives for PCS is a constructive sign that management is prepared to address structurally weak assets. [S1, S6]

Positive

Intelerad strategic logic

Intelerad adds recurring SaaS revenue and high-margin software exposure. GEHC expects low-double-digit revenue growth, >30% adjusted EBITDA margin and high-single-digit ROIC by year five. [S5]

Watch

Capital structure

Higher debt after the $2.3B cash acquisition reduces flexibility. Buybacks are rational only if leverage remains controlled and the core margin trajectory improves.

Watch

CFO transition

Jay Saccaro is scheduled to step down after August 14, 2026; Controller and Chief Accounting Officer George Newcomb becomes interim CFO while a permanent search proceeds. The transition is not inherently negative, but it adds a near-term governance checkpoint during a period of portfolio restructuring. [S9]

07 | Portfolio Fit

Portfolio fit: useful healthcare diversification, but not an income position

The sole portfolio baseline for this section is retirement-portfolio-21.xlsx. GEHC is not currently held. The snapshot has approximately $1.68M of market value and $43,000 of cash (~2.56%). Direct single-name healthcare exposure is modest: Bristol-Myers Squibb is ~1.24% and UnitedHealth is ~1.20%, or about 2.44% combined, excluding healthcare exposure embedded in diversified funds.

Fit benefit

Different healthcare economic engine

GEHC adds medtech, installed-base service, imaging capital equipment, cloud software and pharmaceutical diagnostics. That is meaningfully different from BMY’s pharmaceutical pipeline risk and UNH’s managed-care/reimbursement exposure.

Fit trade-off

Still a cyclical growth equity

Hospital capital budgets, China, rates, supply chains and public procurement can affect results. GEHC diversifies healthcare business-model risk but does not behave like a defensive high-dividend healthcare holding.

09 | Final Recommendation

ADD - attractive asymmetric setup, with clear checkpoints

Rating
ADD
Conviction

GEHC is investable because the core franchise is stronger than the consolidated margin story, while the valuation compensates investors for a meaningful but identifiable set of execution risks.

The attractive part of the setup is not simply that the stock is below its prior high. It is that forward demand indicators are improving at the same time valuation remains restrained. AIS and PDx account for the overwhelming majority of revenue and are growing; the company has a large installed base and expanding recurring-software footprint; and management has acknowledged PCS requires a different outcome. If those pieces translate into margin progression, $92 does not require the full Investor Day aspiration.

The caution is equally important. A 15x earnings multiple can become expensive if the business never advances beyond mid-single-digit EPS growth. The thesis therefore requires observable milestones: PCS improvement or strategic action, sustained backlog conversion, Intelerad integration, leverage control and a credible path from a ~15.5% 2026 adjusted EBIT margin toward the high teens.

Investment checklist

Demand: Q2 orders/backlog support thesis.
Valuation: Attractive at ~15x 2026E EPS.
Moat: Installed base + service + clinical integration.
Margins: Not yet de-risked.
Balance sheet: Manageable, but leverage elevated.
Capital allocation: Strategic, with acquisition execution risk.
Catalysts: PCS, backlog conversion, software mix.
Income: Minimal; dividend is not the thesis.
What would change the rating? A move toward Strong Add would require evidence of sustained AIS/PDx strength plus a clean PCS resolution and improving margin/FCF trajectory without balance-sheet deterioration. A downgrade to Hold/Watch would follow persistent PCS losses, weakening orders, or evidence that the medium-term margin structure is materially lower than management’s framework.

Evidence confidence: High for reported financials, guidance, segment trends, capital structure and company strategy because the analysis relies principally on SEC filings and company releases. Moderate for the 2027 scenario EPS/multiple and price value because those are analyst underwriting assumptions, not company guidance. Underwriting status: Initiation complete, execution-dependent.
