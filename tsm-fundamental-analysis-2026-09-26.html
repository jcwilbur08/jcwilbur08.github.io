1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

This report updates the 22 September 2026 initiation. Four days later the thesis is intact and the numbers have, if anything, firmed: August revenue printed a record NT$514.81B (+53.3% y/y) R, Q3 is tracking with room to spare against guidance, and sell-side FY2027 estimates have drifted up (Erste to $21.91; MarketBeat $21.72) E.

TSMC remains the world's dominant pure-play semiconductor foundry — a 72.5% global foundry revenue share in Q2 2026 (TrendForce) E — and the toll road on AI compute: 77% of wafer revenue from 7nm-and-below nodes, high-performance computing at 66% of sales and growing 20% quarter-on-quarter, and 2nm in commercial production with a steep H2 ramp R.

Thesis O: The foundry moat is widening, not narrowing. Samsung's share has roughly halved since early 2023 while TSMC's hit a record; 2nm pricing power is demonstrable (67.7% gross margin while funding a $60–64B capex year internally); and the demand signal — ~$730B of 2026 hyperscaler capex, raised three times this year by Alphabet alone — remains the strongest external validation of the capacity build. The record Q2 print was flattered ~9% by a one-off Vanguard share gain, and the H2 margin path is genuinely tougher (3–4pp of N2 ramp dilution plus 2–3pp of overseas-fab dilution, both company-guided) R. But at 27.2× FY2026E compressing to ~20.7× FY2027E on ~30% forward EPS growth, the stock is priced for execution, not perfection.

The $50B question O: the "slightly more than 40%" full-year revenue guide implies ≥$171.4B C — which needs roughly $50B in Q4 after H1's $76.1B and the Q3 guide midpoint of $45.2B C. That is ~11% above Q3's guide and well above the $167.1B sell-side consensus E. August already printed at the required monthly run-rate (~$16.1B), so the guide is achievable — but it leaves no room for a soft Q4. Estimate revisions into the October 15 print are the near-term tell.
Bottom line: Maintain BUY; raise the 12-month ADR target to $545 (~21% upside from $450.61), anchored on the same 25× FY2027E consensus EPS of ~$21.8 framework as the prior report C/E. The $15 target raise is purely estimate-driven (consensus FY2027E moved $21.20 → ~$21.8); the multiple framework is unchanged. At $450.61 the stock sits below the $460 add line — the standing accumulation thesis is actionable here.
Portfolio note: Cam holds 24 shares (~$10,815, 0.64% of the ~$1.70M portfolio). Morningstar rates TSM 4 stars with a $534 fair value (Sept 2026 export) — our $545 target sits modestly above both Morningstar and the $523 sell-side average, reflecting the 2nm pricing-power premium.

2. Business Overview & Revenue Mix

Founded in 1987, TSMC pioneered the pure-play foundry model: it manufactures chips designed by others and competes with no customer's products — the "trust model" underpinning its ecosystem lock-in R. In 2025 it deployed 305 distinct process technologies and manufactured 12,682 products for 534 customers R.

6. Technology & Manufacturing Footprint

  • 2nm (N2): first revenue in Q2 2026 — 3% of wafer revenue R. Management calls the N2 family "another large and long-lasting process node," with N2P and A16 enhancements to follow; "steep ramp-up" expected in H2 2026 R. End-2026 capacity is industry-estimated at ~90–130k wafers/month (conflicting estimates — treat as rough) E. Demand confirmation: Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 (unveiled Sept 22) is the first mass-market 2nm mobile chip E.
  • A16 (1.6nm-class): TSMC's first backside-power (Super Power Rail) node; company guidance is production-ready in H2 2026 R; Taiwanese media now point to mass production in Q4 2026, meaningful volume in 2027 E. Disclosed gains: +8–10% speed or −15–20% power vs N2P R. NVIDIA is reported to have booked A16 capacity for next-gen AI GPUs (single source) E.
  • A14 (1.4nm-class): customer tape-outs are ahead of schedule (reported Sept 23) E; +10–15% performance or −25–30% power vs N2; pre-production 2027, volume production 2028. A13 debuted at the April 2026 North America Technology Symposium R.
  • Arizona: Fab 1 in volume production (4nm) since Q4 2024 R; Fab 2 tool move-in through 2026 with volume production pulled forward to H2 2027 (3nm); Fab 3 (N2/A16) under construction; first on-site advanced-packaging line being readied E. Total US investment framework now ~US$265B after the additional US$100B announced alongside Q2 results R. CFO (July 2026): "very happy with the progress in Arizona."
  • Japan (JASM Kumamoto): Fab 1 in volume production since end-2024 R; Fab 2 construction started Oct 2025, reportedly upgraded toward 3nm with a 2028 production target E.
  • Germany (ESMC Dresden): 28/22nm planar + 16/12nm FinFET; 40,000 300mm wafers/month; equipment move-in H2 2026, production targeted late 2027 E.
  • Advanced packaging (CoWoS): industry estimates put current capacity at ~75–80k wafers/month rising to ~120–130k by end-2026 (single source — rough) E; advanced packaging is estimated at slightly over 10% of revenue heading to low-teens in 2026 E. TSMC broke ground on a Kaohsiung packaging-validation park (Sept 21, 2026), operations targeted Q4 2029 E. The 10–20% of 2026 capex allocated to advanced packaging/testing/mask confirms its strategic weight R.
  • Ecosystem: Cadence expanded its TSMC partnership (Sept 24) — A14/N3P/N2P design-tool certification and 3DFabric IP — a leading indicator of customer design-in at the next nodes E.

7. Competitive Position

Foundry market share (TrendForce, Q2 2026) E: TSMC 72.5% (record) · Samsung 5.9% · SMIC 5.4% · UMC 3.9% · GlobalFoundries 3.2%. Top-10 combined revenue US$53.49B, +11.5% q/q (record). Samsung's share has roughly halved since Q1 2023 (12.4% → 5.9%); SMIC is closing on second place.

Moat sources O: (1) process-technology lead — the only foundry shipping 2nm at scale, with A16 and A14 roadmaped; (2) scale/cost — 4.3M+ wafers/quarter amortize R&D (NT$73.1B in Q2 alone R) and the capex base; (3) ecosystem lock-in — 534 customers, 12,682 products, 305 process technologies R; (4) the trust model — a pure-play foundry with no competing chip products. At 72.5% share, TSMC's quarterly foundry revenue exceeds Samsung Foundry's by more than 12× — the market is effectively a monopoly at the leading edge.

Competitive watch — Samsung's 2nm is stirring E/O: Samsung's SF2 yields are reported (leaks) to have recovered from ~50% in late 2025 to ~60%+ in Q1 2026 and possibly ~80% by early September. Tesla's AI5 began prototype production at Samsung's Taylor, TX fab on September 15, 2026 (mass-production verification targeted end-2026; $16.5B deal through 2033), and OpenAI added Samsung Foundry to its silicon program (Sept 10) as a TSMC second source. Tesla also dual-fabs at TSMC Arizona. None of this has moved TrendForce share data — but it is the first credible leading-edge share threat in two years. Samsung postponed its 1.4nm-class node from 2027 to 2029 E, conceding the roadmap race. Intel's 18A entered high-volume manufacturing in January 2026 (Panther Lake) with external foundry volume still exploratory — no share impact.

8. Risks

  1. Taiwan geographic concentration O: the leading-edge base remains in Taiwan; cross-strait escalation is the canonical tail risk. August saw 125 PLA ADIZ incursions — less than half the August 2024/2025 tempo — and Taiwan proposed a record NT$1T (~$31B) 2027 defense budget E. Chronic gray-zone pressure, no acute crisis — but it is the stock's core existential risk. Mitigants in progress (Arizona/Japan/Germany) currently dilute margins (company-stated R) and will take years to reach Taiwan cost structure.
  2. US trade policy — Section 232 E: Proclamation 11002 (Jan 14, 2026) imposed a narrowly scoped 25% tariff on specified advanced computing chips (effective Jan 15, 2026), with broad carve-outs for US data centers, startups, R&D and consumer electronics. A broader "Phase 2" remains in active deliberation — Commerce's September "build in America or pay" framing signals escalation risk, not a settled outcome. Separately, a 15% polysilicon tariff with minimum import prices takes effect December 4, 2026. Do not model a settled broad-based tariff — scope and timing are unresolved O.
  3. Export controls: US restrictions on advanced AI chips to China persist; direct China revenue was 6% in Q2 2026 (down from 9% in Q2 2025) R — manageable directly, but second-order customer-demand effects (NVIDIA/AMD China exposure) matter O.
  4. Customer concentration O: Apple and NVIDIA are widely believed to be the two largest customers (top two ≈36% per industry research E), but TSMC does not disclose splits — size single-customer risk qualitatively, not with invented precision.
  5. Capex intensity & FCF: US$60–64B capex in 2026 vs ~US$36B TTM FCF — the dividend is safe (net cash; ~25% payout), but FCF conversion stays depressed until the build moderates O.
  6. FX: NT$ appreciation compresses reported margins (cited as a drag in 2025) R; Q3 2026 guide assumes NT$32.0/US$ R (Q2 averaged NT$31.60).
  7. Cycle risk: HPC is 66% of revenue R — AI-capex digestion or a smartphone downturn would hit utilization; inventory days are already rising on the N2 ramp O.
  8. Execution: 2nm yield/ramp cadence, Arizona Fab 2 timeline, and the H2 margin bridge (3–4pp N2 dilution + 2–3pp overseas dilution vs the 65–67% Q3 GM guide) are the near-term operational watch items O.
