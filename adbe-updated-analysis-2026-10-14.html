02 · What changed

Two downgrades, one known problem, and a lower share price.

Adobe drifted from roughly $241 on October 8 to $237.92 intraday on October 14; the October 13 close was $236.28. The one-month return through October 13 was −6.3%, and the one-year return was −30.4%. The operating company issued no new guidance in the interval.

The desk is the outlier

Street consensus is Hold with a $272.31 average target. The desk’s $320 is about 17.5% above that average and implies about 34.5% upside from the reference price. This is not a consensus call.

The bear test is measurable

Digital Media net-new ARR declined roughly 38–40% year over year in FQ3. December 9 must show the Q4 acceleration needed to preserve the full-year ARR framework; narrative alone will not clear that bar.

The target needs modest multiple repair—not a return to exuberance.

05 · Competitive tape

Real pressure, uneven evidence.

ArtCraft: real code, premature parity

Seven free open-source creative apps drew roughly 24,000 GitHub stars in their first week. The developer initially promised “100% feature parity” in one month, then moved the timetable to “months, not years.” Hands-on testing shows material gaps. The project is a genuine headline risk; commercial impact on Adobe is unverified.

Canva: clearer go-to-market threat

Canva launched ProSuite on October 11, bundling Affinity and Leonardo AI as a professional alternative to Creative Cloud. Pricing and plan structure were not fully verified in the research, so the desk treats positioning as confirmed but economics as open.

  • 01
    DOL PERM suspensionThe October 8 action stops new and pending PERM processing for Adobe and seven other companies. H-1B is unaffected; duration and Adobe-specific financial impact remain unclear.
  • 02
    Insider sellingAggregators report $50.8M sold versus $1.9M bought over 12 months and about $31.45M sold in FQ3. Much may be routine 10b5-1 activity, but that was not verified.
  • 03
    OpenAI Sora retreatThe consumer video product was shut down and the API removed, reducing one direct threat. It does not remove broader model commoditization risk.
  • 04
    Figma executionFigma’s quarter showed $370.1M revenue, +48% year over year, 136% net dollar retention, and a raised FY2026 revenue guide of $1.463–1.467B.
06 · Capital return

At this price, buybacks can become an operating advantage.

Adobe spent $2.232B in FQ3 to repurchase roughly 9.5M shares at an average near $235. The authorization has $24.55B left—enough to matter against a roughly $94B market capitalization.

FQ3 repurchase$2.232B≈9.5M shares at ≈$235
Nine-month repurchase$6.82B26.1M shares at ≈$261
Cash + ST investments$5.64BVersus roughly $6.4B debt
Authorization left$24.55BProgram runs through Apr 30, 2030

Not free money

Nine-month repurchases absorbed about 90% of operating cash flow. Stock-based compensation consumes part of the benefit, and the balance sheet is not net cash. The program is powerful only if the business value is intact and repurchases continue below intrinsic value.

What breaks the call

Another large net-new ARR shortfall, reliance on acquisition contribution to hold growth, or proof that free alternatives are displacing paid professional workflows—not merely attracting hobbyists.

08 · Risks & signals

The thesis is cheap for a reason.

High · demand quality

Net-new ARR fails to reaccelerate

The largest near-term risk. A weak Q4 would validate Wells Fargo’s critique and make the full-year ARR target appear acquisition-assisted or low quality.

High · disruption

AI compresses seat value

Open-source, Canva, Figma, and model-native workflows can lower switching costs or shift value away from Adobe’s applications.

Medium · execution

Leadership turnover clusters

The CEO, CFO, and creative-product leadership transitions concentrate execution risk while strategy is under maximum scrutiny.

Medium · regulation

PERM suspension disrupts talent plans

The action is open-ended. Scope, duration, and direct financial effect remain unknown.

Medium · capital

Buybacks mask dilution rather than create value

Repurchases only compound value if free cash flow stays durable and stock-based compensation does not absorb the share count reduction.
