Week ended Friday, October 2, 2026

Soft jobs cooled the near-term Fed threat—but not the market’s inflation or duration problem.

The Nasdaq finished higher on semiconductor strength, yet the broader tape remained fragile: long Treasuries and high yield sold off, the dollar strengthened, financials and health care lagged, and the 10-year yield ended near 5.26%. The investable message is selective risk, not broad risk-on.

What changed

Growth

September payrolls rose only 29,000 and unemployment increased to 4.2%. The labor market moved further into “low-hire, low-fire,” even as September manufacturing remained expansionary at 54.5.

Inflation / policy

August PCE was softer than feared, but headline inflation remained 3.4% year over year; the ISM prices index jumped to 77.9 and oil stayed above $100. An October Fed pause is likelier, but a December hike remains plausible.

Leadership

Technology (+1.8% via XLK) and energy (+1.3% via XLE) led. Financials (−2.5%), health care (−2.7%) and real estate (−1.8%) lagged. The mix favors earnings durability and scarce growth over economic beta.

Cross-asset confirmation

TLT fell 2.3%, HYG 1.2%, GLD 3.4% and UUP rose 1.0%. A stronger dollar plus weaker duration and credit is a tightening signal, despite Friday’s equity bounce.

Decision frame: Do not equate one soft payroll report with a durable discount-rate reversal. Require confirmation from services prices, FOMC minutes, oil, and the 10-year yield before treating the Friday bounce as broad regime change.

Rates and liquidity

The 10-year Treasury briefly reached 5.34%, its highest since 2002, and finished around 5.26%; the 2-year ended near 4.83%. The long end’s failure to rally after soft payrolls suggests fiscal supply, term premium and energy inflation are competing with slower growth.

Signal The curve’s positive 2s10s spread near 45 basis points reflects a term-premium problem more than a clean growth acceleration.

Fed reaction function

The September FOMC raised the target range to 3.75%–4.00%. Policymaker messaging and the payroll miss reduced October-hike odds to roughly one in five, but persistent price pressure leaves December live.

Research conclusion The modal path is pause-and-assess, not easing. Equity duration remains exposed to any renewed oil or inflation shock.

Companies, sectors and global

AI strength meets broader operating friction

Semiconductors

Micron: fiscal Q4 results and Q1 guidance exceeded elevated expectations, confirming strong AI-memory demand. Yet MU finished the week down 0.7%, a reminder that excellent fundamentals can meet already demanding positioning.

Foundry/equipment: ASML rose 7.1%, TSM 5.0% and NVDA 4.0%. The chain’s breadth was the clearest equity positive.

Consumer / mobility

Tesla: delivered 486,532 vehicles in Q3, above expectations but down about 2% year over year. The beat supports near-term sentiment; the strategic debate still hinges on autonomy and AI execution.

Nike: announced more restructuring after quarterly revenue fell 4%; the company expects most savings later in the plan. Cost action does not by itself solve China, product or brand momentum.

Portfolio-linked developments

Disney: reported plans to reorganize television around streaming audiences, potentially affecting hundreds of roles. DIS fell 3.8% for the week. Cost discipline is a positive possibility; continued linear-TV pressure is the thesis risk.

Rate sensitivity: AMT fell 4.1%; JPM, GS, MA and AXP declined between 2.0% and 3.5%. XOM gained 2.2% and GEV 3.4%, providing factor offsets.

Global confirmation

Euro-area September inflation accelerated to 3.8%, keeping ECB tightening risk alive. China’s official manufacturing PMI returned to expansion at 50.1; global factory surveys showed AI-related capital spending offsetting parts of the energy shock.

The stronger dollar and simultaneous sovereign-bond selloff argue that global liquidity remains restrictive even as industrial activity improves.
