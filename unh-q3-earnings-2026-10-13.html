01 · Desk call

Maintain Strong Buy. This print supports the live gate.

Q3 did not create a new thesis; it validated the standing one. UnitedHealth converted deliberate membership and value-based-care pruning into a cleaner cost structure, materially better operating earnings, and a higher earnings floor.

The medical care ratio was the decisive print: 88.4% against 89.7% expected. That 130-basis-point beat powered a $4.69 adjusted EPS result versus $4.14 consensus and enabled a third 2026 guidance raise to at least $20.50.

The quality was not pristine. Revenue fell 0.4% year over year, the quarter included $610 million of favorable prior-period reserve development tied to 2026 service dates, and Optum revenue was below FactSet expectations. This was a margin-driven beat—not a top-line breakout.

Portfolio verdict

Rating, conviction, and $475 target are maintained. The accumulate-toward-2% gate remains the live instruction. No gate change follows from a one-day sell-the-beat reversal.

04 · Optum and membership

Prune the wrong revenue; protect the right earnings

Optum Health's 10% revenue decline was intentional. The unit exited value-based-care markets where it lacked the density to sustain clinicians—and operating earnings rose from $41 million to $735 million.

Membership contraction is deliberate—but real

UnitedHealthcare served 48.3 million people versus 50.1 million a year ago. Medicare Advantage enrollment is down 935,000 since year-end 2025; employer and Medicaid rolls also declined in Q3.

Stars remain a forward headwind

At least 69% of MA members are in 4+ Star plans for 2027, down from 78%. Management expects to approach 75% by payment year 2028, but the near-term revenue step-down still matters.

05 · 2027 and valuation

The new bullish data point is the 2027 starting line

Management stopped short of formal 2027 guidance, but the CFO called the $20.50-or-better 2026 base a solid run rate and indicated that the low end of the 13–16% long-term growth range is a more reasonable starting point than current consensus.

Why the target holds

The $475 target already embeds the better 2027 base. Q3 raises confidence in the earnings path, not the multiple. Formal 2027 guidance before year-end is the next valuation test.

Important distinction. The approximately $23.16 figure is desk arithmetic—13% growth on the new $20.50 floor—not company guidance. Management has not issued a formal 2027 EPS range.

Sell-the-beat, not thesis break

Shares opened near $397.63, traded to $399.88, and then reversed to close at $377.76, down 0.20%. The early 4.5% premarket move was surrendered as investors treated the beat-and-raise as priced in. Cantor Fitzgerald maintained Overweight and a $495 target, arguing that 2027-and-beyond upside is becoming more visible.

06 · Risks and watch items

Execution improved. The overhang stack did not disappear.

High

Reserve quality and medical-cost reacceleration

The $610 million reserve benefit flattered Q3 MCR, and the year-ago reserve-development comparator was not disclosed. Specialty pharmacy and acute-care intensity remain elevated.

High

Commercial cost pressure

Commercial trends exceed the original 11% expectation. No Surprises Act arbitration awards, clinician pricing, and specialty drugs could dilute gains from Medicare Advantage recovery.

High

DOJ overhang by silence

There was no incremental DOJ disclosure: no executive addressed the investigations and no analyst asked. The absence of new information is not resolution.

Medium

Membership and Star-rating trade-offs

Profitability is improving partly because the company is shrinking. A 935,000-member MA contraction and lower 4+ Star penetration can constrain the revenue bridge into 2028.

Medium

Optum top-line softness

Optum revenue missed FactSet expectations, though the exact miss was not disclosed in the coverage reviewed. Operating discipline must eventually translate back into durable growth.
