04 / Competitive position and the business being underwritten

Why the moat may persist

Gotham and defense software operate in restricted environments; Foundry integrates operational data with reusable ontology; AIP adds models, agents, governance and workflow deployment. Tight integration, security approvals and forward-deployed engineers create switching costs as customer applications spread.

U.S. commercial NDR at 165% indicates established customers are spending much more, while +148% U.S. commercial growth shows new deployments and expansion at unusual scale.

How the moat can erode

Model providers, cloud platforms (Azure/AWS/Google Cloud), Salesforce, ServiceNow, Snowflake and Databricks can compete for agents, connectors and enterprise workflow budgets. Client-led open standards might reduce vendor lock-in. Sales friction, bespoke deployment and political procurement constraints limit international scale.

Moat strength is not a license to capitalize current margins indefinitely.

Business mix: Q3 total commercial $1.17B (~50.4% of $2.313B), total government $1.15B (~49.6%); U.S. commercial $984M and U.S. government $953M. U.S. sales ~$1.94B, ~84% of total. International revenue increased ~26% per Barron’s reporting, materially slower than the U.S.; geography remains concentrated.

Capital allocation: $10.4B in cash, cash equivalents and U.S. Treasuries, no debt at quarter end. Very low capex and high operating leverage support a cash-rich balance sheet, although equity grants dilute the economic claim and large government contract awards carry option and cancellation risk. No buyback assumption is included in this model.

Weighted scenario outcome: 25% bear + 50% base + 25% bull = $161/share. The broad dispersion is the message: minor shifts in durable growth/terminal discounting produce very different valuations. The base value is roughly -23% versus the $172 purchase price; weighted value is roughly -6%.

Reverse DCF: what must $172 deliver?

Holding the above discount rate, 3% terminal growth, $10.4B net cash and 2.574B diluted shares constant, a $172 share price requires approximately 28.7% annually compounded adjusted FCF growth for a full decade under a deliberately stylized constant-growth path. At the $184.36 regular-session reference, the growth hurdle is ~29.7%.

Economic SBC sensitivity: Q3 stock compensation annualized = ~$1.28B. If the initial $4.9B adjusted-FCF anchor is conservatively reduced by that amount, the base case drops to $100 and the constant-growth hurdle at $172 rises to 33.0%. This is a replacement-cost proxy sensitivity, not a second cash expense mandated by GAAP. Using an SBC cash haircut and an extensive dilution haircut may count the same economic cost twice, so the two approaches should be evaluated as alternatives rather than summed mechanically.

08 / Risks, catalysts, falsifiers and research conclusion

Upside path

  • Existing customer expansion stays above 145% NDR.
  • Commercial contract value re-accelerates into FY2027.
  • Government production awards convert into recurring mission revenue.
  • Margins remain strong even including a meaningful SBC cost.
  • Platform/ontology becomes a de facto layer for sovereign enterprise agents.

Downside path

  • Commercial bookings weakness precedes revenue deceleration.
  • LLM labs and hyperscalers reduce platform pricing power.
  • Large-contract concentration or government options disappoint.
  • International procurement/geopolitical controversy restricts adoption.
  • Long-dated discount-rate pressure compresses the premium multiple.

Next checkpoints — what changes our decision?

  1. Q4 FY2026 earnings (expected early 2027; exact date not yet confirmed): management's $2.649–2.653B revenue guide; degree of beat; margin and billings cash conversion.
  2. FY2027 outlook: whether formal guidance validates >45% sustained revenue growth and credible EPS/FCF conversion.
  3. Commercial TCV and remaining deal value: distinguish timing from a genuine sales-funnel break; watch US commercial TCV growth and RPO simultaneously.
  4. Retention and customer cohorts: NDR 165%, commercial customer additions and evidence bootcamps become production contracts.
  5. SBC and dilution: employee equity expense/revenue and next reported diluted share denominator.
  6. Government execution: Maven/TITAN production ramp, contract options and budget scrutiny.
Proposed thesis-break conditions for CIO review: (a) two consecutive quarters with US commercial revenue growth below ~65% without a credible mix offset; (b) NDR below 125% or repeated customer contraction; (c) GAAP operating margin below 30% alongside decelerating growth; (d) material government program cancellation or security failure; (e) equity dilution or SBC intensity reverses operational leverage; (f) persistent conversion/billings weakness shows AIP deployments are not durable.
