Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Bristol-Myers Squibb is a deep-value pharmaceutical franchise paying investors 4.1% to wait for 2027 pipeline readouts — and the wait just got a little easier. In the seven days since our September 27 initiation, the FDA expanded CAMZYOS into pediatric hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, ZENBEXUS posted a striking Phase 3 win in multiple myeloma, and Piper Sandler raised its target to $82. The stock fell 2.7% anyway, widening price upside to our $68 target to about 11% — and that combination is exactly what the new rating scale was built to capture.

01 | New This Week

What changed since September 27

Seven days, four developments — three constructive, one a reminder that pipeline investing cuts both ways:

Positive

CAMZYOS pediatric expansion approved

On September 30, the FDA approved CAMZYOS (mavacamten) for adults and pediatric patients weighing at least 30 kg with symptomatic obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, based on Phase 3 SCOUT-HCM results (44 adolescent patients). CAMZYOS was already the cardiovascular breakout (+129% in Q2); a pediatric label widens the funnel in a disease with almost no approved pediatric options.

Positive

ZENBEXUS Phase 3 win in myeloma

On September 25, the EXCALIBER-RRMM trial read out: ZENBEXUS (iberdomide) plus daratumumab/dexamethasone delivered a 41.1% minimal-residual-disease-negative complete response rate vs. 20.7% for the comparator (420 evaluable patients, 15.7 months median follow-up), with overall response 88.9% vs. 76.1%. The first approved CELMoD just got its best commercial argument yet — the myeloma franchise Revlimid vacated is being rebuilt with data, not hope.

Positive

Piper Sandler to $82 (Overweight)

On September 16, Piper Sandler raised its target from $75 to $82, explicitly citing upcoming pivotal milestones for milvexian, Cobenfy, and admilparant. The street's bull case now sits 34% above the reference price — a reminder that the pipeline optionality this report underwrites at 9.9x is being priced far more aggressively elsewhere.

Watch

ORM-6151 halted

An undisclosed pipeline asset (ORM-6151) was halted in mid-September — a routine portfolio triage event, but a useful counterweight to the week's good news: not every shot on goal scores, which is exactly why the rating is Buy at 6/10 conviction rather than something heroic.

Price action vs. news flow. The stock fell from $62.86 (Sep 25) to $61.15 (Oct 2), down 2.7% on a week of net-positive company news — the decline tracks broad-market softness, not a BMY-specific signal. For a price-anchored Buy thesis, that divergence is the opportunity: the fundamentals improved while the entry got cheaper.

Dividend declared

The board declared the regular $0.63 quarterly dividend on September 16, payable November 2, 2026 to shareholders of record October 2 — $2.51 annualized, a 4.10% forward yield at the reference price, with a payout ratio of only ~36% of guided non-GAAP EPS. The dividend was not raised this quarter, but at 2.7x earnings coverage it remains one of the safest payouts in large-cap pharma.

02 | Earnings Preview

Q3 earnings preview — October 29

Third-quarter results publish October 29, 2026, with the investor call at 8:00 a.m. ET (confirmed in the company's September 18 release). Twenty-five days out, this is the immediate checkpoint for the Buy thesis.

What to watch

  • Growth Portfolio momentum: Q2 printed +15% to $7.56B with nine double-digit growers. The Buy thesis needs a second consecutive quarter at or above that pace — deceleration toward single digits would undermine the "executing, not promising" conviction upgrade.
  • Eliquis trajectory: Q2's +22% to $4.48B came despite the January 1 Medicare negotiated-price reset. Watch whether volume strength persists into Q3 or the price reset starts biting the growth rate.
  • Gross margin mix: Q2 non-GAAP gross margin slipped 120 bps to 71.4% on product-mix shift. Stabilization here matters more than the revenue line.
  • Operating cash flow: H1 OCF of $4.50B trailed the prior-year $5.87B. The dividend's cash coverage — thinner than its earnings coverage — needs the second-half rebound the guide implies.
  • Guidance: the $49–50B revenue / $6.75–7.00 EPS guide was raised in July; a maintenance of the range is the base case, and any narrowing toward the top would be a positive signal.
Consensus setup. Third-party aggregations put Q3 non-GAAP EPS around $1.55 and full-year 2026 consensus near $6.95 — essentially the top of the company's own $6.75–7.00 band. Expectations are full but not stretched; the bar is a clean in-line print with the growth mix intact. A miss on the Growth Portfolio growth rate would be the fastest way to lose the Buy rating.

04 | The Cliff

The 2028 LOE wall — unchanged, still the ceiling

Nothing in the last seven days moved the cliff. The facts, as verified:

  • Eliquis — generic entry April 1, 2028. Litigation settlements with first-wave generic filers set the U.S. entry date; the composition-of-matter patent expired in November 2026 with pediatric exclusivity extending to roughly May 2027. Generic apixaban already exists on paper and is sold in Canada, the UK, and the EU.
  • The IRA headwind is already here. Eliquis was among the first ten drugs subject to Medicare price negotiation; the negotiated price took effect January 1, 2026. Q2's +22% growth came despite that structural reset — volume strength and commercial execution are real, but the comp only gets harder.
  • Opdivo — LOE around late 2028. The intravenous franchise ($2.49B/quarter, −3%) faces key exclusivity expiry in the same window; subcutaneous Qvantig ($261M and scaling) is the lifecycle bridge.
  • Revlimid is the template. Once BMY's top seller, now −52% and still falling two years into generic competition. Eliquis is roughly ten times Revlimid's current quarterly revenue.
The honest math (our estimate). At ~35% of revenue, Eliquis contributes on the order of $2.50–3.00 of annual non-GAAP EPS, assuming pharma-typical incremental margins on a product whose economics are split with Pfizer. A 60–70% revenue erosion over two to three years post-generic — the standard branded-to-generic decay — would erase roughly $1.50–2.00 of EPS by 2030 before offsets. The pipeline and the $2B cost program must replace that and grow. That bar is unchanged, and it is why this report is a Buy at 6/10 rather than anything stronger.
05 | Pipeline

Pipeline: two steps forward, one halt

The pipeline is deep; the issue remains timing, not quality. The two load-bearing readouts are still 2027 events — but the supporting cast had a good week.

The two pillars (both 2027)

  • Cobenfy (KarXT) — Alzheimer's psychosis (ADEPT program). Already approved for schizophrenia; the Alzheimer's-psychosis expansion is the multi-billion-dollar swing factor. Top-line readouts begin early 2027, spread across the year, with a possible ADEPT-1 interim analysis later in 2026. The prior ADEPT-2 site-integrity issue was resolved via FDA consultation with the trial continuing.
  • Milvexian — oral Factor XIa inhibitor (with J&J). The designated Eliquis successor. Secondary-stroke-prevention data is expected in 2026; the atrial-fibrillation readout is guided to Q1 2027 on slower event accumulation. Even on success, launch revenue scales in 2028–2029 — after generic Eliquis arrives. The baton pass has a gap, and the market knows it.

Delivered and de-risked in 2026

  • ZENBEXUS (iberdomide) — FDA accelerated approval August 13, 2026 (first approved CELMoD for relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma), now backed by the strong EXCALIBER-RRMM Phase 3 data (41.1% MRD-negative CR vs. 20.7%).
  • Mezigdomide — NDA accepted with a May 13, 2027 PDUFA date; together with ZENBEXUS it rebuilds the myeloma franchise Revlimid vacated.
  • CAMZYOS pediatric expansion — approved September 30, 2026 (SCOUT-HCM).

Still to come in 2026

  • Registrational data for Arlo-cel (GPRC5D CAR-T in myeloma), Phase 3 data for RYZ101 (radiopharmaceutical in neuroendocrine tumors), the Phase 3 IPF readout for admilparant, and Phase 3 Sotyktu in lupus — each a real but sub-blockbuster opportunity.
  • Longer-dated: the BioNTech-partnered bispecific pumitamig (Phase 3 in lung and breast cancers) and the SystImmune ADC iza-bren (already winning in China; global Phase 3 ongoing) are the oncology bets for the 2030s.
Net assessment. The pipeline can plausibly replace the LOE hole by 2030–2031 if Cobenfy's Alzheimer's expansion and milvexian both work — two independent binary events, both now 2027 stories. The week's wins (CAMZYOS, ZENBEXUS) de-risk the supporting growth narrative, not the cliff itself. That is precisely the shape of a 6/10-conviction Buy: paid to wait, positioned for the 2027 data, not betting the multiple on it today.

07 | Risks

Risks

  • The 2028 LOE wall. Generic Eliquis (April 2028) plus Opdivo exclusivity expiry in the same window could erase $1.50–2.00 of annual EPS by 2030 (our estimate). This is not a tail risk; it is the base case to underwrite against — and the reason conviction is 6, not 8.
  • Pipeline binary risk. Cobenfy Alzheimer's-psychosis and milvexian AF readouts are 2027 events. A failure in either — or another delay — removes the growth story's load-bearing pillars and likely re-rates the stock toward the low-$50s or below. The week's wins do not hedge this.
  • Q3 print risk (October 29). Full-year consensus sits at the top of the company's own guide band. A Growth Portfolio deceleration or a downtick in the guide would unwind the conviction upgrade quickly.
  • IRA / Medicare pricing. The negotiated Eliquis price is already in effect; future negotiation rounds could capture Camzyos, Opdivo, or other growth products. U.S. drug-pricing politics remain a headline risk.
  • Cash conversion. H1 2026 OCF of $4.50B trails the prior-year $5.87B. If the second half does not rebound, the dividend's cash coverage — and the deleveraging pace — both come under question.
  • Debt. $42B of long-term debt at elevated leverage limits strategic flexibility; a failed 2027 readout cannot be easily papered over with another large acquisition.
  • Concentration. ~35% of revenue from one product is the single biggest idiosyncratic risk in the portfolio's healthcare sleeve.
  • Valuation floor risk. The Morgan Stanley ~$40 Sell thesis (pipeline failure + LOE) is the downside marker — a 35% drawdown scenario that is low-probability but not zero.
