The desk call

Buy maintained, conviction held at 6/10, target trimmed to $80 from $88. Netflix delivered the quarter it promised — EPS of $0.87 against a $0.82 guide and consensus, operating margin 140bp ahead of guidance — and then guided the next one below the Street on every line, while cutting its full-year advertising expectation from roughly $3B to roughly $2.7B. The after-hours market priced the guide, not the quarter: the stock fell roughly 5–7% from the $68.69 close toward the mid-$60s, through a fresh 52-week low.

Position stance: hold the 500 shares. No add into the after-hours washout; trim staging now sits well above the market. The trimmed target still clears the Buy bar on the new rating scale (6/10 conviction with 10%+ implied upside), but the two engines that were supposed to re-accelerate growth — engagement hours and advertising — both decelerated in the same print. That is a thesis to re-underwrite at the Q4 report, not to average into tonight.

Section 01 · Results

The print — a profit beat the market ignored

On the lines Netflix guided, Q3 was a clean beat. On the line the Street cared about most — what comes next — it was a miss. Both things are true, and the tape chose the second.

Revenue of $12,874M (+11.8% year over year, +11% currency-neutral) landed essentially on top of both the company's $12,860M guidance and the $12.87B consensus. The profit lines ran ahead: operating income of $4,459M beat guidance by $191M, operating margin of 34.6% beat the 33.2% guide by 140bp, and diluted EPS of $0.87 beat the $0.82 consensus and guidance by five cents. Free cash flow was $2,884M, up from $2,660M a year ago, and the diluted share count has shrunk to 4,195M from 4,340M on the buyback.

Callout · Beat vs guide

The margin beat is timing, not run-rate

Management attributed the 140bp margin beat "primarily to the timing of expenses" alongside the slight revenue upside. Read it as borrowed, not earned: expenses deferred out of Q3 land in Q4, which is one reason the Q4 margin guide of 26.0% looks so much softer than Q3's 34.6% print. The honest Q3 run-rate sits between the two numbers — and annualising the $0.87 EPS without that adjustment overstates the earnings power the guide actually supports.

Revenue growth was the slowest in nearly three years — the deceleration, not the level, is the bear case in one line.

After-hours reaction · Oct 20, 2026

A guide-down selloff, deepening through the evening

Snapshots as reported through the evening of Oct 20; the after-hours session had not ended when the latest figure was published. Options had implied a ±9.5% move into the print.

The stock came into the print already de-rated: down 1.66% on the day to $68.69 on 64.0M shares, down 26.7% year to date and roughly 40% over the past year. That context matters for the desk call — much of the disappointment was in the price before a single number printed, which is precisely why the response is a target trim rather than a downgrade.

Section 02 · Outlook

Guidance — Q4 misses, the year gets narrower

The Q4 guide missed consensus on every comparable line. The full-year guide survived — barely — only because subscription revenue outperformed enough to cover the advertising shortfall.

For Q4 2026, Netflix guided revenue of $13,442M (+11.5%) against roughly $13.52B modelled, operating income of $3,500M for a 26.0% margin, net income of $2,787M against roughly $3.07B expected, and EPS of $0.67 against $0.73. The Q4 margin includes an approximately 120bp drag from the recently announced headcount reductions — severance and restructuring costs for the roughly 6% workforce cut — which management frames as funding continued aggressive investment in high-impact areas.

Two of the full-year changes deserve separating. The FCF cut to roughly $12.0B from $12.5B is mechanical — attributed primarily to a real-estate transaction expected to close in Q4 — and says little about the operating business; the cash content spend-to-amortisation ratio is still expected near 1.1x. The revenue pin at $51.1B, by contrast, is a composition story: subscriptions ran ahead of plan and advertising ran behind it, and the two roughly cancelled. A guide held together by the mature business covering for the growth business is not the same quality of "held" as the headline suggests.

No 2027 guidance was given — no revenue, margin or FCF outlook for next year. The only dated 2027 items are operational: the ad tier launches in 15 new countries during 2027 (first tranche in March: nine EMEA markets plus Colombia, Peru and New Zealand), and the engagement report moves to annual publication each Q1 from 2027. For a stock whose debate is entirely about 2027 growth re-acceleration, the absence of any 2027 frame is itself information.

Section 03 · The growth engine

Advertising — the $3B promise becomes $2.7B

This is the line that sold the stock. Advertising was the second growth engine — guided in Q2 to roughly double in 2026 to about $3B — and it will land nearer $2.7B.

Callout · The advertising slip

A rollout problem, management says — not a demand problem

The cut was attributed to "some ad product capabilities" rolling out "slightly slower than expected" — an execution and product-timing explanation, not an advertiser-demand one. The distinction matters for the target: a demand shortfall would argue for a multiple cut; a rollout slip argues for a one-year deferral of the ad ramp. We have taken the deferral, not the de-rating — but the burden of proof now sits with the March 2027 expansion tranche, and a second slip would change this read.

The build underneath the headline number is real, if slower than promised. In Q3 the Netflix Ads Suite extended programmatic access through globally available connected-TV marketplace deals, added partnerships with MercadoLibre in Latin America and AdForm in EMEA, and expanded in-DSP measurement across reach, frequency and attribution. The 15-country ad-tier expansion in 2027 materially widens the inventory base. What was not disclosed is equally notable: no Q3-only advertising revenue figure, and no updated ad-tier subscriber or monthly-active scale number anywhere in the release. The desk is being asked to underwrite an advertising trajectory whose two most useful calibration points — quarterly ad revenue and tier scale — remain undisclosed.

Subscription revenue outperforming is the offset, and it is a genuine one: pricing power plus membership growth carried the year. But mix matters to the multiple. Subscription growth at 11–12% is a mature, utility-like stream; the premium in this stock was always payment in advance for advertising becoming a second, faster stream. At ~$2.7B against a ~$51.1B revenue base, advertising is roughly 5% of the business — strategically central, arithmetically still small.

Section 04 · Engagement & content

Engagement — the debate management itself opened

Unusually, the shareholder letter raises the bears' favourite topic before the bears can: "We know there's some investor concern about our view hours growth."

The concern is specific. View hours grew just +2% year over year in the first half of 2026 (more than 97B hours), barely ahead of 2025's +1.5%, against World Cup and Winter Olympics competition. No global Q3 view-hours total was disclosed, and the What We Watched report now moves to annual publication from 2027 — which removes the datapoint quarterly, whatever the stated rationale of keeping focus on revenue and operating profit. Management's defence is coherence rather than growth: response to the 2026 price increases is consistent with prior adjustments, churn improved and beat forecast, and engagement quality and variety "remain healthy."

India is the proof point the letter leads with — view hours up 24% year over year, hours per member up low-to-mid single digits, the largest quarter ever for paid net additions in the market, and revenue up roughly 18% currency-neutral, powered by local originals including a first daily reality series that became the most-viewed title in India ever. The international engine plainly still works: non-English programming is now more than a third of all viewing, up from under 10% a decade ago, and Q3's biggest titles were led by The Last House at 91M views in its first 91 days.

The problem market is the home one. On the earnings call, Sarandos conceded: "We do need better execution in the U.S." Third-party estimates pointing to monthly US subscriber losses through 2026 were disputed by the company as unrepresentative — the estimates exclude wholesale bundles — but the direction of management's own language is not in dispute: a week before the print, Sarandos said publicly that "overall, we're not growing as fast as I want us to." The Q4 slate is the first test of the response: the biggest quarterly live slate to date (three NFL games including a Thanksgiving Eve game and a Christmas Day doubleheader, plus Fury vs. Joshua), the Emily in Paris series finale, The Diplomat S4, and the Fincher-directed, Tarantino-written Cliff Booth film.

The reset behind the slate

New content leadership, ~1,000 jobs cut — days before the print

Roughly 6% of staff — about 1,000 jobs — were cut in the week before earnings, the deepest reduction since 2022, with the cost landing in Q4 guidance. The chief content officer and the film chief both departed; a former Disney/Fox executive, Peter Rice, takes over content. Bulls will call it decisive; bears will note that companies rarely restructure their creative leadership mid-flight when the slate is working. Either way, 2027's content output is now a new team's output, and that cuts both ways for visibility.

Section 05 · Capital & balance sheet

Capital — a record buyback against a softer guide

Whatever the debate about growth, the capital machine is not debatable: Q3 was the largest repurchase quarter in the company's history.

Netflix repurchased 63.9M shares for $4.8B in Q3, topping Q2's $4.7B, with $22.3B of authorisation remaining after April's additional $25B approval. Cash and equivalents stood at $8,194M at September 30; gross debt was $15.3B (net debt roughly $7.1B), up about $1B in the quarter because a $1B bond was issued to pre-fund a $1B Q4 maturity — refinancing hygiene, not new leverage appetite. Capital allocation language was unchanged: reinvest organically and through selective M&A, keep the balance sheet healthy, return the excess. Acquisitions were zero in Q3.

The buyback pace matters to the per-share math: at the after-hours price near $64, the remaining authorisation retires shares roughly 7% cheaper than the $68.69 close and far below where the year's repurchases were executed. A shrinking share count against flat-to-modest earnings growth is how EPS keeps compounding even in a deceleration year — the quiet support under the $80 target.

Reading the 2026 accounts · Warner footnote

Strip the $2.8B breakup fee before annualising anything

The Warner Bros. Discovery contest ended in February 2026: Netflix declined to top the rival ~$111B bid, and the $2.8B breakup fee paid to Netflix sits in Q1 2026 other income ($2,852M that quarter), flattering nine-month net income of $12,348M and any trailing EPS built from it. There is no live Warner overhang — the deal is dead, the fee is banked, and the Q3 letter does not mention it. Any 2026 EPS annualisation that does not strip the fee out of Q1 materially overstates underlying earnings power. The residual issue is competitive, not financial: the Warner library now sits with a rival.

Section 07 · Valuation & the desk call

Why the target goes to $80 — and why the rating survives

The trim is arithmetic honesty: the ad ramp moves a year right, Q4 comes in below the modelled path, and the target follows the guide down. The Buy survives because the stock had already paid for most of this disappointment before the print.

Start from what the $88 target assumed: advertising roughly doubling toward $3B in 2026 on the way to a materially larger 2027, and low-teens revenue growth sustained. Tonight's print defers the first and dents the second — Q4 guided to +11.5% against +11.8% in Q3 and a full-year revenue pin 0.2% below the prior midpoint. Rolling the ad trajectory out a year and marking Q4 to guide takes fair value to $80, still +16.5% above the $68.69 reference close — and roughly +25% above the after-hours level near $64, if that is where the stock opens.

What holds the call up: a 31.5% full-year operating margin guide that did not move; operating income still guided to grow more than 20% in 2026; roughly $12B of free cash flow even after the real-estate-driven cut; a record buyback retiring stock into the de-rating; and a core subscription business that absorbed 2026 price increases with churn better than forecast. What caps conviction at 6 rather than 7: both growth engines decelerated simultaneously, US execution is conceded to be lagging, view-hours disclosure is being reduced just as it becomes the central debate, and there is no 2027 guidance to underwrite against. On the desk's conviction-anchored scale, 6/10 with 10%+ upside is a Buy — a measured one.

  • Position: hold the 500 shares through the print aftermath. The pre-print stance — hold through Q3 — is unchanged in direction; the position was sized for exactly this volatility.
  • No add into the washout. An after-hours guide-down in a stock with an open engagement debate is not a staged-entry trigger; the desk wants the October 21 analyst resets and at least one regular session of price discovery first.
  • Trim staging sits well above the market. With the target at $80, any trim discipline belongs in the high-$70s to $80 zone on a recovery, not anywhere near the mid-$60s.
  • Next checkpoint: Q4 results in January — ad revenue trajectory language, any 2027 frame, and the first slate under the new content leadership.

Section 08 · Risks

What would change the call

  • A second advertising slip. The $2.7B read rests on the rollout-timing explanation. If the March 2027 expansion tranche slips, or 2027 ad commentary guides flat, the deferral thesis fails and the target — and likely the rating — comes down.
  • View hours go negative. The first annual engagement report (Q1 2027) is now a binary event. Hours growth at or below zero, against a growing member base, would confirm the saturation bear case.
  • US deterioration becomes measurable. Management has conceded execution, not yet outcomes. Evidence that US churn or net additions are worsening — rather than merely flat — breaks the "healthy engagement" defence.
  • Reset execution risk. A new content chief and ~1,000 departures can disrupt a slate pipeline for quarters. Weak Q4 content performance — the biggest live slate to date is the first test — would compound the guide-down.
  • Competitive structure. The Warner library now sits with a stronger combined rival; if that shows up in engagement share rather than just headlines, the international growth offset gets harder.
  • To the upside: a clean Q4 ad re-acceleration signal, 2027 guidance at the January print above the deferred path, or buyback execution at de-rated prices compounding EPS faster than modelled — any of these argues the trim was too cautious.
