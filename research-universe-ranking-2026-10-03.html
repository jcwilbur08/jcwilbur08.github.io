1. Executive summary

Four names earn the highest-conviction designation out of 52. They share one trait: the market is underpaying for visibility — of demand (NVDA, AVGO), of normalization (UNH), or of sentiment versus fundamentals (GEHC).

The coverage universe has tripled in two weeks, from the first initiations on September 22 to 63 published reports across 52 securities today. The ranking that follows sorts every one of them by conviction-weighted expected return: implied twelve-month upside multiplied by research conviction and business quality, applied with judgment rather than a formula. That judgment matters most at the top. Uber carries the highest implied upside in coverage at +54%, yet sits in Tier 2 — the autonomous-vehicle debate caps conviction, and upside alone does not buy a Tier 1 slot.

Below the top tier, the watchlist is where the marginal opportunity now lives. Ten names are under coverage but not in the portfolio; Adobe and Lowe’s offer the best risk-reward among them, each at conviction 7/10, each with a defined proving point — the fourth-quarter ARR inflection for Adobe, the housing-cycle turn for Lowe’s — and each with a recommended entry gate that has not yet been installed. The structural read is separate from any single name: at roughly 2.5% of NAV versus an 11–13% benchmark weight, healthcare is the book’s largest underweight, and it coincides with the cheapest sector in the market at 0.8x the S&P multiple. VHT is the instrument; the decision is the CIO’s.

2. Methodology

Universe. 52 securities with published fundamental or fund-overview reports — 63 reports in total, with update notes superseding earlier editions of the same name. Every figure in this ranking is drawn from those reports, not re-researched.

Implied upside. Each report’s twelve-month target divided by the October 2, 2026 reference price. For held names the reference is the Morningstar export-54 workbook price; for watchlist names it is the reference close stated in the initiating report. Implied upside is therefore a clean comparison of what the research underwrote against the same tape.

Tiers are judgment, not a formula. The ordering weights upside against conviction and business quality. A large implied upside with capped conviction stays in Tier 2 — Uber at +54% is the deliberate example. Conversely, a moderate upside with high conviction and a live entry gate can outrank a larger number. The tiers answer one question: where would new capital go first.

Staleness. Targets were set between September 22 and October 3, and several prices have moved since their reports printed. Treat every upside figure as directional, not live — verify the tape before acting on any of them.

Ratings. BUY / ADD / HOLD / REDUCE for equities; BUY / KEEP / TRIM for funds and ETFs. All recommendations are advisory; the CIO executes.

1. NVDA — NVIDIA Corp ADD

Target $340+45.3% implied upsideConviction 8/10Held

Thesis. NVIDIA has the best demand visibility of any company in coverage. Hyperscaler capital spending is running toward roughly $800 billion this year, and NVIDIA is the primary toll collector — the base case targets $340 with a probability-weighted $328, and the stock can be built in stages inside the $215–235 add band rather than chased. The risk that keeps this at 8/10 rather than higher is the demand-quality debate: $94 billion of ecosystem stakes and a $105 billion guarantee cap mean the growth is real but increasingly encumbered.

Key risk. Demand-quality debate ($94B ecosystem stakes, $105B guarantee cap); AI-capex cyclicality.

Entry. Dashboard gate $220; band top $235.

2. AVGO — Broadcom Inc BUY

Target $535+50.6% implied upsideHeld

Thesis. Broadcom is the quieter leverage on the same AI capex wave. The fourth-quarter guide came in about 0.6% light, a reminder that lumpy custom-silicon ramps cut both ways, and the China SASAC switch review is a live regulatory risk.

Key risk. China SASAC switch review; lumpy custom-silicon revenue; FQ4 guide was 0.6% light.

Entry. Recommended gate $340 toward ~3% — not installed; needs CIO approval.

3. UNH — UnitedHealth Group Inc BUY

Target $475+27.7% implied upsideHeld

Thesis. UnitedHealth is the best contrarian setup in coverage. The stock was crushed on medical-cost and regulatory fears, then the second quarter delivered a huge beat with the medical cost ratio at 86.7% and guidance raised — the DOJ probes are effectively the entire discount. If the cost ratio keeps improving, the re-rating has room; if it stalls, the regulatory overhang will keep the multiple pinned. Accumulation is structured through the live toward-2% gate.

Key risk. DOJ regulatory overhang persists; medical cost ratio must keep improving.

Entry. Accumulate-toward-2% gate is live.

4. GEHC — GE HealthCare Technologies Inc Common Stock ADD

Target $92+43.1% implied upsideConviction 7/10Held

Thesis. GE HealthCare sold off on a reiteration of known headwinds, not new fundamentals: the second quarter beat, guidance was reaffirmed, orders grew 11.1%, and backlog hit a record $23.9 billion. The core imaging and diagnostics franchises are compounding while the market prices the whole company on the weakest division. Conviction is capped at 7/10 by Patient Care Solutions losses, China pressure, an interim CFO, and litigation overhang.

Key risk. PCS losses (-$26M EBIT); China down; interim CFO; litigation overhang.

Entry. $62 gate live toward ~0.5%.
