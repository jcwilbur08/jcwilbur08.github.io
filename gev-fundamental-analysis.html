1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

GE Vernova — the April 2024 spin-off of GE's energy businesses — sits at the center of the two most powerful demand cycles in industrial history: the AI-driven data-center power buildout and global grid modernization. Q2 2026 delivered record orders of $24.2B (+88% organically) R, a $176B backlog up $13B sequentially R, and a dramatic free-cash-flow guidance raise to $11.5–12.5B from $6.5–7.5B R. Gas Power has 116 GW of firm backlog plus slot reservations (targeting ≥125 GW by year-end) R; Electrification data-center orders exceeded $5B year-to-date — more than double all of 2025 R.

The problem is the price. At $950.75 the stock trades at ~40× FY2027E consensus EPS and ~38–44× FY2026E EV/adjusted EBITDA C/E — premium multiples that price in flawless execution on the capacity ramp, continued order growth, and containment of Wind losses (~$400M guided for FY2026) R. Q2 EPS of $2.47 missed consensus by $0.70 even as revenue beat E, a reminder that order strength converts to earnings slowly and unevenly. And the GAAP earnings picture is a mirage: TTM net income of ~$9.5B bundles a $2.9B tax valuation-allowance benefit (FY2025) and $4.5B of pretax M&A gains (H1 2026) R — anyone screening on the ~27× headline P/E is understating the true multiple materially.

Thesis O: GEV is the highest-quality demand story in large-cap industrials — a net-cash, order-rich, dividend-growing compounder riding a genuine super-cycle. But the stock, up ~79% off its 52-week low and trading at ~40× forward earnings with the Wind segment still bleeding, offers balanced rather than compelling risk/reward at current levels. The right posture is to hold the 1.85% position and add on weakness (a Wind-driven miss, a tariff scare, or a broad de-rating toward $850) rather than chase the cycle here. Key risks: Wind project-cost overruns, execution on the 20→30 GW turbine output ramp, and concentration on the gas-power/data-center cycle.

Bottom line: HOLD with a $1,100 12-month target (~16% upside), anchored on ~46× FY2027E consensus EPS of $24.08 C/E — between Morningstar's $1,050 fair value and the $1,145.83 Street consensus average. The demand thesis is exceptional; the multiple already knows it.
Portfolio note: Cam holds 33 shares (~$31,375, 1.85% of the $1.70M portfolio). Morningstar rates GEV 3 stars with a $1,050 fair value (Sept 2026 export) — squarely consistent with our HOLD: fairly valued, not a sell, not an add at $950.
Earnings-quality warning (read first): FY2025 GAAP net income includes a $2.9B U.S. tax valuation-allowance benefit R; H1 2026 GAAP net income includes $4.5B of pretax M&A net gains (primarily the Prolec GE remeasurement) R. Headline TTM GAAP EPS (~$35), P/E (~27×), and ROE (~42%) are mirages. This report values the company on adjusted EBITDA, revenue, FCF, and forward consensus — with caveats noted throughout.

2. Business Overview

  • Company: energy technology company providing equipment, software, and services for electricity generation, transmission, and distribution across natural gas, nuclear, hydroelectric, wind, solar, and battery storage R.
  • Independence: spin-off from General Electric completed April 2, 2024; began NYSE trading under "GEV" that day R. CEO Scott Strazik; headquarters Cambridge, MA R.
  • Scale: more than 80,000 employees across 100+ countries at spin-off R; over 7,000 gas turbines (world's largest installed base), approximately 55,000 wind turbines — technology the company says helps generate ~30% of the world's electricity R.
  • Three segments: Power (gas/steam turbines, generators, nuclear, hydro, maintenance services); Wind (onshore + offshore turbines, development, servicing); Electrification (grid technologies, power conversion, controls, solar and storage) R.
  • Segment realignment: effective January 1, 2026, some business units were realigned among segments; 2025 figures were recast — use recast data for 2025 comparisons R.
  • Prolec GE: acquisition of the remaining 50% closed February 2, 2026 R; ~$5B added to Electrification equipment backlog R; partly financed by a $2.6B senior-notes issuance R.
  • Dividend & buybacks: initiated at $0.25/quarter (Dec 2024), doubled to $0.50/quarter (Dec 2025); $2.00 annualized, 0.21% yield R/C. Buyback authorization raised to $10B (Dec 2025); $3.9B of capital returned YTD through June 30, 2026 R.
  • Investment commitments: $6B capex from 2025–2028 (incl. $1B for Prolec GE 2026–2028) and $5B R&D from 2025–2028 R.

7. The Gas-Power Super-Cycle & Data-Center Demand

The core investment thesis, in company-stated facts R:

  • Gas Power 116 GW combined backlog/slots after Q2 (53 GW firm + 63 GW slots), up from 100 GW; targeting ≥125 GW by year-end 2026.
  • Planned gas-turbine output: 20 GW/yr by Q3 2026 → 24 GW in 2028, with actions underway for 30 GW in 2030.
  • Equipment margin in backlog rose +$8B with 6 pts of accretion at FY2025 year-end — pricing power is real.
  • Data-center power is the demand engine: Electrification data-center orders >$5B YTD (2× all of 2025); the Blue Energy 2.5-GW gas-plus-nuclear project in Victoria, Texas moved into engineering/licensing in August 2026 E.
  • GEV is a "picks and shovels" play on AI compute growth without semiconductor cyclicality O — turbines and grid equipment have multi-year backlogs and services annuities, not quarterly order books.

8. Nuclear / SMR Optionality

  • BWRX-300 SMR (GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear Energy): first unit under construction at Ontario Power Generation's Darlington site — described as on schedule to be the first SMR operating in the OECD E.
  • December 2025: completed Step 2 of the UK's Generic Design Assessment — no fundamental safety/security/safeguards/environmental shortfalls E.
  • July 2, 2026: developer SGE announced plans for fourteen BWRX-300 units across three UK sites (4.2 GW), targeting first commercial operation in 2034 E.
  • Other 2026: Studsvik selected GVH and Samsung C&T for a proposed 4-unit (~1.2 GW) Swedish project; GE Vernova–Hitachi MoU (March 2026) to explore BWRX-300 deployment in Southeast Asia E.
  • Nuclear Power led Power services orders growth in Q2 R.
  • Why this matters O: SMR revenue is a 2030s story — do not model it in the 12-month target. But the pipeline de-risks the nuclear narrative, supports the services franchise today, and is free optionality on the data-center power thesis.

9. Competitive Position

  • Gas turbines: world's largest installed base (7,000+ units) vs. Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power. The services annuity on this base is the moat — competitors can sell turbines, but GEV services the fleet R/O.
  • Grid/electrification: competing with Siemens Energy, Schneider Electric, ABB. Electrification's +700 bps organic margin expansion (to 18.4%) signals pricing power in a capacity-constrained grid market R/O.
  • Wind: deliberately shrinking — selectivity over share vs. Vestas and Siemens Gamesa. The strategy is margin containment, not leadership R/O.
  • SMR: BWRX-300 is among the furthest-advanced SMR designs globally (Darlington under construction, UK GDA Step 2 passed) — ahead of most competitors still in design phases E/O.

10. Risks

  • Valuation: ~40× FY2027E EPS and ~38–44× FY2026E EV/adj. EBITDA price in flawless execution. The street-low $470 target (GLJ Research) and Morgan Stanley's Underweight downgrade show the bear case is real C/E/O.
  • Wind losses: −$275M EBITDA in Q2 (−13.6% margin); ~$400M loss guided for FY2026; ~$450M tariff impact through 2028. Offshore project-cost overruns are the principal earnings-drag risk R.
  • Earnings quality: $2.9B tax benefit + $4.5B pretax M&A gains inflate GAAP figures; anyone screening on headline P/E or ROE materially overstates normalized earning power R/O.
  • Q2 EPS miss: $2.47 vs. $3.17 consensus — order strength does not immediately translate to earnings; mix (Wind, Prolec integration) can surprise E/O.
  • Concentration: the thesis hinges on the gas-power/data-center cycle persisting; a slowdown in hyperscaler capex or power-demand forecasts would hit Power orders and the 116 GW slot pipeline O.
  • Execution on capacity ramp: scaling gas-turbine output 20→30 GW/yr requires supply-chain, labor, and facility execution; $6B capex through 2028 is committed R/O.
  • Technical: ~20% below the $1,195.94 52-week high, below the 50-day moving average (~$1,027), rising short interest (3.3% of float, +10.4% m/m), beta 1.15 — positioning is long and the bar for Q3 is high P/O.

  • Q3 2026 earnings (estimated Oct 27–28, 2026 — not company-confirmed): order trajectory toward the ≥125 GW year-end target and any Wind-loss update are the key prints; the bar is high after the Q2 EPS miss E/O.

  • Data-center power deals: the 2.5-GW Blue Energy gas-plus-nuclear project (engineering phase) and further hyperscaler turbine orders E/O.
  • SMR milestones: Darlington construction progress, UK SMR program decisions, SGE's 4.2-GW UK fleet development E.

B. Glossary

  • RPO (remaining performance obligations): contracted backlog not yet recognized as revenue — $176B at Q2 2026.
  • GW (gigawatts): power-generation capacity; the unit of gas-turbine backlog (116 GW combined firm + slots).
  • Slot reservations: customer reservations for future turbine manufacturing slots — convert to firm orders over time.
  • HA-class turbines: GE Vernova's heavy-duty gas turbine platform (e.g., 7HA) for large power plants.
  • BWRX-300: GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear Energy's small modular reactor design (300 MW).
  • GDA (Generic Design Assessment): the UK's regulatory process for new nuclear reactor designs.
  • Adjusted EBITDA: earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, excluding restructuring, M&A gains, and other non-operating items — GEV's primary profitability metric.
