1. Executive Summary

Investment thesis

Siemens is evolving from a diversified European industrial conglomerate into a more focused, higher-quality industrial technology platform positioned at the intersection of electrification, industrial automation, engineering software and physical AI. The operating setup is the strongest it has been in years: Smart Infrastructure is capturing exceptional data-center and grid demand, Digital Industries is recovering while software expands the recurring digital mix, and the planned Siemens Healthineers separation should simplify the valuation framework.

The issue is price. At roughly EUR 284 per ordinary share, Siemens trades at about 25.0x the midpoint of raised FY2026 EPS pre-PPA guidance. That is a premium multiple for a business still exposed to industrial cycles, China, FX and capital spending. We see a reasonable 12-18 month fair value near EUR 307, or approximately $177 per SIEGY ADR at the report FX assumption. The resulting expected return is positive but not compelling enough for an outright Add rating today.

3. Company Overview and Business Model

Siemens AG is a global industrial technology company whose core industrial businesses are Digital Industries, Smart Infrastructure and Mobility. Siemens Healthineers remains a separately listed subsidiary in which Siemens owns roughly 67%, but management has initiated a process to deconsolidate the stake. Siemens Financial Services supports customer financing and selected investment activity.

Business model strengths

  • Installed base creates replacement, service and software opportunities.
  • Engineering workflows have high switching costs because Siemens tools are embedded in design and production processes.
  • Cross-selling connects software, automation, electrification and service.
  • Long-cycle infrastructure and rail backlogs provide earnings visibility that offsets shorter-cycle factory automation.
  • Global manufacturing footprint and large U.S. presence improve proximity to customer investment cycles.

Business model weaknesses

  • Industrial demand remains cyclical and inventory corrections can be abrupt.
  • Large project businesses create execution and timing risk.
  • Conglomerate accounting and Healthineers consolidation complicate comparability.
  • Acquisitions increase integration and purchase-price-accounting complexity.
  • Currency translation can materially affect reported revenue and orders.
Analytical takeaway: Siemens should increasingly be valued as a hybrid industrial-technology company rather than a conventional machinery conglomerate. That supports a premium multiple, but the premium is only durable if software growth, Smart Infrastructure margins and free-cash-flow conversion remain strong through a full cycle.

5. Segment Analysis

Digital Industries - recovery plus software optionality

Digital Industries is strategically the most important segment for the long-term re-rating case. It combines shorter-cycle automation hardware with software that can generate higher margins, recurring revenue and customer lock-in. In Q2 FY2026, DI generated EUR 4.6 billion of revenue, with software revenue of roughly EUR 1.6 billion, organic ARR growth of 11% to EUR 5.5 billion, profit of EUR 857 million and an 18.5% margin. In Q3, comparable revenue rose 10% to EUR 4.93 billion while orders rose 9% to EUR 4.85 billion; order growth was below the company's compiled market expectation.

Investment interpretation: DI is no longer a pure cyclical automation story. The mix shift toward PLM, EDA, simulation, digital twins, industrial copilots and life-sciences software can support a structurally higher earnings multiple. However, hardware demand in China and global factory capex still has enough weight to create quarterly volatility.

Smart Infrastructure - the current earnings engine

Smart Infrastructure is benefiting from simultaneous secular investment waves: data-center electrification, grid modernization, energy efficiency, semiconductor expansion and building automation. Q2 orders reached a record EUR 7.5 billion, up 35% on a comparable basis, while revenue grew 10% to EUR 5.9 billion and profit reached roughly EUR 1.1 billion at an 18.6% margin. After Q3, Siemens raised SI's FY2026 comparable revenue-growth outlook to 10%-11% and its margin range to 18.5%-19.5%.

Reuters reported that Siemens works with nine of the ten largest data-center providers and that data-center orders increased at a triple-digit rate during the first nine months of FY2026. This is a critical point: Siemens is not simply selling "AI software" into a crowded narrative; it is supplying the physical electrical and control infrastructure required to build the AI capacity itself.

Mobility - backlog and infrastructure exposure, but lower margins

Mobility offers long-duration visibility but remains less profitable than DI and SI. Q2 orders were EUR 5.3 billion, up 41%, while revenue was about EUR 3.0 billion and profit EUR 208 million for a 6.9% margin. U.S. tariffs and project call-off delays pressured profitability. The segment's rail vehicles, signaling, electrification and services provide valuable diversification, but sustained margin improvement is needed before it deserves the same quality multiple as Siemens' software and electrification assets.

6. Strategy - ONE Tech Company, Industrial AI and Software

Siemens' ONE Tech Company program is designed to make the group more focused and scalable. Management raised its mid-term comparable revenue-growth ambition to 6%-9% excluding Healthineers, targets high-single-digit EPS pre-PPA growth and aims to double digital-business revenue by 2030. Siemens also committed more than EUR 1 billion to scale AI over three years.

Industrial AI - why Siemens has a credible right to win

  • Industrial AI requires domain-specific data, physics models, automation controls and safety/reliability - not just general-purpose models.
  • Siemens already sits inside the design, engineering and operating workflows of industrial customers.
  • Its digital twins connect engineering intent to real-world production and infrastructure data.
  • The partnership with NVIDIA combines Siemens' software/domain stack with accelerated computing, Omniverse, AI libraries and infrastructure.
  • AI can increase the value of existing Siemens software while also stimulating demand for the electrical infrastructure that powers AI factories and data centers.

Software expansion via M&A

Altair: acquired in March 2025 for EUR 9.5 billion, adding simulation and analysis software that broadens Siemens' engineering stack.

Dotmatics: acquired in July 2025 for $5.1 billion, extending the digital thread into life-sciences R&D. Siemens said Dotmatics was expected to generate more than $300 million of 2025 revenue at an adjusted EBITDA margin above 40% and identified meaningful revenue-synergy potential.

The strategic rationale is strong, but investors should demand evidence that organic growth and cross-selling justify the acquisition prices.

NVIDIA partnership - strategic signal, not yet a valuation substitute

In January 2026 Siemens and NVIDIA expanded their partnership to build what they call an "Industrial AI Operating System," covering design, engineering, manufacturing, operations and supply chains. The companies are targeting AI-native electronic design, simulation, adaptive manufacturing and next-generation AI factories. Siemens' Erlangen electronics factory is intended to serve as an initial fully AI-driven adaptive-manufacturing blueprint.

The opportunity is material because Siemens can monetize AI in multiple layers: software subscriptions, simulation/EDA workloads, automation hardware, edge computing, electrification and data-center infrastructure. The risk is that investors capitalize the narrative before the revenue base is large enough to offset cyclical weakness elsewhere. We therefore do not assign a separate speculative "AI premium" in our base valuation; the premium must show up through EPS growth and sustained margins.

Variant perception: We think the market correctly recognizes Siemens as an AI/data-center beneficiary, but may still underestimate how the software, automation and electrification pieces reinforce one another. Our offsetting variant is valuation: the strategic quality deserves a premium, yet the current ~25x FY2026 EPS multiple already prices in a meaningful portion of that improvement.

9. Siemens Healthineers Separation - What It Means for SIEGY

Siemens announced in November 2025 that it intends to deconsolidate its roughly 67% stake in Siemens Healthineers, with a preferred structure that transfers 30% of Healthineers shares directly to Siemens AG shareholders. In April 2026 Siemens said it plans to put the direct spin-off to a shareholder vote at the February 2027 AGM. Reuters reported after Q3 that German tax authorities had agreed tax treatment intended to make the planned distribution tax-free to Siemens shareholders under the transaction structure.

Illustrative value bridge

Using a recent Healthineers market capitalization of roughly EUR 43.7 billion:

  • 67% Siemens stake: approximately EUR 29.3 billion.
  • 30% of Healthineers distributed: approximately EUR 13.1 billion at the same market value.
  • Illustrative value per Siemens ordinary share distributed: approximately EUR 16.8, before transaction terms and market moves.
  • Remaining 37% stake at the same value: approximately EUR 16.2 billion.

Critical interpretation

The spin is not an incremental EUR 16.8 of free value. When the distributed Healthineers shares detach, the value of Siemens AG should adjust lower by the value transferred. The potential creation of value comes from better strategic focus, simplified financial reporting, a reduced conglomerate discount, and the possibility that each asset receives a more appropriate standalone valuation.

For U.S. ADR holders, broker handling, fractional shares, ADR mechanics, tax treatment and fees may differ from the ordinary-share process. Investors should confirm the final ADR treatment when transaction documents are published.

The transaction is strategically sensible. Core Siemens' industrial businesses are increasingly coherent around automation, electrification, industrial software and mobility. Healthineers is a high-quality asset, but its healthcare economics are different and its separate listing already provides a transparent market value. Deconsolidation should make Siemens easier to analyze and compare with Schneider Electric, ABB and industrial technology peers.

U.S. positioning

Siemens has identified the U.S. as its largest market and in 2025 announced more than $10 billion of recent/planned U.S. investment, including new and expanded factories in Texas and California and a more-than-doubling of electrical-equipment production capacity serving critical infrastructure such as AI data centers. This local capacity is strategically important because it can improve responsiveness, help meet domestic-content requirements and mitigate some trade friction.

China remains the most important cyclical question

Weak Chinese industrial demand and customer inventory normalization have weighed on Digital Industries over the prior two years. The recovery now appears to be broadening, but investors should resist extrapolating SI's data-center strength into all of Siemens. A durable re-acceleration in DI orders - particularly outside one-time software or large project effects - is the most important confirmation signal for a stronger earnings-growth regime.

Primary method: forward earnings multiple

A sum-of-the-parts approach is conceptually appropriate for Siemens, but the planned Healthineers deconsolidation and acquisition accounting make a highly precise SOTP easy to overfit. We therefore use a transparent forward EPS multiple as the primary method and an asset/value bridge as a secondary check.

At the current reference price, the stock trades around 25.0x the midpoint of FY2026 EPS pre-PPA guidance. For a company targeting 6%-9% mid-term revenue growth excluding Healthineers and high-single-digit EPS growth, a mid-20s multiple is reasonable only if the business mix continues to improve. It offers limited protection if growth merely returns to historical industrial-cycle norms.

Secondary check: market-implied core value

Using approximately 782 million Siemens shares and an EUR 283.95 reference price implies equity value around EUR 222 billion. Subtracting the illustrative market value of Siemens' 67% Healthineers stake (~EUR 29 billion) leaves roughly EUR 193 billion of equity value attributed to core Siemens before considering the detailed balance-sheet and financing-company bridge. This is not a formal enterprise-value SOTP, but it shows why the core businesses must continue delivering strong margins and cash flow to justify the present group valuation.

Fair-value conclusion

Our probability-weighted scenario value is approximately EUR 307 per ordinary share, equivalent to about $177 per SIEGY ADR at the report FX assumption. That represents mid-single-digit price upside from the ordinary reference price plus a gross dividend yield near 1.9%. The expected return is acceptable for an existing holder, but below the threshold we would normally require to initiate an aggressive new position in a cyclical industrial technology stock.

Thesis-break conditions

We would consider the investment thesis materially impaired if two or more of the following occurred without a credible temporary explanation:

  • Smart Infrastructure organic growth falls to low single digits while margin drops below the mid-to-high teens.
  • Digital Industries orders fail to establish sustained growth despite easier comparisons and inventory normalization.
  • Software ARR growth falls below high single digits and acquisition-related margin dilution persists.
  • Group free-cash-flow conversion falls materially below earnings for a sustained period.
  • Management pursues another large acquisition before demonstrating sufficient returns from Altair and Dotmatics.
  • Healthineers deconsolidation is abandoned and the strategic rationale for retaining control becomes unclear.

15. Portfolio Fit and Implementation

Siemens is attractive as a long-duration industrial-technology diversifier for investors whose equity exposure is already concentrated in U.S. technology and consumer franchises. It adds direct exposure to European industrial technology, global electrification, factory automation, rail infrastructure, industrial software and physical AI. It also has substantial U.S. operating exposure, so it is not simply a bet on European domestic growth.

Why it can fit well

  • Diversifies AI exposure away from semiconductors and hyperscalers toward power, automation and industrial productivity.
  • Combines cyclical upside with long-cycle backlog and infrastructure demand.
  • Offers a modest dividend and active buyback program.
  • Potential Healthineers separation can create two independently valued securities.
  • Business quality and installed-base economics support long holding periods.

Implementation cautions for SIEGY

  • SIEGY is OTC and less liquid than the primary Xetra listing.
  • Two ADRs represent one ordinary share; dollar returns include EUR/USD translation.
  • Foreign dividend withholding and ADR fees can reduce net income received.
  • Final Healthineers distribution mechanics for ADR holders should be verified when transaction documents are available.
  • Because valuation is full, staged purchases are preferable to an immediate full-size position.

16. Final Investment View

Recommendation: WATCH | Conviction: 7/10

Siemens is one of the better positioned global industrial technology companies for the next decade. It has genuine exposure to the physical side of AI, not merely a marketing overlay: electrification and power distribution for data centers, automation and controls for factories, EDA and simulation for advanced engineering, and digital twins that connect software with physical operations. Smart Infrastructure is producing exceptional growth, Digital Industries is improving, and the group has a credible path to a cleaner corporate structure through the Healthineers deconsolidation.

The stock, however, is no longer priced like a misunderstood cyclical. Around 25x FY2026 EPS guidance and close to its recent high, Siemens requires continued execution to deliver attractive returns. Our 12-18 month fair value of approximately EUR 307 / $177 SIEGY implies positive but only moderate upside from the current reference value. For an existing holder, the thesis supports holding. For new capital, we prefer to wait for a pullback toward roughly $150-$156 or for earnings estimates to rise enough that today's price represents a lower forward multiple.

The 5 checkpoints that matter most

  1. Smart Infrastructure: can data-center and electrification demand stay strong without sacrificing margins?
  2. Digital Industries: do orders catch up with revenue and confirm a durable automation recovery?
  3. Software: does ARR and organic growth prove the Altair/Dotmatics strategy is creating value?
