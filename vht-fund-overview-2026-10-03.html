1. Executive Summary & Verdict

VHT is the best single-ticket core healthcare exposure for a retirement portfolio: 414 holdings across pharma, biotech, medtech, and managed care at 0.09% a year, with a 10-year annualized return of 10.6% and a distribution yield near 1.5% that has grown roughly 20% in the last year alone. Against the obvious alternative (XLV), VHT is broader (414 vs ~60 holdings), less concentrated (top 10 is 51.5% vs 61.6%; Lilly 12.8% vs 16.5%), and reaches into mid- and small-cap healthcare the S&P-500-only XLV structurally excludes — at essentially the same cost (0.09% vs 0.08%). Ten-year returns are nearly identical, so the choice is structural, not performance-chasing: breadth wins for buy-and-hold. Verdict: BUY — initiate as the portfolio's core healthcare holding toward 5–8% of NAV, per the October 3 healthcare sector outlook. The healthcare sleeve is currently ~2.5% (BMY/UNH/GEHC); VHT is the cleanest way to close the gap to a full weight.

2. Objective & Index/Methodology

VHT passively tracks the MSCI US Investable Market Health Care 25/50 Index by full replication — the all-cap US healthcare market (large 65.6%, plus mid/small tails) with RIC-compliant capping: no single issuer above 25%, and issuers above 5% capped at 50% in aggregate. Incepted January 2004; open-ended NYSE Arca ETF with quarterly distributions. The 25/50 capping is the reason Lilly sits at 12.8% here versus 16.5% in XLV — concentration is structurally contained.

3. Costs

Expense ratio 0.09% — $9 a year per $10,000 invested. XLV charges 0.08%; FHLC (Fidelity) also 0.08%. At these levels the 1-basis-point gap is immaterial against the breadth advantage. No loads, no 12b-1; bid/ask spreads are penny-wide on ~$20B of assets. Morningstar: 4 stars, Bronze medalist rating (quantitative, September 2026).

8. Risks / What Could Change the Verdict

(1) Concentration: Lilly alone is 12.8% — a GLP-1 disappointment reprices the fund, not just the stock. (2) Policy: IRA negotiation (round-2 prices live Jan 2027), MFN/TrumpRx pricing pressure, and MA scrutiny fall on VHT's top holdings directly. (3) US-only: ~100% domestic — no Novo/Roche/AstraZeneca; pair with IXJ only if international healthcare is desired (it isn't, for this mandate). (4) Sector lag: healthcare has trailed the S&P over 3/5/10 years; the overweight is a valuation call that needs the earnings inflection (expected Q4 2026–2027) to arrive. Downgrade to HOLD if the thesis becomes "index-hug at full weight" — i.e., once the position reaches ~8% and the relative multiple normalizes.
