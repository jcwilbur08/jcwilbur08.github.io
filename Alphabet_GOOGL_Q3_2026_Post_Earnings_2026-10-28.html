04

Earnings call: five decision-relevant debates

1 / AI Search — volume vs yield

Doug Anmuth (JPMorgan) asked why Search revenue slowed while query growth was fastest in years. Management emphasized the AI-driven volume increase; it did not supply granular AI-query ad yield or profit economics. Falsifier: AI usage rises while Search growth weakens for multiple quarters.

2 / TPU sales — revenue vs margin

Ross Sandler (Barclays) requested system-sales revenue and profitability details. CFO said no product-level margin disclosure; inventory is built after signed contracts. This is a meaningful cash-conversion distinction, not proof all hardware revenue earns software-like economics.

3 / Cloud without TPU hardware

Mark Shmulik (AllianceBernstein) asked about organic Cloud growth excluding system sales. Cloud CEO called it very strong but did not quantify it. The absence of a segment bridge limits confidence in a 103% run-rate extrapolation.

4 / Agentic search monetization

Justin Post (Bank of America) pressed on whether agentic flows disintermediate ad inventory. Management characterized agents as additive and sees advertising and subscriptions as opportunities. Conversion economics remain unproven.

5 / AI capacity allocation

Eric Sheridan (Goldman Sachs) questioned capital prioritization. CEO cited long-range ROIC analysis across businesses. CFO anticipates positive ROIC above cost of capital but did not provide project-level historical realized returns.

6 / Cloud supply and commitments

Cloud backlog reached $561B, with slightly more than 55% expected to be recognized in 24 months. Third-party bridging capacity will weigh on Q4 Cloud margins, even if it supports more revenue. Demand strength alone does not establish incremental free cash flow.

EPS quality: distinguish accounting marks from operations

Headline GAAP EPS fell 26%, but underlying operating income rose 30%. The two figures are not contradictory. Other income/expense swung from +$12.759B in Q3 2025 to −$8.221B in Q3 2026, largely related to investment revaluations including SpaceX according to CFO commentary. [S2] [S4]

Illustrative EPS absent entire net OI&E line$2.04$2.66

This approximation removes all net other income/expense rather than just unrealized SpaceX marks; uses each quarter's reported effective tax rate and reported diluted share count; and is not company adjusted EPS, nor a reliable recurring EPS forecast. It simply reveals why the headline EPS comparison can mislead. GAAP operating income growth is the cleaner confirmed signal.

Important contrary evidence: operating expenses, especially R&D and depreciation, still grow rapidly; Q3 operating margin (31.8%) was below the Q2 2026 level (~34%). A temporary mark-to-market loss does not excuse a structurally weakening cash return. [S2] [S4] [S8]

Financing: Alphabet issued 36 million combined Class A and C common shares in Q3 for approximately $12.4B net proceeds under its ATM program, and issued ~$28.7B of senior unsecured notes. Those proceeds and share count deserve greater attention than the historical buyback narrative. Equity dilution can reduce the per-share benefit of operating growth. [S2] [S4]

Why cash may improve: management says the quarter contained inventory build for signed TPU system contracts and a ~$5.2B EC fine cash payment. Cash release from inventory conversion can help future OCF. Why it might not: 2027 capex is expected to increase significantly, outsourced compute has lower near-term margin, and data-center depreciation and power costs will keep rising. Both must appear in the forward model.

Capital allocation checkpoint: request a disclosure of TPU systems backlog conversion, cash collection, gross margin, third-party capacity economics, cumulative project ROIC and 2027–29 capex. Without those, current “strong ROIC” commentary is an assertion, not verified incremental economic value.

Reverse DCF: what the quote requires

Holding the base model's 9.0% discount rate, 3.0% terminal growth, $100B net asset bridge and 12.35B shares constant, and assuming FCF grows linearly from $15B in 2027, the ~$289.43 quote requires roughly $343B of annual 2036 FCF. That is about 17.4× current TTM FCF. This is a demanding outcome, though the 2026 cash trough makes the ratio unusually extreme. A stronger or weaker capex recovery changes the implied requirement substantially.

Traditional multiples, PEG and historical context

On the previous Oct. 20 FY2027 EPS reference of $15.35 (old third-party, pre-print, not updated consensus), $289.43 would represent 18.9× forward EPS. That number is provisional, as estimates may now change following the print. Reported trailing P/E is unreliable because prior quarters had enormous unrealized gains on holdings; TTM FCF yield (~0.55%) paints a much less inexpensive picture. PEG: not reliable until normalized EPS growth, dilution and FCF conversion are reconciled. The prior report's ~$374 blended FV has been reset lower primarily because the cash-recovery assumptions are now more explicit and less generous.

Sensitivity: Base DCF terminal value is highly material. With the base cash path, moving WACC to 10% (3% terminal growth) changes DCF to roughly $232; reducing terminal growth to 2% (9% WACC) changes it to roughly $249. The DCF is a stress test on long-duration economics, not a precise single-price target.

AI expands Google Search activity rather than cannibalizing it; TPU systems broaden the addressable AI-infrastructure market and Cloud scales economically. Backlog supports multi-year visibility, Search funds experimentation, and enterprise adoption accelerates.

Strongest bear argument
