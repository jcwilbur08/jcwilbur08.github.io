01 | Executive Summary

One BUY, seven on watch, three to avoid

ORCL — BUY  ·  MU · ANET · AMAT · LRCX · DELL · MRVL · AMD — HOLD (watchlist)  ·  ARM · PLTR · SNOW — AVOID

Oracle is the only name where quality meets your price discipline — derated 28% YTD to ~17× FY27E with a $664B backlog and +121% OCI growth. Size modestly: $125B of debt, negative free cash flow, and OpenAI concentration cap this as a conviction-limited position, not a core. The seven HOLDs are right stories at wrong prices — each has a stated entry level. The three AVOIDs are quality businesses at unacceptable multiples.

Thesis: The AI buildout is a capex supercycle, not just a chip cycle. The Big Four hyperscalers' 2026 capex guidance sums to ~$720–745B — roughly 3.6× the ~$200B AI-accelerator market — and ~60% of that capex is non-chip (power, cooling, land, networking). Demand structurally exceeds supply into 2027–2028: HBM is sold out through 2026, all four hyperscalers raised 2026 capex guidance in July 2026, and NVIDIA's data-center revenue hit $89.0B (+117% y/y) in Q2 FY2027.

Your AI-stack exposure is $383,502 / 23.3% of the ~$1.644M live-marked book — but it is platform-heavy, not picks-and-shovels heavy: mega-cap platforms (AMZN/MSFT/GOOG/META) are 16.9% while the supply chain (NVDA/AVGO/TSM/ASML/QCOM/SOXQ) is only 5.5%. NVDA itself is just 0.42%. The gaps are clear: no memory/HBM, no AMD, no pure-play networking, AI software only via CRM (0.93%), semicap only via ASML, no AI-cloud landlord, no AI-server OEM.

03 | 2026 Demand Backdrop

Nine hard data points

All company-stated figures below are from primary sources; guidance is flagged separately.

  1. Alphabet — 2026 capex guidance raised to $195–205B (from $180–190B), Q2 2026 (Jul 22); Q2 capex $44.9B (~101% y/y); Google Cloud $24.8B (+82%); cloud backlog $514B; "AI demand exceeds supply."
  2. Amazon — 2026 capex guidance raised to ~$220B (from ~$200B), Q2 2026 (Jul 30); Q2 capex $53.1B; AWS $42.2B (+36.7%, fastest in 18 quarters), backlog $496B. Jassy: "We will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026, and I believe this dynamic will also be true in 2027."
  3. Meta — 2026 capex guidance raised to $130–145B (from $115–135B), Q2 2026 (Jul 29); Q2 capex $31.1B; revenue $60.8B (+28%); free cash flow collapsed 91% to $784M — the capex-digestion warning in miniature.
  4. Microsoft — calendar-2026 capex ~$175B (the ~$15B reduction vs. ~$190B is lease-accounting, not retreat); Q4 FY2026 capex $41B (+~70% y/y); Azure +43%, first $100B+ annual Azure; RPO $678B (+84%); demand exceeding capacity.
  5. NVIDIA — Q2 FY2027 (reported Aug 26): data-center revenue $89.0B, +117% y/y (~93% of total $96.2B); Q3 guided $108B ±2% (no China compute revenue assumed).
  6. TSMC — Q2 2026 revenue $40.2B (+36% y/y), gross margin 67.7%; 3nm 30% / 5nm 33% / 2nm 3% of wafer revenue; HPC 66%; FY2026 growth guidance raised to slightly above 40% USD.
  7. HBM sold out through 2026 — SK hynix; Micron HBM likewise sold out (Q3 FY2026, Jun 24). NVIDIA CFO Kress: "Memory scarcity today is being driven in large part by the AI buildout itself."
  8. Demand > supply into 2027–2028 — Alphabet: "AI demand exceeds supply"; AWS 2027 data-center capacity largely reserved, some into 2028 (executive statements).
  9. Enterprise adoption — Salesforce (Feb 2026): 54% of sales orgs have used AI agents, 88% plan to by 2027; 42% of firms report full AI implementation vs. 11% a year earlier (vendor surveys, directional).
Could not be verified: the primary Microsoft IR URL for the Q4 FY2026 figures; quantitative 2026 accelerator lead times; a 2026 IDC headline enterprise-AI-spend number. These are flagged rather than estimated — the demand case above does not depend on them.

06 | Portfolio Fit & Sizing

What the AI stack means for your book

The shape of your exposure: $383,502 / 23.3% (live-marked Sept 24, CRM/QCOM at post-sale quantities, ~$1.644M adjusted total). It is platform-heavy, not picks-and-shovels heavy — 16.9% in the four hyperscalers vs. 5.5% in the supply chain. You are long AI demand and short AI supply-chain breadth. Additions should diversify the supply chain (memory, networking, semicap) or the application layer (AI software / cloud landlord) — not more mega-cap platform weight.

Concentration risks

  • Double-sided capex bet: the four hyperscalers are your largest AI exposure and the spenders — a capex-digestion pause hits both sides.
  • Networking only via AVGO (no ANET); zero memory/HBM (no MU); zero second-source compute (no AMD).
  • AI software only CRM at 0.93% (no PLTR/SNOW); semicap only ASML (no AMAT/LRCX); no AI-cloud landlord (no ORCL) or AI-server OEM (no DELL).
  • Power/DC-infra only via GRID (~0.6%) — thin for the layer where ~60% of hyperscaler capex actually flows.

07 | Risks

What can break the trade

RiskDetail
Capex digestionBig Four 2026 capex ~$720–745B is ~90% of operating cash flow and 45–57% of revenue — utility-grade intensity. Meta's FCF already collapsed 91% y/y in Q2. Any pause in the "demand exceeds supply" narrative reprices the whole stack, and your book is 16.9% hyperscaler platforms.
China / export controlsNVIDIA assumes zero China data-center compute revenue in its outlook and describes itself as "effectively foreclosed" from China's DC compute market. The MATCH Act could extend controls to DUV servicing (~14–15% of ASML revenue). Escalation is the single largest binary risk to NVDA/TSM/ASML/AMAT/LRCX.
ValuationThe beyond-the-book set trades at 45–400× earnings; even your holdings embed high expectations (ASML ~53× TTM per its report). Multiple compression on any demand wobble is the base-case drawdown path.
Power — the #1 bottleneckPower is the binding constraint on the build-out (Omdia/IDC); ~60% of hyperscaler capex is non-chip; data-center electricity demand +17% in 2025 (IEA). Grid/power delays can strand chip demand — which is also why GRID/GEV belong in the same conversation as this report.
In-house silicon share shiftCustom hyperscaler ASICs are already ~$25B+ and growing (Google TPU via AVGO/MRVL, AWS Trainium, Meta MTIA). Every point of share shift from merchant GPUs to in-house silicon pressures NVDA/AMD pricing power while favoring AVGO/MRVL — a reason to own both sides, not one.
Memory cyclicalityDRAM +4.4× / NAND +9× prices y/y with 86% Micron margins is the textbook top of a memory cycle; new fab capacity arrives 2028–29. Any MU addition must be sized as cyclical exposure.
