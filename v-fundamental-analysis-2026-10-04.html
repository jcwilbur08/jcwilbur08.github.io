Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Visa remains the highest-quality business in the portfolio, and the market has quietly marked it down from "priced for perfection" to "fairly priced with a catalyst." At $360.66 the stock trades at roughly 24.1x fiscal 2027 consensus EPS of $14.98 — a full multiple point of compression from the ~31x trailing multiple that anchored our September 27 HOLD, driven by a 1.8% pullback and, more importantly, by estimates that keep grinding higher. The business crossed $4 trillion in quarterly payments volume for the first time in FQ3, value-added services are compounding in the mid-30s, and the October 1 stablecoin disclosure shows Visa quietly building the rails for the next settlement layer. The September 27 HOLD is upgraded to Buy under the new rating scale — not because the debates disappeared (client incentives still outrun revenue, the regulatory docket is full), but because 6/10 conviction with ~12% upside now clears the Buy bar with room to spare.

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

The toll booth is widening, not narrowing

Visa processed more than $4 trillion of payments volume in a single quarter for the first time in FQ3 2026, on a network with no credit risk and ~67% non-GAAP operating margins. Cross-border volumes, the highest-yield revenue leg, continue to compound on travel recovery and e-commerce growth. The moat is not just scale — it is the two-sided network effect of issuers, acquirers, merchants and 18 billion reachable endpoints that no fintech has replicated at global scope.

2

Value-added services are becoming a second growth engine

Services revenue of roughly $3.8B grew about 34% on a constant-currency basis in FQ3 — faster than Mastercard's ~20% — and now represents roughly one-third of net revenue. Visa Direct, data products, risk and fraud tools, and marketing services convert the network's data exhaust into high-margin, non-volume-linked revenue. This diversifies the growth algorithm away from pure transaction counts.

3

Stablecoins are an opportunity being built, not a threat being ignored

The October 1 disclosure is the tell: Visa supports more than 160 stablecoin-linked card programs, volume across those programs is up nearly 200% year over year, and about 17% of stablecoin-linked card volume in fiscal 2026 to date flowed through business and commercial programs. Moving stablecoin settlement into production-grade Visa Direct infrastructure (with USDC highlighted) positions Visa to tax the new rails rather than be disintermediated by them.

4

The multiple has de-rated while estimates have not

The stock is 6.4% below its $385.57 52-week high and flat-ish year to date on a price basis (+2.8%), while FY2027 consensus EPS has held at ~$14.98 with upward revisions outnumbering downward ones 5-to-0 over the last 30 days. The market is paying 24.1x forward for mid-teens earnings growth with a 67%+ incremental-margin structure. That is a fair price for quality, not a demanding one.

5

The FQ4 print is the near-term arbiter

October 27 (after close) is the proving ground for the two debates that matter: whether client incentives and operating expenses re-converge toward revenue growth, and whether cross-border volume holds into the holiday quarter. A clean print with incentive discipline is the path to multiple re-rating toward the street's ~$416 average target; another expense-heavy beat-and-guide-lower keeps the stock range-bound.

Variant perception. The market treats Visa as a mature compounder whose best multiple days are behind it, citing the incentive drag and the regulatory overhang. The differentiated view is that the services mix-shift and the stablecoin infrastructure build are underappreciated second acts that can sustain mid-teens earnings growth — and that 24x forward is simply not a demanding price for that profile.

05 | Competition

Competition, fintech & disruption

Advantaged

vs Mastercard

The duopoly is stable and rational. Visa's scale (~2x U.S. transaction value) and faster-growing services arm (+34% cc vs MA's ~20%) are edges; Mastercard's edge is a cleaner regulatory narrative. Both compound; owning both is the toll-booth barbell.

Watch

vs real-time payments / A2A

FedNow, RTP and account-to-account flows nibble at debit's edges, particularly in bill-pay. Visa's answer is Visa Direct and participation in the new rails — credible so far, but the mix-shift bears watching.

Watch

vs Big Tech wallets

Apple Pay and Google Pay ride on Visa's rails today, but tokenization shifts economics and the platforms hold the customer relationship. The DOJ's 2024 debit suit alleged Visa paid potential rivals to stay friendly — the competitive moat has a litigation price.

Advantaged

vs stablecoin-native rails

The October 1 disclosure reframes the threat: Visa is embedding itself in stablecoin settlement rather than fighting it. The network's fraud, compliance and dispute infrastructure is genuinely hard to replicate on-chain.

06 | Regulatory

Regulatory & litigation — the docket filled up

Regulation is the permanent tax on Visa's moat. The last week of September added three live items:

September 30, 2026

White House swipe-fee rhetoric returns. The President told a Dallas convention audience Washington would "cut out-of-control credit card swipe fees," reviving the political fight over interchange. Merchants — not issuers — pay the bulk of interchange, but political pressure is the leading indicator of legislative risk (Durbin-Marshall Credit Card Competition Act and successor proposals).

Late September 2026

978 merchants object to the $38B settlement. Nearly a thousand retailers and trade groups (including Walmart and Circle K) asked Judge Brian Cogan to reject the revised swipe-fee settlement that received preliminary approval in June 2026. Their argument: interchange falls by only a tenth of a percentage point for five years — against a 2.36% average merchants paid in 2025 — while binding merchants to network rules for years. Class counsel's response is due October 14. Two earlier versions were thrown out (2016 appeals court; Judge Brodie in 2024), so rejection is a live possibility.

September 2026

$167.5M ATM settlement. Visa ($88.8M share) and Mastercard agreed to settle non-bank ATM surcharge claims without admitting wrongdoing. Financially immaterial; directionally another data point in the drip of antitrust cost.

Ongoing

DOJ debit monopolization suit (filed September 2024) alleging Visa suppressed debit competition through issuer agreements and payments to potential rivals. Visa calls the claims meritless. Timeline and outcome unknowable — but it caps multiple expansion until resolved.

How to underwrite it. None of these items impairs the operating business in 2026–27; collectively they are why Visa trades at 24x forward instead of 30x. The regulatory discount is the opportunity — but it is also real, and a swipe-fee legislative push or settlement collapse would be the fundamental-news break that changes the call.

09 | Risks

Risks & catalysts

Risks

Key

Incentive & expense drag

Client incentives (+18%) and non-GAAP opex (+17%) outran revenue (+14%) in FQ3. If FQ4 shows the same pattern, the "temporary investment" narrative breaks and the multiple deserves to stay compressed.

Key

Regulatory / litigation

Swipe-fee legislation, the $38B settlement's fate (October 14 response deadline), and the DOJ debit suit form a three-front docket. Any adverse turn is a multiple event, not an earnings event.

Watch

Print-day price action

Visa has a habit of selling off on prints even when numbers beat. Position sizing should assume post-earnings volatility, not a relief rally.

Watch

Consumer slowdown

Payments volume is a coincident consumer indicator. A sharper labor-market deterioration flows straight through to the top line.
