01 | What's New

What changed in seven days

The initiation is one week old; the operating facts have not moved. What moved is the catalyst calendar and the news flow — net positive, with two genuine new upside items and two new regulatory headlines to log.

1

Anthropic IPO roadshow began — the unmodeled catalyst

Anthropic has begun its IPO roadshow targeting a Nasdaq debut this month, with reporting putting the potential raise as high as $100B at a valuation near $2 trillion. Alphabet owns roughly 14% of the company, a stake reportedly carried at about $124B on the last private round. If the IPO prices anywhere near the top of the reported range, that holding roughly doubles on paper — a ~$120B+ mark inside a $4.1T market cap. This is the same dynamic as the Q2 SpaceX mark that flattered GAAP EPS: it does not change operating earnings, but it de-risks the balance sheet and validates the private portfolio. Watch for the public S-1 and pricing-range headlines this week.

2

Waymo: $16B at $126B, Las Vegas live, Tokyo next

Waymo closed a $16B funding round at a $126B valuation and is expanding fully autonomous rides into Las Vegas — where Amazon-backed Zoox already competes — while preparing commercial robotaxi expansion in Tokyo. The unit now completes over 500,000 weekly rides. At $126B of funded value, Waymo alone is worth roughly 3% of Alphabet's market cap as a separately priced asset — a genuine second growth story, and a re-rating catalyst if international expansion executes.

3

Street reaffirmed into the print

Piper Sandler raised its target $395 → $400 (Overweight) on September 28; JPMorgan retained its $420 Overweight view on October 2. Consensus per MarketBeat: Buy, average target $419.93 across 44 ratings (6 Strong Buy, 34 Buy, 4 Hold) — implying ~23% upside from the reference close. HSBC's $515 (August) remains the street high; Bernstein's $385 Market Perform and DA Davidson's $350 Neutral anchor the cautious camp.

4

Regulatory: two new headlines, neither structural

On October 1, Alphabet filed a legal challenge against two EU orders that would compel data-sharing with AI firms and search competitors (the DMA enforcement wave), and a Manhattan federal judge authorized publishers to pursue more than $3.2B in damages for alleged ad-tech monopolization. Separately, a €403M EU fine landed in recent weeks. None of these reopens the breakup question — the two US structural cases both ended in behavioral remedies — but they confirm regulatory cost and headline risk remain a permanent tax on the multiple.

5

Cloud commercial proof points keep stacking

Google Cloud signed a five-year agreement with BNP Paribas expanding Gemini model access, integrated AI-security tooling with Thales for agentic AI workflows, and Google is reportedly planning a $15B AI-infrastructure investment in Finland. Google Health Enterprise tied up with Weight Watchers on AI-powered health coaching, and Google was picked as a tech partner for America.gov, the administration's AI-driven government portal. None moves 2026 numbers; all of it is enterprise demand evidence for the backlog.

Variant perception. The market treats Alphabet as an advertising company renting AI credibility. The differentiated view, strengthened this week: it is an AI-infrastructure company (Cloud +82%, TPU sales to external customers beginning, $514B backlog) that happens to own the world's best cash-generative ad franchise — plus a ~$124B Anthropic call option about to get a public price. The market is underwriting the ad business; the AI assets are close to free at $340.
02 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

Cloud is repricing the multiple

Revenue of $24.8B (+82%) on an ~$99B annualized run rate, with operating margin expanding from ~21% to 35.6% year over year and operating income more than tripling to $8.8B. A $514B committed backlog — over 5× the run rate — converts enterprise AI demand into multi-year visibility the ad business cannot provide. This is the profile of a business that earns its capex, the inverse of the "AI spending with no return" critique.

2

Search is monetizing AI, not dying from it

Search revenue grew 17% to $63.3B while AI Overviews appear on roughly 30% of US queries. An independent September survey showed 78% of users naming Google as their primary search engine, up from 70% a year earlier, with 83% preference in the 18–44 cohorts. Commercial-intent queries — the ones advertisers pay for — remain Google's. The moat is intact, though the antitrust remedies (data-sharing, no exclusive defaults) structurally lower entry barriers for challengers over time.

3

Two separately-priced AI assets sit inside the conglomerate

The Anthropic stake (~$124B carried, IPO roadshow underway toward a possible ~$2T print) and Waymo ($126B funded valuation, 500K+ weekly rides, international expansion) are together worth roughly 6% of the market cap as independently validated assets. Neither is in the operating-earnings base the target multiple is applied to — they are free upside to the $410 operating case.

4

The regulatory discount has structurally shrunk

Both US breakup threats ended in behavioral remedies: no Chrome/Android sale (search case, remedies ruling September 2025; appeals now before the D.C. Circuit) and no AdX/publisher-server sale (ad-tech case, September 2, 2026). The stock is up ~65% off its 2025 lows partly on this de-risking. What remains — EU fines, DMA compliance, the $3.2B publisher suit — is cost and noise, not existential.

5

The price still embeds skepticism

At $340.35 the stock sits ~16% below its $404.47 52-week high and ~23% below the street consensus target. The forward operating multiple of ~26× is fair for a 20%+ grower and below cloud pure-plays — reasonable for a business where Cloud grows at 82% and the ad franchise funds the buildout from a $242.5B cash pile.

05 | Regulatory

Regulatory: breakups off the table, costs remain

United States — the structural risk is gone

In the search monopolization case, the September 2025 remedies ruling rejected the DOJ's push to force sales of Chrome or Android — instead banning exclusive default-distribution deals and requiring search-index and user-interaction data sharing with qualified rivals, with remedies explicitly extended to Gemini and AI search products. Google appealed in January 2026; the DOJ cross-appealed in February seeking to reinstate divestiture demands. Both are before the D.C. Circuit with oral argument likely late 2026 or early 2027 — the status quo persists for years. On September 2, 2026, Judge Brinkema rejected the DOJ's bid to force a sale of the AdX exchange and publisher ad server in the ad-tech case, ordering behavioral changes instead (details unsealed after a 14-day redaction review).

Europe and the courts — the permanent tax

Since the initiation: a €403M EU fine, Alphabet's October 1 legal challenge to two EU DMA orders compelling data-sharing with AI firms and search rivals, and a Manhattan federal judge's October 1 authorization for publishers to pursue $3.2B+ in ad-tech damages. Add the overhang of a proposed US bill trimming tax benefits for large AI data centers, which would raise the cost of the Cloud buildout. None of this is existential; all of it is a recurring cost and headline risk that caps how far the multiple can expand.

06 | Capex & Cash Flow

The capex bill: the only legitimate debate

Quarterly capex of $44.9B (roughly doubled year over year) produced the first negative free-cash-flow print in company history: −$5.9B in Q2 (reported). The full-year guide was raised to $195–205B from $180–190B, and management has signaled a significant further increase in 2027. This is the reason the stock fell after hours on an otherwise spectacular quarter and the reason it sits 16% below its high.

Why the bears have a point

$44.9B of quarterly capex is real money leaving the building. If AI-infrastructure pricing collapses or enterprise demand stalls, the spending becomes a stranded-asset story and FCF stays depressed for years. The 10-year at ~5.3% makes every dollar of back-loaded return worth less today.

Why the spending is underwritten

The $242.5B cash and securities pile funds two full years at this capex rate. The Crux AI structure ($22B Blackstone-arranged financing for TPU capacity) shows willingness to share infrastructure risk off-balance-sheet. And Cloud's 82% growth at 35.6% margins is precisely the return stream the spending is buying — this is investment with a visible return, not faith-based buildout.

Underwrite on backlog conversion, not capex decline. The spending continues — the stock works if Cloud revenue and margins keep absorbing it. The quarterly telltales: Cloud growth, backlog conversion, and whether the capex guide moves again without a corresponding Cloud acceleration.

08 | Risks

Risks

  • Capex returns disappoint. $195–205B of 2026 capex (and more in 2027) must convert into Cloud revenue and Search monetization. If AI-infrastructure pricing collapses or enterprise demand stalls, the spending becomes stranded and FCF stays depressed.
  • AI search share loss. The 78% primary-engine survey reading is a point in time. If agentic AI (Meta Muse, OpenAI) or Perplexity-style products shift high-value commercial queries off Google, Search growth decelerates and the multiple compresses hard.
  • Adverse appeal outcomes. The DOJ's cross-appeal seeks Chrome/Android divestiture; a D.C. Circuit reversal would reopen the structural-breakup risk the market has priced out. Oral argument likely late 2026/early 2027.
  • Ad-cycle sensitivity. Roughly 70% of revenue is still advertising. A recession cutting ad budgets hits Search and YouTube simultaneously — the $514B Cloud backlog only partly offsets this.
  • Rate regime. The 10-year at ~5.3% with a December hike still the base case presses every long-duration multiple; Alphabet's rerating can stall on macro alone.
  • Regulatory creep. EU DMA enforcement, the $3.2B publisher damages suit, the €403M fine, and potential US data-center tax-benefit changes add recurring cost and headline risk.
  • Unrealized-gain volatility. The Q2 ~$99B mark (SpaceX, Anthropic stakes) reverses if private valuations fall — reported EPS will swing violently quarter to quarter regardless of operations. Value the operating lines.
  • Momentum. Up ~65% off 2025 lows; a Q3 disappointment on October 28 would reverse fast with positioning this long.
