1. Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Broadcom is the dominant merchant supplier of custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and the leading Ethernet networking provider for AI clusters, paired with a high-margin infrastructure software franchise anchored by VMware R. The AI business is scaling at a pace rarely seen in semiconductors: AI semiconductor revenue hit $16.7B in Q3 FY2026 — up 221% year over year and 54% sequentially R — and management has now framed a long-range path of $58B in FY2026, ~$115B in FY2027, and ~$230B in FY2028, with confidence in over $30 of non-GAAP EPS by FY2028 R.

Yet the stock is priced as if the peak is in hand. AVGO trades ~26% below its June 2026 high of $495 and is up just 5.3% year to date R — having sold off after both the June and September earnings prints despite record results, because the Q4 revenue guide came in a hair below consensus ($34.8B vs ~$35.05B E). Consensus FY2027 non-GAAP EPS of $19.49 implies the stock changes hands at just ~18.8× FY2027E earnings C/E — a multiple more typical of a mid-single-digit grower than a company tripling AI revenue.

Thesis O: this is an expectations reset, not a fundamental deterioration. The de-rating is driven by whisper-number dynamics (an unattainable beat-and-raise bar), not demand — management stated on the call that deployment pacing is constrained by "land, power, and shell availability" rather than chip demand, with five hyperscaler XPU programs, Anthropic visibility to 10 GW by 2028, and 5 GW tied to OpenAI R. At ~19× FY2027E non-GAAP earnings, a 41–46% free-cash-flow margin, a 15th consecutive dividend increase, and a $10B buyback authorization, the market is offering the clearest AI-infrastructure compounder in the market at a value multiple. Key risks: extreme customer concentration, the Google–Marvell competitive read-through, and VMware leverage that still needs retiring.

Bottom line: BUY with a $505 12-month target (~39% upside), anchored on ~26× FY2027E consensus non-GAAP EPS of $19.49 C/E — aligned with the $505 TipRanks consensus average and between Goldman Sachs' $435 and Mizuho's $530 post-print calls. A 2.5% position is appropriately sized; this is a candidate to add on any further expectation-driven weakness, not to trim.
Portfolio note: Cam holds 117 shares (~$42,651, 2.5% of the $1.70M portfolio) — his largest position among the semiconductor holdings and a top portfolio weight overall. Morningstar rates AVGO 5 stars with a $650 fair value (Sept 2026 export) — notably more constructive than our $505 target; both views agree on material undervaluation, differing on magnitude.

2. Business Overview

  • Company: Broadcom Inc., Delaware corporation, headquartered in Palo Alto, CA — a global technology leader in semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions R.
  • Two reportable segments: Semiconductor solutions (70% of Q3 FY2026 revenue; broadband, wireless, networking, storage, industrial, and custom AI accelerators) and Infrastructure software (30%; VMware private cloud, mainframe, distributed, and networking software) R.
  • VMware: the $61B VMware acquisition closed in November 2023, transforming Broadcom into a semiconductor + enterprise software company R. Infrastructure software revenue grew 29% y/y in Q3 FY2026 to $8.75B R.
  • Management: Hock Tan has been President & CEO since the Avago era (2006); his capital-allocation playbook is factual and repeatable — 15 consecutive annual dividend increases, a $10B buyback authorization (Mar 2026), and systematic VMware-era deleveraging R.
  • AI positioning: Broadcom is the leading merchant supplier of custom AI accelerators ("XPUs") to hyperscalers and the dominant Ethernet AI networking provider — the two chips that define AI-cluster economics alongside NVIDIA GPUs R/O.

4. Guidance & Outlook

  • Q4 FY2026 (ending 1 Nov 2026): revenue ~$34.8B (+93% y/y), non-GAAP operating income ~66% of projected revenue R; AI semiconductor revenue guided to $21.7B (+236% y/y) R. The headline guide landed marginally below Bloomberg consensus (~$35.05B) and a ~66.5% whisper op-margin — the mechanical trigger for the post-print selloff E.
  • FY2026 AI revenue: raised to $58B (from $56B at the Q2 call) R. Implied full-year revenue from quarterly actuals + Q4 guide ≈ $105.9B C; implied AI revenue (Q1–Q3 actuals $35.9B + Q4 guide $21.7B) ≈ $57.6B, consistent with the $58B guide C/R.
  • Long-range AI framing (Sept 2 call): FY2027 AI revenue ~$115B ("secured supply"), FY2028 ~$230B, with confidence in >$30 non-GAAP EPS by FY2028 R. Tan publicly reaffirmed these targets in September amid skepticism from Anthropic's CEO about AI buildout pacing R.
  • Street trajectory E: FY2027 consensus ~$173B revenue / ~$19.5 non-GAAP EPS (StockAnalysis, post-print). Mizuho's Buy/$530 case projects $129B/$235B in FY2027/FY2028 AI revenue — above management's frame E.

5. Balance Sheet, Cash Flow & Capital Allocation

  • Cash: $24.0B at quarter-end (up from $19.6B prior quarter) R; quarter-end cash generation is extraordinary — $13.7B FCF in a single quarter, 46% of revenue R.
  • Debt: ~$64B gross debt at the August 2025 10-Q (long-term $62.8B + short-term $1.4B) R — stale; the company repaid $10.53B of debt in the first 9 months of FY2026 R (secondary reporting of company figures; verify against the FY2026 10-K before citation in external use). Net debt is therefore materially lower than the stale gross figure implies.
  • Dividend: $0.65/quarter ($2.60 annualized), payable 30 Sep 2026 — the 15th consecutive annual increase, with ~0.7% yield at current prices R/C. FCF coverage is ample (dividends + buybacks + debt repayment all funded from $31.9B of 9M FY26 FCF per secondary reporting) R/O.
  • Buybacks: $10B share-repurchase authorization (March 2026); $8.45B executed in 9M FY26 R.
  • Financing structure: a quarterly filing disclosed up to $29B in lease obligations tied to an Apollo/Blackstone data-center fund R (per secondary reporting of a company filing — verify in the 10-Q before publishing). This warrants monitoring: it is an off-balance-sheet-style commitment funding AI demand.

6. The AI Engine: XPUs & Networking

Custom AI accelerators (XPUs) are now the majority of Broadcom's revenue. In Q3, AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7B — 56% of total quarterly revenue, up from 49% in Q2 R/C.

  • XPU mix: XPU shipments increased >3.5× y/y and accounted for 73% of AI revenue; AI networking revenue increased >2.5× y/y R (Q3 earnings call).
  • Customers: the company confirms five XPU customers and publicly names Google's program (seventh-generation Ironwood TPU cited as a demand driver) R. Other customer names appear only in media transcript reporting — call commentary, not filing fact. Do not treat them as confirmed.
  • Visibility: Anthropic is on track to be Broadcom's largest XPU customer in 2027–28 with visibility to 10 GW of deployment by 2028; 5 GW tied to OpenAI; demand exceeds guided outlook, with deployment pacing constrained by "land, power, and shell availability" rather than chip demand R (call, via transcript reporting — attribute as such).
  • Supply chain: a reported Samsung MOU exceeding $200B through 2030 (sub-2nm foundry, HBM, advanced packaging) directly addresses single-foundry (TSMC) dependence E (secondary reporting).
  • Why this matters O: the XPU business is a custom model — hyperscalers co-design with Broadcom, creating switching costs and multi-year visibility that merchant GPU sales lack. The concentration that worries the market is the same feature that produces the 73%-of-AI-revenue XPU mix and the >3.5× shipment growth.

7. Competitive Position

  • vs NVIDIA: complementary, not zero-sum — Broadcom's XPUs and Ethernet networking sit alongside GPUs in AI clusters. Hyperscalers use custom silicon for inference and scale workloads while buying NVIDIA for training/flexibility O.
  • vs Marvell: the August 2026 Google–Marvell deal for adjacent AI-infrastructure chip work is the clearest competitive signal — Broadcom extended its TPU engagement in April 2026, but Marvell now handles neighboring programs R (secondary). Watch for further share fragmentation O.
  • Networking moat: AI networking revenue >2.5× y/y with Ethernet the de-facto AI-cluster fabric outside proprietary InfiniBand — Broadcom's Tomahawk/Jericho-class switches are the merchant standard R/O.
  • Software moat: VMware's private-cloud franchise delivers $8.75B/quarter at software margins and growing 29% — a durable cash compounder funding the AI capex cycle R/O.

8. Risks

  • Customer concentration: AI revenue is concentrated in five hyperscaler XPU programs (only Google publicly named). Loss, delay, or re-sourcing of a major program would materially affect guidance R/O.
  • Custom-silicon competition: Marvell's Google deal (Aug 2026) shows hyperscalers will multi-source; AMD and in-house ASIC teams remain threats R/O.
  • Expectations risk: the stock has sold off after two consecutive record prints (June: ~−13.8% after hours; September: ~−5% intraday) because consensus already prices the AI trajectory — any guide that merely meets will be punished R/O.
  • Leverage: ~$64B gross debt (Aug 2025 10-Q, stale) R; plus up to $29B in disclosed Apollo/Blackstone fund lease obligations (verify in 10-Q before publishing) R — total commitments warrant monitoring against $24B cash and ~$39B TTM FCF.
  • Margin mix: gross margin dipped to 76.3% in Q3 on AI product mix (secondary call reporting); management redirected investors to the 66% non-GAAP operating margin instead R. AI-heavy mix structurally lowers gross margin while expanding absolute profit — understand this before reacting to margin headlines O.
  • VMware churn: enterprise churn/backlash risk after Broadcom's licensing changes is opinion-only — no verified company churn data exists; monitor renewals O.
  • Export controls / China: no Broadcom-specific China restriction data was verified in this research; include only as a generic AI-semiconductor exposure O.
  • Semiconductor cyclicality: non-AI semiconductor and software segments remain cyclical; an AI pause would expose the base business multiple O.

10. Catalysts & What to Watch

  • Q4 FY2026 earnings (expected early December 2026 — not company-confirmed): the FY2027 AI revenue guide (~$115B) is the single most important number in the stock; a raise-and-beat print should re-rate the multiple E/O.
  • XPU customer expansion: any sixth customer or expansion of the five-program base; Google TPU cadence updates O.
  • Anthropic/OpenAI deployment pacing: 10 GW by 2028 visibility is the backbone of the FY2028 frame — track land/power/shell milestones R/O.
  • Samsung MOU progress: the reported >$200B through-2030 agreement (sub-2nm, HBM, packaging) would de-risk TSMC single-foundry dependence if confirmed E.
  • Capital returns: $0.65 dividend payable 30 Sep 2026 (record 21 Sep); ongoing $10B buyback execution R.
  • Deleveraging: FY2026 10-K debt disclosure (Nov/Dec 2026) will update the stale gross-debt figure — expect material reduction O.
  • Margin narrative: management's 66% non-GAAP op-margin guide is the right metric; ignore gross-margin-mix headlines R/O.
