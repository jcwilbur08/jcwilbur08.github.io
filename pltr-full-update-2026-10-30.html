The desk call

High conviction in the business. Low conviction in the entry price.

Palantir is Hold, maintained, at 5/10 conviction with the $200 target intact. Nothing in October weakened the operating story — Q2 was the strongest quarter in the company's history, the balance sheet holds $9.2bn of cash and short-term Treasuries with no interest-bearing debt, and the sell-side spent the month raising targets. What October changed is the entry arithmetic: the stock broke its 50-day moving average on the final session, closes the month 13.5% below its high, and goes into a Monday print where consensus already sits above the top of management's own guidance.

That combination — a superb business, a demanding multiple, and a binary catalyst three days out — is precisely what a Hold is for. The desk holds no Palantir shares, and this report starts none. It instead rebuilds the entry plan: the October 4 recommendation of a single starter level at or below $150 no longer works as the only rung, because $150 now sits below every major moving average, 18.5% under the reference price, and would likely fill only into a broken thesis. The revised structure is a ladder — a starter zone at $161–166, the $150 level retained as a deep second tranche, and a valuation anchor near $136 — armed after Monday's print, not before it.

The framing, plainly: we would rather own this company at the right price after the print than own it at the wrong price before it. Patience is the position.

What would change the rating upward

A Q3 beat-and-raise — revenue above the $2.18bn consensus with a full-year guidance lift — followed by the stock holding the $161–166 zone, or reclaiming $186–187 with the thesis confirmed. Either path converts the ladder into a position on evidence rather than hope.

What would change the rating downward

A print at or below the top of guidance with no full-year raise, U.S. commercial growth decelerating below roughly 120%, or bookings and retention rolling over from their Q2 peaks. At 54× guided 2026 sales, disappointment does not get a second chance from the multiple.

The base · Reported August 3, 2026

Q2 was a blowout, and the market paid up violently for it

Palantir's second quarter, reported after the close on August 3, was the fastest growth in company history: revenue up 93% year on year, U.S. commercial revenue up 149%, U.S. government up 90%, and a Rule of 40 score of 155% — a figure no other large software company is currently printing. Against the single consensus source used throughout this report, MarketBeat, adjusted EPS of $0.41 beat the $0.3446 consensus by roughly 19%. The stock jumped 29.45% the next session to $162.66 and went on to the October records. Q2 is therefore both the proof of the thesis and the reason the Q3 bar is set where it is.

Two definitional cautions belong with these numbers. Remaining deal value and remaining performance obligations are different scopes — deal value presumes exercise of all contract options, while obligations are non-cancellable contracted revenue not yet recognised — and the two must not be added together. The print · Monday, November 2, after the close

The Q3 setup — and the trap inside it

Palantir reports third-quarter results on Monday, November 2, 2026, following the close of U.S. markets, with a webcast at 5:00pm ET — a date and time the company confirmed on October 12. Consensus throughout this report is MarketBeat's, a single provider, never blended: EPS of $0.4072 on revenue of $2.18bn, up 84.3% year on year.

Callout — the setup trap

Consensus sits above the top of guidance

MarketBeat consensus revenue of $2.18bn is $16–20m above the top of management's Q3 guide of $2.160–$2.164bn. A print exactly at the top of guidance — a result management would describe as a successful quarter — therefore reads on the tape as a revenue miss. The bar for Monday is not guidance; it is a beat of consensus plus a full-year raise. Anything less invites the multiple, not the quarter, to do the talking.

The four questions that decide the print.

  • U.S. commercial sustainability. Q2 grew 149%. Citi expects 140%+ growth in Q3 despite far harder comparisons. A print below roughly 130% will be read as deceleration — unfair, perhaps, but the market grades this stock on momentum.
  • Government and contract momentum. U.S. government grew 90% in Q2. Watch total contract value, remaining deal value, and any commentary around the federal fiscal year-end on September 30 — the one bearish operating datapoint raised pre-print is a sequential dip in contract revenue.
  • Margin. The Q3 guide implies an adjusted operating margin near 59.8%, down from Q2's 62%. A hold at or above 60% with disciplined stock compensation keeps the Rule of 40 story intact; slippage feeds the cost-of-growth critique.
  • The guidance raise. Citi expects a full-year raise of roughly ten points of growth. Palantir's pattern is beat-and-raise; a beat without a raise has historically been treated by this stock as a miss.

Other compilers publish different Q3 figures, and they are deliberately not mixed into the table above — a single named consensus keeps the beat-or-miss verdict on Monday unambiguous.

The debate that did develop in October was about the model, not the deals. Coverage on October 20–22 relayed a Gartner warning that by 2028 some 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic-AI applications built through forward-deployed engineering on cost and lock-in grounds — the FDE model being the engine of Palantir's commercial growth. On October 27–28, William Blair framed the related risk from the other side: large-language-model labs hiring Palantir's own forward-deployed engineers could rebuild similar applications more cheaply, while also estimating the Maven programme at close to a $1bn annual run-rate. Both arguments return in the risks section; neither is a company disclosure.

Insiders and holders. October's identified insider sales were small — a few million dollars in aggregate — and, where the filings state it, executed under pre-set Rule 10b5-1 plans. The longer window is louder: $876.7m of open-market insider sales over twelve months with no open-market purchases, concentrated in a handful of founders and executives whose plans predate October. ARK Invest trimmed its position repeatedly between October 14 and 23. No capital actions — no buyback, dividend, split, offering, or debt transaction — were found in the window.

Fundamentals & valuation · At the $184.11 close

Fortress finances, an extreme multiple — both at once

Strip the price away and the fundamentals are close to flawless: revenue compounding at 82–93%, a 62% adjusted operating margin, a 63% adjusted free-cash-flow margin in Q2, $9.2bn of net cash, and dilution that has effectively paused — the diluted share count rose just 0.2% year on year, and stock compensation fell to 13.7% of revenue from 15.9%. The problem has never been the income statement. The problem is what the market already charges for it: even after a 13.5% pullback from the high, Palantir trades at roughly 54× its own guided 2026 sales and 97× forward earnings, with a trailing free-cash-flow yield under 1%.

Risk assessment

The bear case needs no business failure — only a smaller multiple

Valuation is the first-order risk, and it has a precedent. Between November 2025 and June 2026 the shares fell 49% while revenue growth accelerated from 63% to 93%. The multiple compressed because it could. At 54× guided sales and a sub-1% free-cash-flow yield, the price already capitalises roughly a decade of exceptional compounding; a return to even 40× guided sales is $136 a share. The stock's beta near 1.6 means it falls roughly one and a half times the software complex on selloff days, as both October episodes showed.

Concentration cuts against the growth story's durability. Just 1,049 customers generate the whole revenue base; the top twenty average $124m of trailing revenue each, and that concentration is rising as expansion outpaces new logos. The United States is 81% of revenue and government is 51% of the Q2 total, with most government contracts terminable for convenience and deal value that presumes options are exercised. No single customer exceeded 10% of first-half revenue, but the engine is geographically and sectorally narrow.

Competition is now aimed at the moat itself. The forward-deployed-engineer model was Palantir's differentiator; the large-language-model labs now hire those same engineers, Gartner projects most enterprises will abandon FDE-built agentic applications by 2028 on cost and lock-in grounds, and well-capitalised rivals compete for the same defence and enterprise budgets. The defence — the Ontology, 157% net dollar retention, hard-won accreditations — is credible, but the model is no longer proprietary. Persistent plan-driven insider selling, $876.7m over twelve months with zero open-market buying, is a weaker signal than the others but removes a natural buyer and feeds the sceptical narrative; ARK's steady October trimming adds to the supply picture.

What a 30–40% fall actually requires. A drop to roughly $129 (−30%) or $110 (−40%) needs only two of: revenue at or below the guide top with no full-year raise; U.S. commercial growth printing under 120%; a bookings or retention rollover; adjusted margin slipping under 58%; or a second leg of the sector selloff. Note the uncomfortable arithmetic — at $129 the stock would still trade near 38× guided 2026 sales. A 30% fall does not make Palantir cheap; it makes it less extreme. The options-implied move alone reaches only about $161, so a fall of that size requires a genuine guidance or credibility break, not one soft print.

The bull case

Palantir is the best-executing enterprise-AI story in the market: compounding revenue near 90% with a 62% adjusted operating margin, a 63% free-cash-flow margin, 157% net dollar retention, more than $9bn of net cash, and no debt — from a base of barely a thousand customers whose spending is expanding far faster than the logo count, which makes the growth expansion-led and more durable than a land-grab. Its Ontology, forward-deployed engineers, and sovereign-AI stack are entrenched in the U.S. defence and intelligence core and spreading through commercial operations where keeping data and models under the customer's own control is becoming a board-level requirement that hyperscaler and model-lab offerings structurally struggle to match. If U.S. commercial growth holds above roughly 130%, government growth stays strong, and Monday brings another raise, the bull targets at $230–$265 are reachable on estimate revisions alone, with no further multiple expansion required — and twelve consecutive quarters of accelerating growth say the burden of proof sits with the bears.

Entry discipline · Recommended only

Rebuild the ladder: $161–166 to start, $150 as the deep tranche

The October 4 plan had one rung: a starter at or below $150 toward roughly 1% of the portfolio. Checked against today's tape and estimates, that single rung fails twice over. First, distance: $150 is 18.5% below the reference price and sits below the 200-day moving average — a fill there requires a full trend break on top of an earnings disappointment, which is exactly the scenario in which a mechanical buy is hardest to defend. Second, opportunity: if Monday is a beat-and-raise, the stock likely never visits $150, and the desk ends up owning nothing of a $200-target company it researched at $184. The $150 level is retained — but in its proper place, as the deep second tranche. It is not a chase level, and nothing in this section presents it as one.

Verification limits

What could not be confirmed

An honest report marks its own gaps. The following items could not be verified on a single consistent basis in the research behind this update, and no figure in the body depends on resolving them:

  • Moving-average precision. The 50-day, 100-day, and 200-day averages vary by roughly a dollar across providers; the 100-day in particular is shown as a range (~$161.0–161.3) rather than a point. No single authoritative exchange series was obtained.
  • A single 2027 consensus. No one-provider 2027 revenue or EPS consensus was verified. Published figures span roughly $11.6–14.6bn of revenue depending on the estimator, so 2027 is treated throughout as a range, never a point.
  • A consistent peer-multiple table. No one-provider, one-date comparison of peer sales and EBITDA multiples was obtained; the peer discussion is limited to the single sourced, non-like-for-like datapoint stated in the valuation section.
  • Segment-level Q3 estimates beyond one provider. Only one compiler published Q3 segment and customer-count estimates; they could not be corroborated by a second provider and are not used in the expectations table.
  • An exhaustive filings re-check to October 30. The most recent explicit check for new filings dates to mid-October. No October capital action or guidance change was found in news coverage through October 30, but the filings database was not exhaustively re-searched on the day.
  • The attribution split of the October 30 fall. A broad software selloff plus pre-earnings de-risking is established; the precise mix of sector, rates, peer-earnings contagion, and valuation cannot be apportioned from the available reporting, and one same-day attribution was machine-generated commentary.
  • Two immaterial date ambiguities. One October initiation is dated the 8th in some reports and the 9th in others (shown as October 9, the trading date), and one late-October target raise is dated the 27th or 28th depending on the compiler (shown as October 27). Neither affects any conclusion.
  • One unverified media report of a sole-source government migration onto Palantir's platform, published October 12, has no primary contract disclosure behind it and is treated nowhere in this report as a contract win.
