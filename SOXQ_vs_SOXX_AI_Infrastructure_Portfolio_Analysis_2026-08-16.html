Executive Summary

Recommendation: maintain an overweight bias to AI infrastructure, but express the next increment of exposure through the semiconductor production chain rather than through another broad technology or AI thematic ETF. For this portfolio, SOXQ is the preferred core semiconductor ETF; SOXX is a high-quality substitute with superior institutional liquidity but a higher fee, while SMH and SMHX add more concentration in names the portfolio already owns or can own directly.

Portfolio Manager Decision
1. Raise SOXQ from roughly 0.13% to 1.25%-1.50% of portfolio value. SOXQ is inexpensive at 0.19%, tracks 30 large U.S.-listed semiconductor securities, and currently provides meaningful exposure to NVDA, AVGO, MU, AMAT, KLAC, ASML, AMD, TSM and LRCX.

2. Initiate a modest direct NVDA position of approximately 0.50%-0.75%. Broad funds already create some indirect exposure, but there is no direct NVDA holding in the accessible portfolio export. NVIDIA remains the highest-quality pure expression of accelerator compute and AI networking, with Data Center revenue up 92% year over year in fiscal Q1 2027.

3. Add a 0.35%-0.50% semiconductor-equipment position, preferably ASML. The portfolio has substantial demand-side exposure through AMZN, MSFT, GOOG and META, but little direct exposure to the lithography/equipment bottleneck. ASML's commentary that chip demand is outpacing supply and customers are accelerating capacity plans is precisely the missing layer.

4. Hold AVGO at its existing ~2.67% portfolio weight; do not add aggressively. The position is already large enough to capture custom accelerator and networking upside. Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue grew 143% year over year in fiscal Q2 and management guided to more than 200% growth in fiscal Q3, but the portfolio does not need additional concentration in the same factor.

5. Do not own SOXQ and SOXX together. Their economic exposure is highly overlapping. Select one. The small fee advantage and better fit make SOXQ the preferred long-term holding; choose SOXX instead only if trading liquidity, options depth, or institutional execution is a priority.

1. The Current AI Infrastructure Investment Cycle

The fundamental signal remains strong: the largest cloud and AI platforms are still increasing infrastructure commitments, semiconductor suppliers continue to report unusually high growth, and leading-edge capacity remains constrained. The cycle is no longer just a narrative about generative AI adoption; it is now visible in capex, accelerator shipments, networking demand, foundry utilization, memory, and equipment orders.

Compute & Accelerators

NVDA, AMD, AVGO
GPUs, custom accelerators and high-speed networking capture the first-order compute buildout.

Foundry & Manufacturing

TSM, INTC
Advanced-node capacity converts accelerator designs into physical chips. TSM remains the critical independent foundry.

Lithography & Equipment

ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC
Tools required to add leading-edge logic, memory and advanced-packaging capacity.

Memory & Interconnect

MU, SK Hynix, MRVL
HBM, storage, optical and data-center connectivity become more important as inference scales.

Hyperscaler Demand

MSFT, AMZN, GOOG, META
These companies finance the buildout and determine the pace of accelerator and data-center deployment.

Power, Cooling & Grid

GEV, VRT, ETN, utilities
Power availability, cooling and grid interconnection increasingly constrain deployment timelines.

Why SOXQ wins for this portfolio

  • Lowest fee among the principal diversified semiconductor choices. At 0.19%, SOXQ costs materially less than SOXX, SMH, XSD and the broad AI thematic products.
  • Balanced AI hardware exposure. It owns NVDA and AVGO, but also gives meaningful weight to MU, AMAT, KLAC, ASML, TSM and LRCX - precisely the manufacturing and equipment layer missing from the current portfolio.
  • Less concentration than SMH / SMHX. The portfolio already has a 2.67% AVGO position. Adding a fund with 15%-20% NVDA and another large AVGO allocation raises single-factor risk faster than necessary.
  • More targeted than AIQ / BAI. The portfolio already has 16.24% in AMZN, GOOG, MSFT and META. Paying a higher thematic fee to rebuy those companies is inefficient.
  • Liquidity is sufficient for a long-horizon retirement portfolio. SOXX's superior scale and options market matter for institutions and tactical traders, but the current portfolio does not require SOXX's roughly $43.5B asset base or ~10M-share 30-day average volume.

Implementation discipline

  • Stage entries across catalysts and volatility. The AI spending thesis is strong, but semiconductor beta is high and recent gains are extreme. Avoid turning a long-duration thesis into a poor short-term entry.
  • Use ETF exposure for the cyclicals, direct exposure for the bottlenecks. Let SOXQ own MU, MRVL, LRCX, KLAC and other cyclical names; reserve direct positions for NVDA and ASML, where the competitive advantage is strongest.
  • Do not let AVGO + NVDA become an uncontrolled single-theme concentration. AVGO is already 2.67%; a 0.50%-0.75% NVDA direct position plus SOXQ is enough to materially increase accelerator exposure.
  • Cap TSM direct exposure because geopolitical risk is non-diversifiable. TSM is a superior company, but its strategic location risk is not captured by ordinary valuation metrics.
  • Retain power/grid diversification. GEV, GRID and SO provide a second-order AI-infrastructure angle that can work even as semiconductor leadership rotates.

Primary risks

  • Capital-intensity / ROI risk: the market may demand proof that AI revenue can earn adequate returns on the enormous capex base.
  • Valuation compression: strong fundamentals do not prevent large drawdowns when expectations are extreme.
  • Export controls: restrictions can affect NVIDIA's China opportunity and equipment sales by ASML and U.S. tool vendors.
  • Taiwan concentration: advanced foundry production remains strategically concentrated, creating a tail risk to TSM and the entire semiconductor ecosystem.
  • Technology substitution: custom accelerators from hyperscalers and Broadcom partners can take share from merchant GPUs even if total AI compute demand continues to rise.
  • Memory / equipment cyclicality: HBM strength can coexist with oversupply elsewhere; fab equipment spending is historically volatile.
  • Portfolio factor concentration: the portfolio already has more than 22% in mega-cap technology/platform holdings before look-through to funds. AI exposure should be added with discipline.

Thesis-break conditions

Reassess the overweight if two or more occur: (1) major hyperscalers guide to a sustained sequential capex decline unrelated to project timing; (2) NVIDIA Data Center growth stalls while inventory/channel metrics weaken; (3) TSM advanced-node utilization or gross margin deteriorates sharply; (4) ASML / major equipment vendors report broad order cancellations rather than timing shifts; (5) AI revenue growth fails to offset depreciation and power costs across hyperscalers; or (6) geopolitical/export-control risk materially impairs access to Taiwan or China markets.

Senior Analyst Conclusion

The investment cycle is real, powerful and still expanding, but this portfolio should not respond by simply buying more "AI." It already owns the hyperscalers, already owns AVGO at a meaningful weight, and already has a power/grid sleeve. The missing economic exposure is the semiconductor capacity chain that converts AI demand into physical compute.

SOXQ is the preferred ETF because it solves that problem efficiently. It provides broad access to NVDA, AVGO, MU, AMAT, KLAC, ASML, AMD, TSM and LRCX at a 0.19% expense ratio. SOXX is equally credible as a product, and arguably superior for tactical/institutional execution, but its role is duplicative and its fee is higher. SMH and SMHX are excellent vehicles for investors who specifically want concentrated leader exposure, but they are less appropriate here because AVGO is already large and the portfolio's main gap is equipment/manufacturing rather than fabless design.

Recommended action: increase SOXQ to 1.25%-1.50%, establish NVDA at 0.50%-0.75%, initiate ASML at 0.35%-0.50%, maintain AVGO at its current weight, keep TSM below roughly 0.75% direct because of geopolitical risk, and avoid opening overlapping positions in SOXX, SMH, SMHX, AIQ or BAI unless one is explicitly replacing SOXQ or serving a different mandate.

Formal portfolio recommendation: ADD semiconductor infrastructure - Conviction 8.5/10. Prefer diversified core exposure plus selective bottleneck equities. Maintain discipline on position size and entry point after an exceptional 2026 sector rally.
