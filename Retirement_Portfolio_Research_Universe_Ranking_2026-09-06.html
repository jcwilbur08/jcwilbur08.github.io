Executive conclusion

The portfolio does not suffer from a shortage of excellent companies; it suffers from concentration. That changes the order in which good ideas should receive capital. The master ranking therefore puts VXUS and AVUV near the very top, keeps TSM as the highest-ranked individual stock, favors under-owned healthcare and differentiated infrastructure, and deliberately pushes down several outstanding businesses that are already major portfolio drivers.

How to read the ranking. This is a next incremental dollar ranking, not a forced sell list. A lower rank can mean: the stock is expensive, the thesis needs proof, or - most importantly - you already own enough of the same risk. For example, MSFT, META, AMZN, MA and JPM are excellent franchises, but adding to them today does less for the total portfolio than adding diversified international, small-value, healthcare or select infrastructure exposure.

Methodology and scope

Scoring framework

  • 30% expected return / valuation: margin of safety, implied expectations, upside versus downside.
  • 25% business quality: moat, ROIC/cash economics, balance sheet, reinvestment runway.
  • 20% thesis momentum: earnings evidence, revisions, catalysts and visibility.
  • 15% portfolio fit: diversification benefit, current weight and factor overlap.
  • 10% risk resilience: cyclicality, leverage, geopolitical/regulatory and implementation risk.

Universe definition

This avoids false precision between fully researched names and first-pass screen results.

Portfolio-manager interpretation

Where the next dollars have the highest utility

  1. Diversification: VXUS first, AVUV second; AVDV becomes the next equity diversifier if you want a new sleeve.
  2. Semiconductors: TSM first and ASML second. Treat AVGO, QCOM, TSM, NVDA, ASML and SOXQ as one risk budget, with the dedicated sleeve capped near 6% absent a new underwriting.
  3. Healthcare: GEHC can be built only in stages; UNH is attractive but requires evidence on medical costs/Optum; VHT is the cleanest diversified alternative.
  4. Infrastructure/power: GRID remains the preferred owned satellite. GEV is a hold/add-on-weakness, while POWR is an alternative rather than an automatic companion position.
  5. Software/cyber: CRWD and UBER rank above additional mega-cap exposure. ADBE is compelling on valuation but should be treated as an event-risk position into September 10 earnings.

Where capital is least useful today

  • Oversized financials: JPM, GS, MA and AXP are not poor businesses; their portfolio weights make new purchases inefficient. GS remains the clearest trim-on-strength candidate.
  • Mega-cap concentration: AMZN and AAPL are too large to add; MSFT/META/GOOG are holds with selective pullback adds only.
  • Duplicative ETFs: VGT, PTF, SOXX, SMH and most broad AI ETFs largely repackage exposures already held directly.
  • Turnarounds without proof: KHC, INTC, FISV, NKE and SBUX need operating confirmation before they deserve scarce capital.
  • Illiquid/narrow themes: JOUL is inferior to GRID/POWR for core implementation despite thematic purity.
Practical implication: if the portfolio receives a fresh $10,000-$20,000 contribution, the ranking argues against simply buying the highest-quality stock you already own. A diversified deployment led by VXUS/AVUV, with a smaller TSM/ASML or healthcare allocation, improves expected portfolio efficiency more than another dollar into the current top-five holdings.

Research caveats and next checkpoints

Rankings are dynamic. The most sensitive near-term names are ADBE (September 10 earnings and new-CEO transition), TTMI/DELL/ALAB (AI infrastructure expectations after large reratings), PLTR (valuation duration), GEHC (PCS and cash conversion), and UNH (medical-cost and Optum evidence). A 10%-20% price move without a fundamental change can materially reorder several adjacent ranks.
