Executive Summary

Investment conclusion

Southern Company is a textbook regulated compounder being priced as a bond proxy. Adjusted EPS of $1.13 in Q2 beat estimates by $0.13, first-half earnings track at or near the top of the $4.50–$4.60 full-year guide, the contracted large-load pipeline stands at 17 GW, and Georgia Power just signed Google to fund nuclear uprates through a new tariff that shields residential customers entirely. The Vogtle construction overhang that defined this stock for a decade is over — both units earn in rate base. Yet the stock closed at $83.72, down 11% over twelve months, because the 10-year Treasury touched 5.34% this week — its highest since 2002 — and utilities are the market's preferred casualty of higher-for-longer.

01 | Thesis

Investment thesis

1

Regulated earnings compound through any rate regime

For a regulated utility, approved capex becomes rate base, rate base earns the allowed return on equity, and the allowed ROE flows through to EPS. Southern's ~7% annual rate-base growth (estimated from the company's capex trajectory) at allowed ROEs of roughly 10.5–11.5% mechanically supports high-single-digit EPS growth before dilution — the arithmetic behind the 7–8% long-term outlook. Earnings do not need a friendly Fed; they need approved investment and load growth, both of which are in hand.

2

The 17 GW pipeline turns load growth into rate base

Every gigawatt that moves from contracted to under-construction drives generation, transmission, and grid capex — each dollar of which enters rate base. The OpenAI 3.2 GW contract (with 1 GW of grid-friendly demand response) and the new Google nuclear-uprate tariff show the pipeline converting into funded, contracted investment. Management's implied trajectory — adjusted EPS approaching $5.30–$5.40 by 2028 (estimated, ~8% annual growth) — would force a re-rating if delivered, because the market currently prices Southern as a 5–6% grower.

3

Regulation is the moat, and it is top-tier

Georgia's framework holds residential rates stable through 2029 while large-load contracts cover 100% of their incremental costs, backed by ~$21B in collateral protections. Alabama runs formulaic, timely cost recovery. The Google uprate filing extends the same principle to nuclear: the hyperscaler subscribes to a dedicated tariff, receives zero-emission credits, and non-participating customers are shielded from the cost. This is the gold-standard regulatory deal for the AI-load era — growth without ratepayer backlash.

4

The Vogtle overhang is over

Units 3 and 4 — the first new U.S. nuclear units in more than 30 years — are operating and earning in rate base. A decade of construction risk, cost overruns, and earnings charges has been replaced by a 60-to-80-year stream of allowed returns on a massive invested base. The Kemper gasifier dismantlement is complete. What remains is execution on conventional generation and grid capex, not first-of-a-kind mega-project risk.

5

Income with downside discipline

The $3.04 forward dividend yields 3.6% at the reference price, is covered ~1.5x by regulated earnings (payout ~66% on 2026E consensus EPS), and has grown for more than two decades. On Cam's 381.4 shares that is roughly $1,159 of annual income. The yield does not make the stock immune to rate-driven multiple compression, but it pays the investor to wait while the 7–8% earnings compounding does the work.

Variant perception. The market sees a bond proxy with a 5.3% 10-year and prices Southern accordingly. The differentiated view is that the earnings stream is not bond-like at all — it grows 7–8% through rate base investment and the strongest load-growth outlook of any major U.S. utility — while the dividend yield plus buyback-free compounding produces a mid-teens total return even if the multiple never re-rates.
02 | Business & Industry

Business quality and competitive position

Southern Company, headquartered in Atlanta and tracing roots to 1912, is one of the largest U.S. regulated utilities, serving about 9 million electric and gas customers across the Southeast. Roughly 90% of earnings come from state-regulated electric and gas utilities; the company owns ~46 GW of rate-regulated generating capacity plus ~13 GW of competitive generation at Southern Power, and employs about 29,800 people.

04 | Update

What changed since September 27

Google to fund nuclear uprates at Vogtle and Hatch — the funding-model event

On September 21, Georgia Power signed an agreement with Google to fund power uprates at its share of the Vogtle and Hatch nuclear units, adding roughly 96 MW of capacity. The mechanics matter more than the megawatts:

  • New tariff: Georgia Power filed a dedicated Nuclear Uprate-1 tariff; Google subscribes and receives zero-emission credits against its climate targets.
  • Customer shielding: non-participating customers are protected from the incremental cost — the agreement projects ~$900M in benefits to customers over the life of the units.
  • Scope: extended power uprates — turbines, pumps, motors, cooling systems — squeezing more output from existing reactors and fuel, far faster than new construction.
  • Status: pending Georgia Public Service Commission approval; a Vogtle uprate was already approved in the 2025 integrated resource plan.
Why this strengthens the thesis. It is the second large-load funding template alongside the OpenAI 3.2 GW contract: hyperscalers finance their own incremental infrastructure through dedicated tariffs while residential customers are held harmless. Each such deal de-risks the political economy of the 17 GW pipeline. Watch the PSC docket — approval is expected, but the tone of the proceeding is the leading regulatory indicator.

Rate environment: multi-decade highs, then a dovish jobs print

The week was a tug-of-war. The 10-year Treasury touched 5.34% on October 1 — its highest since 2002 — as markets priced resilient growth and heavy AI-data-center borrowing competing with the Treasury for capital. Then Friday's September payrolls landed at 29K vs 84K expected, unemployment ticked to 4.2%, and the 10-year retreated to roughly 5.2%. Fed-funds futures now price only ~16% odds of an October hike (about 84% chance of unchanged), though a December hike remains the base case and 16 of 18 FOMC officials pencil in at least one more hike before year-end. Net: the near-term path got friendlier; the level remains the binding constraint on the utility multiple.

Price action and positioning

SO touched its 52-week low of $81.69 on September 29 and recovered to $83.72 by October 2 (+2.5% off the low; +1.8% since the September 27 report). Year-to-date the stock is down ~4% and ~11% over twelve months. The utility sector (XLU) actually gained 0.8% on the week — the derating is pausing, not accelerating. Nothing in the price action contradicts the fundamental picture.

Q3 earnings confirmed: November 5

Southern confirmed it will release Q3 results by 7:30 a.m. ET on Thursday, November 5, with the analyst call at 1 p.m. ET. Consensus sits around $1.63–1.65 EPS on ~$8.2–8.3B revenue (provider aggregations vary). The checkpoints: large-load conversion updates, AFUDC trajectory, the interest-expense trend, and whether the FY2026 guide tightens to the top of $4.50–$4.60.

06 | Risks

Risks: rates dominate, the rest is monitoring

Dominant

Interest rates

Utilities trade as bond proxies. The 10-year at multi-decade highs compresses the sector multiple regardless of earnings; a sustained move above 6% would take the stock lower on perfect execution. This is the single variable that can overwhelm the thesis.

Watch

Interest expense creep

Higher debt balances at higher rates already partly offset earnings growth. Each 100bp on refinanced debt is a direct EPS headwind against rate-base growth.

Watch

Regulatory tone on large load

If data-center growth ever pressures residential rates or reliability, commissions can rewrite the rules. The $21B collateral and 100%-cost-coverage contracts mitigate but do not eliminate this. The Google uprate PSC docket is the near-term read.

Watch

Data-center conversion risk

17 GW contracted is not 17 GW built. Hyperscaler capex cycles, AI-demand disappointments, or project delays would slow the capex-to-rate-base flywheel.

Structural

Southern Power drag

The competitive wholesale arm remains structurally weaker — no regulated return, recontracting risk. Small enough not to break the thesis; large enough to keep an asterisk on consolidated prints.

Watch

Storms and execution

Gulf Coast hurricanes are an annual wildcard with regulatory-lag costs. The 10 GW new-generation program must execute on budget — overruns directly reduce earned returns.
